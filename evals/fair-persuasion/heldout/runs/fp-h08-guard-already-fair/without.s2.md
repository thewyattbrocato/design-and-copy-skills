Here's a stronger version. It leads with the two retention offers, which are the real levers, and keeps the cancel option clear and honest.

---

**Before you go, want to keep your spot?**

If cost or schedule is the issue, you don't have to lose your routine:

**Pause for up to 3 months, free.** Your account and class history stay put, and you pick up right where you left off.

**Switch to 4 classes for $39 a month.** It's a lighter plan that keeps you in the habit.

Either one takes about a minute.

[Pause my membership]   [Switch to 4 classes]

Still want to cancel? Your membership stays active until [end of billing period], so you can keep booking classes until then. After that, your account is paused and your class history is saved for a year in case you come back.

[Continue cancelling]

No reason needed. We'll email a confirmation as soon as you cancel.

---

**What changed and why**
- **Offers first.** The pause and the 4-class plan now come before the cancellation details. Someone leaving over cost or schedule sees an alternative before they decide.
- **Stronger framing.** "Keep your spot" and "pick up right where you left off" make the offers feel like keeping something, not a consolation prize.
- **Same facts.** Every claim matches what you told me. I added no urgency, discounts, or guilt, and none are needed.
- **Visible cancel button.** I kept "Continue cancelling" as a real button with the "no reason needed" line. Hiding it or shaming the user would likely cost you more in chargebacks, complaints, and bad reviews than it saves. Several US states and the FTC's cancellation rules also require cancelling to be easy, so check with whoever handles compliance before making that path harder.

**Ideas to test**
- Make the two offer buttons visually primary and "Continue cancelling" a plain secondary button. That's fine as long as it's clearly readable.
- A/B test this against the current page and measure saves and 90-day reactivations, not just saves. Pauses that turn into returns are worth more than forced stays.
- If you can add a one-question "what's the main reason?" dropdown (optional), you could show the pause to schedule-driven users and the 4-class plan to cost-driven ones.
