Here's a tighter version. It leads with the two offers and keeps the cancel path clear.

---

**Before you go, can one of these work for you?**

Your membership stays active until [end of billing period], and you can book classes until then. After that, your account pauses and your class history is saved for a year, so coming back is easy.

**Pause for up to 3 months, free.** Take a break without losing your account or your history. Takes about a minute.
**[Pause my membership]**

**Switch to the 4-class plan, $39/month.** Keep going at a lower class count and price.
**[Switch to 4 classes]**

[Yes, cancel my membership]

No reason needed. We'll email you a confirmation as soon as you cancel.

---

**What changed:**
- Each offer now has a one-line benefit and a clear price or term, so people can compare them with cancelling.
- "Paused" is spelled out, so people know what happens after cancelling.
- The cancel button says exactly what it does, instead of "Continue cancelling," which can read as a trap.

**What I left out on purpose:** guilt lines ("we'll miss you"), fake urgency, or a hidden or visually buried cancel option. Those tend to produce chargebacks and complaints, and some states' auto-renewal laws (California's, for example) require cancelling to be about as easy as signing up. Have whoever handles your terms check the flow before launch.

**Before shipping:** fill in the [end of billing period] placeholder. To see whether this actually saves more people, A/B test it against the current page and track save rate alongside chargebacks and cancellation complaints.
