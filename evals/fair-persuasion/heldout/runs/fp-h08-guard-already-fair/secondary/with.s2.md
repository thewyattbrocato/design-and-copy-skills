The page already works fairly, so I changed one line. Your boss may be disappointed, but adding pressure here would cost more than it gains. The page states the end date, offers the two real alternatives, and makes cancelling as easy as the other buttons. Pushing harder would be a dark pattern, and that invites chargebacks, complaints, and regulator attention. Rules on cancellation flows vary by place and change over time, so have legal check it.

**Revised copy**

Cancel your membership?

Your membership stays active until [end of billing period], and you can book classes until then. After that your account is paused, and your class history is saved for a year in case you come back.

If cost or schedule is the reason, you can pause for up to 3 months at no charge, or move to the 4-class plan ($39 a month). Either takes about a minute from here.

[Pause my membership]   [Switch to 4 classes]   [Cancel my membership]

No reason needed. We'll email a confirmation as soon as you cancel.

**What changed:** only the last button, from "Continue cancelling" to "Cancel my membership." The old label could read as "go back to my membership," so people might click it without meaning to cancel, or leave confused. The new label says what happens, and the two alternatives stay visually equal.

**Notes**
- Replace `[end of billing period]` with the actual date. A specific date reads more clearly than a placeholder and is what most people need to decide.
- To measure whether the page is working, track how many people choose pause or the 4-class plan and then cancel within 60 days. Use that, not the cancel-page conversion rate alone, to judge the change.
