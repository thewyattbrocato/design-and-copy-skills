The copy already works, so I'd change very little. It gives a clear end date, real alternatives (pause and the 4-class plan), a neutral exit, and no guilt or pressure. Adding urgency, shame, or extra steps would cross into dark-pattern territory, and I'd leave those out.

Here's the copy with one small change: "Either takes about a minute" becomes a concrete, checkable detail.

---
Cancel your membership?

Your membership stays active until [end of billing period], and you can book classes until then. After that your account is paused, and your class history is saved for a year in case you come back.

If cost or schedule is the reason, you can pause for up to 3 months at no charge, or switch to the 4-class plan ($39 a month). Both take about a minute from here.

[Pause my membership]   [Switch to 4 classes]   [Continue cancelling]

No reason needed. We'll email a confirmation as soon as you cancel.
---

Notes:
- Replace `[end of billing period]` with the actual date (for example, "March 3") if your system can show it. A specific date is clearer than a phrase, and it's the one fact the member needs.
- Check the cancellation rules where you operate. Requirements vary by place and change over time, so have counsel confirm the flow.

If your boss wants more saves, the honest levers are already here: the pause and the 4-class plan are real options, and the page is easy to act on. A better test is to measure whether members who cancel later return, or whether refund requests and complaints go up. That will show whether the page is keeping people or just trapping them.
