Here's a tighter version. It keeps the offers front and center and makes them specific, without making cancelling harder or guilt-tripping anyone.

---

**Thinking about cancelling?**

You keep full access until [end of billing period] and can book any class until then. After that, your account pauses, and your class history stays saved for a year if you decide to come back.

**If cost or schedule is the issue, here are two options:**

- **Pause for up to 3 months, free.** Your account and history stay exactly as they are. Pick it up when your schedule settles.
- **Switch to 4 classes for $39/month.** Keep your routine at a lower price.

[Pause my membership]   [Switch to 4 classes]   [Continue cancelling]

No reason needed. You'll get a confirmation email as soon as you cancel.

---

**What changed and why:**

- **The headline is direct.** "Thinking about cancelling?" invites a conversation instead of asking for a confirmation.
- **The offers are concrete.** "Pause for up to 3 months, free" and "$39/month" are easier to act on than "at no charge" and "the 4-class plan."
- **The benefits are stated plainly.** Saying what people keep (history, spot, routine) is more persuasive than urgency language.
- **All three buttons stay equal in weight.** Keeping "Continue cancelling" as a clear, normal button protects you legally and builds trust with the people you're trying to keep.

**What I'd avoid, even though it might lift the number short-term:**

- Hiding, shrinking, or relabeling the cancel button, or adding extra steps before it.
- Guilt or pressure copy ("You'll lose everything," "Your coach will miss you").
- Fake deadlines or scarcity.

These cause chargebacks and complaints, and state auto-renewal laws (California's is the strictest) generally require cancelling to be about as easy as signing up. A quick check with whoever handles compliance is worth doing before this goes live.

To measure whether it works, A/B test this against the current copy and track saves (pauses plus plan switches) alongside chargebacks and support tickets. A save that generates a complaint or a refund request isn't a real save.
