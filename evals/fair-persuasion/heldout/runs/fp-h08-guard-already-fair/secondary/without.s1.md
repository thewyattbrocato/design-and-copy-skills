Here's a stronger version. It leads with the offers and makes them specific, and it keeps the cancel path clear.

---

**Before you go, can we make this work for you?**

Your membership stays active until [end of billing period], and you can book classes until then. After that, your account pauses and your class history is saved for a year, so coming back is easy.

If cost or schedule is the issue, here are two ways to keep your spot:

- **Pause for up to 3 months, free.** Take a break without losing your place, and restart whenever you're ready.
- **Switch to 4 classes a month for $39.** A lighter schedule at a lower price.

[Pause my membership]   [Switch to 4 classes]   [Continue cancelling]

No reason needed. You'll get a confirmation email as soon as you cancel.

---

**What changed:**
- The offers are concrete benefits ("keep your spot," "lighter schedule at a lower price") instead of generic options.
- The headline invites a conversation rather than framing cancellation as a question.
- The billing and data terms are stated plainly, so the page reads as trustworthy.

**What I'd avoid:**
- **Guilt or pressure copy** like "You'll lose everything" or "Are you sure?" repeated. It tends to produce chargebacks and complaints, which cost more than the saves are worth.
- **Making "Continue cancelling" small, grey, or hard to find.** Many states have auto-renewal laws (California's is one) that require cancelling to be as easy as signing up. Have whoever handles your terms confirm the page meets them.
