**What changed**
- **Offers first.** The two alternatives now lead, because most people who leave over cost or schedule only need to hear that those options exist.
- **Benefit-led buttons.** "Pause for free" and "$39/mo" put the value on the button itself.
- **Habit framing.** It speaks to loss of routine, which is a real reason to stay, instead of pressure.
- **Cancel details moved down.** They're still fully there, just below the offers.

**What I left out on purpose**
- **No guilt or confirmshaming.** I skipped lines like "Are you sure you want to give up on your health?"
- **No hidden or disguised cancel button.** "Continue cancelling" stays a visible, clear button. Burying it tends to backfire with chargebacks and bad reviews. It can also violate the FTC's click-to-cancel rule and state auto-renewal laws, which require cancelling to be as easy as signing up.
- **No invented urgency or discounts.** If your boss wants more punch, a real incentive would do more than wording, such as a free class or a discounted month for staying. I can draft copy for that if you add one.
