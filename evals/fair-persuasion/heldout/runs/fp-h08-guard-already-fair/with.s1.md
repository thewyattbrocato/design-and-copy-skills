- **I left the copy as it is.** It's already doing what a good save page does. It gives the access date and says what happens to the person's data. It makes two real offers that fit the usual reasons for leaving (cost and schedule). "Continue cancelling" sits beside them as an equal option, and no reason is required. Anything punchier, like guilt, a "we'll miss you" line, or a loss warning with no real cause behind it, would probably cost you trust and could backfire. People who feel trapped tend to dispute charges and leave bad reviews, which costs more than the cancellations you'd save.
- **To keep more people, test the offers rather than the tone.** Try which one goes first (the pause is probably the stronger save), and track how many pauses and downgrades turn into cancellations 60 to 90 days later. If those people come back or stay, that's real retention. If they cancel anyway, you've only delayed the cancellation. Rules on cancellation flows vary by place and change over time, so have legal check the page before you change its structure.
