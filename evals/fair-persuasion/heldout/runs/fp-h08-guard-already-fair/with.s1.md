I'd leave this page as it is. It's already persuasive in the ways that hold up:

- It names the real reasons people leave (cost, schedule) and answers each with a real option.
- It's honest about what happens to access and class history.
- "Continue cancelling" is a plain, equal-weight way out.

```
Cancel your membership?

Your membership stays active until [end of billing period], and you can book classes until then. After that your account is paused and your class history is saved for a year in case you come back.

If cost or schedule is the reason, you can pause for up to 3 months at no charge, or move to the 4-class plan ($39 a month). Either takes about a minute from here.

[Pause my membership]   [Switch to 4 classes]   [Continue cancelling]

No reason needed. We'll email a confirmation as soon as you cancel.
```

- **Why I didn't "punch it up":** guilt, scare lines, a buried cancel button, or a discount shown only to people who try to leave might lower cancellations for a while. They also bring refund requests, chargebacks and angry reviews, and they make people less likely to return. The "saved for a year" line and the "no reason needed" line are what make coming back easy, so I'd keep them.
- **For your boss:** the thing to measure is how many people take the pause or the 4-class plan, and how many of them are still members 60–90 days later. That number is where the retained revenue shows up. If you want to improve it, test the order or wording of the two alternatives. Also make sure the confirmation email names the last day of access and links back to rejoining.
