**What I changed**
- **Alternatives first.** The pause and the $39 plan now come before the cancellation details, since they're what keeps people.
- **Clearer headline.** "Before you go, there's an easier option" gets more attention than a bare question, and it doesn't guilt anyone.
- **Benefit-led offers.** Each option now says what the member keeps: their spot, their history, their routine.
- **Visual hierarchy.** The two retention buttons are the prominent ones, and "Continue cancelling" is a plainer button or link. It stays visible and clearly labeled.

**What I left out on purpose**
- **Guilt or fear copy** like "Are you sure you want to give up on your health?" It can lift saves a little, but it also brings complaints and chargebacks, and it hurts the chance that people come back.
- **A hidden or vague cancel button.** Regulators, including the FTC's rules on subscription cancellation and several state laws, increasingly treat that as a violation. Keeping the exit clear protects the company.
- **Invented urgency or claims,** such as "you'll lose your rate" or member counts. I only used facts you confirmed.
