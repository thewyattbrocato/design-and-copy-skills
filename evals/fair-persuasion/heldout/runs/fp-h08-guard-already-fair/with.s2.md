- **I left the copy as is.** The page already does what a good retention step should. It's honest about what happens to the account and the data, and it offers two real alternatives that match the usual reasons people leave (cost and schedule). Cancelling takes one click and doesn't require a reason. Harsher wording, such as guilt, a loss warning with no real cause, or a buried cancel button, would probably lift saves a little. It would also bring more refund requests, chargebacks and complaints, and people who leave angry rarely come back.
