**Verdict:** Yes, taken as a whole this is a dark pattern, usually called a "roach motel": easy to get in, hard to get out. The phone-call requirement is the main problem. The rest adds friction on top of it.

## What's fine
- **A pause offer (step 3).** It's a legitimate retention step and often helps the user. Keep it if "No thanks" is as visible as "Pause".
- **One save-offer or retention screen.** Showing one is normal practice.
- **Asking why they're leaving**, as long as it's optional.

## What isn't fine
1. **Phone-only completion (step 4) is the biggest problem.** Sign-up takes two taps online, so cancelling should work the same way. Requiring a call during Mon–Fri 9–5 is a deliberate barrier. It also shuts out people who work those hours, are deaf or hard of hearing, or are abroad.
2. **No confirmation until after the call (step 5).** Users have no proof of cancellation until then, and they have no record if the call goes wrong. They may keep getting billed.
3. **The entry point is buried (step 1).** It's the last item, a small grey link under "Billing FAQs", which makes it hard to find on purpose.
4. **The buttons are lopsided (step 2).** A big green "Keep" next to a tiny grey "Continue" is visual manipulation. "Continue" is also vague about what it does.
5. **The "3x more likely to hit their goals" stat (step 2).** It's persuasion, not information. If you can't back it up with real data, it's a deceptive claim. Even if you can, it's emotional pressure at the moment of cancelling.
6. **The required dropdown (step 4).** It makes the user pay a toll before they can leave.
7. **Three screens before the real step.** Two of them are persuasion, and the user only reaches the actual cancel step on the fourth.

## Legal risk
This is not legal advice. The FTC's "click-to-cancel" rule was struck down in 2025, but the FTC still enforces ROSCA against hard-to-cancel subscriptions. Several state auto-renewal laws, California's among them, require that you can cancel online if you signed up online. The UK and EU are tightening subscription rules too. Your flow is the kind of case these laws target, so have counsel check it.

## What to change
- **Add a "Cancel membership" button** at the same prominence as other Membership > Manage actions, with clear text.
- **Allow full cancellation in-app.** Keep the phone line as an optional alternative, not a requirement.
- **Cut the flow to one retention screen at most**, such as a combined pause-or-cancel screen. Give both choices equal visual weight, and label them clearly: "Pause instead" and "Cancel membership".
- **Make the reason question optional.** Use a skippable dropdown.
- **Remove or substantiate the 3x claim.** If you keep it, show it as neutral information, not guilt.
- **Show a final confirmation screen** with the end date and any remaining charges.
- **Send the confirmation email immediately**, with the effective date.
- **Send a reminder if the cancellation takes effect at the end of a billing cycle**, so users know when access ends.

A good test for the designer: cancelling should take about as many taps as signing up did.
