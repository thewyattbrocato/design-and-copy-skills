**Yes, this is a dark pattern, and the phone-only final step is the main problem.** Sign-up takes two taps, but cancelling requires a phone call during business hours. That gap is what regulators and app stores look for. It's also likely to be illegal in some places (see the end).

## What's fine

- **Offering a pause.** A free two-month freeze is a good alternative, and it can keep members who only need a break.
- **Asking why they're leaving.** Useful product data, as long as it's optional.
- **A retention message.** Showing the user what they'd lose is fine if the claim is true.

## What isn't

| Step | Problem |
|---|---|
| 1. Placement | Cancel is the last item, in small grey text, under "Billing FAQs". Hiding the main action this way is a classic pattern. |
| 2. Retention screen | The keep button is big and green, and the continue link is small and grey. The asymmetry is manipulative. The "3x more likely" stat also needs a source. If it's correlational or invented, it's misleading. |
| 3. Pause screen | Fine as an offer, but "No thanks" should be as prominent as "Pause." |
| 4. Required reason | Making the dropdown mandatory adds friction for no user benefit. |
| 4. Phone-only finish | This is the core problem. It's a one-way door: no online option, limited hours, and the user has to wait on hold. |
| 4. Hours | Mon–Fri 9–5 excludes weekends and most people's work hours. |
| 5. Email after the call | The user gets no record of cancelling until they've finished the call, and they may keep getting billed in the meantime. |

## What to change

1. **Move "Cancel membership" up** on the Manage screen, with a normal label and normal styling. Don't bury it under FAQs.
2. **Make the retention buttons equal.** Use two same-size buttons: [Keep membership] and [Continue to cancel]. Keep or remove the stat, but only if you can cite it.
3. **Make the pause buttons equal.** [Pause for free] and [Cancel anyway].
4. **Make the reason optional**, with a "Skip" option.
5. **Finish cancelling in the app.** Show one confirm screen: "Your membership ends on [date]. You keep access until then. [Confirm cancellation]." Include the effective date and any refund or proration policy.
6. **Send the confirmation email immediately**, with the cancellation date and a reference number.
7. **Stop billing at the right point**, and state clearly when that is.
8. **If you want a phone option**, offer it as a secondary path for people who prefer it, not as a requirement.
9. **Count the taps.** Aim for cancelling to take about as many steps as signing up.

## Legal check

Auto-renewal rules vary by jurisdiction, and I can't verify the current state of each one. Two things to confirm with counsel:

- **California's Automatic Renewal Law** requires a cancellation method at least as easy as sign-up, and online cancellation if the sign-up was online. Other U.S. states have similar rules, and the FTC has pursued this kind of case.
- **The EU and UK** have consumer rules on digital cancellation, and the UK's DMCC Act adds further requirements.

Phone-only cancellation for an online sign-up is the pattern these laws are aimed at, so it's the change to prioritise.
