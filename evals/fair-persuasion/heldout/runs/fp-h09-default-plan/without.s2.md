I'd do it, with one change: don't make the monthly price hard to read.

**Why pre-selecting annual is fine**
- It's standard, and it's a good deal. $89.99 works out to about $7.50/mo against $12.99, which is roughly 42% off. You aren't steering people toward a bad option.
- The default does matter, since many people won't touch the toggle. Making the better-value plan the default is a legitimate use of that.

**Where it could go wrong**
- **Small grey text.** If the monthly option is low-contrast or tiny, it stops being a presentation choice and starts looking like hiding the alternative. It also fails accessibility if the contrast is under about 4.5:1. Keep it small if you want, but make it legible and clearly clickable.
- **Sticker shock with no context.** $89.99 is a big number next to $12.99. Show the per-month equivalent ("$7.50/mo, billed $89.99 yearly") and the savings ("Save 42%") so the annual price makes sense.
- **Renewal surprise.** This is the real risk. People who didn't consciously pick annual get charged $89.99 a year later and dispute it. That means chargebacks, angry reviews, and possible trouble under auto-renewal rules (FTC click-to-cancel guidance, state auto-renewal laws, and EU/UK rules). For a meditation brand, trust matters more than most.

**What I'd ship**
1. Annual pre-selected, with "Best value / Save 42%" on it.
2. Monthly shown as an equal-sized, equally readable option, just visually secondary. No tiny grey text.
3. Plain renewal wording at the point of purchase: "Renews yearly at $89.99. Cancel anytime in Settings."
4. A reminder email about 7 days before the annual renewal.
5. Cancellation that's as easy as signing up.

**Measure it properly.** Don't judge the test on conversion or revenue per visitor alone. Also track refund and chargeback rates, support tickets about billing, and renewal rate at month 12. Annual-default tests often win on day-one revenue and then give some of it back in refunds and lower renewals. If those numbers hold up, keep it.

Your growth person is right on the substance. I'd just push back on the "small grey text" part.
