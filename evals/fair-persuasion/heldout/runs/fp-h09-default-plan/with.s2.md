**Partly fine.** Pre-selecting annual is a normal, legitimate default. The small grey monthly price is the part that steers, because it makes the alternative hard to see. I'd do it with a few changes.

**What I'd do**

1. **Pre-select annual and label it as recommended.** Give a reason, such as "Best value" or "Save $65.89 a year". The default is fine here because a well-informed person could sensibly pick annual.
2. **Show both plans at equal weight.** Put the monthly option beside annual as a full toggle option at $12.99/month, not as grey fine print underneath. The toggle is only a real choice if both sides are readable.
3. **Show the math honestly.**
   - Annual: **$89.99 billed once a year** (about $7.50/month).
   - Monthly: **$12.99 billed monthly**.
   - Next to any percentage, state the saving in dollars. $89.99 against 12 × $12.99 ($155.88) is $65.89, or about 42%.
4. **State the renewal terms next to the button.** For example: "Renews at $89.99 on [renewal date]. Cancel anytime in [location]." Annual charges are where people feel burned, so this is the main place regret comes from.
5. **Send a reminder before the first annual renewal.** Say when the charge will happen, and send it far enough ahead that cancelling is still easy.
6. **Make cancelling as easy as signing up.** It should work in the same place, with the same number of steps. Annual plans make that matter more.

**Why I'd do it**

Annual pre-selected usually lifts annual share and cash upfront. A person who sees both plans clearly and picks annual is also less likely to refund or churn angrily than one who didn't notice the monthly option.

**What to track**

Watch refund requests and support tickets in the first 30 days of annual plans, plus cancellations within a week of the renewal reminder. If those rise along with annual share, the page is winning by being unclear, not by being a better offer.

Rules on pricing display and auto-renewal disclosure vary by place and change over time, so have your renewal wording checked. That matters most in the US states and EU countries with strict auto-renewal laws, and under App Store and Google Play policies if you sell through them.
