I'd pre-select annual, but I wouldn't bury the monthly price. The default is fine. The grey fine print is where it goes wrong.

**Why pre-selecting annual is reasonable:** The annual plan is a real discount, about 42% less than 12 months of monthly ($155.88 vs. $89.99). Defaulting people to the better deal is common and defensible.

**Why the grey text is a problem:**
- **Regulatory risk.** Dark-pattern scrutiny targets exactly this: a default that favors the company and a de-emphasized alternative. Depending on where you sell, auto-renewal laws (California's is the strictest) require clear, conspicuous disclosure of the recurring charge and price. Small grey text is hard to defend under that standard.
- **Refund and chargeback risk.** Annual is a $90 upfront charge. Users who didn't consciously choose it are the ones who request refunds, dispute charges, and leave one-star reviews. For a wellness app, that also hurts retention.
- **Platform risk.** If you sell through the App Store or Google Play, their review teams look at how prices are presented.

**What I'd do:**
1. Pre-select annual, but show both options at comparable size. Something like "**$89.99/year** (just $7.50/mo, save 42%)" next to "**$12.99/mo**," with the selected state clearly marked.
2. Put the total charge in the button, e.g., "Start annual: $89.99 billed today, renews yearly."
3. Make the monthly option easy to pick. Don't hide the toggle.

**Test it.** Run an A/B test of default-annual against no default, but judge it on 60–90 day outcomes: net revenue after refunds and chargebacks, cancellations, and support tickets, not just conversion to annual. If the default wins on conversion but loses on refunds, the lift isn't real.

I'm not a lawyer, so have whoever handles compliance check the disclosure requirements for your markets. But my recommendation is to take the growth guy's default and reject the grey text.
