**I'd do it, but not with the monthly price in small grey text.** Defaulting to annual is a normal pricing move. Hiding the alternative is where it becomes a problem.

**Why the default is fine:** Annual is a good deal for the customer. $89.99 is about $7.50/month versus $12.99, roughly 42% off. Highlighting the better-value option is ordinary merchandising.

**Why the grey text is the risk:** Regulators and courts look at whether the price the customer will actually pay is clear before they commit. Several things can get you in trouble:
- **Auto-renewal laws** (California's is the strictest, and many states have similar rules) require clear disclosure of the recurring charge and amount before purchase. "$89.99 a year" needs to say it renews and when.
- **FTC and state AG scrutiny** of "dark patterns" and hidden pricing has been active. Making the alternative hard to see is a common example cited in enforcement.
- **EU/UK consumer law** is stricter about pre-ticked options and prominence of key terms.
- **Chargebacks and refund requests** rise when people are surprised by an annual charge. Card networks treat that as a risk signal, and it can get your merchant account reviewed.

**What I'd actually ship:**
1. Annual selected by default, and the toggle is visually balanced. Monthly is the same size and weight as the annual option, not grey fine print.
2. Lead with the real charge: "$89.99 billed once a year (about $7.50/mo)." Show the full amount and renewal date on the same screen as the button, not only on the checkout page.
3. Show the savings clearly: "Save $66 vs monthly."
4. Make sure the confirmation email and receipt state the annual amount and renewal date.

**How to settle it:** Run an A/B test with the balanced layout against the growth guy's version. Measure conversion, but also refund rate, chargebacks in the first 60 days, and cancellations or support tickets about "I didn't know I'd be charged for a year." If the grey-text version wins on conversion but loses on refunds, the conversion gain is probably borrowed from future churn and disputes.

I'm not a lawyer, and the rules differ by jurisdiction. Before launch, have counsel review the final pricing page, especially if you sell to California, the EU, or the UK.
