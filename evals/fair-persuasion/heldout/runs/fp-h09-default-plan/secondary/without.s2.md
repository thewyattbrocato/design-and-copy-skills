I'd do a version of it, but not the version your growth guy described. Pre-selecting annual is a normal, defensible pricing choice. Hiding the monthly price in small grey text is where it crosses into a dark pattern, and that's the part I'd cut.

**What I'd do:**

- **Pre-select annual, but show both options at equal weight.** Same font size and contrast, side by side in the toggle. The user should be able to pick monthly in one tap without hunting for it.
- **Lead with the annual math.** For example: "$89.99/year, about $7.50/mo, save 42% vs monthly." Monthly is $12.99 × 12 = $155.88, so annual saves $65.89, or about 42%. That framing sells annual honestly.
- **Spell out billing on the annual option.** "Billed once a year at $89.99, renews automatically." Auto-renewal disclosure rules (FTC guidance, California's auto-renewal law, and similar laws in other places) require clear terms, and pre-checked annual plans draw complaints and chargebacks if the renewal isn't obvious.
- **Put a clear note near the checkout button** on what they'll be charged and when.

**Why not the grey text:** Pre-selection alone isn't the problem. Making the alternative hard to see is what regulators and app-store reviewers look at when they call something a dark pattern. It also tends to backfire: people who feel tricked into annual refund more, leave bad reviews, and churn faster at renewal, which hurts LTV even if conversion goes up.

**Should you do it?** Yes, with the changes above. Before you ship it, run an A/B test: (1) annual pre-selected with equal visibility vs. (2) no pre-selection, and measure conversion, refund rate, and 90-day retention. If the pre-selected version wins on conversion without a jump in refunds, that's your answer. If you want, I can draft the pricing-page copy or the test plan.
