**Partly. Pre-selecting annual steers the choice, and greying out the monthly price makes the other option hard to see. I wouldn't ship it as described.**

What steers:
- Annual is chosen before the buyer picks anything, and it's the option that charges $89.99 up front.
- The monthly price in small grey text makes the second option easy to miss. That works because people don't notice it, not because they chose it.

Fair version, aimed at the same goal:
- The toggle opens with neither plan selected, or with monthly selected. Both prices appear at the same size and weight: "$12.99/month" and "$89.99/year."
- Annual gets a "Best value" label with the real math: "About $7.50/month. Saves $65.89 vs. 12 × $12.99 ($155.88)."
- Above the card field, state the renewal in the same size as "free": "Renews yearly at $89.99 on [first charge date] unless you cancel."

Annual will likely still win on its merits, and that's fine. If the growth team wants it as the default anyway, that's a judgment call, but a default that spends money is the riskier place to make it.

Track refunds and cancellations within the first 30 days beside annual conversion. If conversion rises while early refunds rise too, the lift isn't real.

Rules on renewal disclosures and pre-selected paid plans vary by place and change over time, so have counsel check the renewal wording before launch.
