**Short answer: yes, do it.** Pre-selecting annual with the monthly price shown underneath is a normal, fair pattern, and your instinct is right. It only becomes a problem if the details are done in a way that hides the monthly option.

**Why it's fine:** Annual is a plan a well-informed person could sensibly choose. The monthly option stays visible and one click away. Nothing is charged without the user pressing the button. The default steers, but it doesn't trap.

**What I'd change from the growth guy's version:**

1. **Make the monthly price readable, not small grey text.** "Small and grey" is the part that turns a default into a nudge that only works if people don't notice. Show both plans at equal weight, with the toggle clearly labelled. Pre-selecting annual is the steer, so the monthly price doesn't also need to be hard to read. It also helps accessibility: low-contrast text fails WCAG contrast guidelines.

2. **Show the real math.** $89.99/year is about $7.50/month, and it saves about $65.89 versus 12 months at $12.99 (about 42%). Put the dollar saving next to the percentage. For example: "$89.99 billed yearly ($7.50/mo). Save $65.89 vs. monthly."

3. **State the renewal terms next to the price and before the card field.** Something like: "Renews on [date] for $89.99 unless you cancel. Cancel anytime in Settings." This matters more for annual because a $90 surprise charge is what produces refund requests and chargebacks.

4. **Send a reminder before the annual renewal.** Email 7 to 14 days before it renews. This is the best protection against regret, and it tends to build trust rather than hurt retention.

5. **Make cancelling as easy as signing up.** Same place, no call or survey required. Say what happens to access, and the last day, when someone cancels.

6. **If you add a "Best value" or "Recommended" label,** give the reason ("Save 42%"). Don't add countdown timers or "price goes up Friday" unless that's actually true.

**How to test it:** If you A/B test this, don't just watch the annual take rate. Track refunds, cancellations within the first 30 days, and support complaints alongside it. If annual conversion rises but early refunds rise too, the default is pulling in people who didn't mean to commit. Also note that the fair version (readable monthly price, clear terms) may lift annual signups somewhat less than the small-grey-text version. I wouldn't promise the larger number.

**One note:** Rules on default selections and auto-renewal disclosures vary by country and state and change over time, so have the renewal wording checked, especially if you sell in the EU, UK, or California.
