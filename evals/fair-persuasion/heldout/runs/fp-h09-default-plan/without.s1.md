I'd do a modified version. Pre-selecting annual is fine and standard. The "small grey text" part is where I'd push back.

**Why pre-selecting annual is fine**
- It's the better deal for most people who stick with a meditation app. $89.99 works out to about $7.50/month, roughly 42% less than 12 months at $12.99.
- Defaulting to the plan you want most people on is ordinary pricing-page design. It isn't deceptive as long as the choice is clear.

**Where it turns into a dark pattern**
- If someone can't easily see that they're about to be charged $89.99 today, you'll get more conversions now and pay for it later in refunds, chargebacks, angry reviews, and app store complaints. Meditation-app buyers are especially sensitive to feeling tricked.
- There's also legal exposure. Auto-renewal and negative-option rules (the FTC's, California's ARL, and the EU and UK equivalents) focus on clear disclosure of price, billing frequency, and renewal. Burying the alternative in grey text works against you if anyone looks closely.

**What I'd do**
1. Pre-select annual, but make both options readable. Keep the monthly price at normal body size and contrast, not a de-emphasized footnote.
2. Lead with the real charge: "$89.99 billed once a year." Then add "just $7.50/mo" and a "Save 42%" badge as supporting detail. The per-month figure should never be the only price shown.
3. Put the renewal terms next to the button: "Renews annually at $89.99. Cancel anytime in Settings."
4. Send a renewal reminder email a week or two before the annual charge. It reduces refund requests and is required in some places.
5. Make cancelling as easy as signing up.

**How to test it**
Your growth lead is probably right that annual-by-default lifts conversion. Judge the test on more than conversion, though. Also track refund rate, chargebacks, support tickets mentioning billing, and 30/60-day cancellations. If the tiny-grey-text variant wins on conversion but loses on those, the lift isn't real. I'd expect the clear-disclosure version to capture most of the conversion gain with far fewer problems.
