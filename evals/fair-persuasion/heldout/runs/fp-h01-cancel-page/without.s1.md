Here's a draft. The page asks why they're leaving, then shows one offer matched to the answer. That works better than a wall of offers, and it gives you churn-reason data. The pause offer is marked so you can cut it cleanly if eng hasn't built it.

## Screen 1: Reason

**Headline:** Sorry to see you go.
**Subhead:** Mind telling us why? It takes 5 seconds and helps us fix what's not working.

- It's not saving me enough money
- I'm not using the meal plans each week
- The meals don't fit my household or diet
- Scanning receipts is a hassle
- I'm taking a break / my schedule changed
- Something else

*(Optional free-text field: "Anything else we should know?")*

## Screen 2: Matched offer

**Money:**
> **Pennywise Pantry costs $9/month, or $79/year.** That's about $6.60/month if you switch to annual. Most members spend more than that on one impulse-buy trip. Want to keep the savings going for less?
> **[Switch to annual, $79/year]** **[No thanks, continue canceling]**

*(Only use this if the annual plan really is a better deal for them. Don't push it at someone who's leaving over price after two months. If you want to offer a discount, that's a business decision I can't make for you, so I left it out.)*

**Not using it / taking a break / schedule changed:** *(requires the pause feature)*
> **Need a breather? Pause instead.**
> Pause your subscription for 1, 2, or 3 months. You won't be billed, and your receipt history and saved preferences will be waiting when you're back. We'll email you 3 days before you resume.
> **[Pause my subscription]** **[No thanks, continue canceling]**

**Meals don't fit:**
> **Let's get it right.**
> Tell us about your household (dietary needs, picky eaters, how many people you cook for) and we'll tune your plan before your next billing date. Your plan isn't one-size-fits-all.
> **[Update my preferences]** **[No thanks, continue canceling]**

**Receipt hassle:**
> **That's useful to hear.** Scanning should take under a minute. *[Insert the real tip or shortcut here, e.g. "forward your e-receipt to…" if that exists.]*
> **[Show me the faster way]** **[No thanks, continue canceling]**

**Something else:** skip the offer and go straight to confirmation.

## Screen 3: Confirmation

**Headline:** Cancel your subscription?
**Body:** You'll keep full access until **[billing period end date]**. After that, your meal plans stop. We'll keep your receipt history for **[X days]** in case you come back.
**Primary button:** Yes, cancel subscription
**Secondary link:** Actually, keep my subscription

## Screen 4: After canceling

**Headline:** You're all set. Your subscription is canceled.
**Body:** You have access through **[date]**. If you change your mind, you can restart any time from Settings, and your history will be there.
**Link:** Restart my subscription

## Notes

- **If pause isn't built:** drop the "break" offer entirely and send those users to confirmation. Don't write the copy as if pause exists and fix it later. You could also fake it with a "remind me in 2 months" email, but only if you say that's what it is.
- **Fill the brackets with real numbers.** The access end date and data retention window are the most important, because vague cancel pages erode trust and cause chargeback disputes.
- **Keep "continue canceling" as an equally visible button.** Burying it might lift save rates briefly, but it generates angry reviews and refund requests.
- **Month 2-3 churn is probably a habit problem, not a price problem.** Test pause and preference-tuning against the price offer. Also consider an in-app nudge around day 30 and 60, before anyone reaches this page.
- Check with legal/billing on auto-renewal rules. Some jurisdictions require cancellation to be as easy as sign-up.
