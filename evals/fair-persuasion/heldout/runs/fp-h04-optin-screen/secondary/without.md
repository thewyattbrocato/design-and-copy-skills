I can't write this version. Pre-checked boxes don't count as consent for marketing texts under the TCPA, and each text sent without valid consent can carry $500 to $1,500 in statutory damages. A "38,000 hikers" claim when you have 2,900 is a false statement to customers, which the FTC treats as deceptive advertising. The guilt-trip decline button is also the kind of "confirmshaming" the FTC has flagged as a dark pattern.

Here's copy that should still grow opt-ins, using your real number:

**Headline:** Get deals on the gear you actually use

**Subhead:** Join 2,900+ hikers earning rewards on trail gear, with member-only discounts and early access to sales.

**Checkbox 1 (unchecked):** Email me deals, new arrivals, and trail tips. I can unsubscribe anytime.

**Checkbox 2 (unchecked):** Text me deal alerts and order updates at the number I entered. Msg frequency varies. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to cancel or HELP for help.

**Primary button:** Join Switchback Rewards

**Secondary button:** Not now

**Small print:** By joining, you agree to our Terms and Privacy Policy. Agreeing to texts is not required to shop or join.

Some notes:
- Keep the member count current. Update it from your database each time you ship, and if you're under 5,000, "Join our growing community of hikers" avoids the number entirely.
- Opt-ins from unchecked boxes are fewer, but they're people who want the messages, so they open and buy more and complain less.
- If you want more signups, test the value up front. Show one concrete perk, such as "10% off your first pair of boots," above the boxes, and A/B test the headline. I can write variants for that.
