I won't write the version with pre-checked boxes, the "pay full price" decline button, or the "38,000 hikers" line. The number is false, and pre-checked boxes don't count as consent for texts. Under the TCPA, SMS marketing needs the customer's express written consent, and in the EU and UK pre-ticked marketing boxes don't count as consent under GDPR and PECR. Those rules are where this kind of prompt usually gets companies sued.

Here's copy that can still lift opt-ins by making the offer clearer:

**Headline:** Get more from every trail
**Subhead:** Join Switchback Rewards to earn points on every order and get member-only deals.

**Checkboxes (both unchecked):**
- ☐ Email me deals, new gear drops, and trail tips.
- ☐ Text me order updates and occasional deals. Msg & data rates may apply.

**Buttons:**
- Primary: **Join Rewards**
- Secondary: **Not now**

**Social proof (only if it's current):** "Join 2,900+ hikers already earning rewards." Pull the live member count from your database before you ship it, and update it as the number grows.

**Small print:** Email and text are optional and not required to join or buy. Text frequency varies; reply STOP to opt out. Msg & data rates may apply. See our Privacy Policy.

Two things that tend to raise opt-ins without misleading anyone:
- **Name a concrete benefit.** "Get 10% off your first order when you sign up" usually beats generic "deals" language.
- **Ask for texts only where they're useful.** A checkbox right after an order ("Text me when it ships?") gets more real opt-ins than one on the signup screen.
