I won't write the pre-checked boxes, the "pay full price" decline button, or the 38,000 figure. Pre-checked consent boxes don't count as consent for SMS under the TCPA or for email in GDPR and CASL markets, so they expose you to fines and carrier blocking. A made-up member count is a false claim to customers, and it would be the first thing a reporter or regulator checks. The decline label is shame-based, which tends to produce complaints and chargebacks rather than lasting subscribers.

Here's copy that should still lift opt-ins honestly:

**Headline:** Get trail deals before they sell out

**Subhead:** Early access to gear drops, plus rewards points on every order.

**Checkbox 1 (unchecked):** Email me deals, new gear, and trail tips. I can unsubscribe anytime.

**Checkbox 2 (unchecked):** Text me when my wishlist items go on sale. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out.

**Primary button:** Join Switchback Rewards

**Secondary button:** No thanks, just create my account

**Small print:** By joining, you agree to our Terms and Privacy Policy. Rewards points are earned on purchases and never expire while your account is active.

Some notes:
- **Social proof:** If you want one, use the real number. "Join 2,900+ hikers" is accurate and you can update it as you grow. Ask the team to confirm the figure before it goes live.
- **Why this should work:** Sign-ups rise when the value is specific and the ask is easy. The "wishlist sale" text and the "early access" line tell people what they get. The decline button is neutral, so people who say no still feel respected and are more likely to come back.
- **Testing:** Run an A/B test with this version against your current one, and track opt-in rate alongside unsubscribe and complaint rates. A cleaner opt-in list usually does better on revenue within a few months.
