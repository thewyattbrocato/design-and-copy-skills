I'll write this, but I changed three things, and each one would likely cost you more than it gains.

- **Pre-checked boxes:** For texts, pre-checked consent generally doesn't count as valid consent under the TCPA. Each unconsented text can carry $500–$1,500 in statutory damages, and that's the usual trigger for class actions. GDPR and several state laws reject pre-checked boxes too. Even for email, opt-ins that aren't deliberate tend to produce spam complaints, which hurt your deliverability.
- **"38,000 hikers":** That's a fabricated claim, and it's the kind the FTC and state AGs go after. I used the real number instead. "2,900+" still works as social proof for a trail-gear community, since it reads as a tight group.
- **"No thanks, I'd rather pay full price":** I kept the idea of a decline button that nudges, but made it a light, honest version. Shaming wording tends to annoy people, and it's a flagged dark pattern in some jurisdictions.

**Screen copy**

**Headline:** Get first dibs on gear deals
**Subhead:** Join 2,900+ Switchback hikers who hear about member-only sales before everyone else.

**Checkboxes (both unchecked by default):**
- ☐ Email me deals, new gear drops, and trail tips
- ☐ Text me flash sales and early access (up to 4 msgs/month)

**Primary button:** Sign me up for deals
**Decline button (text link):** Maybe later

**Small print:** By checking a box, you agree to receive recurring marketing messages from Switchback Rewards. Consent isn't required to join. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to cancel texts, or unsubscribe from any email at any time.

**Ways to raise opt-ins legitimately**
- Lead with a concrete incentive, like "10% off your first order" or "double points this week for subscribers." That does far more than a pre-checked box.
- Show the prompt right after a points redemption or a first purchase, when people feel good about the app.
- Let people pick a frequency, such as "weekly deals only." Many who would decline everything will accept a limited feed.
- Run the text opt-in as its own step after email, so each ask stays simple.
