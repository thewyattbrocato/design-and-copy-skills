I'll skip two of those: the fake "38,000" figure and the pre-checked boxes. The fake number is a false advertising claim, and it's easy to check (app store install counts, press, a competitor). Pre-checked boxes don't count as valid consent for marketing texts under the TCPA, or for email under GDPR and CASL. That exposes you to real legal risk, and it gets you a list full of people who never wanted it, which means spam complaints and unsubscribes. The "I'd rather pay full price" button is legal, but it tends to annoy people, so I've swapped in a milder version.

Here's copy that should still lift opt-ins:

**Headline**
Get first dibs on trail gear deals

**Subhead**
Members-only drops, early access to sales, and point bonuses, sent only when there's something worth hiking for.

**Checkboxes (unchecked by default)**
- ☐ Email me deals and early access
- ☐ Text me flash sales and point bonuses (up to 4 msgs/month)

**Buttons**
- Primary: **Get the deals**
- Secondary (text link, not a shaming line): **Maybe later**

**Small print**
Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to cancel texts, or unsubscribe from any email. See our Privacy Policy and Terms.

**Ways to raise opt-ins without deceiving anyone**
- **Make the benefit concrete.** "Get 200 bonus points when you turn on texts" beats any social-proof line.
- **Use a true social-proof line.** "Join 2,900+ hikers getting our deals" is a small number, but it's real. Specific, honest numbers often read as more credible than round big ones.
- **Make one button the clear primary action.** Give "Get the deals" a bold fill and "Maybe later" a plain link. That alone shifts a lot of clicks.
- **Ask at a good moment.** Prompt right after a reward is earned or redeemed, when people are feeling good about the app.
- **Add a single "Email + text" option.** Keep it unchecked, but let one tap select both.
- **Gentle loss framing is fine.** "Don't miss the next member-only sale" is honest and still creates urgency.
