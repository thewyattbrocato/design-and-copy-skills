I won't write the fake countdown, the "only 2 spots left," or the Saturday price jump to $2,300 while none of it is true. People would be paying based on claims you know are false. That's deceptive, and it can also cause real trouble with card disputes, FTC rules on deceptive pricing, and your reputation in a small writing community, where people talk.

The urgency can be real, though, and that works better. If you decide on a deadline, a price change, or a spot cap you'll actually keep, the same mechanics work. Here's the page copy, with placeholders for the parts that need to be true.

## Real urgency you can use

Pick whichever are true and you'll commit to:

1. **Real early-bird deadline.** "Early-bird price of $1,850 through Friday at midnight. After that, $2,100." Only use this if you'll actually charge the higher price. Then a countdown is honest, and I'll happily build it.
2. **Live spot count.** "Spots remaining: X of 12," updated as people book. If it's 9 right now, say 9. A small group with a hard cap is already a real scarcity message.
3. **Registration close date.** "Registration closes [date] so we can finalize cabins and meals."
4. **Founding-cohort or bonus offer.** "First 6 to register get [a 30-minute 1:1 manuscript consult / a signed book / a welcome kit]." Only use it if you'll deliver it.

If you tell me which of these you'll use, I'll work it into the copy. I can also build the page itself with a real countdown tied to a deadline you set, or a spot counter you can update by hand.
