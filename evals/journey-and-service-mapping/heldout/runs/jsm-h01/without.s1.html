Becoming a member: from first Google to month three

What we heard from six new members over the last month. Where it goes well, and where people get stuck or nearly leave.

Stage 1

Finding us

First Google or Instagram
Mixed

What happens

  • Most found us through a friend's Instagram post or a local search

Pain point

  • 2 of 6 almost didn't come because class times on our website were out of date
Stage 2

Trial class

$35 intro session
High point

What works

  • Nearly everyone loved it
  • The teacher made them feel it was okay to be bad at it
Stage 3

Deciding

After the trial
Friction

Pain points

  • 4 of 6 confused by the membership options page
  • Unsure whether the monthly plan includes glaze and firing
  • 1 never got a follow-up email and joined only because a friend nudged her

Outcome

  • 2 joined on the spot at the front desk
  • 4 went home to think about it
Stage 4

First month

Settling in
Mixed

What works

  • They love the open studio hours

Pain points

  • 3 felt in the way of the regulars
  • 2 couldn't figure out the shelf system for unfinished pieces
  • 2 weren't told firing takes 2–3 weeks and thought their pieces were lost
Stage 5

Month three

Renew or drift away
At risk

Reasons for not renewing

  • 2 thinking of leaving because of time
  • 1 felt she wasn't getting better

Ideas to discuss on Thursday

Starting points drawn from the feedback above, not decisions.

Website: check class times and keep them current.
Membership page: say plainly what the monthly plan includes (glaze? firing?).
Follow-up: make sure everyone who doesn't join on the spot gets an email.
Firing: tell people it takes 2–3 weeks when they drop off a piece.
Shelves: a quick walkthrough of the shelf system on a member's first visit.
Regulars: a way to help new members feel welcome in open studio.
Month 3: a check-in on progress and schedule before renewal comes up.
Source: conversations with six new members, last month. Small sample, so read the counts as themes rather than percentages.
