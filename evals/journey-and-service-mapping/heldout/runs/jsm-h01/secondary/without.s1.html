What it's like to become a member

From the first Google search to the third month, as six new members described it to us over the last month.

Based on conversations with 6 new members. Small sample; treat the counts as signals, not measurements.

1

Finding us

Before the first visit

  • Mostly a friend's Instagram post or a local search.
  • 2 of 6 nearly didn't come: our site's class times were out of date.
2

Trial class

$35, first visit

  • Nearly everyone loved it.
  • The teacher made them feel it's okay to be bad at it. That's the moment people stayed.
3

Choosing a plan

After the trial

  • 4 of 6 were confused by the membership options page.
  • Most common question: does the monthly plan include glaze and firing?
4

Joining

Front desk or at home

  • 2 joined on the spot at the front desk.
  • 4 went home to think it over.
  • One of those never got a follow-up email and only joined after a friend nudged her.
5

First months

Weeks 1 to 12

  • Love the open studio hours.
  • 3 of 6 felt in the way of regulars.
  • 2 of 6 couldn't work out the shelf system for unfinished pieces.
  • 2 of 6 thought their pieces were lost during firing.

Keep

What's working

  • The $35 trial is doing its job. People leave wanting more.
  • Teachers set a tone where being a beginner is fine.
  • Open studio hours are what members came for.
Fix

Where to start

  • Put the glaze and firing answer on the membership page, plainly.
  • Update class times on the website now.
  • Send a follow-up email to everyone who took a trial.
  • Tell new members up front that firing takes 2 to 3 weeks.
  • Label the unfinished-piece shelves and brief regulars on open-studio etiquette.
