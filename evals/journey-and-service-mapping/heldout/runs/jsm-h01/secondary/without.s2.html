The journey

Stage 1

Discovering us

Friction

Most found us through a friend's Instagram post or a local search.

  • Class times on the website were out of date.
  • Two nearly didn't come because of it.
Stage 2

The $35 trial class

Working well

Nearly everyone loved it. The teacher made them feel it was okay to be bad at it.

Stage 3

Deciding to join

Friction
  • The membership options page confused four of six. It wasn't clear whether the monthly plan includes glaze and firing.
  • Two joined on the spot at the front desk.
  • Four went home to think. One never got a follow-up email and only joined when a friend nudged her.
Stage 4

First month

Friction

They love open studio hours, but:

  • Three felt they were in the way of the regulars.
  • Two couldn't figure out the shelf system for unfinished pieces.
Stage 5

Waiting on finished work

Friction

Firing takes 2 to 3 weeks. Nobody told two of them that, so they thought their pieces were lost.

Stage 6

Around month three

Friction
  • Two were thinking of not renewing because of time.
  • One was thinking of not renewing because she felt she wasn't getting better.

For discussion

  1. Can we keep class times on the website current, and who owns that?
  2. Can the membership page state plainly what the monthly plan includes, glaze and firing included?
  3. Who sends the follow-up to people who take the trial and go home to think, and can every one of them get it?
  4. How do we help new members settle in alongside regulars during open studio hours?
  5. Can the shelf system for unfinished work be explained on signage or at the first visit?
  6. Do new members hear the 2 to 3 week firing timeline before their first piece goes in?
  7. What would help members see their progress by month three?
