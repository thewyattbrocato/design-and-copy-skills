Honest answer: it's a decent skeleton, but I wouldn't present it to a VP as it stands. It reads like a happy-path funnel, not a journey map.

**The biggest problem: every emotion is a smiley face.**
- A journey map is useful because it shows where the experience breaks. A line of 😀 from awareness to advocacy tells the VP nothing and will look naive. Anyone who knows meal kits will ask, "Where's the churn?"
- Meal kits have a famously leaky retention stage. Many customers sign up on a promo, cancel within weeks, and complain about price, repetitive menus, packaging waste, or the hassle of skipping a week. Your map has no moment of frustration, so it hides the problems the business most needs to solve.
- "Loyal" and "Delighted" are assumed, not shown. Nothing says why Brenda refers anyone.

**Other gaps**
- **No evidence.** Is this based on interviews, survey data, support tickets, or cohort data? If it's team assumptions, say so, or better, back it with a few real numbers like conversion rate, week-4 retention, and NPS.
- **Each stage is one line.** Add what Brenda is thinking, what touchpoints she uses, what pain points she hits, and a metric per stage.
- **The persona is thin.** "Loves cooking, short on time" is an interesting tension, but the map never uses it. Does she want fast meals or enjoyable ones? What does she do on a Wednesday night when she's tired and the box is sitting there? Also consider whether one persona is enough.
- **Stages are missing or oversimplified.** Think about weekly menu selection (decision fatigue), delivery problems, skipping or pausing, and cancellation. For a subscription business, those are where the experience is won or lost.
- **The opportunities are generic.** "Improve personalization, enhance the app, increase engagement" could apply to any company. They aren't tied to a stage, a pain point, or an outcome, and they aren't prioritized. A VP will ask what to do first, what it costs, and what it moves.

**What I'd do before next week**
1. Add a real emotional curve with dips. Likely candidates are checkout (price shock, forced signup), unboxing (too much packaging), the first cook (takes longer than the stated time), week 3 to 4 (menu fatigue), and cancellation.
2. Add a pain points row and a metrics row under each stage.
3. Cut the opportunities down to 3 or 4 specific ones, each tied to a stage. For example: "Week-4 drop-off: test a rotating 'quick 20-minute' menu to reduce menu fatigue. Target: +X% retention."
4. State where the data comes from. If you don't have any, make a recommendation to gather it part of your pitch.

The structure is fine and the persona is a reasonable starting point. The work left is making it honest and specific enough to drive a decision. If you want, paste in any data or research you have and I can help rework it into a stronger draft.
