**Verdict: not ready to present as a finding.** It's a readable sketch of a funnel with happy faces on it. The VP will ask what you learned from it, and right now it can't answer.

**What works**
- It covers the full lifecycle, from first ad to referral, which is a sensible span for a subscription business.
- It's short and easy to scan.
- The persona is tied to the business, so the map is about a real kind of customer.

**What to fix, most serious first**

1. **The feelings carry no information.** Every phase is positive, with no dips and no source. Real journeys have friction, so a map with seven smiles reads as a wish, not an observation. The VP is likely to ask "where do people get frustrated?" and you have no answer. Show where feeling dips, mark each dip as evidence-based or suspected, and name the check for each suspected one.

2. **The phases are your funnel, not Brenda's steps.** Awareness, Consideration, Purchase, Retention and Advocacy describe what marketing measures. Brenda's steps would sound like "sees an ad that makes cooking look easy," "checks whether the meals fit a weeknight," "opens the box and checks what's there," "decides whether to reorder." Those steps show where the business can act.

3. **The persona's defining trait is missing.** Brenda is "short on time," yet time never appears in any phase. Usage says "cooks the meals, Satisfied," with no mention of the weeknight time crunch. That is probably the most important place for the map to show friction, and it's absent.

4. **Almost no content.** Each phase has one action and one feeling. Missing are what Brenda thinks or asks, the touchpoints (the app, emails, the box, the recipe card), and what goes wrong. Without these, the map has nothing to act on.

5. **The opportunities are generic and detached.** "Improve personalization," "enhance the app" and "increase engagement" don't say which phase they belong to, what would change, who owns it, or how you'd measure it. "Enhance the app" isn't an opportunity at all. Attach each one to a specific moment and give it an owner, a measure and a date.

6. **No framing or status line.** The map doesn't say what decision it supports, where the story starts and ends, or whether it's an assumption draft or built from research. If you present it as-is, the VP may assume it's based on customer data it isn't based on. Label it honestly. Something like: "Assumption draft from the brief, to be checked against [sources]."
