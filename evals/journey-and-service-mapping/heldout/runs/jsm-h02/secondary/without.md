**Short answer: it's a solid skeleton, but as written it won't hold up in front of a VP.** The structure is right. The content is too rosy and too thin to support decisions.

**What works**
- It covers the full lifecycle, from awareness through advocacy.
- Brenda is a concrete persona with a clear role and a stated trait (short on time).
- Pairing each stage with a feeling is the right instinct.

**What will get picked apart**
- **Every stage is 😀.** No real customer feels only positive emotions across seven stages. A VP will read this as wishful thinking, and it hides where the business is actually losing people. A journey map is useful because it shows friction.
- **No pain points.** Where does Brenda hesitate, get confused, or feel annoyed? Likely candidates: price versus grocery store, too much packaging, a recipe that runs long on a weeknight, a box she didn't want that week, trouble skipping or pausing, and the cancel flow.
- **Her time constraint never shows up.** She's "short on time," but Usage has no mention of prep time, cleanup, or whether the recipes fit a weeknight. That's the most important thing for this persona, and the map ignores it.
- **No touchpoints or channels.** Instagram is the only channel mentioned. The map should show where each stage happens: email, app, box insert, delivery notification, reviews site.
- **No evidence.** "Loyal" and "Delighted" are assumptions unless they're backed by data or interviews. Mark what's assumed versus measured.
- **Opportunities are generic and unlinked.** "Improve personalization" and "enhance the app" don't say which stage they fix, what problem they solve, or how you'd know it worked. Each one needs a stage, a pain point, and a metric (for example, "skip rate in Retention," "time to first cook in Onboarding," or "cancellations after the second box").

**Before you present, I'd do this:**
1. Add 1–2 pain points and a negative or neutral emotion per stage. Be honest about where the emotion dips.
2. Add a touchpoint row and the channel for each stage.
3. Add one metric per stage. Use real numbers if you have them: conversion, churn, skip rate, NPS, reviews.
4. Rewrite the opportunities as stage-linked bets: problem, proposed change, metric, and rough effort.
5. Pull the persona's goals and frustrations from real customer interviews or support tickets, if you have them. If you don't, say that the persona is a working hypothesis.

Rewriting the opportunities is the highest-leverage change, because that's what the VP will act on. If you paste your team's real data or feedback, I can draft a revised map with pain points, metrics, and linked opportunities. I'd keep the emotion line but make it honest.
