**Verdict: not ready for the VP in its current form.** The idea is sound, but the map reads as a marketing funnel with journey labels, and a VP will likely see that. The most important gap is that the persona is "short on time," and time never appears on the map.

**What works**
- You have a persona and a clear sequence from first ad to referral.
- The Onboarding and Usage phases describe things the person actually does.

**What to fix, most serious first**

1. **Phases come from your funnel, not Brenda's steps.** Awareness, Consideration, Purchase, Retention, and Advocacy are the company's stages. Name the phases by what she is doing, for example "Decide whether it fits a busy week" or "Decide each week what to skip."

2. **The feelings are not evidence.** Every phase is 😀 and the story is smooth. Nobody measured these, and a VP will ask where they came from. Real journeys have friction, so a map with no friction looks like a pitch. Use one or two feeling words per phase, and mark them as assumptions unless you have research behind them. "Loyal" and "Delighted" describe outcomes, not feelings.

3. **The map has no pain points, which is the core of the work.** The likely friction points are the ones the business cares about: cook time versus her weeknight reality, the weekly choice to skip or keep a box, price against alternatives, and cancellation. Without these, there is no reason to change anything.

4. **Missing framing and status lines.** There is no statement of whose map this is, where it starts and ends, or what decision it supports. There is also no statement of what it is built on. Right now the persona's age and name are invented unless you have research behind them.

5. **Opportunities are generic and not tied to moments.** "Improve personalization," "enhance the app," and "increase engagement" could apply to any company. "Enhance the app" is not an opportunity at all. The app also never appears as a touchpoint in any phase, so it is not grounded in the map. Each opportunity should attach to a specific moment, start with a verb, and have an owner, a measure, and a date.

6. **Exits are hidden.** A funnel that only goes forward cannot show where people leave. Suspected exits to check include the first box, the second or third week of skipping, and cancellation.

**A possible restructure (assumption draft, to be corrected by people close to customers)**

Frame: Brenda, a busy home cook, from first ad to staying or leaving. Decision: which moments to fix first.

1. See the meal kit in an Instagram ad (Curious)
2. Check whether it fits a busy week (Unsure, suspected)
3. Compare plans and reviews against alternatives (Interested)
4. Commit and pay (Hopeful)
5. Unpack and sort the first box (Happy)
6. Cook the first weeknight meal (**marked: cook time may exceed her evening; suspected**)
7. Decide each week whether to skip or keep a box (**marked: decision with no clear signal; suspected**)
8. Stay or cancel (Undecided, suspected)

Before the VP meeting, you need to do three things:
- Replace the assumed feelings with data from reviews, support tickets, cancellation reasons, or interviews, and state the source on the map.
- Mark one or two moments that matter, with a reason for each.
- Rewrite the opportunities as testable hypotheses with an owner and a date.
