Honestly, it's a decent skeleton but not ready for a VP. Right now it reads as a description of what you hope happens, not a map of what customers experience.

**What works**
- The seven stages are clear and in a sensible order.
- A named persona gives people something to picture.
- It's easy to read at a glance.

**What will get picked apart**

1. **Every emotion is positive.** All seven stages are 😀, so there's no emotional curve, and the curve is the point of a journey map. A VP will notice immediately, and it makes the whole thing look like marketing copy instead of research. Meal kits are known for high early churn, box fatigue, skipped weeks, and painful cancellation flows. None of that appears.

2. **There are no pain points or drop-offs.** Where do people abandon checkout? What happens when a box arrives late, an ingredient is missing, or a recipe takes 60 minutes instead of 30? For a persona defined as "short on time," that last one is the biggest risk, and it isn't mentioned.

3. **The stages describe activities, not experiences.** "Receives emails and offers" is a channel, not a customer moment. Retention is really the decision to keep, skip, or cancel each week, and that's where your revenue is.

4. **There are no touchpoints, metrics, or owners.** Nothing says which team owns which stage, what you measure (conversion, first-box repeat rate, skip rate, referral rate), or where things stand today.

5. **The opportunities are generic.** "Improve personalization, enhance the app, increase engagement" could apply to any company. They aren't tied to a stage, a specific problem, or evidence, and they aren't prioritized. A VP will ask, "What do we do first, and what's it worth?"

6. **The persona is thin.** Age and job title don't tell you much. What are her goals, her frustrations, her alternatives (HelloFresh, grocery delivery, takeout), and how does she decide?

7. **There's no evidence.** If this came from surveys, interviews, or analytics, say so. If it's assumption, label it as hypotheses.

**How to fix it before next week**
- Add rows for **actions, touchpoints, pain points, and emotion**. Plot the emotion as a line that dips, for example at checkout friction, the first cook when it takes longer than expected, week 3 to 4 fatigue, and cancellation.
- Pull in whatever real data you have: churn by week, NPS or review themes, support ticket reasons, and funnel drop-off.
- Rewrite the opportunities as specific, stage-linked bets, such as "Reduce week-4 skips with a 20-minute-meals filter," each with an expected impact and a rough priority.
- Add a second short persona or a "churned Brenda" scenario to show you understand failure modes.
