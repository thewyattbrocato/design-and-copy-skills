**Short answer:** it's a tidy template, but it isn't ready for a VP. It looks finished and doesn't yet say anything. A VP will ask "so what do we do?" and "how do you know?", and this can't answer either.

**What works**
- It has one clear persona, and the stages run in a sensible order.
- Putting a feeling on every stage is the right instinct.

**Problems, most serious first**

1. **No scope, no status, no decision.** Nothing says what the map is for or what the VP should decide after seeing it. Nothing says whether it comes from interviews, survey data, or your team's guesses. If it's guesses, say so. If it's research, cite it. Without that, people will assume it's invented.

2. **Every feeling is a smiley, and they rise in a straight line.** Curious, interested, excited, happy, satisfied, loyal, delighted. A journey with no friction doesn't need a map. A VP will read it as "nobody looked at the real experience." Meal-kit customers do have rough spots: a late or warm box, a skipped-week deadline you forgot, recipes that take 50 minutes when you promised 30, food waste, and the cancel flow. Retention in your business is where people leave, and here it's "Loyal 😀".

3. **The phases are your funnel, not Brenda's steps.** Awareness through Advocacy describes what the company wants. Brenda's steps are closer to "Wonder if it's worth the price," "Pick meals for the week," "Get home to a box on the porch," and "Decide whether to skip next week." Those are where the real decisions happen.

4. **The touchpoints are generic, and nothing is behind them.** "Visits website" and "gets emails" would fit any subscription business. There's no sign of what Brenda is thinking, what goes wrong, or what your teams do at each stage (fulfillment, delivery, support, lifecycle email). If the point is to show a VP where teams hand off, it needs that.

5. **The opportunities are slogans.** "Improve personalization, enhance the app, increase engagement" aren't tied to any moment, and no one could be assigned them. A VP can't approve or reject them.

6. **Nothing is marked as the moment that matters.** All seven stages get equal weight, so the map doesn't say where to focus.

**What I'd do before next week**
- Add one framing line, for example: "Brenda, from first seeing the ad to her decision to keep or cancel after box 4, to decide where to invest this quarter." Add a status line under it.
- Rename the phases from her side, and keep to five to eight.
- Replace the smileys with honest feelings, including mixed ones ("hopeful but wary of the price," "stressed Wednesday night"). Don't plot a curve unless you have survey data behind it.
- Add a "what goes wrong" row. Pull from support tickets, cancellation reasons, and skip rates to fill it, and cite them.
- Mark one or two moments, such as the first-box-to-first-skip window or the cancel decision, with a reason for each.
- Replace the three opportunities with up to three hypotheses: "At [moment], doing [change] should [effect], measured by [metric] by [date]," each with an owner.

I can rebuild it that way as an assumption draft your team can correct. Without your data I'd have to mark the pain points as suspected, not as findings. Do you have any research, such as support tickets, cancellation reasons or churn by week? That would turn a plausible draft into one you can defend.
