The map is strong. Each stage has a feeling and a number, and the "behind the scenes" line explains each problem. Here is what I'd change before you walk in, most important first.

**1. Check the June check-in date.**
Your data is from April to May, and the board meeting is tomorrow. If that's October, a "first check-in: June board meeting" is either already past or a typo for next June. A board member will notice, so fix it or say which June you mean.

**2. Add a success measure to the check-in.**
"First check-in" doesn't say what you'll report. Each opportunity maps to a number you already collected:
- **Free, no sign-up:** fewer people asking staff or searching online "is it free."
- **Door sign:** fewer parents trying the locked front door. You counted 5, so you have a baseline.
- **ID policy:** fewer people leaving without borrowing. The baseline is 3 of 8.

**3. Make the ask explicit.**
Opportunity 3 (utility bill or temporary card) is likely a policy change, and the board may need to approve it. Opportunities 1 and 2 are things staff can just do. Say that directly, and state what you want from the board tomorrow. Also match each owner to its opportunity in writing. "Marketing, facilities, branch manager" currently reads as an unmapped list.

**4. Say why the opportunities are in this order.**
They read as ranked, but the evidence ranks them differently. Opportunity 3 affects the most visible loss, 3 of 8 leaving without a book. Opportunity 1 affects all 8. Opportunity 2 is the cheapest fix. Tell the board whether the order means impact, effort, or sequence, because they'll probably ask.

**5. Tighten the numbers.**
- "5 parents tried the front door" needs a denominator. Five out of how many observed over the 3 Saturdays?
- "3 of 8 left without borrowing" raises the question of how many of the 8 wanted to borrow. If only 4 did, it's 3 of 4, which is a stronger point.
- The 8 interviewees were people who showed up. You haven't heard from people who saw the flyer and stayed home because they couldn't tell if it was free. That may be your biggest gap, and it strengthens opportunity 1. Say it before someone asks.
- Call it what it is: qualitative findings from a small sample, not a measured rate.

**6. Optional: lead with the songs.**
7 of 8 named the songs unprompted. Open with "the program works, and these three things are in its way." That is a better frame for a board than a list of problems.
