The map is clear and the evidence tags are a strength. These are the changes I'd make, most important first.

**1. Make the ask explicit for the board.**
- Opportunity 3 (accept a utility bill or temporary card) is the fix for your most painful moment, and it's probably a board-level policy decision. But the owners are marketing, facilities, and the branch manager, so nobody owns it.
- Say what you need from the board, for example "approve a temporary card for new residents, or delegate it to the branch manager." Otherwise 1 and 2 will happen and 3 will quietly stall.

**2. State the limits of the evidence up front.**
- Eight exit interviews and three Saturdays is a small sample, so present it as a pattern worth acting on, not a measured rate. Use "3 of 8" and avoid percentages.
- You only spoke to people who came. Parents who saw the flyer, wondered if it was free, and stayed home aren't in your data. That makes opportunity 1 likely more important than the map shows, so it's worth saying aloud.
- Give a denominator for "5 parents tried the locked door first": 5 of how many you watched?

**3. Cover the "Behind the scenes" gaps.**
- That row names no Saturday door owner and a paper-only form, but no opportunity addresses either. A board member may ask about them.
- Either add them as opportunities 4 and 5, or say you're deliberately leaving them out for now and why.

**4. Explain the ordering.**
- Say what "in order" means: impact, ease, or sequence. Opportunities 1 and 2 are cheap and fast, while 3 has the most impact for the parents who left embarrassed. Naming that tradeoff helps the board see why you're asking for different things at different speeds.

**5. Define what you'll report in June.**
- A check-in needs a measure for each opportunity. For example:
  - Fewer "is it free?" questions to staff.
  - Fewer parents trying the locked door.
  - More first-time attendees borrowing a book.
- Without these, June has nothing to compare against.

**6. Add one quote.**
- Add a short verbatim line from the "embarrassed" moment. It will land harder with a board than the count alone. Also make clear this is one visitor type (new to the area, one toddler), not all families.

**7. Check the timeline.**
- Your data is from April to May, and the first check-in is the June meeting. Make sure the dates read correctly for tomorrow's audience, and that "June" means the upcoming one.

Keep the songs finding as a headline, not just a footnote. It shows the program works once people get through the door, so the fixes are about access and not about the content.
