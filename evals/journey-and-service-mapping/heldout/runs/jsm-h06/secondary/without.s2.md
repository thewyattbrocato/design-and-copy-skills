The core story is strong: the flyer, the locked front door, the songs, and the ID wall at the end. Before you walk in, I'd change these:

1. **Put the decision up front.** Board members decide things, and the ID requirement is the one item that needs their authority. Put a "decision requested" line on slide 1 or 2, and make the card policy the ask. First check the current policy text so you're not asking for something that already exists.

2. **Fix the timeline.** You collected data in April and May, and it's now late October. Put the dates on the slide. A June check-in is about seven months away, but the flyer copy and door sign are small jobs that shouldn't wait. Either say they're already done or ask the board to approve them now. Also recheck the flyer and website today, since "the website doesn't say" is a claim someone will test.

3. **Show denominators.** "5 parents tried the front door" needs a total: out of how many arrivals? "3 of 8 left without borrowing" implies all eight wanted to borrow, so confirm that. If only some of the eight were new residents, say so, and don't let "8 of 8" read as the whole population. Put "8 interviews, 3 Saturdays, small qualitative sample" on the slide itself, not just in the notes.

4. **Separate what you saw from what you inferred.** The "Feels" row is your reading of behavior. Label it as an interpretation, or pair each stage with one direct quote from an exit interview. A quote will land harder with a board than an emotion word, and you likely have quotes you didn't include.

5. **Address the root causes.** You listed three behind-the-scenes gaps: no one is assigned to the Saturday door, front desk staff are trained only for members, and the card form is paper-only. None of them appears in the opportunities. Those gaps are why the fixes might not stick. Assign an owner to each. The paper form is probably the cheapest to fix, and the door staffing has no owner yet. If you're leaving them out on purpose, say so.

6. **Add a success measure.** The board will ask how you'll know it worked. Propose two or three measures, such as first-time attendees per Saturday, new-resident cards issued, and the share of respondents who knew it was free before arriving. Capture a baseline now, so June has something to compare against. Sign-in sheets may already give you the attendance number.
