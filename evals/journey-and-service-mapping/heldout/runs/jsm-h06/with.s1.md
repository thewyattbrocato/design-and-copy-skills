The map is strong. Every phase has a count or observation behind it, the phases are the parent's steps rather than a funnel, and you kept the songs as the high point. I'd change the following before you walk in, most important first.

**1. Add a decision line, and ask the board for something.**
The map has a person and a source line but no ask. Open with something like: "Story hour works. Parents lose out before and after it. We need your decision on ID for new residents." Item 3 is the only opportunity that needs the board. The flyer and the door sign are staff-level fixes. I'd lead with item 3 as the ask and present the other two as already in motion.

**2. Say how you ordered the opportunities.**
They read as ranked by impact, but 1 and 2 are the cheapest. Item 3 hits the most painful moment: 3 of 8 left without borrowing, and the parents felt embarrassed. Mark that moment as the one that matters most. Then say the order is by effort, not importance, or reorder it. Someone will ask.

**3. Fill the evidence gaps a board member will probe.**
- **Denominator for the door:** "5 parents tried the locked front door first" is out of how many parents you watched? Add the total.
- **Link between the ID barrier and the 3 who left:** did all 3 name the ID requirement, or is that your inference? "New residents often don't have one" has no source. If it's staff knowledge, say so.
- **Who is missing:** you interviewed people who made it in. Parents who saw the flyer and gave up at the locked door aren't in your 8. Add this as a one-line limit.
- **Feelings:** say whether the words (unsure, flustered, embarrassed) are what parents said or what you inferred from watching. Flustered and embarrassed look inferred.
- **Backstage row:** it has no source. Say it comes from staff conversations or observation, whichever it is.

**4. Turn "owners and first check-in" into testable commitments.**
"Marketing, facilities, branch manager" are teams, not people. For each opportunity, write:
- the change,
- the measure,
- the named owner and a date.

For example: "For a new parent at the flyer, 'free, no sign-up' should cut 'is it free?' questions to staff, measured by tally at the Saturday desk. Baseline: 8 of 8 asked. Owner: [marketing lead]. Date: [date]." Item 2 can be checked by watching the door again, with 5 of [total] as the baseline. Item 3 can be measured by how many new families leave without borrowing, with 3 of 8 as the baseline.

**5. Split item 3 into its two options.**
A utility bill and a temporary card are different policies with different costs and risks, such as card liability and system limits. The board will want to choose between them. Name which you recommend and why, and say what you'd shelve.

**6. Check the date on the check-in.**
My system date is October 2026, and the research was April to May. If that's the same year, the June board meeting has already passed. Confirm the date or change it to the actual next meeting.
