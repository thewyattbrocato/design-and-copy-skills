# Where to start

Don't start by mapping everything. Start by finding out where the third of new patients goes, and build the map around that. A map of the whole experience, drawn without talking to patients, would just be your staff's assumptions in a diagram.

## 1. Renegotiate the scope with your manager (today)
It's Oct 27, so if "end of month" means this week, you can't deliver a researched map of everything. Offer this instead:

> A journey map from referral/booking through the second appointment, with evidence for where patients drop off and the top fixes. Later stages (discharge, long-term retention) come in phase two.

That covers both of your problems, the drop-off and the referral paperwork, and it's honest about what a few hours of therapist time can support. If the deadline is really the end of next month, the plan below fits.

## 2. Get the numbers from the scheduling system (~1 week, admins)
Pull the last 6–12 months of new patients and answer:
- Of the new patients, how many booked a second visit, and how many attended it?
- Was the second visit booked before they left the first one?
- How does this break down by location, therapist, referral source (self, GP, insurer, etc.), and days between visits?
- How many one-visit patients were legitimately discharged, with a single visit meeting their need? Some of your third may not be a problem.

This tells you where to point the interviews. Often the drop-off clusters in one location, one referral type, or among patients who left without a rebooking.

## 3. Trace the referral paperwork (~1 week, admins)
- Take 10 recent referrals and follow each one from arrival to the first appointment. Note every handoff, every missing piece of information, every chase-up, and the elapsed time.
- Have the front desk keep a simple tally sheet for two weeks: what was missing, who was contacted, how long it took, and whether the patient waited.
- Draw it as a swimlane, with a lane for each of patient, referrer, front desk, therapist, and system or paper.

## 4. Talk to patients (the part you can't skip)
- Call 8–10 patients who didn't return, plus about 5 who did. Keep calls to 10 minutes, and have an admin or someone other than the treating therapist make them.
- Ask about what happened, not opinions: "Walk me through how you ended up booking with us. What happened after your first visit? What did you do next about your problem?"
- Early hypotheses to listen for, not assume: they got better, cost or insurance surprises, unclear plan, long wait for the second slot, no one followed up, the referral process was a hassle, or they went elsewhere.
- Check your local privacy rules (HIPAA or equivalent) on contacting former patients for service feedback. A short consent-friendly script usually covers it.

## 5. Use the therapist hours for the first appointment
Spend them on two things: a walkthrough of what happens in a first visit (expectation-setting, treatment plan, rebooking), and reviewing the referral findings for clinical gaps, such as information they need and don't get.

## What the deliverable should look like
One page, ideally one wall-sized sheet or slide, with no software needed:

- **Columns (stages):** find/referral → book → pre-visit paperwork → arrival and check-in → first appointment → plan and rebooking → between visits → second visit
- **Rows:** what the patient does and feels, what the front desk does, what the therapist does, and the systems and paperwork involved
- **Evidence markers:** at each pain point, a number ("40% leave without a rebooking") or a patient quote, so nothing is opinion
- **Drop-off callout:** where the leak is concentrated, from step 2
- **A short list of 3–5 fixes** with effort and impact, including at least one you can do next week (for example, standardizing the referral checklist or booking visit two before the patient leaves)

Google Slides, a spreadsheet, or sticky notes photographed on a wall all work fine.

## Timeline sketch
- **Week 1:** scheduling data pull, start the referral tally, schedule calls
- **Weeks 1–2:** patient calls, referral traces, therapist walkthrough
- **Week 3:** draft the map, review it with the admins and therapist, since they'll spot errors fast
- **Week 4:** finalize, present with the fixes, propose phase two
