**Don't start with the whole experience.** Map one stretch: from a new patient's referral (or decision to book) through their first visit to whether they book a second. That's where the one-in-three loss and the paperwork mess most likely sit. Show the rest of the journey on one page as stubs marked `[to map]`, so your manager gets a complete-looking overview by month end without the detail that would take months.

**Framing (three lines)**
- **Person:** a new patient arriving by referral or self-referral, with a first appointment booked.
- **Slice:** starts when they first contact us about care, ends when they have a second appointment booked or have left.
- **Decision:** which one or two changes to test in the next six to eight weeks.

**Status line:** "Assumption draft from the brief; to be corrected by front desk, admins and the therapist." Change it once the counts below come in.

**Family:** a blueprint. The problem is probably behind the scenes (who handles the referral, where it sits, what gets chased), and a blueprint shows front-stage and back-stage work side by side.

**Phases (the patient's steps, six at most):**
1. Get referred or decide to book
2. Get the paperwork sorted
3. Book the first appointment
4. Arrive and get through intake
5. Have the first session
6. Decide on the next visit (where most exits probably happen)

**Rows (keep to about five or six):** what they do, what they're asking, how they feel, touchpoint and owner, what goes wrong, backstage work and who does it. Fill the feeling row with one or two words per phase and no curve, since nothing has been measured.

**Marked moments (suspected, with the check for each):**
- Referral arrives but is incomplete or unfiled. Check: admins log every referral problem for two weeks.
- Gap between referral and first booking. Check: days from referral received to first appointment, from the booking system.
- End of the first session with no second slot offered or booked. Check: share of new patients with a second booking within 14 days, by location.

**Cheap evidence (no budget, a few hours):**
- **Booking system, this week:** new patients per location per month, and the share with a second visit within 14 days. This confirms or corrects the "one in three" figure, which is worth checking before the map is built on it.
- **Admins, two weeks:** a simple sheet logging each referral problem (missing info, wrong format, late arrival, who fixed it, how long it took).
- **Non-returners:** call 8 to 10 people who didn't rebook. Ten minutes each, three questions: what made them book, what happened with the paperwork, and why they didn't come back. This is the biggest gap in your evidence, and it costs only staff time.
- **Therapist:** a 20-minute debrief after five or six first appointments, asking what patients were unsure about and what they asked for.

**Timeline to month end:**
- Week 1: pull booking numbers, draft the frame and phases, and start the referral log.
- Week 2: make the non-returner calls and debrief the therapist.
- Week 3: a 60-minute walkthrough with the admins and therapist to correct the draft.
- Week 4: present the slice in detail, the overview with stubs, and one or two hypotheses.

**Hypotheses to end with** (fill in the baselines from the booking data):
- "For new patients at the end of the first session, offering the second slot before they leave should raise the share rebooking within 14 days from [baseline] to [target], measured by [date]. Owner: [owner]."
- "For referrals arriving incomplete, a checklist sent at referral should cut the number of incomplete referrals from [baseline] to [target]. Owner: front desk lead."
