**Don't try to map the whole patient experience by the end of the month.** With two admins and a few hours of one therapist, a whole-journey map will be shallow everywhere. Map one segment well: **referral to first visit to booked second visit.** That's where your third of patients are lost, and it includes the referral mess. Tell your manager that's the scope and why. A narrow map with evidence beats a broad one with guesses.

**Start with the numbers, not the map (about 2 hours)**
- Pull new-patient counts for the last 6 to 12 months, split by location and referral source (self, GP, insurer, employer, and so on). Calculate the share with a second appointment for each.
- If drop-off is similar everywhere, the cause is probably in your process. If it's concentrated in one location or referral source, you've found where to look.
- Check whether some of the "no return" is clinically appropriate. A patient who was discharged after one session because they were better isn't a loss. Ask whether your system can tell discharge from dropout. If it can't, your first real finding may be that you can't measure this yet.

**Then talk to people, cheaply**
- **Front desk (both admins), 30 minutes each:** Have them walk you through the referral path step by step, from the fax or email arriving to the second visit being booked. Photograph the paperwork pile. Ask what they chase and how often.
- **Your therapist, 20 minutes:** How do they end the first session? Do patients leave with a plan and a next booking, or with "call us"?
- **Lapsed patients, 10 to 15 calls at about 5 minutes each:** Ask two things: "What made you stop after the first visit?" and "What would have made you come back?" Expect short, honest answers. You don't need a research project. Call the ones who didn't return and weren't discharged as finished.

**What the map should look like**
One sheet, landscape, on a wall or in a free tool like FigJam, Miro, or a shared Google Sheet. Columns are stages:

*Referral received → Referral checked → Booking → Pre-visit → First visit → Plan and next booking → Second visit*

Rows for each stage:
- What the patient experiences
- What staff do, and who owns it
- Where it breaks
- Drop-off rate at that stage, if you have it
- Evidence tag: **number**, **quote**, or **hunch**

Mark every pain point with its evidence tag. This keeps the map honest and tells your manager what you know versus what you suspect.

**The referral paperwork is your fastest win**
Don't wait for the map. Write a one-page "complete referral" checklist covering what each referral source must include, and don't book a patient until it's complete. Ask the admins to draft it, since they live with the mess. This could be done within the month and will probably help the second-visit rate too, because incomplete referrals often mean unclear first-visit plans.

**A rough four-day plan to the end of the month**
- **Day 1:** Pull the numbers. Interview both admins.
- **Day 2:** Interview the therapist. Start the lapsed-patient calls.
- **Day 3:** Finish the calls. Draft the map and the referral checklist.
- **Day 4:** Review the map with the admins and the therapist. Present it to your manager as "here's the segment we fixed first, here's the evidence, here's what we'll look at next."
