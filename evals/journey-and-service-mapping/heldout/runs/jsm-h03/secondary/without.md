**Start with the drop-off, not the whole experience.** A map of everything would take months and wouldn't tell you why a third of patients leave. Scope it to **referral to second appointment** for new patients. That's where the problem is, and it's a map you can finish by the 31st.

## What to do in the next 4 days

1. **Pull numbers (admins, half a day).** For the last 3 months, across all three sites: new patients, referral source, whether they booked a second appointment before leaving, days from first to second visit, and whether the referral was complete at the first visit. Patterns here will tell you where to look. For example, if the drop-off is mostly patients who had incomplete referrals, that's a different problem from patients who never booked a second visit.

2. **Walk the process with the front desk (one 30-minute session per site, or one combined).** Ask them to show you what happens, step by step, from the moment a referral arrives. Ask "what do you do when the referral is missing a diagnosis?" rather than "what's the process?" Put each step on a sticky note. Their complaints about the paperwork will show up here with specifics.

3. **Call 10 to 15 patients who didn't come back (therapist or admin, 2 to 3 hours total).** Keep it short and voluntary. Ask what they expected, what made them decide not to return, and what they'd have needed. Also call 3 to 5 who did return, so you can see what they did differently. Follow your clinic's privacy rules for this, and don't pitch anything during the call.

4. **Draw the map (you, half a day on the 30th or 31st).**

## What it should look like

One page, with stages across the top and rows underneath:

- **Stages (columns):** Referral received → Referral triage/paperwork → Booking the first visit → First appointment → Plan and second-visit booking → Follow-up
- **Rows:**
  - What the patient experiences
  - What staff do, and who owns it
  - Systems and paperwork used
  - Pain points, from staff and patients
- **Evidence row:** For each stage, mark whether the claim comes from data, staff, patients, or your guess. This matters most. It keeps the map honest and tells your manager what still needs checking.
- **Drop-off marker:** Mark the stage where the numbers show the most loss, and put the top 2 or 3 hypotheses next to it.

Make the referral flow its own small one-pager, since that's a concrete, fixable problem. A referral checklist for whoever sends the referral might be a quick win while the rest is still being mapped.

## What to tell your manager

Present it as **a v1 map with known gaps**, with the scope stated plainly: new patients, referral to second visit, with data and interviews behind it. Add a dated plan for the next step, such as a fix to test at one site for a month, and track the second-visit rate there. That's a better answer than "the whole experience," and it's something you can actually deliver with two admins and a few hours of therapist time.
