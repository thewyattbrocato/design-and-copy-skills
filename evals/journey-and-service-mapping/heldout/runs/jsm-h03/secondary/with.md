**Start with one stretch, not the whole experience.** Map the path from referral to second visit in detail, and show the rest of the patient's path as a thin strip marked "not yet looked at." That is an honest "whole experience" map you can finish by month-end. A complete, evidenced map of all of it can't be done with two admins, a few therapist hours, and no budget.

**Frame**
- **Person:** an adult new patient, referred or self-referred, with an injury or pain. This comes from your brief, not from patients.
- **Slice:** from the referral arriving at the clinic to the patient booking a second visit or dropping out. Start a step before the obvious start, because the paperwork problem begins before the patient walks in.
- **Decision it serves:** which one or two fixes to run first in the next 60 days.
- **Status:** assumption draft from the owner's brief, to be corrected by front desk, admins and therapists. No patient input yet.

**The map (draft)**

| | Referral arrives | Books first visit | Arrives and fills forms | First visit | Between visits | Books visit 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Doing** | Doctor's letter or referral form sent | Calls or books online | Signs forms on a clipboard or tablet | Assessment, then a plan | Does exercises at home | Calls back or doesn't |
| **Thinking / asking** | Is this complete? Do I need to do anything? | Which location, and how soon? | How long will this take? | Is this going to work? What does it cost? | Am I doing this right? Is it getting better? | Do I need to come back? |
| **Feeling** `[to measure]` | unknown | unknown | unknown | unknown | unknown | unknown |
| **Clinic touchpoint** | Referral inbox, paper or fax | Phone or booking page, 3 locations | Front desk, forms | Therapist | None, unless someone calls | Front desk |
| **Suspected trouble** ⚑ | Referral incomplete or missing | Referral not linked to booking | Forms re-asked from scratch | Next step and cost not clearly stated | No check-in while the patient wonders | Booking left to the patient |

Feeling is blank on purpose. Don't draw an emotion curve until you have measured it.

**Marked moments (suspected, to check)**
1. **Referral intake.** Paperwork that doesn't match the booking delays or loses the patient.
2. **End of first visit.** If the plan and the next booking aren't set before the patient leaves, they are likely to drift off.
3. **Between visits.** No contact while the patient is unsure whether they are improving.

**How to check, in order of cost**
- **Week 1 (admins, about 2 hours):** pull the last 3 months of new-patient bookings per location, and compute the no-return rate. Confirm the one-in-three figure against this data before you use it. Also tally one week of referrals: complete, incomplete, or missing.
- **Week 1–2 (admins, about 1 hour each):** 30-minute conversations with the front desk at each location, and with one therapist.
- **Week 2–3 (admin or therapist, about 3 hours total):** five short phone calls with patients who didn't return. Ask about their first visit, what they were told next, and why they didn't come back. Use your existing contact method and consent practices.
- **Week 4 (about 1 hour, whole team):** a workshop to correct the draft map, pick the one or two moments to act on, and assign owners.

**Telling your manager**
"By month-end we'll have a map of the full patient path with one stretch evidenced in detail, and the rest marked as not yet examined. The first fix is the referral intake, with a measure: the no-return rate at 14 days, reported by location."

**Notes**
- The one-in-three figure and the "messy paperwork" claim are both currently unverified. The Week 1 data check settles them.
- The ⚑ entries are hypotheses, not findings.
