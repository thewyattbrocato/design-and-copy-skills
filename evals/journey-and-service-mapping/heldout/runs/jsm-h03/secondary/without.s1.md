**Start with the drop-off, not the whole experience.** A map of everything will be vague and won't be done by month end. Map only the path from referral to second visit, and say so to your manager. That path is where the third of patients is lost, and it's small enough to map well in four days.

## Week-one plan (no patient interviews needed yet)

1. **Pull the numbers (admin, about half a day).** Export every new patient from the last 3 to 6 months from your booking system: location, referral source, therapist, whether they booked a second visit, and days from first visit to second. Then ask whether the non-returners were discharged clinically or just dropped. A third may include people whose care was complete after one visit, so this split matters before you call the drop-off a problem.
2. **Audit referrals (admin, half a day).** Take one week of incoming referrals and record for each: how it arrived (fax, email, patient-carried), whether it was complete, how many needed chasing, and days from receipt to first appointment. This will likely explain the "mess" your front desk describes, with specific counts.
3. **Ask the front desk and your therapist (30 minutes each).** Ask what they see happen. Where do patients stall? What do they ask on the phone? What do they say when they leave? Write down the actual words.
4. **Call 5 to 10 non-returners (you or an admin, about 2 hours).** Use a short script: thank them, ask one or two open questions ("What made you decide not to book again?"), and offer to rebook. This is the only real patient research you can do this month, it's cheap, and some of those patients may come back. Use your existing contact channel and keep the call focused on scheduling and their experience.

## What the map should look like

One page, a swimlane diagram:

- **Columns (stages):** Referral received → Booking → First visit → Between visits → Second-visit booking.
- **Rows (lanes):** Patient, referrer/doctor, front desk, therapist, systems (paper, phone, email, booking software).
- **Each step:** who acts, what tool they use, and how long it takes. Mark where patients wait or drop off.
- **Evidence markers:** a number or quote next to each pain point, such as "38% of referrals incomplete (1 week audit)" or "median 9 days from referral to first visit." Mark anything you haven't verified as an assumption.
- **Header:** a simple funnel for the path, such as referrals received → booked → attended → rebooked, per location.

Label it clearly as **v1: based on 1 week of referrals, 5 patient calls, and staff input.** Gaps stated openly are more credible than a polished map that hides them.

## What to hand your manager

- The one-page map with its evidence.
- A ranked list of three fixes you'd test, for example a standard referral checklist, a rebooking call within 48 hours, or a single intake form.
- A note on what you couldn't cover yet (the rest of the journey, and patients who haven't been interviewed in depth) and a proposed next step for that.
