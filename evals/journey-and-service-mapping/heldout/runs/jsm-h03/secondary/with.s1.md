**Start narrow: the stretch from referral to second booking.** A whole-experience map can't be done well in a month with a few hours of therapist time. The one-third drop-off after the first visit and the referral mess are both in this stretch, and the admins can measure it from existing records. Tell your manager the map covers this stretch, and why.

## Frame (three lines)

- **Person:** a new patient, referred by a GP or self-referred, who has had a first appointment at one of your three sites.
- **Slice:** starts when the referral first reaches you, ends when they book a second session or drop out (about 3 to 4 weeks). Wide enough to show the seam, narrow enough to fix this month.
- **Decision:** which one of two or three changes to test first (referral intake, booking the next session before they leave, or follow-up after a missed rebooking).

**Status line:** "Assumption draft from the owner's brief, to be corrected by front desk, admins and patients."

## Family

A **journey** with one **backstage** row. The complaint about paperwork is internal, so the map has to show who holds the referral and how it moves between sites.

## Phases (the patient's steps, six)

1. Get the referral and decide to call
2. Book the first appointment
3. Arrive and fill in forms
4. Leave the first visit with a plan
5. Decide whether to book the next session
6. Attend, or drift away

## Rows (five, plus backstage)

| Row | Example cell (to be verified) |
|---|---|
| Patient does | Calls, then waits for a callback |
| Patient asks or thinks | "Does my GP's letter count?" |
| Feeling | `[to measure]`, no curve until you have it |
| Touchpoint | Reception phone line, referral letter |
| What goes wrong | Referral arrives without the GP's details |
| Backstage | Who logs the referral, which site it lands at |

Cut any row that could be said about any clinic.

## Marked moments (two, both suspected)

- **Phase 4 to 5, the hand-off to the next booking:** the patient leaves with no booked next session and no one owns the follow-up. Check: share of first-visit patients with a second booking within 14 days, by site.
- **Phase 2, the referral hand-off:** the referral is received at one place and used at another. Check: trace 20 referral letters from arrival to first booking and count missing details and delays.

## Evidence without budget

Roughly a day of admin time, and your therapist does not need to be in it:

1. **Booking export (admins, one afternoon):** new patients, second-booking rate within 14 days, split by site and referral source. This gives the baseline.
2. **Referral trace (admins, one afternoon):** the 20 letters above.
3. **Front desk log (two weeks):** each referral call, logged with the patient's question.
4. **Five short phone calls (admin, about 30 minutes total):** patients who did not return, with consent. Ask one open question: "What made you decide not to book again?"

## Timeline

- **Week 1:** export and referral trace.
- **Week 2:** front desk log running; five calls.
- **Week 3:** 90-minute workshop with the two admins and the therapist. Draw the map on a whiteboard or as a sketch, and mark the moments the data supports.
- **Week 4:** pick one hypothesis, name an owner, set a date, and present the map.

Example hypothesis, to fill in once you have data:

> For referred patients at the end of the first visit, booking the second session before they leave should raise the 14-day rebooking rate from `[baseline]` to `[target]`, measured in the booking export by `[date]`. Owner: `[owner]`.

Shelve the other ideas with a one-line reason each.

## What to tell your manager

"The map covers the referral-to-second-visit stretch, built from booking data and patient calls. It will name one change to test this quarter." That is a finished map with a decision in it, which is what "a map of the whole experience" usually needs to be useful.

Two notes: the one-third figure is your estimate, so check it against the export before it goes on the page. And if the data shows drop-off happening earlier, before the first visit, the slice moves, not the method.
