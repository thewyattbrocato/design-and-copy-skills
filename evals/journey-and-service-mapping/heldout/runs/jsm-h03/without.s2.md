**Start with the drop-off, not the whole experience.** "A map of the whole patient experience" is what your manager asked for, but what you need is to find out why a third of new patients vanish. Build the map around that question, and you'll get something useful and still satisfy the request.

## What to do, in order

**1. Pull numbers (admins, about 1 week).** For the last 3 months of new patients, per location:
- First visit → second visit rate, and how many booked a follow-up before leaving.
- How the patient arrived: self-referred, GP, insurer, or injury claim.
- Time from referral or enquiry to first appointment.
- Whether referral paperwork was complete at the first visit.

A third overall often turns out to be 15% at one location and 50% at another, or concentrated in one referral source. That tells you where to look.

**2. Map the front-desk process as it actually runs (admins, 1–2 sessions).** Have the two admins walk you through what happens to one referral from arrival to first appointment. Draw it on a whiteboard or sticky notes, including the workarounds. This is where the paperwork mess will show up, and it's the part of the journey you can fix with no budget.

**3. Talk to 8–12 patients (the most important step).**
- **Lapsed patients:** 5–6 who came once and didn't return. A short phone call or text from the clinic works: "We're trying to improve, could I ask 3 questions?" Keep it low-pressure and don't try to win them back on that call.
- **Retained patients:** 4–5 who stayed, for contrast.
- **Questions:** How did you end up here? What did you expect from the first visit? What was confusing or annoying? What happened after you left? Why did you stop (or what made you continue)?

Have the therapist's hours cover the clinical side. They can review 10–15 first-visit cases and say whether the plan, expectations, and rebooking conversation happened. Often the problem is that patients feel better after one visit, or never understood they needed a course of treatment.

**4. Synthesize.** Look for the 2–3 points where patients are lost.

## What the deliverable should look like

A one-to-two page journey map, with stages across the top:

> Discovery/referral → Booking → Pre-visit (paperwork, reminders) → First visit → Leaving (plan, rebooking) → Follow-up → Discharge

Under each stage, add rows for:
- What the patient does and feels, with a direct quote where you have one.
- What staff do behind the scenes.
- Pain points, with evidence (a number or a quote).
- Drop-off, with where you see it in the data.

Mark the two or three worst points, and add a short list of fixes you can try without money. Likely candidates:
- A single referral checklist or intake form.
- A reminder call or text before the first visit.
- Booking the second appointment before the patient leaves.
- A "here's what the next 4–6 weeks look like" script for therapists.

Mark anything you haven't verified as a **hypothesis**. That keeps a draft honest and shows your manager what the research will test.

## Pitfalls
- Don't try to cover all three locations in depth. Pick the worst one to dig into, and use the others for comparison.
- Don't build the map from staff opinion alone. Staff usually blame no-shows or patient motivation, while patients more often cite confusion, scheduling friction, or not understanding the plan.
- Keep patient notes anonymous, and get verbal consent for any quotes you use.
