**Start with the drop-off, not the whole experience.** "The whole patient experience" is too big for two admins and a few therapist hours in a month. You have a specific problem (a third don't return) and a specific suspect (referral paperwork). Build the map around the path from referral to second visit. Sketch the rest at low resolution so your manager still gets the full-journey picture.

## Week 1: Check what your data already says
You can do this with no patient contact, using your scheduling or EMR system.

- **Define "doesn't come back."** Some patients are legitimately done after one visit, such as a one-off assessment or a referral for advice only. Separate them from patients with a plan of care who never rebooked.
- **Split the drop-off rate** by location, therapist, referral source (self-referred, GP, insurer, or other), payer, and days between booking and first visit. A third overall often hides something like "15% at one site and 50% for one referral source." That tells you where to look.
- **Check whether the second visit was booked before the patient left.** In many clinics, that one habit explains most of the drop-off.

## Week 1–2: Walk the paperwork with the front desk
Have your admins trace 10 to 15 real recent referrals from arrival to first appointment. For each one, note:

- What arrived, in what format, and from whom
- What was missing or wrong
- How many touches it took to fix
- How long it took
- Whether the patient was waiting or got chased

Count the problems rather than just collecting complaints. "Four in ten referrals lack X" is much harder to dismiss than "it's a mess."

## Week 2–3: Talk to patients
You said you haven't yet, and this is the biggest gap.

- **Call 8 to 10 patients who didn't return, and 4 or 5 who did.** Keep it to 10 minutes. Ask open questions: "Walk me through how you ended up booking with us. What happened after your first visit? What got in the way of coming back?" The admins can make the calls, and the script can be five questions.
- **Check your privacy rules first.** Outreach to current or recent patients is usually fine, but confirm with whoever handles compliance.
- Expect some people not to answer. Calling 20 to get 8 is normal.

## Therapist hours
Use them for two things:

1. **A walkthrough of a first appointment**, covering what happens in the first 60 minutes and what the patient is told about the plan, frequency, and cost.
2. **A review of the drop-off cases** for clinical reasons, such as pain resolved quickly, no perceived progress, or an unclear plan.

## What the deliverable should look like
Aim for **one page, in a swimlane format**:

- **Columns (stages):** Find us → Referral/booking → Pre-visit paperwork → First visit → Rebooking → Visits 2–3 → Discharge. The first four or five get detail. The rest get a sentence or two.
- **Rows (lanes):** What the patient does and feels, what the front desk does, what the therapist does, and what systems or paperwork are involved.
- **Pain points** marked on the map as red dots, each tied to evidence (a count, a call quote, or an observation).
- **A short list below**: the top 3 to 5 problems ranked by how many patients they affect, each with a small fix you could try now.

Use a whiteboard, a Google Slides page, or Miro's free tier. The tool doesn't matter.

## What I'd tell your manager
Frame it as: "Here's the journey, here's where we lose people, here's the evidence, here's what we'll try first." A map that points to action is more useful than a comprehensive one. Be clear that the later stages (ongoing care, discharge, reactivation) are sketched but not yet researched, and offer them as phase two.
