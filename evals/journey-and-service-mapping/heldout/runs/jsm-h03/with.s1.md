# Where to start

**Start with the drop-off, not the whole experience.** Your manager asked for "a map of the whole patient experience", but your real problem is that a third of new patients don't return. Map one person's path from the moment they decide to get help until they either book a second visit or quietly leave. That's narrow enough to finish in a month and tied to a decision.

**Talk to patients before you polish anything.** The most valuable thing you can do is call 6 to 8 new patients who didn't return, plus 3 or 4 who did. Your admins can make the calls, since they know the system and the patients' names. The therapist should spend their hours on two things:
- Reviewing the call questions.
- Telling you what is supposed to happen in the first visit and the plan hand-off.

Keep the calls to about 10 minutes, ask for consent, and keep notes free of clinical detail. Ask the same open questions each time:
- "What led you to book?"
- "Walk me through getting from booking to walking in."
- "What happened after the first visit?"
- "What made you decide to come back or not?"

**Pull a few counts from your booking system.** Split new patients who didn't rebook by location, by referral versus self-referral, and by whether a follow-up was booked before they left the clinic. That tells you which transitions to look at first. I'm not guessing at any of these numbers.

**Ask your manager one question:** "What will you decide after seeing this?" My suggested default is "which one or two fixes we pilot next quarter." That keeps the map from becoming a poster nobody uses.

## The map

**Frame:** Map of a new patient, from deciding to get help to either booking a second visit or leaving. It serves the choice of which one or two fixes to pilot next quarter. It is one patient type, so referral and self-referral differences are flagged rather than given separate maps.

**Status:** Assumption draft from your brief, to be corrected by the front desk, the therapist and patient calls. Every cell is a guess until marked otherwise.

| | Decide to get help | Book first visit | Sort out referral and forms | First appointment | Wait, then decide | Second visit or exit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Does** | Asks GP or searches | Calls or books online | Chases referral, fills forms | Attends, assessed, treated | Weighs time, cost, pain | Rebooks, or lets it lapse |
| **Asks** | Who can I see? | Which location suits me? | Is my referral valid? | What's wrong, how long? | Is it helping? | Do I need more? |
| **Feels** | Worried, unsure | Hopeful | Frustrated | Anxious, then hopeful | Uncertain | Relieved or indifferent |
| **Touches the clinic** | Website, GP | Phone, online booking | Front desk, email, fax | Therapist, front desk | Silence, or a reminder | Phone, desk |
| **Goes wrong** | No clear info on the process | Wrong location or slot | Missing paperwork delaying the visit | Plan unclear at the end | Nobody follows up | No next visit booked |
| **Backstage: front desk** | Answers enquiries | Books by hand | Chases GPs, re-keys data | Collects forms at check-in | Not part of anyone's job (suspected) | Reactive rebooking |

All of these cells are guesses except the paperwork mess, which your front desk reported.

## Marked moments (both suspected)

1. **The end of the first appointment.**
   - Why it may matter: it's a hand-off between the therapist and the front desk, and the patient decides whether to commit.
   - Check: the share of new patients who left with a follow-up booked, and what the therapist says about the plan.
2. **Sorting out referral and forms.**
   - Why it may matter: it's an earlier promise, "book and come in", meeting messy delivery.
   - Check: how many first visits start with missing paperwork, and whether those patients return less often. Walk three referrals through the process by hand.

## Next step

Draft hypotheses to test after the calls. Fill in the baselines from your booking data:

- **Hypothesis 1:** For a new patient at the end of the first appointment, booking the next visit before they leave should raise the rate of second visits, measured against the current share of patients who leave with a follow-up booked `[baseline]`. Owner: `[owner]`. Date: `[date]`.
- **Hypothesis 2:** For a patient sorting out their referral, a single checklist sent at booking should cut first visits that start with missing paperwork, measured against `[baseline]`. Owner: `[owner]`. Date: `[date]`.

Ideas to shelve until the calls show they matter: reminder texts between visits, a patient app, and a redesigned website.

**Two notes:**
- If your system doesn't record whether a follow-up was booked at the desk, a simple tally for two weeks will do.
- Ask your manager whether "whole experience" is a hard requirement. If it is, present this as phase one of the map, with the later stretch (ongoing treatment, discharge) as `[to collect]`.
