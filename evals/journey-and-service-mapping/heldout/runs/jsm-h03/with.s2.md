## The map

**Frame:** A new patient, referred or self-referred, from deciding to book through to booking (or not booking) their second visit. The decision it serves: which one or two moments to fix first to cut first-visit drop-off.
**Status:** Assumption draft from your brief. Every cell is a guess for the front desk, therapist and patients to correct. Nothing here is measured.
**Family:** Blueprint-style journey. The trouble sits between the referrer, front desk and therapist, so I included backstage rows.

**Marked moments (both suspected, each with a check):**
1. **Booking to first visit (paperwork hand-off).** Your front desk already points here, and information gets passed through several hands. *Check:* trace 10 recent referrals from arrival to first visit, noting what was missing, who chased it and how long it took.
2. **End of the first visit (the decision point).** Patients have just heard a plan, and rebooking may be the only point where you can keep them. *Check:* compare, by therapist and location, the share of new patients who leave with a second visit booked. This is the number to pull first.

## A month, with your resources

- **Week 1 (admins, ~2 hrs):** Run a 60-minute session with both admins to correct this draft. Then pull the data: new patients per location, how many booked a second visit, and referral source, for the last 3 to 6 months. Don't chase precision.
- **Weeks 1–2 (admins):** Call 10–15 patients who didn't return, plus 4–5 who did. Keep it to five minutes with three questions: what you expected, what happened, and what made you decide about the next visit. Use a short script, say it isn't a sales call, and record answers in a shared sheet. Fewer calls still help if they're honest.
- **Week 2 (therapist, 2–3 hrs):** Review the paperwork trace and describe what an ideal first visit and plan hand-off looks like. Their time is best spent on moment 2.
- **Week 3:** Revise the map with what you found. Replace guesses with evidence, labeled by source.
- **Week 4:** Present it to your manager: the story in order, the two moments, and the evidence behind each.
