# Recommendation

Use a wall printout for Thursday and a short deck for the execs. The two audiences need different things from the same map.

## Thursday: 12 people, working session

**Main artifact: the full map as a large printout on a wall.** Aim for A0 or plotter size, or tape together tiled A3 sheets. A slide can't hold 7 stages × 4 rows plus emotions, pains and ideas, and shrinking it loses the point. The point is that support, product, sales and onboarding can stand in front of it and see where their own row meets someone else's.

**What goes on the wall:**
- **Above the map:** one framing line and one status line.
  - Framing: who the map is about (the restaurant owner), where it starts and ends (signed contract to first successful payroll run), and what it's for (the decision you want from the room).
  - Status: what it's built from, e.g. "built from X interviews and Y tickets; the out-of-sight row rests on staff accounts only". If parts are guesses, say so.
- **The map:** 7 stages as columns, named by what the owner is doing or trying to do. Rows are owner, onboarding specialist, software, and out-of-sight work. The emotion line goes at the top as one or two named feelings per stage, not a smooth curve, unless you measured it.
- **Cells:** 3 to 7 words each. Move longer evidence to a notes sheet with its source.
- **Pain points:** put the 11 on the map as one color of sticky or dot, placed at the stage where each happens.
- **Ideas:** don't put all 9 on the wall at the start. Keep them on a separate sheet or as a second color you add later in the session. Showing them first makes the room argue about fixes before agreeing on what happens.
- **Mark 2 or 3 moments** with a reason each (a hand-off, a wait with no status, a step that can't be undone). Marking all 11 pains equally leaves the room with nothing to focus on.

**How to run it (suggested 45–60 min):**
1. Spend 5 minutes on the frame, then walk the story left to right, stage by stage, not row by row.
2. Give people markers and ask them to correct it. Support and onboarding will know things the map gets wrong. Mark those corrections in a different color.
3. Have each person dot the 2 or 3 pain points they'd fix first, then discuss where the dots cluster.
4. Only then bring out the 9 ideas and attach them to the clustered moments.
5. Finish with owners and next steps.

**Leave-behind:** an A3 or letter-size copy of the map plus the pain/idea list, so people can refer to it later.

## Next week: execs, 10 minutes

**Format: slides, 4 or 5 of them, and no more.** Send the full map as an appendix or attached PDF, not something to read live.

1. **Frame and status.** Who, start to end, what you're asking for, and what the map is built from.
2. **The journey on one strip.** The 7 stages with the emotion words under them. Hide the four rows. Highlight the 2 or 3 moments that matter and say why in a few words.
3. **The story at those moments.** One slide per moment, or one combined, showing what the owner experiences against what happens out of sight. This is where the cross-team seam is easiest to see.
4. **Up to 3 hypotheses,** each in the form: "For [owner] at [moment], doing [change] should produce [effect], measured by [metric] by [date]." Each has an owner (a role is fine) and a baseline, or `[to measure]` if you don't have one. Say that the other ideas are shelved and why.
5. **The ask.** What decision, resource or owner you need from them.

Don't read the map aloud to the execs. Tell the story and ask for the decision. At 10 minutes, expect about 2 minutes for the frame, 4 for the moments, 2 for the hypotheses and 2 for the ask.

## Two cautions

- **Don't invent numbers for the exec version.** Execs will want durations, drop-off, and revenue impact. If you don't have them, put `[to measure]` and make measuring them part of the ask.
- **Keep the status line on both.** It is what makes the map credible. It also tells people what they can correct.
