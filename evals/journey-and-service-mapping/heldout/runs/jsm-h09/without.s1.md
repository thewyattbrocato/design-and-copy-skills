# Thursday (12 people): print the map and work from the wall

For a working session with 12 people from four functions, the full map should be physically in the room. Support, product, sales and onboarding each see different parts of it, and they need to stand in front of it together and point.

**What to bring**
- **One large printout of the full map**, roughly 4-6 feet wide, taped to a wall. Stages run across the top and the 4 rows below, with the emotion line drawn as a curve through the middle where everyone can see the dips.
- **Pain points and ideas as sticky notes or color-coded dots on the map.** Number the 11 pain points and put each under the stage it belongs to. Keep the 9 ideas in a separate color, either beside the map or attached to the pain points they address.
- **A one-slide opener** with the 7 stages and the emotion line only. It sets context for 3 minutes, then you walk to the wall.
- **Letter-size handouts of the full map.** They're small, but people will want to take notes on them.

**Flow (about 45-60 min)**
1. **Walk the 7 stages left to right** (10 min). Narrate from the owner's point of view and mention the other rows only where they matter. Spend your time on the hidden row (bank verification and similar), because that's where people say "I didn't know that."
2. **Mark the emotion dips** (5 min). Ask where the room thinks the owner is most anxious.
3. **Go through the pain points** (15 min). Ask each function to confirm, correct or add. Corrections are the main thing you want from this session.
4. **Dot-vote on the 9 ideas** (10 min). Each person gets 3 dots, and you tell them to vote for what fixes the most painful moments, not for what they like best.
5. **Capture owners and next steps** (5 min).

Photograph the wall afterward. The dots and edits become your input for the exec version.

# Exec team (10 min): slides, plus a one-page leave-behind

Ten minutes means you can't walk the full map. Show the shape of the journey, then make a specific ask.

**5-6 slides**
1. **The headline.** One sentence, something like "New customers take X days to first payroll, and the biggest drop in confidence is at Stage N." Use real numbers if you have them.
2. **The journey, simplified.** Show 7 stages in one row with the emotion line above them. Drop the 4 rows, and mark the 2-3 worst dips.
3. **Where it breaks.** Pick the 3-4 pain points that matter most out of the 11. Tie each to a stage and, if possible, to a cost: churn risk, support tickets, days delayed. Don't show all 11.
4. **The hidden part.** One slide on what the owner can't see, such as bank verification. Execs rarely know this layer exists, and it explains why delays feel arbitrary to customers.
5. **Recommended ideas.** Pick the top 3 of the 9, using the Thursday vote as backing. For each, give the pain it fixes, the rough effort and the owner.
6. **The ask.** One decision you need from them, such as a pilot, headcount or a priority change.

**Leave-behind**
- A one-page, letter-size version of the full map, legible but not detailed, with all 11 pain points and 9 ideas listed. Send it before or after the meeting, not during.
- Keep the full map in the appendix of the deck for questions.

**Tips**
- Put the full map on a slide at the end, not the start. If it comes first, they'll spend the 10 minutes reading it instead of listening to you.
- Say that the Thursday session, with its 12 people across 4 teams, validated the findings. That adds credibility.

# Why different formats

| | Thursday | Execs |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Validate and prioritize | Decide |
| Detail | Everything | 20% of it |
| Medium | Wall printout | Slides + one-pager |
| Who talks | Everyone | You |
