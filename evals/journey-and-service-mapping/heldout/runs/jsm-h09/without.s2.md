# Thursday (12 people): a big printout on the wall

The map was built to be read as a whole, and this room has the knowledge to improve it. Support, sales, and onboarding will each spot things the others missed. Slides would hide the whole picture and turn the session into a presentation instead of a working session.

**What goes on it**
- The full map, printed large (roughly 3 to 4 feet wide, or taped-together sheets). Stages run across the top. The four rows are owner, onboarding specialist, software, and out-of-sight work. The emotion line runs along the bottom or through the middle.
- The 11 pain points as numbered markers placed on the stage and row where they occur.
- The 9 ideas on a separate strip or on sticky notes below the map, each linked to the pain points it addresses.

**How to run it (about 45 to 60 minutes)**
1. **Walk the map (10 min).** Go left to right, one stage at a time, and tell it as one customer's story. Spend extra time on the out-of-sight row, because that's where people from other teams usually learn something.
2. **Correct it (10 min).** Ask "what's wrong or missing?" Give people a different color of sticky note for corrections.
3. **Dot-vote (10 min).** Everyone gets 3 dots for the pain points that matter most and 3 for the ideas worth pursuing. The votes show where the room agrees, and people back what they helped shape.
4. **Discuss the top few (15 min).** Pick the top 2 or 3 from the votes and talk about owners and next steps.

Also hand out a one-page letter-size version of the map for people to take away. Take a photo of the wall at the end to record the votes and corrections.

# Next week's exec slot (10 minutes): slides

Execs need a decision or a takeaway, and a full 7×4 map is unreadable on a screen. Use 5 to 6 slides and keep the full map out of the main flow.

1. **The headline.** One sentence, such as "It takes X days from signed contract to first successful payroll, and the owner is most stressed at stages A and B." Use your real numbers if you have them.
2. **The journey on one line.** Show the 7 stages as a simple chevron strip with the emotion line over it. The point is where customers feel worst.
3. **The 2 or 3 moments that matter.** Pick the worst moments. For each, give one customer-eye quote or example and the hidden cause, especially if it's in the out-of-sight row, like bank verification delays.
4. **Pain points, grouped.** Don't show all 11. Group them into 3 or 4 themes and give a rough sense of the impact, such as time to first payroll, churn risk, or support load.
5. **Ideas, prioritized.** Show the 3 or so that the Thursday vote and your own judgment favor, with rough effort versus impact. The other ideas go in the appendix.
6. **The ask.** Say what you need: a decision, an owner, or a pilot. Without this, the slot becomes a briefing.

Put the full map in the appendix as a backup slide, and bring a printed copy so anyone curious can look at it. Rehearse it to 8 minutes so you leave time for questions.

# Optional: a short doc

A one- or two-page written summary is useful as a leave-behind for both audiences. It should cover the headline, the pain-point themes, the top ideas, and the next steps, and link to the full map. It's optional, but it keeps people who weren't in the room from having to rely on a photo.
