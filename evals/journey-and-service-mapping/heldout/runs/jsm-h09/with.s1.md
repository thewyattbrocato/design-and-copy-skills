**Thursday (12 people): a big printout on the wall. Exec slot: a short deck with the full map as a leave-behind.** The two sessions have different jobs. Thursday is for checking the map and finding what's missing. The exec slot is for getting a decision.

## Thursday: wall printout, about 60 minutes

Print the whole map as a poster, roughly A0 or 3 to 4 feet wide, or tile it on a wall. Don't use slides. Twelve people from four functions will each know a different stretch, and they need to see all 7 stages at once, point at things, and add to it.

**What goes on it**
- **A header with three lines.** Say who the map is about (the restaurant owner, plus a one-line stub from your sources). Say where it starts and ends (signed contract to first successful payroll run). Say what the room is deciding. Add a status line saying what it's built from, for example "built from X interviews and Y tickets" or "assumption draft". Mark any cells that are guesses.
- **The grid.** Seven stages are the columns, named by what the owner is doing or trying. The rows are owner, onboarding specialist, software and out of sight. Keep cells to 3 to 7 words. Put longer evidence in a footnote with its source.
- **The emotion line.** Use one or two named feelings per stage ("anxious", "relieved"). Only draw a curve if you measured it. Otherwise a smooth line implies precision you don't have.
- **Pain points.** Put the 11 pain points as stickers on the stage where they occur. Mark only 2 or 3 moments as the ones that matter, each with a reason. Good reasons are a hand-off, a wait with no status, or something that can't be undone. The bank-verification wait is a likely candidate, but only if your evidence backs it.
- **Ideas.** Keep the 9 ideas off the main grid. Put them on a separate strip below, or in a stack of cards to place later. Each idea should attach to a pain point or a marked moment.

**How to run it**
1. Walk the map in order as a story, left to right. Don't go row by row.
2. Give people sticky notes in two colors: "this is wrong or missing" and "I've seen this happen".
3. Ask each function to check its own row. Support and onboarding will catch errors in the specialist and out-of-sight rows fastest.
4. Spend the last 15 minutes on the ideas. Have people vote on which 2 or 3 to take forward, and photograph the wall before you leave.

## Exec meeting: 10 minutes, 5 or 6 slides

Execs don't need the grid. They need the one story it tells and what you want from them.

1. **Frame and status (about 1 minute).** Who, from where to where, what it's built from, and the decision you need.
2. **The whole map, simplified.** Show the 7 stages as a strip with the emotion words and the 2 or 3 marked moments. Keep it legible from the back of a room and leave off the detail rows.
3. **Moments 1 to 3 (about 4 minutes).** Use one slide per moment, or one slide for all three. Say what happens to the owner, what's happening out of sight, and the evidence. Include a real number or quote only if you have a source for it.
4. **Recommendations (about 2 minutes).** Pick up to 3 of the 9 ideas. For each, say what it changes at which moment, how you'd measure it, who owns it and by when. Fill in `[owner]`, `[date]` or a baseline placeholder if you don't have them yet. Say briefly why the other ideas were shelved.
5. **The ask.** For example: approve the 3 tests, name owners, and agree when you'll report back.

Appendix and leave-behind: the full map as a one-page PDF with the pain points and ideas. Send it after the meeting, and don't show it in the 10 minutes.

## Two things to settle before Thursday

- **Evidence labels.** Know what each row rests on. The out-of-sight row often rests only on staff accounts, so say so on the map.
- **Narrowing 11 pain points to 2 or 3 moments.** Use Thursday to do this with the room. Then you can tell the execs that the cross-functional team picked these moments, which is stronger than your own ranking.
