**Thursday (12 people): a big printout on a wall. The exec slot (10 minutes): a short slide deck, with the full map as a backup slide.** The two audiences want different things. The team needs to check the map and add to it. Execs need to make a decision.

## Thursday: wall printout

12 people can stand around a printed map. They can't do that around a screen, and the point of this session is for support, product, sales and onboarding to correct it. Each of them sees a different stretch of the process, and the seams between those stretches are where your pain points sit.

**What goes on the print**
- **Top strip:** one framing line and one status line. The framing line names the person, where the story starts and ends, and the decision it serves. For example: "Restaurant owner, from signed contract to first successful payroll run; used to choose what onboarding, product and support fix first." The status line says what the map is built from, for example "built from [N interviews/tickets, dates]; the out-of-sight row rests on staff accounts only". Name your real sources there.
- **The map:** 7 stages as columns, named by what the owner is doing or trying. Use 4 rows plus the emotion line, with 3 to 7 words per cell. Put the out-of-sight row (bank verification and similar) at the bottom, with a visible line separating it from the rest.
- **Emotion line:** use one or two named feelings per stage, not a smooth curve. Only draw a curve if you measured it.
- **Pain points and ideas:** number the 11 pain points as small tags in the stage where they happen. Keep the 9 ideas **off the map**, on a separate sheet or in a column of sticky notes. If they sit on the map, people start arguing about fixes before they agree on what happens.
- **Marked moments:** circle **2 or 3**, each with a one-line reason. Good reasons are a hand-off, a wait with no status, or a step that can't be undone. Bank verification is a likely candidate. Mark by evidence, or label the mark "suspected" and name the check.

**How to run it (assuming about 45 to 60 minutes)**
1. Spend 5 minutes walking the story left to right. Tell it as a story, not row by row.
2. Give everyone sticky notes in two colors: "this is wrong or missing" and "I've seen this too". Give them 10 minutes of silent time at the wall.
3. Discuss the marked moments and whatever got the most stickies.
4. Dot-vote on the 9 ideas.
5. Close by naming an owner for each of the top ideas.

Photograph the wall afterward and update the map from it before the exec meeting.

## Exec slot: 10 minutes, about 4 slides

Don't show them the whole map and talk through it. They'll read the dense grid instead of listening, and you have 10 minutes.

1. **Frame and status (1 min):** the same two lines as the wall version, plus the one-sentence answer: where setup breaks down and what you're asking for.
2. **The journey, simplified (3 min):** the 7 stages in a single strip with the emotion words underneath. Use only the **2 or 3 marked moments**, with one line each on what happens there and the evidence. Hide the rest of the grid.
3. **Hypotheses (3 min):** 3 at most, in the form "for [owner] at [moment], doing [change] should produce [effect], seen as [measure] by [date]". Give each an owner and a date. Where you have no baseline, write `[baseline to measure]` and don't guess one. Say in a line why the other ideas are on hold.
4. **The ask (2 min):** the decision you want, such as approving the three tests, naming the owners, or assigning a person to measure the baselines.

**Backup slide:** the full map, for questions. Keep the 11 pain points and 9 ideas there and in the leave-behind. The execs shouldn't see the full list in the 10 minutes.
