**Use a different format for each room.** Thursday is a working session, so put the map on the wall. The exec slot is a 10-minute briefing, so use slides.

## Thursday: 12 people, support, product, sales, onboarding

**Format: a large printout (A0 or similar) taped to the wall, plus sticky notes.** Slides would hide the thing that makes this useful, which is seeing all seven stages side by side. Use the printout as the room's shared surface.

**What goes on the printout:**
- The 7 stages as columns, left to right, with the 4 rows as swimlanes: owner, onboarding specialist, software, and behind the scenes (bank verification and similar).
- The emotion line running across the top, so the dips are visible.
- The 11 pain points as numbered markers placed at the stage where they happen.

**What stays off the wall until later:** the 9 ideas. If you show them first, people will debate solutions before everyone agrees on the problems. Put them on separate cards in a pile.

**Suggested run:**
1. Walk the map once, stage by stage. Ask each function to say whether the map matches what they see.
2. Give people sticky notes to add pain points the map misses. Those are often the most valuable.
3. Reveal the 9 idea cards. Have people place each one next to the pain point it addresses, then dot-vote on what to pursue.
4. Capture the votes and gaps in a short doc afterward. That doc is the only handout you need.

## Exec team, 10 minutes

**Format: 5 slides, with the full map as an appendix.** Execs won't read a dense poster in a 10-minute slot. Plan roughly 2 minutes per slide:

1. **The finding in one sentence.** For example: "A new customer takes [X] weeks to reach their first payroll, and the experience is worst at [stage]."
2. **The simplified map.** Keep the 7 stages and the emotion line. Cut the 4 rows to 2: "what the customer does" and "what we do." Name the bank verification step explicitly, since it's often a surprise to leadership.
3. **Top 3 pain points.** Each one gets a stage and a cost in time, churn, or support load. Use real numbers if you have them. If you don't, say so out loud rather than estimating on the slide.
4. **Top 3 ideas.** Tie each one to a pain point and give a rough effort level.
5. **The ask.** A decision, a budget, or a priority call. Without one, the exec team will just nod.

**Appendix (not presented):** the full map, all 11 pain points, and all 9 ideas. Keep it ready for questions.

**One thing to decide before you build the exec deck:** what you want from them. Your slide 5 depends on it, and I'd make that the first thing you pin down.
