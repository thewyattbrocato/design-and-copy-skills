**Recommendation:** use a large printout on Thursday, and slides plus a one-page handout for execs. Send the doc to both groups afterward as the reference.

## Thursday: 12 people in the room (support, product, sales, onboarding)

**Format: a wall-size printout.** Tape or print the map on a few sheets so the whole 7-stage journey fits on one wall, with the four rows visible. These are people who work in this process, so they'll want to mark it up, not just watch it. Slides would make them passive. Give them sticky notes and markers.

**What goes on it:**
- The full map as-is: 7 stages, 4 rows, and the emotion line drawn across the top as a curve.
- The 11 pain points printed on cards, ready to place on the stage where they happen.
- The 9 ideas on separate cards, kept off the map until the group places them.

**How to run it (roughly 60–90 min, assuming that's your slot):**
1. Walk the map stage by stage, asking the room to correct anything that's wrong. Their corrections matter more than your polish.
2. Have people place the pain point cards. Ask: "Which of these have you personally seen?" Flag any cards nobody recognizes.
3. Have people add anything missing, in a different color.
4. Ask the ideas question last: "Which of these would you own, and what would it take?" Get a name next to each one you want to keep.

**What to bring out of the room:** the marked-up map, a list of corrections, and named owners for the ideas.

## Exec team: 10 minutes

**Format: 4–5 slides, plus a one-page handout.** A 10-minute slot fits about 4–5 slides at roughly 2 minutes each. Execs will want the headline and a decision, not the whole map. The handout is a shrunk copy of the map they can refer back to.

**What goes on the slides:**
1. **The finding in one sentence.** For example: "A new restaurant takes X days to its first payroll run, and most of the delay happens at Y." Fill in X and Y from your data. I don't have the numbers, so don't use a placeholder you haven't verified.
2. **The map, simplified.** The 7 stages across the top, with the emotion curve under them. Show the dip and point to where it happens. Skip the full 4-row detail here.
3. **The top 3 pain points.** Pick by impact, not by count. Each should name the stage, what goes wrong, and the cost (time, churn, support tickets) if you have that.
4. **The top 3 ideas and what you're asking for.** Each idea needs a decision: approve, fund, or investigate. Execs leave with a clear ask, not a list.
5. **Optional backup:** the full map or the 11 pain points, only if someone asks.

**Handout:** the simplified map with the top pain points marked.

## The doc

Write it as the record, not the presentation. Include the full map, all 11 pain points and 9 ideas, the corrections from Thursday, and owners. Send it to the Thursday group first, then link it in the exec follow-up. Don't put it in front of execs in the meeting.

**Order of work:** build the Thursday printout first, since its corrections will change the exec slides. Then make the exec deck from the corrected map.
