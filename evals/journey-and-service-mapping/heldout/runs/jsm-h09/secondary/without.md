**Thursday (12 people, working session): a large printout, plus a one-page doc as pre-read.**

The room includes the people who will argue with the map, so they need to write on it. A wall-size print (A0, or 2–3 A1 sheets taped together) lets everyone stand around it and put sticky notes on stages.

- **On the printout:** all 7 stages, all 4 rows, the emotion line, all 11 pain points, and all 9 ideas. Pain points and ideas can be numbered tags or sticky notes so people can add to them.
- **Leave blank:** a few empty sticky-note zones labeled "missing" or "wrong" so they can correct it.
- **Optional doc:** one page with the stage names and a sentence per stage, sent beforehand so nobody has to read the map cold.
- **Skip slides** for this session. They pull attention to the front of the room, and you want people looking at the map.
- **Exercise:** have each person dot-vote their top 3 pain points and top 2 ideas, then discuss where the votes cluster. Keep the marked-up printout and photograph it afterward.

**Exec team (10 minutes): a short slide deck, 4–5 slides, with a printed one-pager to hand out.**

Executives want the problem, the evidence, and a decision. They don't need all 11 pain points or the full grid. Suggested slides:

1. **The problem in one sentence.** For example: "A new restaurant takes X weeks from signed contract to first payroll run, and Y% of that time is spent waiting on steps we don't control." Use a real number here if you have one. This is the most important slide.
2. **The map, simplified.** Show the 7 stages as a single horizontal strip with the emotion line drawn above it. Shade the stages where the emotion line dips. Skip the 4-row detail.
3. **The 3 pain points that matter most.** Choose them by business impact (churn, delay, support load), not by count. Merge related items so you present 3 clusters, not 11 items.
4. **The 3 ideas and what each costs.** Give each one a rough effort and impact estimate. End with the specific decision or resource you need from them.

Leave about 3 minutes for questions. The one-pager is a handout that carries the full map for anyone who wants detail afterward.

**Key differences:** Thursday is for building a shared picture and collecting input, so show everything and let the room sort it. The exec session is for a decision, so reduce the material to the few items that matter and bring a clear ask.
