**Use two formats, one master map.** The room on Thursday needs to correct the map. The execs need to decide something. Those call for different artifacts.

## Thursday: 12 people, working session

**Format: a large printout (A0 or the largest you can get), with a one-page pre-read.**

Put the full map on the wall or table so people can write on it. Slides hide the detail and make correction awkward. A pre-read lets people arrive knowing the framing without squinting at a seven-column grid.

**Goes on the printout:**
- **Frame line at the top:** who the person is (new restaurant owner), where the story starts (signed contract) and ends (first successful payroll run), and the decision it serves. You need to name that decision. If you don't have one, a reasonable default is "which onboarding fixes to fund first."
- **Status line, right under the frame:** say what the map rests on. If it comes from your team's knowledge and not customer interviews, write "built from [team]'s knowledge, to be corrected by people near customers." Don't let the room assume more evidence than exists.
- **All 7 phases, 4 rows, and the emotion line.** Mark guessed cells as guesses.
- **All 11 pain points,** as cells on the map. This room knows them and will want to check them.
- **The 1 to 3 marked moments,** each with a short reason. Mark them as "suspected" if there's no evidence yet.
- **Blank space and sticky notes.** Leave room for corrections. Put the 9 ideas on separate notes in a "parking" area, not on the map, so the room doesn't treat them as decided.

**Pre-read (one page):** the frame, the status line, the 7 phase names in order, and a short list of the marked moments. Keep it to one page.

**Ask the room:** "What's wrong or missing?" before "What should we do?" The map's job here is to get agreement on what happens.

## Exec team: 10 minutes

**Format: 4 to 5 slides, with a one-page handout if they want something to keep.**

Execs need the story and a decision, not the full grid. Use roughly two minutes per slide.

1. **Frame and status.** Who, start and end, decision, and what the map is built on. Keep the status line visible.
2. **The map, simplified.** Seven phases in a row. Show the feeling as words per phase, not a curve, unless you measured it. Show only the 2 or 3 marked moments.
3. **The 1 or 2 pain points that matter most,** each tied to a marked moment. Leave the other 9 in the appendix or doc.
4. **Up to three hypotheses.** Format: "For [owner/customer type] at [moment], doing [change] should [effect], measured by [measure] by [date]." Each needs an owner and date. Name the ideas you're not pursuing and why, in one line each.
5. **The ask.** A specific decision or resource request. If you can't name one, the map isn't ready for execs yet.

**Keep the full 11 pains, 9 ideas, and the 4-row map in a doc** they can read later.

## Before Thursday, fill in

- The **decision** the map serves
- The **status line** (what it's built on)
- **Owners and dates** for the hypotheses. Use `[owner]` and `[date]` placeholders if you don't have them yet, but don't show execs placeholders.
- Whether the **emotion line** was measured. If not, present it as an estimate or drop the line.
