Use two different formats. The two meetings have different jobs.

## Thursday (12 people): big printout on a wall

This group needs to check the map, argue with it, and prioritize. A slide can't show 7 stages × 4 rows legibly, and a screen makes people passive.

**What to print:** The full map, as large as you can get it (plotter or tiled sheets, 4–6 ft wide).
- Stages run across the top.
- Rows run down: owner, onboarding specialist, software, out of sight.
- The emotion line runs across the full width so the dips are visible from across the room.
- The 11 pain points are numbered markers pinned to the exact stage and row where they happen.
- The 9 ideas go on a separate strip along the bottom or side, each tied to the pain points it addresses.

**How to run it (about 45 minutes):**
1. Spend 10 minutes walking it left to right. Tell it as one customer's story.
2. Ask people to add sticky notes wherever the map is wrong or missing something. Support and onboarding will know things the map doesn't, especially in the out-of-sight row.
3. Give everyone 3 dots for pain points and 3 for ideas, then vote.
4. Photograph the result.

**Also bring:** A letter-size copy of the map and a numbered list of the pain points and ideas, for note-taking. Have a digital copy ready for anyone who's remote.

## Exec team (10 minutes): short deck plus a one-page leave-behind

Execs don't need the map. They need the finding, the evidence, and the decision. Ten minutes means 4–5 slides at most.

1. **Headline:** One sentence on what you found, with your best number, such as time from signed contract to first payroll or where customers stall.
2. **The journey on one strip:** The 7 stages with the emotion line, and the lowest point highlighted. Show the owner's view only. Bring in the out-of-sight row only where it explains a dip. For example, bank verification delays are invisible to the owner but cause real frustration.
3. **Top 3 pain points:** Use Thursday's vote to pick them. Give each one a line of evidence, such as a quote, count, or delay.
4. **Top 3 ideas:** Include rough effort and expected impact.
5. **The ask:** The decision, owner, or resource you need from them.

**Appendix:** The full map and the complete lists of pain points and ideas, in case someone asks.

**Leave-behind:** Send the full map as a one-page PDF before or after the meeting, so anyone who wants detail can find it without it eating your 10 minutes.

Don't walk through all 11 pain points and 9 ideas with the execs. Choosing the top few is how you show you've done the prioritizing for them. Thursday's vote also gives you a defensible way to choose.
