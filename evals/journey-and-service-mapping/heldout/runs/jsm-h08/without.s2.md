# Customer journey: where subscribers leave (months 1-4)

| Stage | What the customer experiences | Evidence | Friction or risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Sign-up and first box (month 1)** | The intro discount makes the price feel low. The dog tries the food. | No complaints in your data. | The discount sets a price expectation that later boxes won't meet. |
| **2. Settling in (month 1-2)** | Deliveries become routine. Some boxes arrive on days nobody is home. | Support tickets: "my box came on a day I'm not home". | Delivery timing is a logistics problem, but it feels like a product problem to the customer. |
| **3. Recipe change (about month 2-3)** | The dog is moved to a new recipe, or the recipe changes. | **11 of 31 cancellations (35%)**: dog wouldn't eat the new recipe. | This is the biggest single reason. The customer has no easy way to go back or swap, so cancelling becomes the only option. |
| **4. Discount ends (about month 2-4)** | The price goes up to full rate. | **9 of 31 (29%)**: price after the intro discount ended. | The customer reassesses the subscription right when the price jumps. |
| **5. Food piles up (month 3-4)** | The dog eats less than the delivery schedule assumes, or the customer is away. Boxes stack up. | **6 of 31 (19%)**: too much food. Tickets: "how do I skip a delivery". | The skip option exists but people can't find it, so they cancel instead. |
| **6. Cancellation** | The customer cancels. | 5 of 31 (16%) gave other reasons. | Cancel is easier to find than skip or swap. |

## The story for the team

1. **Roughly 80% of cancellations (25 of 31) look fixable.** They come from the recipe change, the price step-up, and pile-up. Only the 5 "other" answers are unexplained.
2. **Several cancellations may be avoidable with controls you already have.** Customers who cancel over pile-up, a bad delivery day, or a recipe the dog refuses may only want to skip, reschedule, or swap. The tickets suggest they can't find those options.
3. **Three of the four problems cluster in months 2-4.** The recipe change, the discount ending, and the stockpile all land in that window, which matches your churn pattern.

## Suggested fixes by stage

- **Recipe change:** Let customers go back to the old recipe or swap in one click. Warn them before the change, and consider a smaller trial portion.
- **Price:** Announce the step-up before it happens. Taper the discount, or offer a loyalty price instead of a cliff.
- **Pile-up and skip:** Put "Skip next delivery" on the main account page and in the delivery reminder email. Offer "pause" or "change frequency" on the cancel screen.
- **Delivery day:** Let customers pick a delivery day or a hold-for-neighbor option at sign-up, and send a tracking reminder.

## Caveats to mention when you present

- **The sample is small.** 31 responses is enough to show direction, but 11 vs. 9 is not a reliable ranking.
- **Timing is inferred.** Your data doesn't say which month each person cancelled in or when each reason applied. The stage placement is my reading of the 2-4 month pattern, so check it against cancellation dates if you have them.
- **Ticket counts are missing.** I don't know how frequent the skip and delivery-day complaints are compared with the cancellation reasons.
- **The link between skip friction and cancellation is a hypothesis.** It's plausible, but nothing in the data proves that people who couldn't find skip then cancelled. You could test it by checking whether cancelling customers had earlier skip-related tickets.
- **Survey bias.** Only people who cancelled and answered are included. Customers who quietly stayed, or cancelled without responding, aren't represented.
