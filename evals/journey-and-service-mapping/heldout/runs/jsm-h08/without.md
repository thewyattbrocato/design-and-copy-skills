# Customer journey: where month 2-4 churn happens

Based on 31 survey responses plus the support themes, here is a draft you can put in front of the team.

## The journey

| Stage | What the customer experiences | Evidence | Failure point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Sign-up (month 1)** | Gets an intro discount and starts a trial | Inferred from the price-after-discount complaints | Sets a low price anchor that expires later |
| **2. First deliveries (month 1-2)** | Box arrives, dog eats the starting recipe | No complaints cited here | Delivery on a day nobody is home (support tickets) |
| **3. Recipe change (about month 2)** | Dog is moved to a new recipe | **11 of 31 (35%)** cancelled because the dog wouldn't eat it | Biggest single cause. The dog refuses the food and the customer has no easy fix |
| **4. Stockpile builds (month 2-3)** | Boxes keep coming, but the dog eats less, or the household is away | **6 of 31 (19%)** cancelled over too much food | The customer wants to skip and can't find how ("how do I skip a delivery" tickets) |
| **5. Discount ends (month 3-4)** | First full-price charge | **9 of 31 (29%)** cancelled over price | The value has to stand on its own, and a customer who is already unhappy leaves |
| **6. Cancellation** | Cancels, with "other" as the reason for 5 (16%) | Survey | Cancelling is easier than skipping |

## The story for the team

1. **The product problem (35%).** The recipe change is the largest drop-off. A dog that rejects the food gives the customer a reason to leave.
2. **The control problem (19% plus the tickets).** Skip is hard to find, so a customer who just needs a pause ends up cancelling. Delivery-day mismatches add to this. Skip and delivery-day control are both fixable without changing the product.
3. **The price problem (29%).** The discount cliff turns mild dissatisfaction into a cancellation.

The three problems compound. A customer whose dog refuses the new recipe, who can't skip the next box, and who then faces full price has three reasons to leave.

## Caveats to state before you present

- **The sample is small.** With n=31, a gap of 11 vs 9 is not a real difference, so I'd present these as "three roughly comparable causes", not a ranking.
- **Timing is inferred.** The survey doesn't say which month each person cancelled. Mapping reasons to stages is a reasonable guess, so check it against cancellation dates.
- **The skip-to-cancel link is a hypothesis.** The tickets and the "too much food" answers fit together, but nothing shows that the same people did both. You can test this by matching ticket submitters to cancellers.
- **Ticket volume is missing.** I only know the themes are "common", not how many tickets there were. Counts would let you size the delivery-day and skip issues against the survey.
- **I don't know how the recipe change works.** I don't know if it's a forced rotation, a transition after a trial, or something else. That decides whether the fix is gradual mixing, opt-in, or a fallback recipe.

## Quick checks that would make this stronger

- The month of cancellation for each survey respondent.
- Whether people who cancelled for price were also on the new recipe.
- The skip-page click path or search terms, to confirm people can't find it.
