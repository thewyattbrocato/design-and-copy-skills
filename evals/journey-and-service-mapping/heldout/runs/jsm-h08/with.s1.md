**Map for the team:** a dog owner on the monthly subscription, from ordering at the intro price to the day they stay or cancel. It's meant to help the team choose which of three moments to fix first.

**Status:** built from 31 cancellation survey answers and the common support-ticket themes. Ticket counts, timing by month, and every feeling are assumptions for people close to customers to correct.

| | **1. Order at intro price** | **2. Receive the box** | **3. Get the dog eating it** | **4. Hold the next boxes** | **5. See the full price** | **6. Stay or cancel** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Does** | Picks a recipe, takes the discount | Finds the box, brings it in | Switches food, tries a new recipe | Stores food, looks for a way to skip | Opens the renewal charge | Weighs keeping it against quitting |
| **Asks** | Is this worth trying? | Will it be here when I am? | Why won't my dog eat this? | Where's the skip button? | Is it still worth it? | Do I cancel or keep going? |

**Evidence by phase:**
- **Phase 3:** 11 of 31 cancellations said the dog wouldn't eat the new recipe.
- **Phase 4:** 6 of 31 said too much food was piling up. The ticket theme "how do I skip a delivery" shows people can't find the control.
- **Phase 5:** 9 of 31 cited the price after the intro discount ended.
- **Phase 2:** the ticket theme "my box came when I'm not home."
- **Other:** 5 of 31 gave other reasons.

**Moments that matter**
1. **Phase 3, the dog refuses.** This is the biggest single reason (11 of 31), and it sits in the month 2–4 window where you're losing people. The cancel survey is the first moment we hear about it, so people may leave before any recovery offer. I assumed "new recipe" means a recipe change after the first box, which needs checking.
2. **Phase 5, the price steps up.** 9 of 31 left here. The discount ending is a known date, so this is a promise meeting delivery that we control.
3. **Phase 4, skip is hidden.** Tickets say people can't find it, and 6 of 31 left over stockpiling. It's plausible that these people would have stayed if they'd found skip, but that's *suspected* until we check whether those 6 ever contacted support or visited the skip page.

The delivery-day problem is real in tickets but isn't in the cancellation reasons, so I haven't marked it. It may be an annoyance rather than a reason to leave. Check whether cancelers had a missed-delivery ticket first.

**Caveats on the evidence**
- 31 answers is a small sample, and it covers only people who cancelled and answered.
- Reasons overlap. A dog refusing food also produces leftover stock, so "piling up" and "wouldn't eat" may be partly the same people.
