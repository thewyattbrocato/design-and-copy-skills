Here's the journey as the data shows it. The breakpoints are the stages where people hit friction and the cancel survey or support tickets pick it up.

## Customer journey: where people leave

| Stage | What the customer experiences | Where it breaks | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Sign-up** | Discounted intro price, first box ordered | Discount is the reason they came, not the reason they stay | Price after intro discount ended: 9 of 31 |
| **2. First boxes arrive** | Box shows up, dog gets new recipe | Dog won't eat it. Usually within the first few weeks | Wouldn't eat new recipe: 11 of 31 (largest single reason) |
| **3. Early routine** | Food accumulates, box cadence feels fixed | Food arrives faster than the dog eats it, so the house fills up | Too much food piling up: 6 of 31 |
| **4. Box arrives on the wrong day** | Delivery lands while nobody's home | No way to reschedule or redirect, so the box sits or gets wasted | "Box came on a day I'm not home" (support tickets) |
| **5. Wants to pause or skip** | Looks for a way to skip one delivery | Can't find it. Support is getting the question | "How do I skip a delivery" (support tickets) |
| **6. Cancels** | Cancel is the only exit they can find | Cancel is the default, so skip-or-pause saves never happen | Survey: 31 cancellations, 5 in "other" |

**The main story:** Stages 3 through 5 are where the cancellations happen. A customer who has too much food, or a box on the wrong day, wants to pause or skip. Since skip is hard to find, the only option they can see is to cancel. The 6 "too much food" responses are likely people who would have stayed if skip were visible. Taste (stage 2) is a product problem and probably needs a separate fix: a sampler, a transition plan, or a refund that's easy to claim.

## Caveats before you present this

- **31 responses is a small sample.** Treat the percentages as directional, not precise.
- **The survey is self-reported and seems to be single-choice.** Customers often give the reason that's easiest to name, not the one that caused the cancel. Skip and delivery timing might be hiding under "too much food" or "other."
- **The survey doesn't say when people cancelled.** You can't yet confirm that month 2 to 4 lines up with the discount ending or the first full-price charge. Pull cancel date minus first-order date, and check it against the intro discount length.
- **Support tickets have no counts in your notes.** Get the number of tickets for each issue so the team can compare them with the survey.

## What I'd pull before the meeting

2. Count of "skip" and "reschedule" support contacts per month.
3. Share of cancellers who ever opened the account's delivery settings. If few did, skip is a discoverability issue.
4. Reorder and feeding-complaint rates for the new recipe versus the old one.
