## Customer journey: where it breaks

| Stage | What the customer experiences | Where it breaks | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Sign-up** (Month 0) | Intro discount, first box arrives | Discount hides the real price, so the customer is committed before they know the cost | Price complaints appear only after the discount ends |
| **2. First feeding** (Weeks 1–4) | Dog is served the new recipe | **Dog refuses to eat it.** Nothing in the journey helps them switch or get a replacement | 11 of 31 (35%) cancelled for this, the largest single reason |
| **3. Discount ends** (Month 2 bill) | Price jumps to full rate | **Sticker shock at the first full-price charge** | 9 of 31 (29%) |
| **4. Stockpile builds** (Months 2–3) | Boxes keep arriving, food piles up | **No easy way to pause or reduce the cadence**, so food goes unused | 6 of 31 (19%) said too much food; ties to the skip-delivery tickets |
| **5. Delivery day** (ongoing) | Box arrives when nobody is home | **No way to change the delivery day or window** | Recurring support tickets |
| **6. Self-serve fix attempt** | Customer looks for "skip" or "pause" | **Can't find the control**, so they contact support or give up | Recurring support tickets |
| **7. Cancel** (Months 2–4) | Cancellation flow asks for a reason | Cancel is the only visible lever left, so it becomes the default exit | Survey response total: 31 |

## The pattern

Stages 4 to 6 share one root cause. Three of your four cancel reasons (too much food, wrong delivery day, and the skip problem behind the tickets) come from the subscription not flexing to the customer's schedule or consumption. The customer's only visible option is to cancel. Stages 2 and 3 are different problems: product fit and price.

## Caveats before you present this

- **31 responses is a small sample.** Treat the percentages as directional.
- **The survey only covers people who cancelled.** It doesn't show how many people hit the same problems and stayed frustrated.
- **The ticket themes aren't counted.** You don't yet know how many tickets they represent, so you can't rank them against the survey.
- **Timing is inferred.** The Month 2–4 window is consistent with the discount ending and the stockpile building, but you haven't tied each cancellation to a tenure date.

## What would confirm it

1. **Tenure at cancel:** Pull the cancel date for each of the 31 and compare it to the billing cycle. If most cancel right after the first full-price charge, that supports stage 3.
2. **Ticket counts:** Tag the last 90 days of tickets by theme and count them.
3. **Skip/pause visibility:** Check whether the account page has a skip or pause control, and how many clicks it takes to find it.
4. **Recipe switch path:** Check whether a customer whose dog refuses the recipe has any way to swap it. If not, that's the gap for stage 2.
