# Customer journey: where subscribers drop off

Subscribers who cancel around months 2 to 4 hit three problems in sequence: the new food doesn't work for the dog, the discount ends, and the box keeps arriving when they can't stop or move it. The survey shows the reasons, and the tickets show the mechanics behind two of them.

## The journey

| Stage | What the customer experiences | What goes wrong | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Sign up on intro discount** | Low first-box price, expectation of a smooth switch | Discount frames the subscription as temporary, so the real price comes later | 9 of 31 cancel on price after the discount ends |
| **2. First box arrives** | Switching the dog to a new recipe, usually all at once | No clear transition guidance, or the dog refuses the food | 11 of 31 (35%) say the dog wouldn't eat the recipe. This is the largest single reason. |
| **3. Feeding trial (weeks 1 to 4)** | Dog eats little or none. Owner has extra food and doesn't know what to do. | Owner has no easy way to pause, swap, or get help before the next box | Connects to the 6 "too much food piling up" responses |
| **4. Intro discount ends (about month 2 to 3)** | First full-price charge, often while the dog is still refusing food | Price increase lands on a product that isn't working | Overlaps with stage 2 cancellations. These may be the same people hit twice. |
| **5. Ongoing deliveries** | Recurring box on a fixed schedule | Customer can't find the skip option ("how do I skip a delivery"). Box arrives when no one is home ("came on a day I'm not home"). | Support tickets, both recurring |
| **6. Cancel attempt** | Customer decides to leave and goes through the cancel flow | The flow is the last chance to offer a skip, pause, or swap. It probably doesn't. | Survey answers are collected here, so they may reflect the reason the customer finally acted on, not the first one |

## The main break points

1. **Stage 2 to 3: the recipe failure.** This is the biggest reason and the most fixable. Options include a slower transition plan (mix old and new food over 7 to 10 days), a sample or smaller first size, and a refund or swap if the dog refuses it. Check whether the people who said "dog wouldn't eat" were still on the intro-price period when they cancelled.

2. **Stage 3 to 5: food overload is probably a skip problem.** Six people say there's too much food, and the skip-finding tickets show customers can't pause deliveries. These could be one problem. A dog that won't eat the food keeps getting boxes, and the owner can't find the skip button, so the food piles up. Cancelling is the only exit they can see.

3. **Stage 4: the price cliff lands on a bad experience.** Nine customers left after the discount ended. Those who also had a refusal or an overstock problem were paying full price for food they weren't using. Price is probably less of a standalone cause than it looks.

4. **Stage 5: delivery timing has no owner.** "Box came on a day I'm not home" is an operations fix. Options include letting customers pick or change a delivery day, showing the expected arrival window, and offering a hold or reschedule link in shipping notifications.

5. **Stage 6: the cancel flow doesn't offer the alternatives.** A cancel flow should offer skip, pause, a different recipe, or a discount, depending on the reason. If it offers only "confirm cancel," the survey is capturing the reasons people chose to leave, not the points where you could have kept them.

## Caveats before you present this

- **31 responses is a small sample.** Treat the percentages as directional. Don't put a precise number on any single cause.
- **Survey answers are self-reported and given at the moment of leaving.** Customers often name the last straw. Support tickets are a better signal for mechanics.
- **"Other" (5) is unexplained.** Read those responses before presenting, since they may contain a fifth cause.

## What would confirm this

- **Cancel timing:** Plot cancellations by months since signup. If there's a spike at the discount-end month, the price cliff is real.
- **Cross-tab the survey:** Of the 6 "too much food" respondents, how many also filed a skip-related ticket or never used the skip feature?
- **Skip-feature usage:** Count how many subscribers used skip or pause before cancelling. If it's near zero, the feature is hard to find.
- **Tickets before cancel:** For cancelled customers, check whether they contacted support in the 30 days before cancelling. This shows which problems come before exit.
