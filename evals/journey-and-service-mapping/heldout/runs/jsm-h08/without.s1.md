# Customer journey: where the month 2-4 drop-off happens

## The journey

| Stage | What the customer experiences | What your data says | Failure type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Sign-up (intro offer)** | Joins at a discounted price. | No direct data. The discount sets a price expectation. | Setup for a later problem |
| **2. First boxes (month 1-2)** | Dog tries the food. Boxes arrive on a fixed schedule. | "Box came when I'm not home" (tickets). **Dog wouldn't eat the new recipe: 11 of 31 (35%)** | Product fit, plus delivery friction |
| **3. Discount ends (about month 2-3)** | The price steps up to full rate. | **Price after the intro discount: 9 of 31 (29%)** | Price shock |
| **4. Ongoing (month 2-4)** | Food builds up faster than the dog eats it. They look for a way to skip. | "How do I skip a delivery" (tickets, people can't find it). **Too much food piling up: 6 of 31 (19%)** | Control and flexibility |
| **5. Cancel** | Cancelling is the option they can find. | Other: 5 of 31 (16%) | Exit |

## The story for the team

1. **Product risk lands early.** The largest cancel reason (35%) is a dog refusing food. Nothing in your data shows a safety net, like a swap, a smaller trial, or a transition guide.
2. **The price step arrives right as doubt starts.** Anyone whose dog is lukewarm, or who already has surplus, hits the full price at that point. The 29% citing price probably includes people with another problem underneath.
3. **Skip is hard to find, and "too much food" is a skip problem.** People who can't find skip may end up cancelling instead. That would make the 19% "piling up" group, and possibly some of the "other" group, recoverable with a visible skip button.
4. **Delivery-day issues add friction to every box.** A box that arrives when no one is home is a bad experience, and it compounds the other problems.

Roughly 55-70% of cancellations (piling up, plus part of the price and recipe groups) look like **flexibility problems rather than "I don't want this" problems**. That is my inference, not something the survey shows.

## Caveats to state up front

- **31 responses is small.** One or two answers can move a share by several points, so treat the ranking as directional.
- **Cancellation timing isn't tied to reasons.** I placed each reason at a stage by inference. A table of reason by cancel month would show whether price cancellations really cluster right after the discount ends.
- **Ticket volumes are missing.** I can't rank "skip" against "delivery day" without counts.
- **"New recipe" is ambiguous.** It could mean the first recipe they tried, or a recipe change mid-subscription. The fix differs, so check which it is.
- **The skip-to-cancel link is a hypothesis.** To test it, check whether customers who opened a skip ticket cancelled within the following 30 days.
