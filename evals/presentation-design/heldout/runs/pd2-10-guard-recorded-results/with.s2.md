**Rough timeline (6 min, about 4 content slides)**

| Time | Beat | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:45 | Takeaway first | One claim slide, e.g. "Q3 revenue [X], margin down [Y] points, and here's why" |
| 0:45–1:45 | Headline numbers | One slide, 2–3 big numbers, nothing else |
| 1:45–2:45 | Why gross margin fell | The margin slide (below) |
| 2:45–3:45 | Customer story | The story slide (below) |
| 3:45–5:15 | What changes in Q4 | One claim slide |
| 5:15–6:00 | Close | The takeaway again, and any ask with an owner and date |

I put margin before the story so the story can land as evidence of where the business is going. If the story is about margin recovery, that order works even better. If it isn't, swap them.

**Margin minute**
- The slide is a single bridge chart from last quarter's margin to this quarter's. Label only the one or two biggest drivers. Use a claim title like "Margin fell [Y] points, mostly because of [driver]".
- Build the bars in the order you say them. The final frame stays up for the last 10 seconds so viewers can pause and read it.
- Pause for about 2 seconds after you say the size of the drop, and again before you move to the cause. Cut the silence tight in editing so it reads as a beat, not dead air.
- Keep the derivation and the full cost table off the slide. If people will want them, link them from the intranet post.

**Customer story**
- Use one full-bleed photo, logo or quote, with the customer's name and one line at most. The slide should be calm enough that people listen instead of read.
- Take a 1–2 second beat before you start, and another at the end before you cut to the next slide. Hold the image through both.
- Tie it back to the takeaway in one sentence as you leave.

**For laptop viewers**
- Keep body text at 32 px or larger on a 1920-wide frame, and keep each slide under about 30 words.
- Put your face in a small corner or skip it. Don't let it cover the chart.
- Add captions, since many people will watch without sound.
