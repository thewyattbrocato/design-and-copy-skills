Because people will watch this alone on laptops, don't blank the screen at any pause. In a recording, a blank screen just looks like a glitch. Pause your voice for a beat and put up a quiet slide tied to that moment.

**Assumptions:** 6 minutes, about 5 content slides (some are a single image or number), and a takeaway you still need to write: *"Q3 [hit/missed X], and margin fell because of [reason], which we're fixing by [action]."*

| Time | Slide (claim title) | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:45 | "Q3 [headline result], with margin down [X pts]" | One big number or two. Say the takeaway out loud here. |
| 0:45–2:00 | "Revenue grew [X%] on [driver]" | One chart with the key bar or line labeled directly. |
| **2:00–3:15** | **Customer story** | See below. |
| **3:15–4:15** | **"Gross margin fell [X pts] because of [cause]"** | See below. |
| 4:15–5:30 | "Here's what we're doing about it" | Two or three short lines, each with an owner. |
| 5:30–6:00 | Recap | Repeat the takeaway. Say what, if anything, you want viewers to do. |

**Customer story (about 75 seconds)**
- **Before it starts:** Pause about one second and say a signpost line like "Let me show you what this looked like for one customer."
- **On screen:** One full-bleed photo or the customer's logo, if they've approved its use. Add one line at most, such as `[Customer], [what they achieved]` or a pull quote under 20 words. Keep the text large, since laptop viewers sit close but the screen is small.
- **No bullets, no metrics table.** If one result number matters, show it alone on a second beat of the same slide.
- **After it ends:** Return to the numbers with a bridge line, like "That's the upside. Now the part that cost us."

**Margin explanation (about 60 seconds)**
- **Before it starts:** Pause for a beat and state the question: "Why did gross margin fall?"
- **On screen:** One bridge (waterfall) chart from last quarter's margin to this quarter's, with 2–3 labeled drivers. The title states the answer, so nobody has to wait for it.
- **Build the chart in the order you speak it.** Each driver appears as you name it, so the viewer's eye follows your voice. Don't add other animation.
- **Keep it to what you'll mention.** Put detailed cost tables in a linked appendix or handout, not on screen.
- **End on the so-what:** One line on whether the drop is temporary or structural. That's what people will remember.

**Recording tips**
- Keep pauses to 1–2 seconds. Viewers can scrub, and long silences feel like dead air.
- Add chapter markers on the intranet for "Customer story" and "Why margin fell." People will skip to them.
- If you have the numbers and the customer, send them and I'll tighten the titles and the spoken transitions. I haven't invented any figures here.
