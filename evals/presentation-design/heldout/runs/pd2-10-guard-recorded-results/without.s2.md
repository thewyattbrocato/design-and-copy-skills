Since nobody is watching live, you don't need pauses for reactions or questions. Use short beats of about 1 to 2 seconds so people can absorb a number or a shift in topic. Longer silences read as dead air, and viewers can pause or scrub themselves. Put the effort into what's on screen instead.

**Rough 6-minute shape**

| Time | Segment | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Headline: the 3 numbers that matter, including that margin fell | You on camera, then one slide with 3 big KPIs |
| 0:30–2:15 | Core results (revenue, pipeline, etc.) | One simple chart per idea, 20–30 seconds each |
| 2:15–3:15 | Customer story | See below |
| 3:15–4:30 | Why gross margin fell | See below |
| 4:30–5:30 | What it means and what's next | One slide with 2–3 bullets at most |
| 5:30–6:00 | Close and where to ask questions | Contact or channel name, held for 5+ seconds |

**Customer story (about 60 seconds)**
- Put a beat before it and a beat after it, each marked by a clean cut or title card. Don't pause in the middle.
- Show the customer's name and logo, then a photo, product shot, or a 10–15 second clip of them if you have one. Cutting away from your face signals "this is a story."
- Pick one number that proves the point and show it large as a lower third. Hold it for a second or two after you say it.
- Pause once, right after the punchline, before you tie it back to the quarter.

**Gross margin (about 60–75 seconds)**
- Use a bridge (waterfall) chart from last quarter's margin to this quarter's. Build it one bar at a time, in sync with your narration, so viewers don't read ahead of you.
- Pause for about 2 seconds after you state the drop (for example, "down 3 points to X%"). Then pause again before the biggest driver, which should be the one you spend the most time on.
- Label only the two or three drivers you name aloud. Give the cause in plain words ("hosting costs rose faster than revenue"), and say whether it's temporary or structural. That is what people will want to know.
- End on a one-line takeaway on screen, such as "Expect X through Q4, then Y."

**Laptop and on-your-own-time details**
- Use large type, with no more than about 3 elements per slide. Assume people are watching in a window, not full screen.
- Add captions, since many people will watch without sound.
- Add chapter markers or timestamps in the intranet post, so people can jump straight to margin or to the story.
- Give a way to ask questions afterward (Slack channel, email, or a short Q&A form). That replaces the live session.
- Record the margin section last, or at least re-listen to it. Pacing there matters most, and you may want to trim after you hear it.

If the customer story is upbeat and margin comes right after, a quick bridging line helps. "That growth is real, and so is the cost we paid for it" keeps the margin section from sounding like bad news you buried behind good news.
