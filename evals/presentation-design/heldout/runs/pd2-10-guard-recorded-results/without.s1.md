**Skip the long pauses.** In a live session a pause gives people time to react or ask a question. In a recorded video nobody is there, so a long pause just sounds like dead air. Viewers can pause and rewind themselves, so use short beats and change what's on screen instead.

**Suggested 6:00 layout**

| Time | Segment | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Headline: revenue, margin, and "here's what to watch for" | You on camera, with one summary slide |
| 0:30–1:30 | Key results (3 numbers max) | One big number per slide, with the prior-year or prior-quarter comparison |
| 1:30–2:45 | **Why gross margin fell** | Chart (see below) |
| 2:45–3:45 | **Customer story** (around 60 seconds) | Customer photo or logo, then a pull quote and one result number |
| 3:45–5:30 | Q4 outlook and priorities | Short slides, 3 points |
| 5:30–6:00 | Close and where to send questions | You on camera |

I put margin before the story so the drop is explained right after viewers see it. The story then comes at the midpoint as a break, and you can tie it back to the margin theme if it fits.

**Pauses (about 1 second each)**
- Take a beat before the margin section and say "Now the part you're probably wondering about." Then cut the silence out in editing.
- Take a beat after the key margin number so it sinks in.
- Take a beat when you move into and out of the story.

**Margin explanation (about 75 seconds)**
- Use one bridge (waterfall) chart: last quarter's margin, then each driver as a step, then this quarter's margin. Limit it to 2–3 drivers.
- Build the chart one step at a time as you speak. Leave each state up for 3–4 seconds so people can read it.
- Label the bars directly, with no legend. Use large text because many people will watch on a laptop at reduced size.
- End on the full chart for a couple of seconds. People will pause on it or come back to it.

**Customer story (about 60 seconds)**
- Open with the person or company, not the data. Show a photo or logo and a one-line setup.
- Put the quote on screen as text while you or they say it.
- End with one concrete result number, then cut straight back to you.
- Cut away from slides here. A change of visual is what makes it feel like a story.

**Because it's on-demand**
- Add captions. Many people will watch at their desks with the sound off or low.
- Add chapter markers on the intranet page ("Results," "Gross margin," "Customer story," "Outlook") so people can jump to or rewatch the margin explanation.
- Put the margin bridge chart in the page description as a static image so people can reference it later.
