Nobody is in the room, so there's no audience reaction to wait for. Viewers can also pause and scrub on their own. Your "pauses" are short beats of 1 to 2 seconds of silence, plus a change in what's on screen. They tell viewers a new section is starting and give them a moment to absorb a number.

## Suggested run of show

| Time | Section | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Headline: Q3 in one sentence and 3 numbers | Single slide, 3 big numbers, nothing else. You on camera in a corner or intercut. |
| 0:30–2:00 | Results overview (revenue, key metrics, wins) | One chart per idea. Reveal one at a time and highlight the number you're talking about. |
| **2:00–3:00** | **Customer story** | Full-screen switch away from slides. Use customer footage or photo, name and logo, and a one-line outcome (for example "cut onboarding from 6 weeks to 9 days"). |
| **3:00–4:00** | **Why gross margin fell** | A waterfall chart: last quarter's margin, then each driver, then this quarter's margin. |
| 4:00–5:15 | Outlook and what changes in Q4 | Simple timeline or 3 bullets, with big text. |
| 5:15–6:00 | Recap and what you want people to do | Back to the opening layout, with one action and a contact or link. |

## Where to pause

- **Before and after the customer story.** Hold a beat on the transition. Cut to a different visual style so it feels like a chapter change. End the story on the outcome number, pause, then say one line tying it back to the quarter.
- **After you state the margin drop.** Say "Gross margin fell from X to Y." Hold 2 seconds on that number alone, then explain.
- **After each driver in the margin explanation.** Limit it to two or three drivers. Each gets one sentence and one bar on the waterfall. Add a short beat between them so the chart can register.
- **Before the outlook.** This is a reset beat after the harder topic.

## Laptop and self-paced viewing

- **Captions on.** Many people watch at their desks with the sound low or off.
- **Big text and few words.** Laptop windows are small. Keep one idea per slide and use numbers over 40pt.
- **Chapter markers** if the intranet player supports them, for example "Customer story" and "Gross margin". People will skip to those, so each section should make sense alone.
- **Put the margin answer first.** Start the minute with the one-sentence reason, then show the detail. Viewers who stop early still get the answer.
- **Consider the order.** Placing the story right before the margin section softens the shift into bad news. If the story is connected to the margin (mix, pricing, a big account), say so explicitly as the bridge. If it isn't, don't imply it is.
