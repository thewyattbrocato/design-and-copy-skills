Since most people can't see your face or gesture, your voice has to do the pointing. Here's a flow that works for that.

**1. Say the takeaway before the chart appears (~15 sec)**
"One thing to know from this quarter's NPS: APAC has dropped two quarters in a row, from 41 to 33, while every other region has held roughly flat."

People then know what to look for, so they aren't scanning four lines and guessing.

**2. Orient them to the chart (~15 sec)**
"This shows the last eight quarters. Time runs left to right, NPS is up the side, and each line is a region."

Name the lines and say where they sit, for example "NA and EMEA are up top, LATAM is in the middle." Someone who joined late or is half-listening can still follow.

**3. Show the stable regions first (~20 sec)**
"NA, EMEA, and LATAM have barely moved. They've stayed within a few points of where they started."

The contrast is the point of the chart, so establish the baseline before the exception.

**4. Then APAC, with exact locations (~30 sec)**
"Now look at the APAC line. Up to [quarter] it was tracking with the others, at 41. Then it fell in [quarter], and again in [quarter], to 33. That's an 8-point drop over two quarters."

Say the quarter labels and numbers aloud, since nobody can rely on reading small axis text. Pause for a beat after "33."

**5. Say what it means for them (~30+ sec)**
With sales and support in the room, they'll want to know why it matters to them. Cover what you know (cause, hypotheses, what you're investigating) and what you're asking of them, such as "if you work APAC accounts, tell me what you're hearing." If you don't know the cause yet, say so. That's better than guessing.

**6. Close by repeating the takeaway (~10 sec)**
"So again: three regions steady, APAC down 8 points in two quarters, and here's what happens next."

**Fix the chart so it does the work too**
- Make APAC a bold, saturated color and the other three a muted gray or light tone, so the eye lands on APAC.
- Label lines directly at their right edge instead of using a legend, which saves people from matching colors.
- Annotate the 41 and the 33 on the APAC line.
- Use large fonts. Zoom compresses the screen share, and small text turns to mush.

**Zoom-specific tips**
- Share the screen early and confirm: "Can everyone see the chart?"
- Don't use a laser pointer or cursor. It's easy to miss, so use words.
- Since you can't read the room, build in one check-in, like "I'll pause here. Anything to ask before I go on?" Or ask for questions in chat.
- If you can, have a backup slide with the same chart, APAC alone, in case someone asks for a closer look.
