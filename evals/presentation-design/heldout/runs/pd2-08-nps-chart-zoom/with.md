Here's a walkthrough for one chart, about 3–4 minutes of talking, built for a Zoom call where people can't point and many aren't on camera.

## Fix the slide first

- **Title as the claim:** "APAC NPS has fallen two quarters in a row, from 41 to 33, while every other region held flat."
- **Make APAC the only loud line.** Use one strong color and a thick line. Set NA, EMEA and LATAM in light gray or one muted tone.
- **Label the lines directly at their right-hand ends**, not in a legend. People on a shared screen shouldn't have to match colors to a key.
- **Put "41" and "33" on the APAC line** as callouts, plus the two quarters where it fell. Skip the other data labels.
- **Avoid red for APAC** if red already means "alarm" in your sales and support dashboards. A deep orange or blue works too. Check that it still stands out in grayscale.
- Keep any extra words under about 30.

## Build it on screen, if you can

Zoom screen share handles builds fine. Use three steps that match what you say:
1. Axes only, or all four lines in gray.
2. The three flat regions.
3. APAC in color, with the 41 and 33 callouts.

If a build is too fussy, show the finished chart with APAC already highlighted.

## What to say

**1. Say the point before the chart gets decoded (about 20 seconds).**
"One thing in this quarter's NPS I want everyone to leave with: APAC has dropped two quarters running, from 41 to 33. The other three regions are basically flat."

**2. Teach the chart (about 15 seconds).**
"Quick orientation: each line is a region, across the last eight quarters. Higher is better. The gray lines are NA, EMEA and LATAM."
Say this because with cameras off you can't see who's lost.

**3. Establish "normal" (about 30 seconds).**
"These three have moved a couple of points either way for two years. That's the baseline. Nothing to act on there."

**4. Bring in APAC (about 45 seconds).**
"Now APAC. It was steady at 41, then dropped in [Q-name], and dropped again in [Q-name], to 33. That's 8 points, and it's the only line moving."
Pause here for a few seconds. In a silent Zoom room, the pause is what lets people absorb it.

**5. Say what you know and don't know (about 30 seconds).**
Only claim what you have. For example: "What we know: it's both quarters, so it isn't a one-off. What we don't know yet: [cause or 'we're still digging']."
Don't invent a cause for the room. If you have no cause yet, saying so is stronger than guessing.

**6. Close with the ask, with an owner and date (about 30 seconds).**
"For sales and support, [specific ask: flag APAC escalations to X / join the review on DATE / send examples to Y]." Leave this on screen or in chat for questions.

## Zoom-specific tips

- **Don't ask "any questions?" into silence.** Say: "I'll pause for 20 seconds. Put questions or APAC examples in chat." Or name someone beforehand, such as the APAC lead, to react.
- **Say the numbers aloud.** Not everyone is looking, and some are on a phone.
- **Anticipate the top two questions** and have answers ready. They're probably "Is it statistically meaningful?" (have sample sizes ready) and "What's driving it?" Put the sample sizes on a backup slide.
- **Keep the chart at the same size and position** if you flip to another slide and back, so people don't re-orient.

I assumed the ask is that people investigate or act on APAC. If it's purely informational, close on the takeaway instead. I also don't know the cause, so I left a placeholder. Don't present a theory as fact unless you have evidence.
