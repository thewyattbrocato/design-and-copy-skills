Since most people won't be on camera, you can't read the room. Narrate the chart so that someone half-listening still gets the point.

**Structure (about 3-4 minutes)**

1. **Lead with the conclusion.** Say it before showing the detail.
   > "One headline this quarter: APAC's NPS has dropped two quarters in a row, from 41 to 33. NA, EMEA, and LATAM are roughly flat."

2. **Orient them to the chart.** Say what's on each axis and where to look, because people will be tabbing in and out.
   > "This is NPS over the last eight quarters, one line per region. Time runs left to right. I'm going to point you to the right-hand side of the chart."

3. **Show the baseline first, then APAC.** Name the three flat regions in one breath: "These three lines are bunched together and barely moving." Then: "APAC is the line that peels away. It was at 41, and it's now at 33."

4. **Give the size and the timing.**
   > "That's an 8-point drop over two quarters. Nothing else on this chart moved like that."

   If you know the drop's size relative to normal quarter-to-quarter noise, say so. It heads off the "is this just variance?" question.

5. **Say what you know and what you don't.** Pick whichever fits:
   - If you know the cause: "Here's what we've found so far..."
   - If you don't: "We don't yet know why. Here's what we're looking at."

   Either is fine. Don't speculate on causes you haven't verified, because sales and support will fill the gap with their own theories.

6. **End with a specific ask for each audience.** This keeps 60 people from passively nodding.
   > "Sales: if you've heard anything from APAC accounts about X, tell me. Support: if you've seen ticket themes in that region, drop them in the chat or message me after."

7. **Recap in one sentence.**
   > "APAC is down 8 points over two quarters, everything else is steady, and I'd like your help figuring out why."

**Tips for Zoom with cameras off**

- **Use the chat to check in.** Ask one low-effort question, like "Drop a 👀 if APAC's drop surprises you." It gives you a pulse and wakes people up.
- **Pause after the headline.** Two seconds of silence feels long to you but works fine on a call.
- **Say directions out loud.** "Look at the far right" and "the orange line" work better than pointing, since screen-share cursors are easy to miss.
- **Expect questions in the chat.** Tell them at the start: "Put questions in chat and I'll take them after the chart."

**Chart tweaks that help (if you still have time)**

- Color APAC strongly and make the other three a muted gray, so the eye goes there immediately.
- Label the lines directly instead of using a legend.
- Annotate the endpoints with "41" and "33".
- Mark where the decline starts, so you can say "right here."
