**Takeaway:** APAC's NPS fell two quarters in a row, from 41 to 33, while NA, EMEA and LATAM held roughly flat.

On a Zoom call with cameras off, nobody can see you point. Everything has to be said out loud and shown on the slide itself.

**Prep the chart**
- **Title as the claim:** "APAC NPS fell 8 points in two quarters while every other region held flat." Don't use "NPS by region."
- **Highlight APAC:** Draw it in one strong color and the other three in muted gray. Label the lines directly at the right edge instead of using a legend.
- **Label the two numbers:** Put 41 and 33 on the APAC line, with the quarter where the drop starts.
- **Keep other text small:** Keep the rest to the title and axis labels, which leaves the chart as the one thing to look at.

**Talk track (about 3–4 minutes)**
1. **Orient (20 sec):** "This is NPS for each region over the last eight quarters. Higher is better. Three regions are in gray, and APAC is in color."
2. **Set the baseline (30 sec):** "Start with the gray lines. NA, EMEA and LATAM have moved very little over two years. That's our normal."
3. **Show the break (45 sec):** "Now the colored line. APAC was at 41 and then dropped in two consecutive quarters, to 33. That's 8 points, and it's the only region moving."
4. **Say why it matters (30 sec):** "One bad quarter can be noise. Two in a row, with the other regions steady, looks like something specific to APAC."
5. **Be honest about what you know (30 sec):** Give the cause if you have it. If you don't, say so: "We don't yet know why. That's what I'd like help with."
6. **Make the ask (30 sec):** Name what you want from sales and support, who owns it and by when. For example: "Support: flag any APAC patterns in recent tickets. Sales: share what you're hearing from APAC accounts. Send it to [owner] by [date]." I don't know your actual ask, so fill that in.

**Tips for this audience**
- Say where to look every time: "the colored line, bottom right." Use quarter names or positions, not "here."
- Pause after the "41 to 33" line. With cameras off, silence is the only cue that something matters.
- Ask for input in chat or by name instead of "any questions?" Silent rooms stay silent otherwise. For example: "Support folks, does this match what you've seen? Drop a line in chat."
- Keep this slide up during discussion so the ask stays visible.
- Have one backup slide ready for the likely question, such as sample size or response counts per quarter in APAC, if you have them. Someone will ask whether 33 is statistically meaningful.
