**10 minutes, 5 content slides, about 2 minutes each. Takeaway:** "Churn is up 1.3 points since the new onboarding emails, and they're our leading suspect. One cohort doesn't fit, so I'm asking you to approve a holdback test that settles it."

I assumed Leo owns or sponsors the emails and may be defensive about them. I also assumed the decision you want is a test, because you can't yet justify pausing the emails. If you want something else, the last slide changes.

## Slides

1. **Monthly churn rose from 3.1% to 4.4% in three weeks.**
   - Visual: weekly churn line with a marker on "new emails, the 2nd".
   - Say aloud that the jump starts at the marker, if the weekly data shows it. Check this before you commit to the claim.

2. **The new emails are the leading suspect, but I can't prove it yet.**
   - Visual: two short lines, "For: timing, and nothing else shipped that we know of" and "Against: next slide".
   - Say the "can't prove it" part out loud. Leo will trust the rest more if you do.

3. **One cohort doesn't fit: March annual signups churned less after the change (1.2% vs 1.6%).**
   - Visual: two bars, before and after.
   - The message is that the effect may differ by segment. The emails may help annual plans and hurt monthly ones, or the spike may have another cause.

4. **One cut would separate "the emails" from "something else": churn for people who got the new emails vs. people who didn't.**
   - Visual: a 2×2 sketch of recipients vs. non-recipients, before and after. Leave it as a placeholder if you don't have the numbers yet.
   - If the new emails only go to new signups, they can't explain churn among older customers. That cut tells you quickly whether they can be the main driver.

5. **Decision: approve a holdback so we know within [N] weeks.**
   - Visual: the ask in one line. Hold back the new emails from [X%] of new signups, with [owner] setting it up by [date] and a readout on [date].
   - Leave this slide up during questions.

## Opening (about 90 seconds)

"Churn went from 3.1% to 4.4% in three weeks. The new onboarding emails went out right at the start of that. I think they're the most likely cause, but I can't prove it, and one cohort points the other way. I'm here for one decision: approve a small holdback test so we can settle it."
