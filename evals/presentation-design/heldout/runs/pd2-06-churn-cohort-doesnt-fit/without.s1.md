**Don't present this as "the emails caused the churn spike."** You can't prove that yet, and Leo will probably poke at the March cohort. Present it as a leading hypothesis with a cheap test and a decision you need from him. That holds up under questions, and a 10-minute slot suits it.

## Slides

**1. The headline and the ask (30 seconds)**
"Monthly churn rose from 3.1% to 4.4% since the 2nd. The new onboarding emails are the leading suspect. I'd like your OK to run a 2-week holdback to confirm."

**2. What we know (2 minutes)**
- A chart of weekly churn with a vertical line at the 2nd.
- The size of the jump: +1.3 points, or about 42% relative.
- Nothing else shipped in the window, as far as you know. Say "as far as I know" and ask Leo if marketing, pricing, or billing changed anything.

**3. What doesn't fit, and what it means (3 minutes)**
Show the March annual cohort (1.2% vs 1.6%) yourself before he raises it. There are two likely reasons it doesn't contradict your hypothesis:
- Annual plans mostly can't churn month to month, so that cohort may not have been exposed to the emails in a meaningful way. Check whether those users even received them.
- The blended rate may be driven by monthly-plan users, so look at annual and monthly separately.

If the March cohort did get the emails and still churned less, that is real evidence against you. Say so and don't explain it away.

**4. How we'll find out (3 minutes)**
Show the cuts that separate the hypotheses:
- **Churn by exposure:** users who got the new emails (signed up after the 2nd) vs. older users who didn't. If older users also spiked, it isn't the emails.
- **Churn by plan type:** monthly vs. annual.
- **Timing:** how many days after the emails does churn happen? Check unsubscribes and spam complaints too.
- **Holdback:** send the old sequence to ~10–20% of new signups for 2 weeks. This is the only way to actually prove it.

**5. Decision needed (1 minute)**
Approval for the holdback, and who owns it.

## Before tomorrow
- **Run the exposure cut (new signups vs. older users) tonight if you can.** It's the fastest single check, and the slides read very differently depending on the result.
- **Check the dates.** Today is Nov 4, so "three weeks since the 2nd" doesn't match either Nov 2 or Oct 2 (about five weeks). Make sure the window on your chart is what you think it is.
- **Check that the comparison is fair.** Three weeks of data against a monthly rate can be skewed by partial periods, seasonality, or a change in the signup mix. Compare against the same weeks last year or against prior cohorts at the same tenure.

If the exposure cut comes back clean (only post-change signups spiked), you can upgrade slide 1 to "the evidence points strongly at the emails" and make the holdback a confirmation step. If it doesn't, you've avoided blaming the wrong thing in front of the VP.
