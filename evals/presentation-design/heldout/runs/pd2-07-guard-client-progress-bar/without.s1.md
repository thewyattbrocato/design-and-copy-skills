Seven section names across the top of every slide only fit if each label is one or two short words. I'd use a short label for the progress bar and a longer message title in the slide body. The bar stays compact, and the slide can still make its point.

| # | Progress-bar label | Slide title (in the body) | Time |
|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Delivery | Q3 delivery performance: [X% on-time vs. Y% target] | 2 min |
| 3 | Late shipments | Late shipments trace back to [top 2–3 causes] | 3 min |
| 4 | Quality | Quality incidents: [N incidents, main defect type] | 2 min |

| 6 | Fixes | Proposed fixes for delivery, quality and cost | 3 min |

**Fitting the template**
- **Label length:** Keep each label to 12 characters or fewer. "Late shipments" and "Next steps" are the longest. If they wrap or crowd the bar, use "Late ship." or "Root causes" and "Actions."
- **Parallel wording:** Use single nouns throughout (Delivery, Late shipments, Quality, Cost, Fixes). Don't mix in phrases like "Q3 delivery performance."
- **Agenda slide:** The progress bar already works as an agenda. Make slide 1 a one-line framing of the three topics plus the ask. That frees about a minute for the root-cause and fixes slides.
- **Body titles:** Fill in the bracketed numbers with your real figures. Procurement teams respond to a stated result more than to a topic label.
- **Timing:** Fifteen minutes is tight for seven slides. The times above total 15, so give the late-shipment root causes and the fixes the most time. Move any detail to an appendix.
