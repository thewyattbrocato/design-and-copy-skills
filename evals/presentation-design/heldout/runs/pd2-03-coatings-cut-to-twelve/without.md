**Short version:** aim for 8-9 main slides, with the rest held as backup for Q&A. At 12 minutes, about 1.25 minutes per slide is a safe pace. Your audience wants to know what works, what to avoid, and what they can do next. They don't need the chemistry.

## Suggested spine

| # | Slide | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | Quick |
| 2 | The problem: signal drift in seawater and what it costs (recalibration, replacement, data loss) | Frames why they should care, in their terms |
| 3 | What we tested: 6 formulations, 18 months in real seawater, success = under 5% drift | One slide on method, with no detail beyond that |
| 4 | **Headline:** C2 and C5 hold under 5% drift at 18 months | One clean drift-vs-time chart |
| 5 | Why we trust it: replicates, conditions, any independent validation | Credibility for skeptical buyers |
| 6 | C4: good drift, but peels under temperature swings. It may suit stable-temperature deployments. | Honest and useful, because some buyers will fit this niche |
| 7 | What didn't work: C1, C3, C6, one line each | Saves them from repeating dead ends |
| 8 | What this means for you: availability, cost, compatibility with your sensor types, how to get access or license | Probably your most important slide |
| 9 | Next steps and the ask | Pilot partners, co-testing, contact details |

## What to cut or move to backup

- Chemistry and mechanisms: composition, synthesis, why each formulation behaves as it does.
- Characterization data such as microscopy and spectroscopy.
- Per-formulation failure analysis for C1, C3 and C6. One slide covers it, and the detail goes in backup.
- Intermediate time points, such as 3- and 6-month results. Show the 18-month curve, with maybe a single early-warning note.
- Lab history, timeline and team slides.
- Statistical tables. Keep one error-bar chart and put the tables in backup.

## Rules of thumb

- **Lead with the result.** The first content slide after the problem should already show C2 and C5 working.
- **Show C2 and C5 on one chart**, with the other four faded or grouped. Put the 5% threshold on the chart as a line.
- **Keep a backup section** of 10-15 slides (mechanisms, C4 peeling data, failure details) for questions. Don't present it.
- **Spend most of your time on slides 4, 6 and 8.**

## Questions that would sharpen this

1. Does your internal deck cover cost, scale-up, supply, or IP and licensing? Buyers will ask, and internal reviews often skip these. If you have nothing yet, slide 8 should say so honestly and name who to contact.
2. What temperature range does C4 tolerate before it peels? That decides whether slide 6 is a real recommendation or just a caveat.
3. What do you want from the room: pilot partners, funding, licensees, or just visibility? That sets slide 9.
