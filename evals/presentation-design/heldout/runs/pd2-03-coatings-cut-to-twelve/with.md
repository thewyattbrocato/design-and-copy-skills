Slide 4 matters because the buyers are choosing a coating. "C4 works if your deployment doesn't see big temperature swings" is useful to some of them. Failures tell buyers what not to spend money on, so keep them to one line each.

Slide 5 builds trust with a procurement audience and answers their first question in advance. Fill it only from what you actually tested. I haven't assumed any substrates, temperature ranges or depths.

## What stays out

- **Chemistry:** compositions, mechanisms, synthesis and characterisation methods. Buyers can't judge them and will assume you did them properly.
- **Why C1, C3 and C6 failed:** the mechanisms and per-formulation detail. "They failed" is enough.
- **Interim time points and statistics:** show the 18-month result and the trend line, and keep tables off the live slides.
- **Three-year chronology:** don't present it in the order the work happened.
- **Lab, team, funding and acknowledgements slides:** put names on the last slide, or say them aloud.
- **Any slide with a table or more than about 30 words:** it goes to the appendix.

## Backups for the likely hard questions

Park these after the last slide and don't present them:

1. Cost and scale-up: what does it cost per sensor, and can you make it at volume?
2. Availability and IP: who can license it, and on what terms?
3. Beyond 18 months, or in other conditions: do you have longer or colder data?
4. C4 failure detail: how bad is the peeling, and at what temperature swing?
