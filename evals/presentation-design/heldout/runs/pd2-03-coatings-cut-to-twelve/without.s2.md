**Filter:** keep a slide only if a buyer could act on it, such as shortlisting a coating, avoiding one, or getting in touch. Mechanisms and methods are backup material.

At about one minute per slide, aim for 9 to 11 slides. Everything else goes in an appendix, which you can leave in the deck and skip over.

## What goes in

1. **Title and one-line takeaway.** For example: "Two of six marine sensor coatings hold under 5% drift after 18 months in seawater."
2. **Why it matters.** One slide on what drift costs buyers, such as recalibration, sensor replacement and data quality. Use their terms, not ours.
3. **What we tested.** One slide covering six formulations, three years, 18-month seawater exposure, and what "5% drift" means in practice. This is your credibility slide, so keep it to the essentials.
4. **Results overview.** One chart or table showing all six coatings against drift. This is the centerpiece, and the audience should see the whole picture before the detail.
5. **C2.** Drift result, plus anything a buyer would ask about: cost, ease of application, compatibility with sensor types.
6. **C5.** Same structure as C2, so the two can be compared directly.
7. **C4, the conditional option.** Good drift, but it peels under temperature swings. Say who it suits (stable-temperature deployments) and who should avoid it.
8. **C1, C3 and C6 on one slide.** Frame it as "what not to spend money on." Give the headline result and maybe one plain-language reason each. Skip the failure chemistry.
9. **Recommendation.** Which coating for which use case. If C2 and C5 differ in some way that matters to buyers, make that call explicitly.
10. **Next steps and how to engage.** Licensing, pilot partnerships, samples, and who to contact. This is what turns the talk into follow-up.

## What comes out

- Formulation chemistry and synthesis detail
- Characterization data (spectroscopy, microscopy, adhesion mechanics) unless one image makes the C4 peeling obvious
- Per-batch or per-sample data, intermediate time points, and statistical details. Show the 18-month endpoint and perhaps one drift curve.
- History of the project, dead ends, and internal review process slides
- Detailed failure analysis for C1, C3 and C6
- Acknowledgments beyond one closing line

## Things to check

- **Cost, scalability and supply.** Procurement people will ask about these first. If you have numbers, they're worth more than another data slide. If you don't, say so and say what you're doing about it.
- **Sample size and confidence.** If C2 and C5 results come from few samples or a single site, say so in one line. It protects your credibility.
- **Seawater conditions.** Temperature range, depth and biofouling matter to buyers deploying in different environments.
- **Jargon.** Replace terms like "hydrophobic crosslinked matrix" with outcomes. Define drift once.
