Build the talk around what a buyer can do with your result, not around the three years of work. At 12 minutes you have room for about 8 to 10 slides, so roughly 25% of your 41.

## Suggested structure

| # | Slide | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Headline:** C2 and C5 hold under 5% drift after 18 months in seawater. Say it before any background. | 1 min |
| 2 | **Why it matters:** what drift costs sensor makers (recalibration, field failures, warranty). One slide, in their language. | 1 min |
| 3 | **How we tested:** six formulations, 18 months, real seawater, and what "drift" means. Keep it to one simple diagram. | 1 min |
| 4 | **The main result:** one chart with drift over time for all six, with C2 and C5 highlighted and the 5% line marked. This is your most important slide. | 2 min |
| 5 | **C2 and C5 in detail:** what each is good for, plus any differences in cost, application method, or thickness. | 2 min |
| 6 | **C4, the conditional case:** good drift, but it peels under temperature swings. Say which deployments it's fine for (stable-temperature, deep water) and which it isn't. | 1 min |
| 7 | **What didn't work:** C1, C3 and C6 on one slide, with a one-line failure mode each. | 1 min |
| 8 | **What this means for you:** a recommendation by use case, and what's still unknown, such as beyond 18 months or other water conditions. | 1 min |
| 9 | **Next steps and contact:** how to get samples, run a pilot, or license. | 1 min |

Keep two or three backup slides for Q&A. Good candidates are the failure mechanisms, the test protocol details, and the raw data tables.

## What to cut

- **Chemistry and mechanism detail.** Buyers need to know that it works, not why. Put it in backup.
- **The history of the project**, including formulation iterations and dead ends.
- **Per-sample or per-replicate data.** Show one summary chart.
- **Detailed failure analysis** for C1, C3 and C6. One line each is enough.
- **Methodology beyond the basics.** One slide that establishes credibility is enough.
- **Internal-review framing**, such as team structure, timeline and lessons learned.

## Judgment calls

- **Keep the failures, but make them short.** They show the testing was rigorous and save the audience from trying those formulations. They shouldn't take more than a minute.
- **Don't bury C4's limitation.** A buyer who hears about the peeling from someone else later will trust the rest of your results less.
- **Use one chart, not several.** If two slides show the same data, keep the clearer one.
- **Test each slide with one question:** if this slide were gone, would a procurement person make a worse decision? If not, cut it.

## What I need from you

Buyers will ask about these four things, and your answer shapes slides 5, 8 and 9:
1. Do you have cost, scalability, or supply data for C2 and C5?
2. What's the commercial path: licensing, partnership, or open publication?
3. Is there any difference in performance between C2 and C5 that would make a buyer choose one?
4. What is the one thing you want people to do after the talk?
