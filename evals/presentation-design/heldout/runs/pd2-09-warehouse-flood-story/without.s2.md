**During the story, show one full-bleed photo with no text, or a black screen.**

- **Best option:** a real photo from that night. The wet floor, the pallets, or the night crew would all work. A real, imperfect phone photo beats anything polished, and 400 people will believe it more. Skip stock flood imagery.
- **If you have no photo:** go to black (press B or . in PowerPoint or Keynote). For two minutes, you are the only thing on stage to look at, and that suits a story.
- **Optional:** bring in "3,000 orders" as large type at the moment you say the number. Don't add a timeline, a map of the two sites, or bullets. If people are reading, they aren't listening.

Put the photo up as you start the story, not before. Leave it up through the last line. Make sure the photo looks fine from the back of a big room.

**Right after: one slide with the thesis sentence, in large type.**

> **The plans that work are the ones the front line can change without asking.**

Finish the story, pause a beat, then click. The audience has just felt the point, and the slide gives them words for it.

If you want a stronger bridge, add one slide before the thesis. It reads **"Who approved this?"** Say the answer out loud: "Nobody. They didn't wait to be told." Then click to the thesis. This works if the crew really did act without sign-off, and it sets up your argument well. If a manager did approve it, skip this slide and go straight to the thesis.

Keep the thesis slide up for a few seconds of silence before you move on to the next section. Minute 12 of 30 is where the talk turns from setup to argument, so let that slide carry the transition.
