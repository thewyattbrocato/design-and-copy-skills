# Running order (20 minutes)

Tom and Jess's objection is the real agenda item, so don't save it for the end. Name it in the first minute and show you have an answer. Leave at least 6 minutes for discussion, because that's where the decision gets made.

| Time | Segment | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-2:00 | **Open: the ask, the reason, the handover headline** | Maria knows what she's deciding. Tom and Jess hear their concern first. |
| 2:00-5:00 | **Why change** | 2-3 numbers on the problem you're solving (turnover, absence, fatigue, cost, whatever is true for you). |
| 5:00-9:00 | **What changes and how handovers work** | Show the new schedule and a concrete handover design: who overlaps with whom, for how long, and what gets written down. |
| 9:00-11:00 | **Legal and rest periods** | Ana confirms rather than discovers: overtime treatment, rest between shifts, max hours. |
| 11:00-17:00 | **Discussion** | Ask Tom and Jess directly: "What would have to be true for handovers to work?" Turn their answers into conditions of the pilot. |
| 17:00-20:00 | **Decision** | Restate the ask as a time-boxed pilot with success measures and a review date, then ask Maria for a yes. |

## What to do before Tuesday (this matters more than the slides)

- **Talk to Tom and Jess separately, 15 minutes each.** Ask for their specific handover failure scenarios. If those scenarios are in your deck with answers, you're no longer ambushing them. If you can't answer one, you'll know before the meeting.
- **Run the legal question past Ana in advance.** The key one is whether any daily overtime threshold applies, because 10-hour shifts would trigger it in some jurisdictions. If it does, that changes the cost case, and you don't want to find out in the room.
- **Ask for a pilot, not a permanent change.** For example, 8-12 weeks, one site or one crew, with a review date. A yes is much easier to give when it can be reversed, and it gives Tom and Jess a way to agree without conceding.

So I've built the proposal around it. [One-line headline: e.g. 'a 30-minute structured overlap, a written handover log, and Tom and Jess each signing off on the handover format before we start.']
>
> Ana is here to confirm the overtime and rest-period side, and I'll leave time for her.
>
> Over the next 18 minutes I'll cover why now, how the handovers would work, and the pilot terms. Then I'd like to hear where Tom and Jess think it breaks. If we can't make it work on paper, we shouldn't try it on the floor."

This opening does three things:
- It puts the decision first, so Maria isn't guessing what you want.
- It treats the handover objection as legitimate and shows you have a plan, so the managers don't feel they have to defend their position.
- It tells the room that the discussion will shape the pilot.
