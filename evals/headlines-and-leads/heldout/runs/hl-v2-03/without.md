**Recommended title:** #1. It matches how people search and says what the reader will get.

**Opening**

> You step off a four-hour flight or climb out of the car after a long drive, and your lower back feels tight, achy, and a lot worse than when you left. It's not your imagination, and it's not a sign that something is wrong with your spine. Hours in one position load the same tissues without a break, and a few simple habits can make a big difference.

A few notes:

- I kept the claim modest ("not a sign that something is wrong") so it's accurate for most readers without sounding like a diagnosis. You may want to add a line later telling readers when to get checked, such as numbness, leg weakness, or pain that doesn't ease.
- If you'd like a more personal tone, you could open with a patient scenario instead, such as a sales rep stepping off a cross-country flight and heading straight to a client dinner.
