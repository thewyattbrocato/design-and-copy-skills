Here are a few directions. I'd lead with the first.

**1. Straightforward (my pick)**
- **Headline:** Three glazes. Fired and photographed at cone 6.
- **Subhead:** Every month, Kilnbox mails you three test tiles and a card showing how each glaze actually fires, so you know what you're buying before you commit a whole bucket.

**2. Problem-first**
- **Headline:** Stop guessing how a glaze will fire.
- **Subhead:** Each month we send three cone 6 test tiles and a card showing the real result. You can try new glazes without buying a full jar of each.

**3. Playful**
- **Headline:** Little tiles, big glaze decisions.
- **Subhead:** Three cone 6 test tiles and a firing card land in your mailbox every month. Brought to you by two sisters and one very busy kiln.

**4. Short and punchy**
- **Headline:** Test the glaze before you buy the bucket.
- **Subhead:** A monthly box of three cone 6 test tiles, with a card showing how each one fires.

Some notes:
- Option 1 works best because it says what's in the box in the first line. Potters scan for specifics like "cone 6" and "test tiles."
- Option 3 uses the sister detail. A two-person team is a selling point for a hobbyist audience, so it could also go in the "about" section if you'd rather keep the top of the page product-focused. If the kiln is really yours, keep that line. If you fire elsewhere, cut it.
- I left out claims like "trusted by potters" or "loved by hundreds," since you've only shipped to friends so far. Once you have a few real quotes, a line like "Join the first 50 potters" or a testimonial under the subhead would add credibility.
