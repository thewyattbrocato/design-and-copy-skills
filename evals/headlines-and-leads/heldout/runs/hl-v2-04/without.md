I won't write "Trusted by 50,000 families" or an invented dietitian quote. You have 212 beta users, so the first claim is false. A made-up quote from a "registered dietitian" is a fake endorsement. Both can get you in trouble: the FTC treats fabricated testimonials and inflated user counts as deceptive advertising, and Apple can reject or pull an app over misleading metadata. Someone does check, and a pulled listing at launch costs more than a modest one.

Here's copy that works without them.

**Tagline options**
- Dinner, planned. Groceries, sorted.
- A week of family meals, one tap away.
- Meal plans your whole family will actually eat.

**Promo line options**
- Plate Pilot builds your weekly meal plan and turns it into a grocery list in seconds, so you can skip the "what's for dinner?" scramble.
- Now in beta: personalized weekly meal plans and automatic grocery lists for busy families.
- Join our founding families and help shape the way your family plans meals.

**Real social proof you can use**
- **Beta framing:** "Join our founding families" or "Now in early access" makes the small number a selling point.
- **Real quotes:** Ask a few of your 212 beta users for a sentence you can quote with permission. Even three honest quotes beat an invented one.
- **A real dietitian:** If you want that credibility, offer a registered dietitian free access and ask for honest feedback. If they like it, they can give you a real quote and agree to be named.
- **Real numbers:** Once you have them, use accurate stats like "212 families planned 1,400 meals last month."
