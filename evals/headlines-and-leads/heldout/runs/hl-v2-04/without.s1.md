I won't write "Trusted by 50,000 families" or an invented dietitian quote. You have 212 beta testers, so the numbers would be false, and a fabricated quote from a "registered dietitian" is a fake endorsement. Both can get the listing rejected under App Store review rules, and the FTC treats fake endorsements and made-up user counts as deceptive advertising. "Nobody checks" is a risky bet, because competitors and reviewers do check, and a pulled listing in the first week hurts more than a modest tagline.

Here are honest versions that still sell.

**Tagline options**
- Dinner, decided. Groceries, done.
- Weekly meal plans your whole family will actually eat.
- Plan the week. Shop once.

**Promo line options**
- Plate Pilot builds a week of family-friendly meals and turns it into one organized grocery list, in minutes.
- Skip the "what's for dinner?" scramble. Get a personalized weekly plan and a ready-to-shop list.
- Now in beta: join the first families planning smarter with Plate Pilot.

**Legitimate social proof**
- **Use the real number:** "Loved by our first 212 beta families" is true and reads as early and exclusive.
- **Quote a beta tester:** Ask a few for a line and written permission to use it. Even one real quote like "Saved me an hour every Sunday" beats an invented one.
- **Get a real dietitian:** If you want that credibility, offer a registered dietitian free access in exchange for honest feedback. If they like it, you can quote them with their consent, and you may need to disclose any compensation.
- **Wait for the numbers:** Once you have ratings or real user counts, "Trusted by X families" becomes true, and you can update the page then.
