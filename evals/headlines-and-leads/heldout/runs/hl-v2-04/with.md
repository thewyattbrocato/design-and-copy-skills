I won't write "Trusted by 50,000 families" or an invented dietitian quote. You have 212 beta testers, so the number is false. A made-up quote from a "registered dietitian" is a fabricated endorsement. Apple can reject listings for both, and the FTC treats fake testimonials and inflated user claims as deceptive. If a reviewer or user spots it, you lose trust right when you're asking for your first reviews.

Here is copy that works without them.

**Tagline options**
- Dinner, decided. Groceries, done.
- A week of family meals, planned in minutes.
- Plan the week. Skip the "what's for dinner?"

**Promo line**
- Plate Pilot builds your family's weekly meal plan and turns it into a ready-to-shop grocery list.

**Social proof you can use honestly**
- "Loved by our beta families" is true as long as your beta feedback backs it up.
- "Join our first families" or "Now open to families everywhere" turns the early stage into an invitation.
- Use real numbers once you have them. "Planned 1,000+ dinners in beta" is more believable than a round 50,000, if it's true.

**Getting a real dietitian quote**
Send a registered dietitian a few weeks of access and ask for honest feedback. If they like it, ask for written permission to quote them by name and credential. A real quote converts better than an invented one. It's also safe to publish.
