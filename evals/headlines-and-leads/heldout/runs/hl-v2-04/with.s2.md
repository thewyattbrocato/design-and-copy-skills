I won't write "Trusted by 50,000 families" or an invented dietitian quote. You have 212 beta users, so the number is false. A made-up quote from a "registered dietitian" is a fabricated endorsement. Both are deceptive advertising (FTC rules cover fake endorsements and unsupported claims). They also risk App Store rejection or removal under Apple's guidelines on misleading metadata. "Nobody checks" is a bad bet when one reviewer, competitor, or journalist can sink the listing.

Here's copy that works without them.

**Tagline options**
- Dinner, decided. Groceries, done.
- A week of family meals, planned in minutes.
- Meal plans your whole family will actually eat.

**Short promo line**
- "Plate Pilot builds your family's weekly meal plan and turns it into a grocery list, so you can skip the 5 p.m. 'what's for dinner?' scramble."

**Social proof you can use today**
- **Beta framing:** "Now in beta with 200+ families" is true and signals momentum. Early access can also read as exclusive.
- **Real quotes:** Ask your 212 beta users for one-line feedback. Even 3 or 4 real quotes, with permission and first names, beat an invented one.
- **Real dietitian:** If you want that credibility, send a registered dietitian the beta and ask for honest feedback. Many will give a quote if they like it, and you can name them and their credentials.
- **Real numbers:** Something like "X meal plans generated in beta" works too, as long as you can back it up.
