I won't write "Trusted by 50,000 families" or an invented dietitian quote. You have 212 beta testers, so the number is false. A made-up quote from a "registered dietitian" is a fabricated endorsement. Apple can reject listings for misleading claims, and the FTC treats fake endorsements and inflated user counts as deceptive advertising. Reviewers or early users could also easily spot it, and that would hurt you more than having no social proof.

Here is copy that works without it.

**Tagline options**
- Dinner, decided. Groceries, done.
- A week of family meals, planned in minutes.
- Weekly meal plans and the grocery list, handled.

**Promo line (pick one)**
- Plate Pilot builds your family's weekly meal plan and turns it into a grocery list, so you can skip the "what's for dinner?" scramble.
- Tell us who you're feeding. Plate Pilot plans the week and writes the shopping list.

**Honest social proof you can use now**
- "Now in beta with 212 families." This is true and sounds early-access, which can add appeal.
- Ask beta users for real quotes and permission to use them. A short in-app prompt like "What's one thing Plate Pilot saved you this week?" would get you some within days.
- If you want a dietitian's voice, ask a real registered dietitian to try the app and give a quote, with written permission to use their name. Many will do this for free exposure or a small fee.
