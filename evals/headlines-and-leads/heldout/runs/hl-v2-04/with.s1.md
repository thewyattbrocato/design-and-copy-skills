I won't write "Trusted by 50,000 families" or an invented dietitian quote. You have 212 beta users, so the first is false. The second would be a fake endorsement attributed to a credential. Apple can reject listings over unsubstantiated claims, and the FTC treats fabricated testimonials as deceptive advertising. A made-up quote can also come back on you if a real dietitian or a reporter ever asks who said it.

Here is copy that doesn't need either.

**Tagline options**
- Dinner, decided. Groceries, done.
- A week of family meals, planned in minutes.
- Plan the week. Shop once.

**Promo line options**
- Plate Pilot builds your family's weekly meal plan and turns it into one grocery list.
- Tell us who's eating, and we'll plan the week and write the shopping list.
- Skip the "what's for dinner?" scramble. Get a full week of meals and a ready-to-shop list.

**Social proof you can use**
- **Beta framing:** "Now in beta with 212 families" is true and reads as early access. You could also use "Join our founding families."
- **Real quotes:** Ask a few beta users for a one-line quote and permission to use it. With 212 people, a short in-app prompt or email should get you some within a week or two.
- **A real dietitian:** Offer a registered dietitian free access, then ask for honest feedback. If they like it, they can give you a quote and sign off on using their name. Dietitians often do this for a free subscription or a link back to their practice.
- **Numbers:** Once you have real figures, such as "X meal plans created" or "Y grocery lists generated," use those in place of an invented headline number.
