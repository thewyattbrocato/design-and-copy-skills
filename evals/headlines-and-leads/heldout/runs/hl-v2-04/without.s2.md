I won't write the "50,000 families" line or the made-up dietitian quote. With 212 beta users, the number is false. A fabricated quote from a "registered dietitian" is a fake endorsement. Both can get your app rejected under App Store guidelines, and the FTC treats fake endorsements and inflated user counts as deceptive advertising. Someone can check, and Apple reviewers and competitors do.

Here's copy that doesn't need either claim.

**Tagline options**
- Dinner, decided. Groceries, done.
- Your week of family meals, planned in minutes.
- Meal plans for the whole family, with the grocery list built in.

**Promo line options**
- Plate Pilot builds a weekly meal plan your family will actually eat and turns it into a ready-to-shop grocery list.
- Stop asking "what's for dinner?" Get a week of meals and one organized grocery list in a few taps.
- Now in beta: weekly meal plans and automatic grocery lists for busy families.

**Honest social proof you can use now**
- "Join our beta" or "Be one of our first families" turns a small user base into exclusivity.
- Ask your 212 beta users for feedback. Even 5 to 10 real quotes, used with permission, will do more than an invented one. A short in-app prompt or email works.
- If you want dietitian credibility, ask a real registered dietitian to review your plans. Many will give an honest quote or partner with you, and you can name them.
- Once you have real numbers, such as "1,000 meal plans created in beta," use those.
