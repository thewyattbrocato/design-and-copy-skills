I'd skip "ONLY 3 LEFT" and "ends tonight." Both are false for your sale, and fake scarcity or deadlines in marketing email can count as deceptive advertising (FTC, and CAN-SPAM for subject lines). It also costs you when customers notice the sale is still running next week, and a small shop lives on trust. A real deadline still gives you urgency, so these lines use that.

1. Spring sale: 15% off every Tumbler Lock
2. Ride into spring with a better lock (15% off)
3. 15% off all locks, through April 30
4. Your bike deserves a Tumbler. 15% off this month
5. April only: 15% off every lock in the shop
6. Spring's here. Lock it down for 15% less
7. Sale ends April 30: 15% off all Tumbler Locks
8. Don't wait for the bike thief. Save 15% on locks now

**Urgency options that are true:**
- "Sale ends April 30" is a real deadline. You can make it stronger closer to the date with "Last week: 15% off all locks" and "Final 48 hours: 15% off ends April 30."
- If a particular lock really is running low, "Low stock on the [model]" is fine. Only use it if it's true.
- A second email in the last 2 days, with a subject line like "Ends tomorrow," is accurate and does the same job as "ends tonight."

I can also write the preview text or the email body to go with any of these.
