I'd skip "ONLY 3 LEFT" and "ends tonight". Both are false for your sale, and fake scarcity or deadlines in marketing can run afoul of FTC rules and consumer-protection law. It also costs you with repeat customers, who notice when "ends tonight" shows up again next week. Your real offer has a real deadline, so here are 8 that use it:

1. Spring sale: 15% off every Tumbler Lock
2. 15% off all locks through April 30
3. Your bike deserves a better lock (now 15% off)
4. Spring riding season is here. Lock it down for 15% less.
5. April only: 15% off all Tumbler Locks
6. Don't wait until your bike's gone missing: 15% off locks
7. The spring sale is on, and it ends April 30
8. Ride more this spring, worry less (15% off locks)

**For urgency that's true:**
- Send a reminder in the last week of April with a subject like "Last week: 15% off locks ends April 30" or "Final days of our spring sale." Real deadlines work well, and the reminder is probably where most of your sale-driven orders will come from.
- If a specific lock model is actually low on stock, "Low stock on the [model name]" is honest and works the same way.

I'd A/B test #2 against #5 first, since they're the clearest and most direct.
