## The A/B test

Test **headline #1 against headline #3**, and use opening B in both versions.

- **Version A:** "We Stopped Guessing Which Projector Belongs to Which Room"
- **Version B:** "41 Classrooms, 5 People, 2 Weeks: How One District Labeled It All"

These two differ in one way. A leads with a human problem and B leads with scale and proof. If you change only the headline, any difference in results comes from the headline. If you also change the opening, you won't know which one caused it.

**Setup**
1. **Write your hypothesis first.** For example: "Readers will click more on the problem-led headline than on the numbers-led one." Then you learn something whichever version wins.
2. **Split randomly.** Have your email tool divide the list 50/50. Don't split by customer type or signup date.
3. **Keep everything else identical.** That means send time, sender name, preview text, images, and body.
4. **Pick one success metric before sending.** If the headline doubles as the subject line, use open rate. If it only appears inside the newsletter, use clicks on the story or time spent reading it. Opens and clicks answer different questions, so choose one.
5. **Set a decision rule.** Decide in advance how big a gap counts, such as "at least 3 percentage points", and how long you'll wait, such as 5 days.
6. **Be honest about sample size.** If your list is small, a modest gap could be chance. Treat it as a lean, not proof, and re-run the same contrast next quarter with a different story.

**Next issue:** If you want to test the openings too, keep the winning headline and compare opening B with opening C. Testing one thing at a time takes more issues, but each result is easier to interpret.
