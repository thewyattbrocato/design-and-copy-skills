## Openers

**A. Quote-led:** "We stopped guessing which projector belongs to which room." That's how Dana R., facilities lead at a mid-sized Midwest public school district, describes what changed last June.

**B. Problem-led:** Before last June, keeping track of a school district's classroom equipment meant a spreadsheet and a roll of masking tape. Then the facilities team picked up a Tagwren.

**C. Numbers-led:** Five people. 41 classrooms. About two weeks. That's what it took one Midwest school district's facilities team to label classroom equipment ahead of the summer inventory.

**D. Question-led:** How do you know which projector belongs to which classroom? For one district's facilities team, the honest answer used to be "we guess."

**E. Contrast-led:** Handwritten masking-tape labels and a spreadsheet got one district's facilities team through years of summer inventories. Last June, they tried something different.

## My pick

I'd use **headline 1** with **opener C**. The headline is specific, it's in the customer's own words, and it names the problem your readers will recognize. Opener C then adds the scale (five people, 41 rooms, two weeks) without repeating the quote. Don't pair headline 1 with opener A, because the quote would appear twice.

## The A/B test

Test one variable only, so you can tell what caused any difference.

- **Version 1:** headline 1, the quote ("We stopped guessing…").
- **Version 2:** headline 3, the numbers ("How a Five-Person Facilities Team Labeled 41 Classrooms in Two Weeks").
- **Keep identical:** opener (use B, which suits both headlines), body, images, send time, and subject line.

The question is whether your readers respond more to a customer's voice or to concrete scale. Either answer helps with future issues.

**Setup**
1. Split the list randomly, not by customer type, region, or signup date.
2. Write down your success metric before sending. Use clicks on the story, or read-through if you can measure it. Opens only tell you something if the headline appears in the subject line or preview text.
3. Send both versions at the same time and let them run the same length of time.
4. Decide in advance how large a gap counts. With a small list, a few clicks of difference is noise. Treat a small gap as "no clear winner" and don't act on it.

**Afterward**
- Record the result along with the hypothesis, whichever way it goes.
- If there's no clear winner, run the same contrast on next quarter's story. Pairing it with a different story helps show whether the pattern holds.

Two cautions on wording. The notes don't say how long the old process took, so avoid "faster" or "saved X hours" claims. And name Dana R. as "facilities lead" exactly as permitted.
