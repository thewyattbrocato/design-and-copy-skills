Here are subject line options, grouped by tone.

**Clear and direct**
- New pickup hours at Greenfold Market, starting Oct 6
- Greenfold pickup changes: new weekday hours, no more Saturdays
- Weekday pickup is now 3-7 pm (and Saturday pickup is ending)

**Lead with the benefit**
- More time to pick up: weekday pickup now 3-7 pm
- A wider pickup window at Greenfold, plus one change to Saturdays

**Heads-up / reminder**
- Heads up: Greenfold pickup times are changing
- Your Greenfold pickup schedule is changing Oct 6
- Reminder: last Saturday pickup is Oct 4

**Reassuring**
- Pickup changes at Greenfold (prices and ordering stay the same)
- New pickup hours, same easy ordering

My pick is **"Greenfold pickup changes: new weekday hours, no more Saturdays"**. It's specific, it names both changes so nobody is surprised by the Saturday news, and it doesn't bury the bad news under the good. If you want a softer tone, use "More time to pick up: weekday pickup now 3-7 pm", but make sure the Saturday change is near the top of the email body.

Today's date is Oct 7, which is after both the Oct 4 and Oct 6 dates. If the email hasn't gone out yet, the "starting Oct 6" and "last Saturday pickup is Oct 4" lines will read as already past. In that case, use present-tense wording like "Weekday pickup is now 3-7 pm." If the dates are meant to be in the future, ignore this.
