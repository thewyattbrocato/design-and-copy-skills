Hi everyone,

I want to talk about something that's been on my mind for a long time: the packaging your Fernwood boxes arrive in. Since we started shipping in 2021, our boxes have been lined with foam coolers. I told myself that was just the cost of getting fresh food to your door safely, but it never sat right with me, and I suspect it never sat right with many of you either.

So in January, I sat down with Priya, who runs our packing floor, and we set a goal: replace the foam with something you can put in your curbside recycling or compost, by the end of summer.

It wasn't easy. The first material we tried, a molded plant-fibre liner, looked great but kept food cold for only about 18 hours. Our far-suburb routes can take up to 30 hours in hot weather, so we had to drop that supplier. It cost us about six weeks and a fair amount of money in samples.

The second try, a recycled-paper liner made of layered corrugated paper around a thin paper-based barrier, worked far better. In our trials it kept food at a safe temperature for up to 36 hours, which covers even our longest routes. It also arrives flat, so it takes up less warehouse space, and it goes straight into your curbside paper recycling. We started shipping with it on March 4th, and today every Fernwood box that leaves our warehouse uses it.

I also want to be upfront about what hasn't gone perfectly. First, the new liner costs us about 40 cents more per box. We're absorbing that, and we don't plan to raise prices because of it this year. Second, and I'm a little embarrassed about this one, in the first two weeks a number of boxes arrived with soggy bottoms. Some of you wrote to tell us, and you were right to. The ice packs were sweating more than they had inside the foam, and the paper soaked it up. We've switched to an ice pack with a sealed outer film, and the soggy-box emails have stopped. I'm sorry for the mess it made of some of your dinners.

One piece of the puzzle is still unsolved: the ice packs themselves. They're plastic film filled with a non-toxic gel, and they can't go in curbside recycling. Many of you keep them for lunch bags and picnics, which is great, but that's not a real solution for everyone. We're working on a returnable program, where you leave the packs in the empty box on your porch and our driver collects them on the next delivery. We'll trial it with about 200 households this fall, and if it works well, we'll roll it out to everyone. I'll share an update in the September newsletter.

Thank you to Priya and the whole packing team, who were endlessly patient while we changed how every single box is assembled. And thank you to all of you for putting up with a few bumps along the way. Your feedback makes Fernwood better every week.

If you'd like to know more, just reply to this email. It comes straight to me, and I read every one.

Thank you, as always, for choosing Fernwood and letting us feed you and your families.

Warmly,
Dana
Founder, Fernwood Meal Kits
