Hi everyone,

I want to talk about the packaging your Fernwood boxes arrive in. Many of you have noticed, and some of you have told me directly, that our boxes have been lined with foam coolers since 2021. For a long time I told myself that was the cost of getting fresh food to your door safely. But it never sat right with me, and I suspect it didn't with many of you either.

So back in January, I sat down with Priya, who runs our packing floor, and we listed everything that goes into each box and what we could change without compromising the food. Our goal was to replace the foam with something you can put in your curbside recycling or compost by the end of summer.

It was not easy. The first material we tried was a molded plant-fibre liner from a supplier in the next province. It looked great, and in our first test shipments it kept food cold for about 18 hours. The problem is that our far-suburb routes can take up to 30 hours in hot weather. By the end of that window the food was no longer cold enough to be safe. We dropped that supplier, which cost us about six weeks and a fair amount of money in samples.

The second material was a recycled-paper liner made of layered corrugated paper around a thin paper-based barrier. It performed much better. In our trials it kept food at a safe temperature for up to 36 hours, which covers even our longest routes with room to spare. It also arrives flat, so it takes up less warehouse space, and it goes in your curbside paper recycling with no special steps. We started shipping with this liner on March 4th, and as of today every Fernwood box leaving our warehouse uses it.

Two things have not gone perfectly.

First, the new liner costs us about 40 cents more per box than the foam did. We are absorbing that for now, and we do not plan to raise prices because of it this year.

Second, and this one embarrasses me a little, in the first two weeks some boxes arrived with soggy bottoms. A handful of you wrote to tell us, and you were right to. The ice packs we were using sweated more than they had inside the foam, and the paper soaked up the moisture. We have switched to an ice pack with a sealed outer film, and the soggy-box emails have stopped. But I want you to know we heard you, and I'm sorry for the mess it made of some of your dinners.

One piece is still unsolved: the ice packs themselves. They're plastic film filled with non-toxic gel, so they can't go in curbside recycling. Many of you keep them for lunch bags and picnics, which is great, but that isn't a real solution for everyone. We are working on a returnable program. You would leave the packs in the empty box on your porch, and our driver would collect them on the next delivery. We plan to trial it with about 200 households this fall, and if it works well, we'll roll it out to everyone.

Thank you to Priya and the whole packing team, who have been patient while we changed how every box is assembled. And thank you to all of you for putting up with a few bumps. Your feedback is what makes Fernwood better every week.

If you'd like to know more, reply to this email. It comes straight to me, and I read every one. I'll share an update on the ice pack trial in our September newsletter, so keep an eye out for it.

Thank you, as always, for choosing Fernwood and for letting us feed you and your families.

Warmly,
Dana
Founder, Fernwood Meal Kits
