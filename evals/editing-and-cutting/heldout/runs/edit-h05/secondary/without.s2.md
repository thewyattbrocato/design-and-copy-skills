Hi everyone,

I want to talk about the packaging your Fernwood boxes arrive in. Many of you have noticed, and some of you have told me directly, that our boxes have been lined with foam coolers since we started shipping in 2021. For a long time I told myself that was just the cost of getting fresh food to your door safely. But it never sat right with me, and I suspect it didn't sit right with many of you either.

So back in January, I sat down with Priya, who runs our packing floor, and we listed everything that goes into each box and what we could change without compromising food safety. Our goal was to replace the foam with something you can put in your curbside recycling or compost, by the end of summer.

It wasn't easy. The first material we tried was a molded plant-fibre liner from a supplier in the next province. It looked and felt great, and in early tests it kept food cold for about 18 hours. Our delivery routes in the far suburbs can take up to 30 hours in hot weather, though, and by the end of that window the food wasn't cold enough to be safe. We abandoned that supplier, which cost us about six weeks and a fair amount of money in samples.

The second material was a recycled-paper liner: layered corrugated paper wrapped around a thin paper-based barrier. It performed much better. It kept food at a safe temperature for up to 36 hours, which covers even our longest routes. It also arrives flat, so it takes up less warehouse space, and you can break it down and put it in your curbside paper recycling. We started shipping boxes with it on March 4th, and as of today every Fernwood box uses it.

I want to be open about two things that haven't gone perfectly. First, the new liner costs us about 40 cents more per box than the foam did. We've decided to absorb that for now, and we don't plan to raise prices because of it this year.

Second, and this is the one I'm a little embarrassed about, during the first two weeks of the new liners a number of boxes arrived with soggy bottoms. A handful of you wrote to tell us, and you were right to. The ice packs we were using sweated more than the ones in the foam coolers, and the paper absorbed the moisture. We've switched to an ice pack with a sealed outer film, and the soggy-box emails have stopped. I'm sorry for the mess it made of some of your dinners.

One piece we haven't solved yet is the ice packs themselves. They're plastic film filled with a non-toxic gel, so they can't go in your curbside recycling. Many of you keep them for lunch bags and picnics, which is great, but I don't think that's a real solution for everyone. We're working on a returnable ice pack program: you leave the packs in the empty box on your porch, and our driver collects them on the next delivery. We plan to trial it with about 200 households this fall, and if it works well, we'll roll it out to everyone.

Thank you to Priya and the whole packing team, who have been incredibly patient while we changed how every box is assembled. And thank you to all of you for putting up with a few bumps along the way. Your feedback is what makes Fernwood better every week.

If you have questions, reply to this email and it comes straight to me. I read every one. I'll share an update on the ice pack trial in our September newsletter, so keep an eye out.

Thank you, as always, for choosing Fernwood and for letting us feed you and your families.

Warmly,
Dana
Founder, Fernwood Meal Kits
