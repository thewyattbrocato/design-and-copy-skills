Hi everyone,

I want to talk about the boxes your Fernwood meals arrive in. Many of you have noticed, and several of you have told me directly, that we've been lining them with foam coolers since 2021. For a long time I told myself that was the cost of getting fresh food to your door safely. But it never sat right with me, and I suspect it never sat right with many of you either.

So in January, Priya, who runs our packing floor, and I sat down and went through everything that goes into each box. We set a goal: replace the foam with something you can put in your curbside recycling or compost, and do it by the end of summer.

I would love to say it was easy, but it wasn't. The first material we tried was a molded plant-fibre liner. It looked great and felt great, and it kept food cold for about 18 hours in our tests. But our far-suburb routes can take up to 30 hours in hot weather, so we had to drop that supplier. It cost us about six weeks.

The second material was a recycled-paper liner made of layered corrugated paper around a thin paper barrier. In our trials it kept food at a safe temperature for up to 36 hours, which covers even our longest routes. It arrives flat, so it takes less warehouse space, and you can break it down and put it in your paper recycling. We started shipping with it on March 4th, and as of today every Fernwood box uses it.

Two things haven't gone perfectly, and I want to be upfront about both.

First, the new liner costs us about 40 cents more per box than the foam did. We're absorbing that for now, and we don't plan to raise prices because of it this year.

Second, I'm a little embarrassed about this one. In the first two weeks, a number of boxes arrived with soggy bottoms. Some of you wrote to tell us, and you were right to. The ice packs were sweating more than they had inside the foam coolers, and the paper was soaking up the moisture. We've since switched to an ice pack with a sealed outer film, and the soggy-box emails have stopped. I want you to know we heard you, and I'm sorry for the mess it made of some of your dinners.

One piece is still unsolved: the ice packs themselves. They're plastic film filled with non-toxic gel, so they can't go in curbside recycling. Many of you keep them for lunches and picnics, which is great, but I don't think that's a real answer for everyone. So we're working on a returnable ice pack program. You'd leave the packs in the empty box on your porch, and our driver would collect them on the next delivery. We'll trial it with about 200 households this fall, and if it works, we'll roll it out to everyone.

Thank you to Priya and the whole packing team, who stayed patient while we changed how every box gets assembled. And thank you to all of you for putting up with a few bumps along the way. Your feedback is what makes Fernwood better every week.

If you have questions, reply to this email. It comes straight to me, and I read every one. I'll share how the ice pack trial is going in our September newsletter.

Thank you, as always, for choosing Fernwood and for letting us feed you and your families.

Warmly,
Dana
Founder, Fernwood Meal Kits
