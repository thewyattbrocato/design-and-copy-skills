Hi everyone,

I want to talk about the packaging your Fernwood boxes arrive in. Since we started shipping in 2021, every box has been lined with a foam cooler. Many of you have told me directly that you don't love it, and for a long time I told myself it was just the cost of getting fresh food to your door safely. It never sat right with me, and I suspect it never sat right with many of you either.

So back in January, I sat down with Priya, who runs our packing floor, and we went through everything that goes into each box. We set a goal: replace the foam with something you can put in your curbside recycling or compost, and do it by the end of summer.

It was not easy. The first material we tried was a molded plant-fibre liner from a supplier in the next province. It looked and felt great, and in our first test shipments it kept food cold for about 18 hours. But our routes to the far suburbs can take up to 30 hours in hot weather, and by the end of that window the food wasn't cold enough to be safe. We had to drop that supplier, which cost us about six weeks and a fair amount of money in samples.

The second material was a recycled-paper liner: layers of corrugated paper wrapped around a thin paper-based barrier. In our trials it kept food at a safe temperature for up to 36 hours, which covers even our longest routes. It also arrives flat, so it takes up less warehouse space, and you can break it down and put it in your curbside paper recycling with no special steps. We started shipping boxes with it on March 4th, and as of today every Fernwood box that leaves our warehouse uses it.

Two things have not gone perfectly, and I want to be upfront about both. First, the new liner costs us about 40 cents more per box than the foam did. We're absorbing that for now, and we don't plan to raise prices because of it this year.

Second, and this is the one I'm a little embarrassed about, in the first two weeks we had a number of boxes arrive with soggy bottoms. A handful of you wrote to tell us, and you were right to. The ice packs we were using sweated more than the ones in the foam coolers, and the paper absorbed the moisture. We've switched to an ice pack with a sealed outer film, and the soggy-box emails have stopped. I'm sorry for the mess it made of some of your dinners.

One piece is still unsolved: the ice packs themselves. They're plastic film filled with a non-toxic gel, and they can't go in curbside recycling. Many of you keep them for lunch bags and picnics, which is great, but I don't think that's a real solution for everyone. So we're working on a returnable ice pack program. You'd leave the packs in the empty box on your porch, and our driver would collect them on the next delivery. We plan to trial it with about 200 households this fall, and if it works, we'll roll it out to everyone.

Thank you to Priya and the whole packing team, who have been patient while we changed how every box is assembled. And thank you to all of you for putting up with a few bumps along the way. Your feedback is what makes Fernwood better every week.

If you want to know more, reply to this email and it comes straight to me. I read every one.

Thank you, as always, for choosing Fernwood and for letting us feed you and your families.

Warmly,
Dana
Founder, Fernwood Meal Kits
