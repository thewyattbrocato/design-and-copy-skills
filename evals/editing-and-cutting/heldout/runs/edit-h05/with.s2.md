Hi everyone,

I want to talk about the packaging your Fernwood boxes arrive in. We've lined our boxes with foam coolers since we started shipping in 2021. I told myself that was just the cost of getting fresh food to your door safely. It never sat right with me, and I suspect it didn't with many of you either.

In January, I sat down with Priya, who runs our packing floor, and we set a goal: replace the foam with something you can put in your curbside recycling or compost, by the end of summer.

It wasn't easy. The first thing we tried was a molded plant-fibre liner from a supplier in the next province. It looked great, but it kept food cold for only about 18 hours, and our far-suburb routes can take up to 30 hours in hot weather. We had to drop that supplier, which cost us about six weeks and a fair amount of money in samples.

The second try was a recycled-paper liner: layered corrugated paper wrapped around a thin paper-based barrier. In our trials it kept food at a safe temperature for up to 36 hours, which covers even our longest routes. It arrives flat, so it takes less warehouse space, and it goes in your curbside paper recycling with no special steps. We started shipping with it on March 4th, and today every Fernwood box uses it.

A couple of things haven't gone perfectly. The new liner costs about 40 cents more per box than foam. We're absorbing that, and we don't plan to raise prices because of it this year.

The other one I'm a little embarrassed about. In the first two weeks, a number of boxes arrived with soggy bottoms. The ice packs were sweating more than they had inside the foam, and the paper soaked it up. A handful of you wrote in, and you were right to. We've since switched to an ice pack with a sealed outer film, and the soggy-box emails have stopped. I'm sorry for the mess it made of some of your dinners.

One piece is still unsolved: the ice packs themselves. They're plastic film filled with non-toxic gel, and they can't go in curbside recycling. Many of you keep them for lunch bags and picnics, which is great, but that isn't a real answer for everyone. So we're working on a returnable program. You leave the packs in the empty box on your porch, and our driver collects them on the next delivery. We'll trial it with about 200 households this fall, and roll it out to everyone if it works.

Thank you to Priya and the packing team, who were patient while we changed how every box is assembled, and to all of you for putting up with a few bumps.

Reply to this email anytime. It comes straight to me, and I read every one. I'll share how the ice pack trial is going in our September newsletter.

Thank you, as always, for choosing Fernwood.

Warmly,
Dana
Founder, Fernwood Meal Kits
