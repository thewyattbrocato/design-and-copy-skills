Hi everyone,

I want to talk about something that's been on my mind for a long time: the packaging your Fernwood boxes arrive in. Since we started shipping in 2021, our boxes have been lined with foam coolers. For a long time I told myself that was just the cost of getting fresh food to your door safely. But it never sat right with me, and I suspect it never sat right with many of you either.

So in January, I sat down with Priya, who runs our packing floor, and we set a goal: replace the foam with something you can put in your curbside recycling or compost, and do it by the end of summer.

It was not easy. The first material we tried was a molded plant-fibre liner from a supplier in the next province. It looked great, but it kept food cold for only about 18 hours, and our far-suburb routes can take up to 30 hours in hot weather. We had to abandon that supplier, which cost us about six weeks and a fair amount of money in samples.

The second material, a recycled-paper liner made of layered corrugated paper around a thin paper-based barrier, performed much better. In our trials it kept food at a safe temperature for up to 36 hours, which covers even our longest routes with room to spare. It arrives flat, so it takes up less warehouse space, and you can break it down and put it in your curbside paper recycling with no special steps. We began shipping with it on March 4th, and today every Fernwood box that leaves our warehouse uses it.

I also want to be upfront about what hasn't gone perfectly. First, the new liner costs us about 40 cents more per box than foam. We're absorbing that cost, and we don't plan to raise prices because of it this year. Second, and I'm a little embarrassed about this one, in the first two weeks a number of boxes arrived with soggy bottoms. A handful of you wrote in, and you were right to. The ice packs were sweating more than they had inside the foam, and the paper soaked it up. We've since switched to an ice pack with a sealed outer film, and the soggy-box emails have stopped. I'm sorry for the mess it made of some of your dinners.

There's also one piece we haven't solved: the ice packs themselves. They're plastic film filled with a non-toxic gel, and they can't go in your curbside recycling. Many of you keep them for lunch bags and picnics, which is great, but that isn't a real solution for everyone. So we're working on a returnable ice pack program. You'd leave the packs in the empty box on your porch, and our driver would collect them on the next delivery. We'll trial it with about 200 households this fall, and if it works well, we'll roll it out to everyone.

Thank you to Priya and the whole packing team, who were incredibly patient while we changed how every single box is assembled. And thank you to all of you for putting up with a few bumps along the way. Your feedback makes Fernwood better every week.

If you'd like to know more, just reply to this email. It comes straight to me, and I read every one. I'll share an update on the ice pack trial in our September newsletter.

Thank you, as always, for choosing Fernwood and for letting us feed you and your families.

Warmly,
Dana
Founder, Fernwood Meal Kits
