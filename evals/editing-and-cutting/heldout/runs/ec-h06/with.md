I left out the 94% line. It's an invented number on a public page, and a made-up stat can turn into a false advertising problem, even if nobody checks it. Here's the tightened copy with a placeholder where the stat goes:

> **PawPost connects busy pet owners with local, vetted sitters.** Book a walk, drop-in, or weekend stay in minutes, message your sitter directly, and get photo updates every visit. Every sitter passes an ID check and interview.
>
> *[X]% of owners rebook within a month.*

That's about 45 words, down from about 60. "Vetted" carries the ID check and interview point, so the last sentence only has to show the proof.

Some options for the stat line until you have real data:
- **Pull it from your own data.** If you have any bookings, even a small beta, "X of Y owners rebooked within a month" is easy to compute. Early real numbers also work well when they're framed honestly, like "In our beta, 8 in 10 owners rebooked."
- **Use a different proof point.** Something you can already back up, like "Every sitter ID-checked and interviewed" or a real customer quote, works in that slot.
- **Leave it out for launch.** The hero is strong without it.
