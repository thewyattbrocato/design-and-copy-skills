Honest take: the substance is good, but the structure works against you. The post is credible and not defensive, and the fixed-appointment offer is a strong answer to the obvious objection. The problems are mostly order and one weak statistic.

**1. The news is in paragraph 5.** Customers who open this want to know what's changing and when. Right now they read about 2009, growth and last winter first. Lead with the change (windows go from two hours to four on June 3), then explain why. Cut the founder-and-two-trucks opening or shrink it to a line. "We're proud of that" is filler.

**2. The 11 to 4 stat will draw skepticism.** A missed delivery is presumably measured against the window. A four-hour window is easier to hit than a two-hour one, so fewer misses is partly built in. Sharp customers and dispatchers will notice. Either say how you measured it (for example, "missed against the window you were given"), or add a number that doesn't depend on the window size, like average arrival-time variance. Also say it was three routes over [how many weeks]. You already admit it was a pilot, which is good.

**3. The apology paragraph is vague and ends up patting yourselves on the back.** "Some of that we deserved" doesn't say which part. Name it (late deliveries, poor communication, whatever it was), or cut the line. "Our dispatchers worked overtime and our drivers deserve a lot of credit" is true, but placed right after an apology it reads as deflection. Move the credit elsewhere or drop it.

**4. Acknowledge the cost to the customer.** A four-hour window means someone has to staff the dock longer. You say "we know it sounds like a step backward," but you don't say what it costs them. One plain sentence would help.

**5. The offer needs more detail.** The fixed appointments are the best part, and they're buried near the end. Add:
- How to request one: phone number, email, and whether there's a deadline before June 3.
- What "at no extra charge for existing accounts" means for new accounts.
- Whether appointments are limited.

**6. Check the date.** It reads as "June 3" with no year. If this posts in the fall, readers can't tell whether that's next June or one that has passed. Add the year.

**7. The closer is boilerplate.** "Continued trust... continue to grow" repeats itself and says nothing. End on the call-to-action: call us by [date] if four hours won't work.

A tighter order would be: the change and date, why (reliability matters most to customers), the pilot results with a clear definition, the cost and the appointment option with contact details, then a short, specific acknowledgment of last winter. I can draft that version if you'd like.
