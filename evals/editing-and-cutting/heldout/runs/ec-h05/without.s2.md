Overall it's solid. The tone is plain, you don't grovel, and the fixed-appointment offer is the strongest part. Here's what I'd change, most important first.

**1. You bury the news.** Customers click on "A note about delivery windows," and the actual change doesn't show up until paragraph 4. Three paragraphs of history and apology come first, so it reads like you're softening them up. Lead with the change, then the why. For example: "Starting June 3, our delivery windows are going from two hours to four. Here's why, and what you can do if it doesn't work for you."

**2. The headline stat will get challenged.** "Missed deliveries fell from 11 to 4 per 100" is partly true by definition, because a wider window is easier to hit. A sharp customer will see that right away, and it can look like you moved the goalposts. Be upfront about it: "Yes, a wider window is easier to hit. But the bigger gain is that dispatch can absorb a late gate or a slow dock without blowing up the rest of the route." If that's actually the mechanism, say so. Also say what "missed" meant in the pilot, and mention that it was three routes. Small samples invite skepticism, and admitting it builds trust.

**3. The benefits are mostly yours.** "Drivers spent less time idling" helps you, not the customer. Either drop it or tie it to something they care about, like fewer late arrivals or more predictable pickups.

**4. The apology paragraph is vague.** "Some of that we deserved" is the right instinct but too hedged. Name what went wrong ("we missed too many windows last winter") and move on. Also, "Growth hasn't always been easy" makes it sound like the growth was the problem, which isn't the message you want.

**5. Gaps customers will ask about:**
- Is the 30-minute text new, or is it the same as before? If it's the same, a longer window with the same warning is a real downgrade for dock scheduling, so address that directly.
- "For loads that need one": who decides? Say how to request an appointment (phone number or email).
- "No extra charge for existing accounts" implies new accounts pay. Is that intended? If so, say what they pay. If not, reword.

**6. The close is boilerplate.** "Continued trust... continue to grow" repeats itself. End on the call-to-action instead: "Questions or a dock that can't work with this? Call [number]."

**7. Check your dates.** Today is October 14, 2026, so June 3 has either passed or is eight months away. Make sure the year is right. "This spring" and "last winter" will also read oddly if the post goes out well after those seasons.

The nostalgic opening isn't bad, but cut it to a sentence or two, or move it to the end.
