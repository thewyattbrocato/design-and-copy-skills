It's a decent post. The tone is honest, "some of that we deserved" is the best line in it, and the pilot numbers and the appointment option are the right things to include. The problems are order and one weak spot in the argument.

**1. The news is buried.**
The change doesn't show up until paragraph 4. Customers whose docks run on a two-hour window will skim for it, so the first paragraph should hold it. The 2009 history and the growth story are nice, but they delay the point and read like a warm-up for bad news. I'd cut the history to a clause or drop it. I'd also retitle the post something like "Delivery windows are changing to four hours on June 3".

**2. The headline stat invites an obvious objection.**
Missed deliveries fell from 11 per 100 to 4 per 100, but a four-hour window is easier to hit than a two-hour one. A sharp dispatcher or dock manager will see that you changed the target and then reported fewer misses. Say what "missed" meant in the pilot. If it was measured against the old two-hour window, say so, because that is much more convincing. If it was measured against the new one, own that and lean on the idle-time point instead. Also say how many stops the pilot covered, since "three routes" alone sounds thin.

**3. The appointment offer is the most useful part, so it shouldn't sit near the end.**
"At no extra charge for existing accounts" also raises the question of what new accounts pay. Either say, or rephrase so it doesn't invite the question. Include a phone number or email in this paragraph, since "call us" has nowhere to go.

**4. The apology is vague.**
"Bad weather, two driver retirements and a software migration" is specific, which is good. "Many of you felt it" and "some of that we deserved" are fine. But the post never says which failures were yours, such as late deliveries or unreturned calls. One concrete admission would do more than the overtime and driver-credit lines, which read like defending yourselves.

**5. Smaller cuts.**
- "Thank you for your continued trust in us as we continue to grow" is filler, and "grow" already appeared twice. End on the appointment option and contact info.
- "Lately we have been asking what customers value most" can become "In our surveys, reliability comes up ahead of price."

**6. Check the date.**
Today is October 14, 2026, so a June 3 start is either already past or about 7 months out. Confirm the date and year before Friday. Give customers real notice if it's the future date. If it's already in effect, the wording needs to change.

I can do a tightened version with the news up front if you'd like. I'd need the pilot's stop count and the definition of "missed" before I could fix point 2.
