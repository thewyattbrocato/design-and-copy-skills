The substance is good, and the structure is the main problem.

**What works**
- You admit that a longer window sounds like a step backward.
- You give real numbers (11 to 4 per 100 stops).
- You offer fixed appointments, and the 30-minute text is a concrete detail.

**What I'd change**
1. **Lead with the news.** Customers don't learn that windows are going from two hours to four until paragraph 4. Anyone who skims will feel ambushed. Open with the change and the date, then explain.
2. **Cut or shrink the history.** The 2009 origin story and "we're proud of that" delay the point. One clause is enough, or cut it.
3. **Make the apology specific.** "Some of that we deserved" reads as grudging, and the bad-weather, retirements and software list sounds like excuses. Say plainly that you missed too many deliveries last winter, then move on. "Our drivers deserve a lot of credit" is a non sequitur in an apology.
4. **Address the obvious objection.** A skeptical dock manager will think that a wider window makes missed deliveries fall because the target is easier to hit. Say what "missed" means in the pilot, or why customers come out ahead anyway. Also say what the pilot covered, such as the number of stops and the weeks it ran.
5. **Tighten the offer.** "No extra charge for existing accounts" raises the question of what new accounts pay. Also say how to request an appointment, with a phone number or email. As written, "call us" has no number.
6. **Fix the closer.** "Thank you for your continued trust... as we continue to grow" repeats "continue" and says nothing. End on the appointment offer and the contact info.

**Check before Friday**
- Today is Oct 14, so make sure June 3 is the right date and the notice period is long enough. If it means next June, say the year. If you meant an earlier date, it's already past.
- Confirm the "reliability ahead of price" survey claim and the pilot numbers are ones you can back up if a customer asks.
