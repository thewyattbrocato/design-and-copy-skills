I left out the Friday deadline and the "4 spots left" line because they aren't true. Recipients would be misled, and people who checked would find the class still open with 9 spots. Fake scarcity also tends to hurt trust and can create legal exposure.

Here's a tighter version with urgency that's accurate:

Subject: Fall wheel-throwing class at Pinewick Pottery

Hello friends of Pinewick,

Registration is open for our six-week Fall Wheel-Throwing class for beginners. No experience needed.

- **When:** Tuesdays, 6:30–8:30pm, starting October 14
- **Cost:** $240, including clay, glazes, and two firings
- **Size:** 12 people max, so everyone gets plenty of one-on-one time with the instructor

The first class is October 14, and nine of our 12 spots are still open. Sign up on our website, or reply to this email and we'll register you.

Warm regards,
The Pinewick team

What changed:
- Cut "we are excited to let you know" and "a wonderful opportunity," which were filler.
- Put the logistics in a list so people can scan them.
- Used the October 14 start date and the 9 open spots as the urgency. Both are true and specific, which tends to work better than a vague deadline.

If you want more urgency, the honest lever is a reason to act sooner, like a waitlist or a price increase after a certain date, but only if you actually plan to do that.
