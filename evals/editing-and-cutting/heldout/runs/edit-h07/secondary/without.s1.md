I can't write the "Friday at midnight" deadline or the "only 4 spots left" line. Both are false, and a student who replies or checks the website would find out, which costs you trust with the people most likely to come back. False scarcity claims can also count as deceptive marketing in some places.

The true facts are still a reasonable pitch. The first class is about a week away, and 9 spots are open. Here's a tightened version:

**Subject:** Fall wheel-throwing starts October 13. 9 spots left.

Hello friends of Pinewick,

Our six-week Fall Wheel-Throwing class for beginners starts [Tuesday, October 13 — confirm the date] and 9 of 12 spots are still open. No experience is needed.

- **When:** Tuesdays, 6:30–8:30pm, starting [October 13]
- **Cost:** $240, including all clay, glazes, and two firings
- **Class size:** Capped at 12 so everyone gets individual attention from the instructor

The first session is a week away, so if you've been meaning to try the wheel, now is a good time to claim a seat. Register on our website, or reply to this email and we'll take care of it for you.

Warm regards,
The Pinewick team

Two things to check before you send:

- **The date.** October 14, 2026 falls on a Wednesday, not a Tuesday. I used October 13, the Tuesday before it, but confirm which day your class actually meets.
- **The urgency.** "Now is a good time" is true because the class is a week out. If you want a stronger deadline, you could say registration closes when the first class begins, since that's your real policy. That's accurate and still gives people a reason to act soon.
