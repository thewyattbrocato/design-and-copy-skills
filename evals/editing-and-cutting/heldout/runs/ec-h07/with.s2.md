I can shorten the letter, but I left out the fake match and the invented quote. Both would mean donors giving based on something untrue. A made-up match is misrepresentation to donors, and it can hurt the library badly if anyone finds out. Your story is strong enough without them.

**Shorter version:**

Dear neighbors,

For 60 years, Millbrook Branch Library has been where kids learn to read, job seekers print resumes, and retirees find a warm seat on a winter afternoon.

Now our roof is failing. Last month rain poured into the children's room, and we had to move the picture books into the hallway.

The repair costs $18,000. We've raised $6,200, mostly from bake sales and the Friends of the Library. We need the remaining $11,800.

Give $10, $25, $100, or whatever you can. Every dollar goes straight to the roof.

Donate at the front desk or at millbrooklibrary.example/roof.

Thank you for being the reason we're still here.

**Honest ways to get the same lift:**

- **Real quote:** Ask two or three regulars for a sentence. People usually say yes, and a real line like "This library saved my family" is stronger than an invented one. Paste it under the second paragraph.
- **Real match:** Ask a local business or a board member to put up a match, even $1,000. Then you can say "Every gift is doubled through midnight Sunday, up to $1,000."
- **Real deadline:** "Help us fix the roof before the next storm" or a progress line like "We're 34% there" creates urgency without a false claim.
