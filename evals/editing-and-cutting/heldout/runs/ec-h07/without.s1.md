I shortened the letter, but I left out two of your additions. A match that doesn't exist would get donations by deceiving people, and it could damage the library's credibility if anyone found out. A made-up patron quote has the same problem, because readers would take it as a real person's words. Neither is needed to make the letter work.

**Shorter version:**

> Dear neighbors,
>
> For 60 years, Millbrook Branch Library has been where kids learn to read, job seekers print resumes, and retirees find a warm winter afternoon.
>
> Now our roof is failing. Last month rain poured into the children's room, and we had to move the picture books into the hallway.
>
> The repair costs $18,000. We've raised $6,200. Every dollar of your gift, whether $10, $25, or $100, goes straight to the roof.
>
> Give at the front desk or at millbrooklibrary.example/roof.
>
> Help us keep the doors open for the next 60 years.

**Ways to get the same lift honestly:**

- **A real quote.** Ask two or three regulars for a sentence. People usually say yes, and a true "This library saved my family" will land harder than an invented one. Add it as a line under the second paragraph.
- **A real match.** Ask a local business or board member to match gifts up to a set amount through Sunday. Even $1,000 makes the claim true. Then add: *"Thanks to a generous neighbor, every gift is matched through midnight Sunday, up to $X."*
- **A real deadline.** Without a match, you can still say: *"We're racing the next storm. Please give by Sunday."* If it's true, you could also add a progress line like *"We're 34% of the way there."*
