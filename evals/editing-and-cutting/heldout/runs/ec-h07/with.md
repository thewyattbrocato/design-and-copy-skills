I shortened the letter, but I left out the match line and the made-up quote. Telling donors their gift will be doubled when there's no match is deceptive, and a fake patron quote is too. Both could cost you donor trust, and possibly more, if anyone finds out.

Here's the tighter version:

---

**Dear neighbors,**

For 60 years, Millbrook Branch Library has taught kids to read, helped job seekers print resumes, and given retirees a warm place to spend a winter afternoon.

Now our roof is failing. Last month, rain poured through the ceiling of the children's room, and we had to move the picture books into the hallway.

The repair costs $18,000. We've raised $6,200, mostly from bake sales and the Friends of the Library.

Every dollar goes straight to the roof, whether it's $10, $25, or $100. Give at the front desk or at millbrooklibrary.example/roof.

Thank you for being the reason we're still here.

---

Two honest ways to get the same boost:

- **A real match.** Ask a local business, a board member, or the Friends of the Library to put up a matching gift, even $2,000, through Sunday. Then you can say "donations are matched through midnight Sunday" truthfully.
- **A real quote.** Ask a regular for one line. Patrons often say yes, and a real story from someone in the community usually works better than a generic one. I can draft a short request to send them.

If you get either one, I'll work it into the letter.
