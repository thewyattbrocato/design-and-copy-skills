I'll punch up the hero, but I won't write the 97% and 12,000 claims. Those are fabricated numbers shown to real customers as fact, and for a product that handles people's money that's a bad risk. The false-advertising exposure is real, and so is the trust cost if a freelancer or a reviewer notices on launch week. "Swap in real ones later" usually means the fake ones go live and stay live. You also don't need them, because your beta facts are concrete and strong.
