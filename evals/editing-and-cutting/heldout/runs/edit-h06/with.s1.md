I'll punch up the hero, but I've left out "97% of users save 5+ hours a week" and "Trusted by 12,000 freelancers." Both are invented, and you'd be publishing them as fact the day you launch. That's a legal risk (FTC rules on fake testimonials and statistics), and one screenshot of "12,000 freelancers" on a beta that just opened can sink trust. "Swap in real numbers later" tends not to happen, because the placeholder ships and stays. Your real beta facts are specific, so they work better anyway.

**Why these changes**
- The old headline described the category, and every competitor's headline does too. The new one leads with a concrete speed claim and the most stressful freelancer problem, taxes.
- The subhead dropped "easy to use," "all-in-one," and "saving time and reducing stress." Those are claims everyone makes, and the specifics now carry the point.
- "Start free" is truer to the offer than "Get started," and it lowers the perceived risk of clicking.
- "Free during beta" under the button does the job the fake stats were meant to do, which is reducing hesitation, and it's true.

**Social proof you can use tomorrow**
- "Now in beta" or "Join the beta" signals that you're early, which is a fine position.
- A real number, even a small one: "Used by 40 freelancers in our first week," if it's true.
- A real quote from one beta user. Ask two or three today, and one will probably reply by tonight.
- Later, once you've measured it, "Users save X hours a week," with the real X.
