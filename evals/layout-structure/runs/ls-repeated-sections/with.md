**Diagnosis:** The page has one template used five times. Nothing tells the reader which section matters, and the content isn't shaping the structure. Each section's material is different, so each should get a different form.

**What to change, section by section:**

- **Features:** Keep the card row, but make it the calm, parallel section. If one feature is the product's main idea, such as conflict-free scheduling, give it a larger cell or its own band with a product screenshot. Don't put it in a circle icon.
- **How it works:** This is a sequence. Show numbered steps connected along one line, or alternate screenshot and text rows. Cards imply the three items are interchangeable, and steps aren't.
- **Integrations:** This is a set of logos. Use a dense logo grid or strip, with no titles or body text. Two lines of copy per integration is filler.
- **Testimonials:** Use one large pull quote with a name, role and photo, or a quote beside a result like "cut no-shows 40%". Three cards of icon plus text is the wrong form for a quote.
- **Pricing:** Make this the loud section. Use side-by-side plans that are comparable, with the recommended plan visibly raised. Pricing needs to be scannable by row, not by icon.

**Page-wide:**

- Vary the backgrounds and vertical spacing. For example, alternate light and tinted bands, give the logo strip tight padding, and let pricing breathe. Right now the page has no rhythm.
- Left-align headings and subtitles on a shared content edge in sections where the content is a sequence or comparison. Keep centering for the short hero and perhaps pricing.
- Use real product visuals (calendar UI, booking flow) in at least two sections. Identical decorative icons carry no information.
- Cut the subtitles that only restate the heading.
- Check the stacked mobile order. The primary call to action should reappear after pricing and not only in the hero.

**Order:** Consider moving testimonials up, between How it works and Pricing. Social proof then sits right before the ask.
