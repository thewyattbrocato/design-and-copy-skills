No, don't narrow the grid. The 60-75 character measure applies to prose, where the eye has to travel back to the start of the next line. A data grid isn't read that way. People scan down columns and across rows to compare values, so width helps them.

Capping 14 columns at 70 characters would force truncated text, wrapped cells, or horizontal scrolling. All three make scanning and comparison harder.

What to do instead:
- **Keep the grid full width.** Prioritize the columns that matter most, and consider freezing SKU/name when scrolling horizontally.
- **Cap prose elsewhere.** Descriptions, help text, notes fields, and empty-state messages should stay around 65-75 characters.
- **Set cell limits by content type.** A long free-text column can have its own max width with truncation and a tooltip.
- **Help row tracking.** Use row hover, subtle zebra striping, or consistent alignment (numbers right-aligned).

Tell the reviewer the rule was meant for paragraphs, not tables.
