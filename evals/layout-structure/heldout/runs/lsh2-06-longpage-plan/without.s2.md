# Ledgerlark landing page plan

## Principle

Don't present five features and three quotes as eight equal boxes. Organise the page around what a sole trader dreads, which is the shoebox of receipts, chasing payment, and the tax bill they didn't see coming. Each feature then sits under one of those worries. Your visitors are probably on a phone between jobs, so design mobile-first.

## What the top of the page has to do

In about five seconds, a visitor should know:

1. **What it is.** Bookkeeping for freelance electricians and plumbers.
2. **That it's for them.** Say "electricians and plumbers" in the headline or subhead, not "small businesses".
3. **The main payoff.** Photograph a receipt and it's done.
4. **The next step.** One button, "Start your 30-day free trial", with "$12/month after. Cancel anytime." in small text beside it.

Hero contents:

- **Headline:** outcome-first and plain. For example, "Bookkeeping that happens while you're on the job." Avoid cleverness.
- **Subhead:** one sentence naming receipts, mileage, invoices and tax.
- **Visual:** a phone showing a receipt being snapped and categorised. Use the demo video as a poster frame that opens on click, or autoplay a few muted seconds of the receipt moment. Don't make the visitor watch the whole video to get the point.
- **Trust line under the hero:** one short quote, a single sentence with a name and trade. It should be a line, not a card.
- **Nav:** logo and one CTA only.

## Section sequence

| # | Section | Job | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero | Say what it is, who it's for, and the next step | Split layout: text on the left, phone on the right |
| 2 | Quote 1, a one-liner under the hero | Early proof | Thin strip, one line, no box |
| 3 | The problem | Name the shoebox, the Sunday-night admin, and the April surprise | Big type, no imagery, 2–3 short lines |
| 4 | Demo video | Show it working | Full-bleed, dark background, short caption |
| 5 | Receipts | Lead feature | Large split: screenshot next to 3 lines of copy |
| 6 | Mileage and bank feed | Supporting features | Compact two-up, smaller than #5 |
| 7 | Invoices with card payment | "Get paid faster" | Split layout, mirrored from #5 |
| 8 | Quarterly tax estimate | Emotional payoff: "know what you owe before it's due" | Full-bleed band with one large number on a phone screen |

## Matching quotes to sections

- **Quote 1** should be about time saved. It sits at the top.
- **Quote 2** should be about taxes or no more surprises. It sits beside the tax estimate.
- **Quote 3** should be about switching from a spreadsheet, or about price being worth it. It sits near pricing, where the doubt is.

If your three quotes don't fit these slots, move them to fit rather than forcing the sequence.

## Rhythm: how to avoid the wall of boxes

- **Alternate density.** Follow a heavy section with a light one. For example, the problem statement is only text, then the video is full-bleed, then a feature split follows.
- **Vary the layout, not just the content.** Use these in turn: split left, split right, two-up, full-bleed, centred single column, table. Don't repeat the same layout twice in a row.
- **Alternate backgrounds.** Light, dark, light, tinted. The video and tax sections are good candidates for the dark or accent bands.
- **Rank the features.** Receipts and the tax estimate get the most space. Mileage and bank feed share one row. This shows what matters, where equal cards would suggest it all matters equally.
- **Vary scale.** Include one huge number (the tax figure), one big pull quote, and one tiny one-liner. Avoid uniform 18px text.
- **Write short copy.** Give each feature a headline of 6 words or fewer, plus 2–3 lines of body text. Put anything longer in the FAQ or on a separate features page.
- **Use cards sparingly.** Use boxes only where content really is a set: the comparison table and the FAQ. Everywhere else, use whitespace and alignment.
- **Repeat the CTA** in the hero, after the video, after the tax section, at pricing, and in the final band. Use the same wording each time.

## What I'd cut or hold back

- A feature grid with five icons. It's the main cause of the "wall of boxes" problem.
- Anything that duplicates the demo video. Pick one place to explain each idea.
- Long comparison rows. Keep the spreadsheet table to 5–6 rows of the real differences: time per receipt, mileage, tax estimate, getting paid, and errors.

## Questions that would sharpen this

- What do the three quotes actually say, and who said them? Are any of them about a specific feature?
- How long is the demo video, and does its strongest moment come early?
- Do you have real screenshots, or will the phone visuals need mockups?
- Does the bank feed need a security reassurance (read-only access, which banks are supported)? If so, it goes in the FAQ and in a single line in section 6.
