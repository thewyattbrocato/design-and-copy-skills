# Ledgerlark landing page plan

Don't organise the page by your five features. Organise it by what an electrician or plumber does in a working week, so the features become three beats rather than five equal boxes.

## What the top of the page has to do

In about five seconds, a visitor should know three things: what this is, that it's for them, and what to do next.

- **Headline:** name the person and the outcome. For example, "Bookkeeping that keeps up with the van" or "Bookkeeping for electricians and plumbers, done from your phone." Avoid "streamline your finances."
- **Subhead:** one sentence that carries the feature list. For example, "Snap receipts, track mileage, send invoices people can pay by card, and always know what you owe for taxes."
- **One primary button:** "Start your 30-day free trial." Put "$12/month after" in small text beside it, so price doesn't surprise anyone later.
- **One visual:** a phone showing the receipt snap, or the video poster frame. Don't use an abstract dashboard.
- **One proof line under the button:** a short fragment of your best customer quote, with name and trade.

Keep the nav to a logo, a "Pricing" anchor and a login/trial button. Nothing else competes with the button.

## Section sequence

| # | Section | Job | Form |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero | What, who, next step | Split: text left, phone right |
| 2 | Demo video | Show it works in under a minute | Single centred object, lots of air |
| 3 | **Capture:** receipts and mileage | "It happens while you work" | Alternating text/phone screenshot, two short blocks |
| 4 | Quote 1 | Proof, early | Large pull-quote, no box |
| 5 | **Get paid:** invoices with card payment | "Money arrives faster" | One big feature with a mock invoice and pay button |
| 6 | **Know what you owe:** bank feed and quarterly tax estimate | "No surprise in April" | One large number as the visual, with the bank feed as the source |
| 7 | Quote 2 | Proof, tied to money or tax | Inline, small, beside section 6 |
| 8 | Spreadsheet comparison | Answer "I already have a spreadsheet" | Side-by-side table, 5–6 rows |
| 9 | Quote 3 | Last push before price | Full-width band, dark or tinted |
| 10 | Pricing | Remove doubt | One plan, huge "$12", short list, trial note |
| 11 | FAQ | Clear remaining objections | Collapsed list, 5–6 items |
| 12 | Final CTA | Repeat the hero ask | Short headline plus button |

Why this order:
- **Video early:** it does the most persuading for the least reading, so it shouldn't wait until section 6.
- **Bank feed inside the tax beat:** it's the reason the estimate is trustworthy. Giving it its own section would only add another box.
- **Comparison just before pricing:** the last objection is "why pay when I have a spreadsheet?", and the price lands right after the answer.
- **Quotes spread out:** three in a row would read as filler. Spaced out, each one backs up the claim next to it.

## Keeping the rhythm varied

Each section gets one job and one visual type, and no layout repeats back to back.

- **Alternate density.** Follow every heavy section (3, 6, 8) with a light one (4, 7, 9) that is mostly whitespace and one sentence.
- **Alternate alignment.** Hero is split, video is centred, capture zigzags, the pull-quote is full-width, pricing is centred. Never three left-text/right-image sections in a row.
- **Change the background twice, not constantly.** Use one tinted or dark band for the quote before pricing, and keep the rest light. More than that becomes striping.
- **Give the visuals different shapes.** The page should contain a phone screenshot, a video, an invoice mock, one oversized number, a table and a type-only quote. If every section has "icon, heading, two lines," you've built the wall of boxes.
- **Change the copy length.** Section 3 can carry two short paragraphs. The quotes and the pricing section should be nearly bare.
- **Build in one big moment.** The quarterly tax number in section 6 should be the largest thing on the page apart from the headline.

## What to cut or demote

- No icon grid of the five features. If you want a summary, make it one line under the hero.
- Don't give mileage its own section. It's a single line inside "Capture."
- Keep each quote to one or two sentences, plus a name and trade. If one is long, trim it to its strongest line.

## Questions that change the copy

1. Is a card required to start the trial? Say so near the button either way.
2. What are the card processing fees on invoices? Tradespeople will look for this, so put it in the FAQ at least.
3. Which country's tax rules does the quarterly estimate follow? State it where the estimate appears, because the feature won't be trusted without it.
