# Ledgerlark landing page plan

**Material:** bands of different content, so horizontal bands on one shared content edge. Each band gets its own inner shape. The reader is a tradesperson who is probably on a phone between jobs, so I planned the stacked order first.

## What the top has to do

The first screen needs to answer three things in plain words: what it is, who it's for, and what to do next.

- **Headline:** name the person and the pain, not the category. For example: "Bookkeeping that keeps up with the van." Or be literal: "Bookkeeping for electricians and plumbers. Snap the receipt, we do the rest." Pick the literal one if you're unsure. Nobody should have to decode it.
- **Subline (one sentence):** receipts, mileage, quarterly tax estimate and invoices, all from your phone.
- **One action:** "Start your free 30 days", with a small line beside it: "$12/month after. No card needed" (include that last part only if it's true).
- **A visual that shows the product:** the phone with a receipt being categorised. Don't use stock tradesperson photos. Put the demo video's poster frame just below or beside it, not as the hero itself, so the headline and button stay visible. Autoplaying video competes with the button.
- **Navigation:** logo on the same left edge as the headline, with 3 or 4 anchor links and the trial button at the right. Keep it minimal.
- **On a phone:** headline, subline, button, then visual. The button has to be above the fold.

## Sequence and rhythm

| # | Section | Pattern | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hero** (above) | Text left, phone visual right | Open |
| 2 | **The problem in one line** | One large statement: "Tax time shouldn't mean a shoebox of receipts." | Open, very short |
| 3 | **Core features, 1–3** (receipts, mileage, invoices with card payment) | Alternating text-and-screenshot rows, image side flips each time | Woven |
| 4 | **Quote #1** | Single quote, large, with name and trade, set into the margin | Open |
| 5 | **Tax estimate and bank feed** | A pair, unequal: tax estimate gets a big screenshot, bank feed gets a short text block | Woven, shorter |
| 6 | **Demo video** | Full-width player, one caption line | Loud candidate |
| 7 | **Spreadsheet vs Ledgerlark** | Side-by-side table with shared row labels | Full |
| 8 | **Quotes #2 and #3** | Two quotes in a row, no cards | Open/light |
| 9 | **Pricing** | One plan, one price, a short list of what's included, trial button | Open, centered |
| 10 | **Questions** | Short Q&A list (trial, cancellation, tax rules, data export) | Full, but plain |
| 11 | **Closing band** | One line and one button | Open |

I put the features in order of how often a tradesperson feels the pain, not the order you listed them. Receipts, mileage and invoices are the daily jobs. The tax estimate and bank feed are what they're relieved to get once they've seen the rest.

## How to keep it from becoming a wall of boxes

- **Only one section is parallel enough to be a grid, and I didn't make it one.** Your five features aren't equal. Splitting them across a row of five icon cards is what would make the page feel like boxes. Three get full rows with screenshots, two share a lighter band.
- **Alternate the three density states.** Open, woven and full follow each other, and no two neighbours share a template. The only adjacent "woven" bands are 3 and 5, and the quote in between separates them.
- **Make one section loud.** I'd pick the demo video: a full-bleed dark or strongly coloured field, with everything else calm. The alternative is the spreadsheet comparison, if that's your sharpest argument. Don't make both loud.
- **Spread the three quotes out.** One goes after the features, two later near pricing. Quotes that sit together read as a testimonial grid. Spread out, each one answers a doubt at that point in the page. If one quote mentions a specific feature, such as the tax estimate, move it next to that section.
- **Share one content edge.** Backgrounds can run edge to edge and change from band to band, but text and images stay on the same left edge as the logo.
- **Cap text at about 60–70 characters per line.** The wide layouts come from images and the table, not from long lines of text.
- **Name any break from the structure.** Two I'd allow: the quote hanging into the margin (it marks the point where the page turns from features to proof) and the full-bleed video band (it's the loud section). If you find a third, make it a rule or drop it.

## Content decisions to make

- **Cut copy before cutting sections.** Each feature gets a headline, two sentences and one screenshot. Details go in the questions list or a "more" link, not in extra boxes.
- **The comparison needs honest rows.** Five or six: capturing a receipt, mileage, quarterly estimate, sending an invoice, getting paid by card, time per week. Spreadsheet cells that say "manual" are fine. Don't invent hours-saved numbers you can't back up.
- **Put the price in the hero line as well as in section 9.** For a $12 product, the price is a selling point, not something to hide.
- **Trade-specific language.** Use words like "van", "supplier receipt", "call-out" and "materials". It does more for the page than any visual treatment, because it tells them it was made for them.

## Checks before design

1. Read the page as a single column, top to bottom: hero, problem, features, quote, tax/bank, video, comparison, quotes, price, questions, close. Does the story still hold?
2. Does the trial button appear in the hero, after the comparison, in pricing and in the closing band? Four times is fine on a page this long.
3. Do the longest quote and a three-line feature headline fit without breaking the layout?

Two assumptions I made. I assumed the trial doesn't need a card. And I assumed you have real product screenshots, not mockups. If either is wrong, tell me and I'll adjust sections 1 and 3.
