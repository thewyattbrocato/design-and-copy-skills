The page makes visitors work to find out what the event is, and it's built from parts that don't line up with each other. I'd expect these problems, roughly in order of damage.

**1. The first screen never says what the event is.**
- A full-bleed video with only a logo shows a name and nothing else. There's no date, city, one-line description or "Register" button.
- People decide in a few seconds whether this is for them, and that screen gives them nothing to decide with.
- Video also costs load time, especially on mobile. It is often the biggest reason for a quick bounce.
- **Fix:** Put the date, location, a one-sentence pitch and a primary registration button over the video, or directly under it. Use a poster image so the page doesn't wait on the video.

**2. Twelve sponsor logos come before any content.**
- They fill the second screen with other people's brands before a visitor has learned anything about the event. They also read as an ad wall.
- **Fix:** Move them lower, just above the footer. Show only top-tier sponsors higher up, as a single small row.

**3. The body paragraph is 1300px wide.**
- At that width a line holds about 150–200 characters. That's more than twice the readable maximum of roughly 60–75, so nobody tracks from one line to the next. People skip the text, and that paragraph is the main description of the event.
- **Fix:** Cap the text at about 60–70ch, around 600–700px. Put it beside something, such as key facts (date, venue, price) or a schedule. Don't let it stretch across the page.

**4. The navigation is left-aligned while everything else is centered.**
- The nav and the content have no shared edge, so the page looks broken, as if two layouts were stacked.
- The nav also sits fourth from the top, below the video, the sponsors and the paragraph. Because it's sticky, it appears out of nowhere partway down.
- **Fix:** Put it at the top, aligned to the same container edge as the content, or centered like the rest.

**5. The nav has 11 items.**
- That is too many to scan, and it will wrap or overflow on a phone.
- **Fix:** Cut to 5–6 destinations (for example Schedule, Speakers, Venue, Sponsors, Register) and fold the rest into a menu or the footer. Make "Register" a visibly distinct button.

**6. Everything is centered, and the sections all look alike.**
- Centered paragraphs are hard to read.
- Logo grid, text block and speaker grid are all the same kind of band, so the page has no rhythm and no clear focal point.
- **Fix:** Left-align the text. Give the sections different structures and let one section, probably the speakers, be the strongest.

**7. The speakers section is the most persuasive content, and it's buried.**
- For a conference, speakers and schedule are what make people register, and they're third from the top. There's no schedule or ticket section in what you listed.
- Check that the 3-column speaker grid collapses cleanly on narrow screens, with consistent photo ratios and a worst case such as a long name or title.

**Suggested order**
1. Nav at the top, with a Register button.
2. Hero with the date, location, pitch and call to action.
3. Short description (capped width) with key facts beside it.
4. Speakers.
5. Schedule and tickets.
6. Sponsors.
7. Footer.
