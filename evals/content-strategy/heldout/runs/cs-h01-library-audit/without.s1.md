# Content audit plan (six weeks, part-time, one helper)

You don't need to read every page. You inventory everything with data, then read closely only a sample and the pages that matter.

## Week 1: Define the goal and pull the inventory

- **Ask your director one question:** what decision will this audit support? Likely answers are cleaning up, redesigning, migrating platforms, or meeting accessibility requirements. The answer sets how deep you go.
- **Get a full page list.** Export it from your CMS, or crawl the site with Screaming Frog (free up to 500 URLs) or a similar tool. Put it in a spreadsheet with one row per URL.
- **Add data columns:** page title, content type (event, branch, blog, other), last-modified date, author or owner if available, and 12 months of page views from your analytics. Add inbound links if your crawler reports them.

## Week 2: Sort automatically before reading anything

Use filters and formulas to bucket pages:

- **Events with past dates.** These are almost certainly archive or delete candidates.
- **Blog posts** by year, and by traffic.
- **Branch pages**, grouped by branch. Expect duplicates and conflicting hours, addresses, and phone numbers.
- **Zero or near-zero traffic** over 12 months.
- **Not modified in 2+ years.**
- **Possible duplicates**, such as identical or near-identical titles.

By the end of this week, you'll probably find that most of the hundreds of pages fall into a few obvious groups. These are the pages you can decide about in bulk.

## Weeks 3–4: Review, with your helper

Define your decisions and your criteria before anyone starts reviewing. Four options are enough:

- **Keep** as is.
- **Update** (name what needs fixing).
- **Merge or redirect** into another page.
- **Remove or archive.**

Then split the work:

- **Bulk-decide the obvious groups.** Examples are expired events, dead blog posts with no traffic, and duplicate pages. Spot-check about 10% of each group to make sure the rule holds.
- **Read individually** the pages that matter most: the top ~50 by traffic, all branch pages (there are only 11, but they're high stakes), and anything tied to services, policies, cards, or accessibility.
- **Have your helper do first-pass review** with a one-page rubric. Is it accurate? Is it current? Is it duplicated elsewhere? Does anyone use it? Check the first 20 of their rows together to calibrate, then spot-check as they go.
- **Add notes** only where the decision isn't obvious. Don't write commentary on every row.

Check the branch pages for a central source of truth. Hours, addresses, and phone numbers should live in one place, not be hand-typed on many pages.

## Week 5: Validate and prioritize

- **Ask branch managers** to confirm their own branch page and any pages you're unsure about. Send a short list, not the full spreadsheet.
- **Check the blog** for anything with legitimate value, such as evergreen reading lists. Decide whether the blog gets a revival plan or an archive.
- **Prioritize the work** by impact and effort. Fixing wrong branch hours is high impact and low effort, so it goes first. Mass-deleting expired events is cheap and goes early.
- **Flag the policy gaps** the audit exposed. Who owns each content type? Who removes events after they happen? Without answers, the site will get messy again.

## Week 6: Write up and present

**What to hand your director:**

1. **One-page summary** with headline numbers: total pages, how many fall into each keep/update/merge/remove bucket, and the top 3–4 findings. For example: "X% of event pages are past-dated" or "branch hours conflict on N pages."
2. **Recommendations**, ranked by priority, each with a rough effort estimate (hours or staff-weeks).
3. **A proposed governance fix:** content owners by type, an expiry rule for events, a review cadence (such as annual), and a decision on the blog.
4. **The spreadsheet** as an appendix, with one row per URL and the decision for each. Your director probably won't read it, but you'll use it to do the work.
5. **What you didn't cover**, and how you sampled. This keeps the findings credible.

Include a short "next 90 days" plan so the audit leads to action instead of a shelf document.

## Tips

- **Don't let it become a rewrite project.** The audit decides what to do. The fixing is a separate phase.
- **Keep the scope in check.** If the crawl turns up PDFs, images, and other files, put them on a second tab and treat them as a later pass.
- **Protect against deletion mistakes.** Before removing anything, set up redirects for pages that get search traffic or inbound links. Keep a backup export.
- **If you fall behind,** shrink the individual-read list. Don't skip validation with branch managers.
