## Roles (three, not a hierarchy)

| Role | Who | Can do |
|---|---|---|
| **Admin** | You | Publish anything, manage users |
| **Reviewer** | The vet | Approve and publish blog posts. Backup approver for clinic pages. |
| **Contributor** | Everyone else with a login | Draft and edit, but can't publish |

Most website systems (WordPress, Squarespace, Wix and so on) have a setting like this. In WordPress, for example, it's the Author and Contributor roles. Start by removing publish rights from everyone except you and the vet. That alone would have stopped the holiday-hours problem.

## Two tracks, based on risk

**Track A: Clinic pages, hours, appointment info.** Wrong information here affects real pets and clients.
1. A clinic staff member drafts the change. Nothing goes live directly.
2. You check it against the source of truth (the clinic's own confirmation).
3. You publish. The vet covers if you're out.

**Track B: Blog posts.** The main risk is a medical claim that's wrong.
1. A writer drafts the post.
2. The vet reads it for clinical accuracy.
3. You do a quick check of tone, links and typos, then publish.

Typo fixes and photo swaps don't need review. Anything with hours, phone numbers, addresses, prices or medical advice does.

## Holiday hours

This was your actual incident, so it gets its own routine:
- Keep one shared sheet with a row per clinic and holiday, and a "confirmed by clinic manager" column.
- Around 3 weeks before each holiday, you put a calendar reminder to update the pages from that sheet.
- After publishing, someone at each clinic opens their own page and replies "looks right." That takes about a minute per clinic.
- Check the Google Business listings at the same time. They usually drift out of sync too.

## One-page rules to share with staff

1. Draft, don't publish, unless you're an Admin or Reviewer.
2. Hours, contact details and medical content always get a second pair of eyes.
3. Each clinic names one person who confirms its info. Just one, not a committee.
4. If something is wrong on the site, tell the practice manager. Don't quietly fix it.
5. Once a quarter, you and the vet spend 30 minutes skimming every clinic page for stale details.
