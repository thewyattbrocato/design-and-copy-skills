# Content ownership and approval, for two people

**The problem:** nobody owns any page, and anyone with a login can publish. That's how the wrong holiday hours stayed up for a week. I assumed your website tool has user roles. I haven't seen it, so check that it can do step 1.

## 1. Cut editing access down
- **Publish rights:** you and the vet only. Everyone else is a contributor who drafts or suggests but can't publish. If the tool can't separate those, remove edit access from everyone else and have them send changes to you.
- **Review the logins now.** Remove anyone who has left or doesn't need to edit.

## 2. Who answers for what

| Content | Owner (final say) | Supplies the facts | Checks | Publishes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clinic pages (hours, phone, address, staff) | You | Whoever runs the front desk at each clinic | You, plus the clinic's contact on the live page | You |
| Appointment info | You | Front desk staff | The vet checks anything medical (fasting, what to bring, emergency advice) | You |
| Pet care blog | The vet | The vet, or anyone drafting | The vet for medical accuracy, you for tone and typos | You |

Each clinic's front-desk person has to confirm their own hours. They are the only people who know the real hours.

## 3. Review rules
- **Hours, phone, address, prices:** the clinic contact confirms in writing (email or message is fine). You publish. The contact then looks at the live page and replies "correct."
- **Medical content (blog, appointment prep):** the vet approves before it goes live. If she hasn't replied in 3 working days, the post waits. Silence doesn't count as approval for medical content.
- **Everything else:** you decide alone.

## 4. Holiday hours, the thing that broke
- Keep one shared list of each clinic's holiday hours, with a date by which each clinic contact must confirm.
- Ask for confirmations 3 weeks before each holiday. You publish them, and each contact checks their live page the day before.
- **Do this now** for the end-of-year holidays, since they're the next ones.

## 5. Emergency path
For a sudden closure (weather, illness, power cut), any clinic contact messages you. You edit the page right away with no review. The clinic contact checks the live page afterward, and the vet and you glance at it at the next monthly look.

## 6. Staying correct
- **Monthly, 20 minutes, first Monday:** you and the vet open each clinic page and the appointment pages and compare them with what the front desks say.
- **Also check immediately when** hours, staff, prices or a clinic's services change, or when the front desk keeps getting the same question.
- **Keep a simple log:** one line per page with last checked, by whom, and what would bring the next check forward. A spreadsheet is enough.

## 7. What to stop
Pause new blog posts until the vet has skimmed the existing ones and flagged anything out of date or wrong. Fix or remove those. Restart only if someone has time to write and the vet has time to check, at a pace you've agreed on, not a target.

**To confirm:** I assumed the vet can spare a few hours a month. If she can't, the blog stays paused and the clinic and appointment pages take priority.
