**The problem:** nobody is answerable for the facts on the site, and anyone can change anything. That's an ownership problem, not a writing problem. The holiday-hours mistake got through because nobody was assigned to check it.

## 1. Who does what

| Content | Owner (decides, publishes) | Supplies facts | Checks before it goes live |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinic pages (hours, address, phone, staff, services) | You | Whoever runs that clinic day to day | That same person confirms the page matches reality |
| Appointment info (how to book, what to bring, cancellation terms) | You | You | The vet, once |
| Pet care blog | You | The vet or a clinic vet who wrote or suggested it | The vet, for anything medical (dosing, symptoms, "when to come in") |

- **Editing rights.** Make just two or three logins editors and publishers: you, and the vet if the site allows it. Everyone else becomes a "suggester" or loses access. Most website tools can restrict roles; I'd need to know which one you use to give exact steps. If yours can't, the rule is "send changes to the practice manager" and only you log in to publish.
- **Hours changes** need the clinic's own confirmation, whether by email or text. That confirmation is your check.
- **Timeouts.** The vet gets 3 working days to review. If there's no reply, you can publish non-medical items. Medical blog posts wait.

## 2. Make, check, approve

1. Anyone on staff can request a change by emailing you, or by using a shared note or form if you have one. Say what should change, and for which clinic.
2. You draft it, or the vet drafts it for medical topics.
3. The fact-supplier confirms it, and the vet reviews it if it's medical.
4. You publish, then open the live page once and look at it.

**Urgent path (closures, wrong hours, emergency numbers):** the clinic tells you, you fix it right away, and you get the clinic to confirm it after. No vet review is needed unless it's medical. Look back at it the next day.

## 3. Staying fresh

- **Holiday hours:** every clinic confirms its hours **[2–3 weeks]** before each holiday. Put the holiday dates in your calendar once, as repeating reminders. Afterwards, remove the holiday banner or notice. Old notices cause as much trouble as wrong ones.
- **Clinic pages:** one read-through by each clinic every 6 months, and straight away when staff, services, phone numbers or opening hours change.
- **Appointment info:** check it whenever booking software or cancellation policy changes. Also check it each January.
- **Blog:** each post gets a "reviewed" date. The vet re-checks medical posts every 12 months. Anything that can't be re-checked gets retired.
- **Review log:** keep one spreadsheet with a row per page: owner, last checked, who checked it, next check. A first pass over the whole site is the first task, since it shows what's already out of date.

## 4. What to pause or cut

- **Pause new blog posts** until the first-pass check of clinic and appointment pages is done. Accurate hours matter more than new content.
- After that, keep the blog to what you and the vet can really manage, such as one post a month or one every other month. Don't set a schedule you can't keep.
- Retire any blog post that nobody can vouch for.
