# Content plan for the clinic website

**The problem:** nobody owns any page, and nothing gets a second check before it goes live. That's how the wrong holiday hours stayed up for a week. The fix is named owners and one simple check. It doesn't need more layers.

## 1. Who can do what

| Role | Who | Can do |
|---|---|---|
| Publisher | You (practice manager), plus one backup you choose | Publish and edit anything |
| Clinical checker | The vet | Approve anything that gives medical advice |
| Everyone else with a login | Whoever has one now | Draft only, or no access if they don't need it |

Most website tools can do this: an "author" or "contributor" role can save drafts but can't publish. Cut the logins down to the people who actually update the site.

## 2. Owners and checks

| Content | Owner | Facts come from | Checked by | Publishes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clinic pages (hours, address, phone, team) | You | One named person at each clinic, usually whoever runs the rota | That same person, who reads the draft before it goes live | You |
| Holiday and closure hours | You | Same person at each clinic, confirmed in writing (email or text) | A second look: you compare the draft to the written confirmation | You |
| Appointment info (booking, what to bring, cancellations, fees) | You | You | The vet, for anything about preparation, fasting or emergencies | You |
| Pet care blog | The vet | The vet | You (links, tone, spelling) | You |

Two rules apply to all of it:

- **Nobody publishes their own change to hours or fees without someone else looking.** This is a policy, not a guideline.
- **A reviewer's silence never counts as yes for clinical content.** If the vet hasn't approved a post within about a week, it stays a draft. Nothing is lost by waiting.

## 3. Holiday hours routine

1. About three weeks before each holiday, ask each clinic's named person for their hours.
2. Get their answer in writing.
3. Enter the hours, then compare the draft to what they wrote.
4. Publish, and add a "last checked [date]" line to the page.
5. Put a reminder in your calendar for the day after the holiday to remove the special hours.

## 4. Emergency path

Use this for a sudden closure, illness or weather.

- Any clinic can message you directly.
- You change the page and add a banner straight away. The second check is skipped.
- The clinic confirms the live page within a day.

## 5. What goes stale

| Content | Goes stale when | Look at it |
|---|---|---|
| Hours | Holidays, rota changes, staff leaving | Before every holiday, plus the monthly look |
| Fees, cancellation rules, booking steps | Any change to them | Monthly look |
| Team and services | Hires, departures, new services | Monthly look |
| Blog posts | Clinical guidance changes, or a post gets a client complaint | Once a year, vet's call |

**Monthly 15-minute look:** you open each clinic page and compare it to what's actually true. A shared sheet with one row per page is enough. It needs a page ID, the owner, the last check date, who checked, and the next check date.

## 6. What we're not doing

- No blog schedule. Two people can't keep one up. Publish when the vet has a post ready and has approved it.
- No new approval committee or ticket system.
- Old blog posts that give medical advice get hidden as drafts until the vet has skimmed them. Restore the ones that pass and retire the rest.
