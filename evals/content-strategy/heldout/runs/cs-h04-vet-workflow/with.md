**2. Owners by content type**

| Content | Owner (answers for it) | Facts come from | Checked by | Publishes |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**3. Review rules**
- **Hours, prices, phone numbers:** the clinic contact confirms the change in writing. You publish, then they look at the live page and reply "correct". No reply within 2 working days means you chase them. It doesn't mean it's fine.
- **Blog posts:** the vet signs off on medical content. If she doesn't respond in 5 working days, the post waits. Silence isn't approval for health advice.
- **Typos and layout:** you decide alone, with no review.
- **Urgent changes** (a clinic closes suddenly, a phone number breaks): the clinic contact tells you by phone or text. You publish right away and check afterward. The look-back is a quick "was it right?" the next day.

**4. What makes content go stale**
- **Holiday hours:** put dated reminders in your calendar, about 3 weeks before each holiday `[list your holidays]`. Each clinic contact confirms its hours, you update, and they check the live page. This is the fix for last month's problem.
- **Any page that states a price, hour or policy:** check it whenever that thing changes. Otherwise do a quick check once a quarter.
- **Blog posts:** the vet re-reads a post only if guidance changes or a client question suggests it's wrong.
- **Record it:** keep a simple sheet with one row per page: page, owner, last checked, next check, and what would bring the check forward. Do a first pass through the four clinic pages and the appointment pages now. That gives you a baseline.

**5. What to pause**
Don't publish new blog posts until the vet confirms she can write or review them at a regular pace `[posts per month she can really do]`. Four accurate clinic pages matter more than a busy blog. Review the existing posts once, and retire or update any that are out of date or that nobody can vouch for.
