## Proposed process

**1. Three roles (you only have two people, so one person can hold two)**
- **Editors:** staff who draft or update content. They can save drafts but can't publish.
- **Approvers:** you and the vet. They are the only ones who can publish.
- **Admin:** you alone, for users and settings.

Take editing rights away from everyone who has them now, and add them back only for people who need them. This fixes the "anyone can edit anything" problem.

**2. Split content by risk**

| Type | Examples | Who checks | Turnaround |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High-risk facts** | Clinic hours, holiday hours, phone numbers, addresses, appointment and emergency info, prices | Practice manager, plus the clinic's own manager confirms | Same day |
| **Clinical content** | Blog posts, pet care advice | Vet reviews for accuracy; you review tone and links | Within about a week |
| **Low-risk edits** | Typos, photos, formatting | Either approver | Whenever |

The vet should only see clinical content. Don't make them check hours.

**3. Holiday hours (the problem you actually had)**
- Each clinic manager sends the hours to you in writing, using a simple template: clinic, date range, open/close times, and any emergency referral.
- Do this about three weeks before each holiday. Put a recurring reminder in your calendar for it.
- You publish the hours and the clinic manager checks the live page within one business day and replies "confirmed".
- Hours should live in one place, such as a single hours block or table that the clinic pages pull from. That way a change can't be made in one place and missed in another.
- Once a quarter, spend 15 minutes checking all four clinic pages against reality.

**4. Blog posts**
1. A staff member drafts the post and marks it "needs vet review".
2. The vet checks the medical claims and replies with changes or "approved". They don't need to touch the site.
3. You do the final read, check the links and publish.

**5. Paper trail (one shared sheet or doc)**
Columns: what changed, page, who requested it, who approved it, date. It takes a minute per change, and it shows you what changed if something goes wrong again.
