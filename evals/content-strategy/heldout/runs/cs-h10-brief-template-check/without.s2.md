It's mostly good. I'd make a few changes before Monday.

**What works**
- It's one page, and every field has a clear job.
- "Source of truth" with a date, "Retire this article if", and "Not covering" are the fields most briefs lack. They prevent stale and bloated articles.
- Naming one owner and a tester forces accountability.

**What I'd change**

1. **Split "Owner (one name) and who will test the steps."** This is two fields in one, and people will fill in only the first. Make them separate lines: Owner, Tester. Add "(not the owner)" to Tester if you want the steps checked by someone fresh, which is the point of testing.

2. **Split "Who it's for, and what they were trying to do when they got stuck."** This is also two questions. Use "Who it's for" and "What they were trying to do / where they got stuck." The second one is the most useful field on the page, and it deserves its own line.

3. **Add where the need came from.** For example: support tickets, search terms, a request from a particular team. Without it, you can't tell whether an article is worth writing, and you can't tell later whether it worked. A link to a few tickets is enough.

4. **Add a success signal (optional).** How will you know the article helped? For example, fewer tickets on topic X, or a search term that now resolves. This also makes the "Retire this article if" decision easier later.

5. **Make "Existing articles it replaces or links to" a clearer instruction.** Replacing and linking are different actions. If it replaces something, someone needs to redirect or archive the old article. Try: "Replaces (to be redirected/archived):" and "Links to / from:".

6. **Tighten "Product version or plan it applies to."** Add "and what it doesn't apply to," or people will write "all." Also consider "Date confirmed" next to it, since versions drift.

7. **Re-check date.** The 6-month default is fine, but "default" invites everyone to leave it blank. Ask for an actual date. If the product ships fast, you might trigger a re-check on a release instead of a date.

8. **Consider a one-line "Article type" field** (how-to, troubleshooting, reference, concept). It's optional, but it stops a how-to from growing into a reference doc.

**Lower priority**
- "What they should be able to do after reading it" is good. It reads better as an outcome that can be checked, such as "Can export a report as CSV without contacting support." You could add that example in the template.
- Decide where the brief lives and who approves it before writing starts. That isn't a brief field, but it affects whether people fill it in.

If you want to keep it to one page, items 1, 2, and 3 matter most. The rest are optional.
