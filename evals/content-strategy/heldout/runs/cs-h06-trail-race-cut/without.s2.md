# Recommendation

Runners matter most, but the other three audiences matter for different reasons. The site should treat them differently.

| Audience | Why they matter | How much site they need |
|---|---|---|
| Runners | Entry fees and the mailing list keep the events alive | Most of the site |
| Volunteers | Races can't happen without them | One page, plus links in emails |
| Sponsors | Money, but only a few people ever read it | One page and a contact |
| Landowners and permit people | Low traffic, but losing their trust ends the races | One credible page, rarely visited |

Landowners are the odd one. Few of them visit, but a lost permit is the one failure you can't recover from. That page needs to be accurate and professional, not prominent.

## Content to have

- **Home page:** the next race, its date, a register button, and a mailing list signup. Everything else is secondary.
- **One page per race, all built from the same template:**
  - date, location, distances, price, and registration status
  - course map and elevation
  - cutoffs, parking and logistics, and gear rules
  - FAQ, past results, and a "volunteer for this race" link
- **Volunteer page:** roles and time commitments, the signup, and a short "what to expect." List the upcoming races on it.
- **Sponsor page:** who you've had, who the events reach (entrants, email list size, social following), what sponsors get, and one contact. A one-page PDF works well.
- **About / Land and permits:** who you are, your land stewardship practices, and a contact for landowners and land managers. This is also where you thank landowners and land agencies publicly.
- **Mailing list signup** in the footer of every page.

## Fold together or drop

- **Fold the land and permits page into About.** Give it a short section or subpage, and put a one-line version ("Run on private land with permission, please stay on the trail") on each race page, where runners will read it.
- **Prune the blog hard.** A large archive of generic training tips takes time to maintain and doesn't do much for your three events.
  - Check analytics for the 5–10 posts that actually get search traffic and keep and refresh those.
  - Merge the rest into a few guides, such as "First trail race" and "Training for our distances," or archive them.
  - Redirect old URLs rather than deleting them.
  - Stop publishing new tips unless you enjoy it. Race-specific updates are worth more than general advice.
- **Navigation:** Races, Volunteer, Sponsors, About, with Blog demoted to the footer or a "Resources" link. Equal prominence for everything is the problem you described, and a short nav fixes most of it.

## What to do first (about 5 hours a week)

1. **Week 1, about 2 hours:** Pull analytics for the last 12 months: top pages, and where registrations come from. This replaces my guess with data on what runners actually use. Then rebuild the home page around the next race, register, and email signup.
2. **Week 1–2, about 3 hours:** Write one race page template and apply it to your next race. Clone it for the others.
3. **Weeks 3–4:** Cut the navigation down and fold the land page into About. Tighten the volunteer and sponsor pages to one screen each.
4. **Month 2:** Do the blog audit. Keep, merge, or redirect, working through it a few posts at a time.
5. **Ongoing:** Spend a recurring hour each week on the next race page and the email list. Those are the two things that fill your events.
