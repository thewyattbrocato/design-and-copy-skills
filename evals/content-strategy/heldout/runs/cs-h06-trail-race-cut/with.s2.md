**Decision:** what to keep, fold or drop so that five hours a week is enough. The answer is to organize the site around the next race and stop treating everything as equally important.

## Who matters most

Your guess is probably right: runners come first. I'm assuming entry fees pay for the races. If sponsors cover most of the costs, sponsors rise in priority.

1. **Runners** are the main audience, because entries and the mailing list drive everything else.
2. **Volunteers** matter in bursts, a few weeks before each race.
3. **Sponsors** matter a few times a year, and mostly when you pitch them directly. The page backs up that pitch.
4. **Landowners and permit officials** will rarely browse the site, but losing their trust costs you a race. They need a page that is accurate and easy to point to, and it can be small.

## What the site should have

| Page | Job | Owner | Goes stale when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Home** | Shows the next race, date, a Register button and the mailing-list signup | `[name]` | A race happens or registration opens or closes |
| **One page per race (3)** | Date, course, cost, cutoffs, parking, schedule, rules, FAQ, then results afterward. Includes a "training for this race" section and links to its volunteer signup | One person per race | Date, price or course changes, and after each race |
| **Volunteers** | One page with roles and a signup per race | `[name]` | Each race cycle |
| **Sponsors** | Who you reach, what sponsors get, past sponsors, one contact | `[name]` | Before each sponsor pitch |
| **Our trails** (land, permits, stewardship) | Thanks to landowners, trail rules, the permit basics you're comfortable sharing, a contact | `[name]` | A permit renews or a landowner changes |
| **Mailing list** | The main channel for updates | `[name]` | n/a |

Each page needs one named owner who can say no. "The volunteers" or "both of us" means nobody owns it.

## Fold together or drop

- **Blog:** stop adding new posts now. A large archive of training tips is likely your biggest maintenance burden and the part least tied to getting people to enter.
  - Check which posts get found through search or lead to a race page. I haven't seen your analytics, so that check is the first thing to do before cutting anything.
  - Fold the best few into the "training for this race" sections.
  - Leave the rest up but out of the main navigation, or retire them if they are out of date or wrong. Don't delete by traffic alone, because a low-traffic post may just be hard to find.
- **Land and permits:** shrink it into "Our trails". Keep whatever you're legally or contractually required to show, even if nobody reads it.
- **Sponsor pages:** make it one page, with logos on the race pages. Don't create a page per sponsor.
- **Past races:** after each race, cut its page down to results, photos and a "next year" signup. Don't maintain full old race pages.
- **Navigation:** put Races first and the mailing-list signup on every page. Volunteers, Sponsors and Our trails go in a secondary menu. This does more than any wording change, since it ends the "everything equally prominent" problem.

## First, within five hours a week

- **Week 1:** Walk the three race pages as a first-time runner would. Fix anything wrong, such as dates, prices or broken registration links. Name the owner of each page.
- **Week 2:** Rebuild the home page and navigation as described above. Add the mailing-list signup everywhere.
- **Week 3:** Pull the blog list with page views, entries that followed from each post, and last-edited date. Pick the handful worth keeping and fold them into the race pages.
- **Week 4:** Merge the land and permits page into "Our trails". Combine the sponsor pages into one.
- **Afterward:** Spend about an hour a month on a check of dates and links. Add a calendar reminder for a pre-registration review and a post-race cleanup for each race.

**Two things to confirm:**
- **Money:** where does most of your revenue come from, entries or sponsors? That changes the priority order above.
- **Entrants:** how many people enter each race? Use that real number as the headline on the sponsor page.
