**Direction:** the site exists to get runners signed up for the next race, and to give volunteers, sponsors and landowners one clear, correct page each. Runners matter most for the site itself. Landowners matter most to the races, because losing a permit means no race. Their page rarely gets visitors, but it has to be right and someone must own it.

## What to have

| Content | For | Verdict | Owner | Stale when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home page | Runners | Next race, register button and mailing list signup. Nothing else competes with them. | `[staff A]` | A race opens, closes or finishes |
| Race pages (3, one template) | Runners | Keep. Same fields on each: date, distances, cost, registration link, cutoffs, course map, parking, packet pickup, FAQ, past results. | One race director per race | Registration opens or closes. After each race, roll to next year's date or mark "TBD". |

| Training blog | Runners | Freeze. See below. | `[staff A]` | Not applicable while frozen |

## What to fold or drop

- **Blog:** stop writing new tips. Don't delete posts based on traffic alone, because low traffic may just mean nobody can find them.
  - Pick the handful that bring in search visitors or that runners actually ask about, and link them from the relevant race pages.
  - Fold the rest into one "Training" page with a short list of links, or archive them.
  - Keep a few evergreen posts and drop any that are out of date, such as old dates or gear advice.
- **Volunteer, sponsor and land pages:** remove them from the main menu's top level. The menu becomes **Races · Volunteer · Sponsors · About**, with the land page under About.
- **Equal prominence:** this is the real problem, and it costs nothing to fix. The home page should lead with one thing, which is the next race.

## What to do first

1. **This week (about 2 hours):** rewrite the home page so the next race and the signup lead. Reorder the menu as above.
2. **Next (about 2 hours):** put the three race pages on one template. Check every date, price and link on them. Wrong or stale race details cost you entries.
3. **Following week:** pull a list of every page with the columns title, URL, last edited, visits and where visitors came from. Choose which blog posts survive. I can help sort it if you paste it here.
4. **Then:** fold the volunteer, sponsor and land pages and give each a named owner.
5. **Ongoing:** give each page a "last checked" date. Put a 30-minute monthly review on the calendar, and a check each time a race opens or closes.

**Notes**
- I assumed the mailing list is your main way of reaching runners. Check how people actually enter: if most come from the list, put your remaining hours into the list and race pages, not the blog.
- I haven't seen the site, so which blog posts to keep is something to measure, not guess.
