**The decision:** with five hours a week, what gets prominence and what gets merged, frozen or retired. I haven't seen the site, so this is based on your description.

## What to have

Runners come first. They're the ones who enter, and entries pay for everything else. The land and permit page is the exception to "least visited is least important". Races don't happen without it, so keep it, but it doesn't need prominence.

| Content | Audience | Fate | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Race pages** (one per race: date, course, entry, cutoffs, logistics, results, a volunteer link) | Runners | **Front and center.** Fold volunteer signup into each race page. | [name, one of the two staff] |
| **Mailing list signup** | Runners, volunteers | **Make prominent on every page.** It's your main channel. | [name] |

| **Land and permits** | Landowners, permit officials, runners | **Keep as one page** in the footer and "About". Include the land acknowledgments, rules and contact. Required content stays even if few people read it. | [name] |

Each item gets one named person with the right to say no. "We both" means nobody.

## Fold together or drop

- **Blog:** don't delete by traffic alone. Low traffic may just mean nobody can find a post. Pull a list of every post (title, date, URL, views if you have them). Mark each one **keep** (evergreen, still correct), **merge** (several posts on the same topic become one), or **retire** (dated, race-specific, or thin). Link the keepers from race pages, such as "Training for [race]", so the blog supports entries.
- **Volunteer signup:** merge it into the race pages so it isn't a separate destination.
- **Sponsors:** collapse multiple pages into one.
- **Navigation:** the order should reflect the audiences: Races, Volunteer, Sponsors, About (with land and permits), Blog last.

## What makes content stale

- **Race pages:** update when the date, course, price or permit status changes. After each race, archive it as a results page and open the next year's page.
- **Volunteer page:** update before each race's signup opens.
- **Sponsor page:** update when sponsors change, plus once a year.
- **Land and permits:** update whenever a permit renews or a landowner's terms change. Check once a year either way.
- **Blog keepers:** look once a year. If it's wrong or dated, fix it or retire it.

## What to do first (about 5 hours a week)

1. **Week 1 (2 hrs):** Assign the owners above. Fix the nav and put the mailing list signup on every page.
2. **Week 1–2 (3 hrs):** Pull the blog post list.
3. **Weeks 2–4:** Triage the blog (keep, merge or retire), about an hour or two a week. Fold volunteer signup into the race pages.
4. **Month 2:** Consolidate sponsors into one page. Set the review dates.
5. **Ongoing:** No new blog posts until the triage is done. After that, publish only what a specific upcoming race needs.

Two notes:
- **Assumption:** I've treated runners as the priority audience. To check, look at which pages entrants visit before they register, and ask a few recent entrants how they found you. If sponsors or volunteers are what limits your growth, the order changes.
- **To measure:** the blog's real value. Check whether people read a training post and then enter a race, or whether posts are only found by search. That decides how many keepers to keep.
