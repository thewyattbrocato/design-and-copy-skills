**The decision:** nothing on the site is ranked, so every page competes for the same attention. With five hours a week, the fix is to rank the pages, fold the minor ones into the major ones, and shrink the blog.

## Audiences, in order

1. **Runners.** Their entries are the reason the site exists, so you're right about this. The home page and navigation should serve them first.
2. **Landowners and permit people.** They rarely visit, but they can end a race by saying no. The land page is a low-traffic, high-stakes page. Keep it accurate and easy to find, but don't make it prominent.
3. **Volunteers.** They need to find one signup, with dates and roles.
4. **Sponsors.** Current sponsors want to be visible. Prospective sponsors need one page and a contact.

## What to have

| Content | Verdict | Notes | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | Keep, rebuild | Next race first, with date, a register button and the mailing list signup. Nothing else gets equal weight. | Whoever runs registration today |
| Race pages (3) | Keep, one template | Date, location, distance, price, register, course, cutoffs, parking and logistics, refund/transfer policy, results. Archive past years instead of leaving stale pages up. | Same person |
| Mailing list signup | Keep, put on every page | It's the one channel you own. Use it for race updates and the best training content. | Whoever sends the emails |
| Volunteer signup | Keep as one page | Roles and dates per race. Link it from each race page. | Whoever coordinates volunteers |
| Sponsor pages | Fold into one page | A thank-you with logos, what sponsoring gets a company, and a contact. Put current sponsor logos on race pages and the footer. | Whoever handles sponsor relations |
| Land and permits | Keep, retitle | Something like "Land, trail access and conduct." Thank the landowners, state the rules for runners, and give a contact. Check whether any permit requires public notice. | A named staff member, since this is the relationship at risk |
| FAQ | Create or fold in | Fold repeat questions into race pages and delete duplicates. | Race page owner |
| Blog | Cut hard | See below. | A named staff member |

## The blog

The blog is probably your biggest time sink for the least benefit.

- **Stop publishing new posts** for now.
- **Keep** a short list of evergreen posts that help someone prepare for one of your races. Move them into a "Prepare" section on the matching race page.
- **Archive the rest.** Don't delete by traffic alone. A post with low traffic may just be hard to find, and a post that brings in search visitors may be worth keeping.
- **Take it out of the main navigation** and put it in the footer.

## Navigation

Races, Volunteer, Sponsors, About (with the land page under it), and the Newsletter signup. Five items, with Races first.

## What to do first (about 5 hours a week)

1. **Week 1:**
   - Spend 1.5 hours listing every page with its audience, last-updated date, owner and verdict.
   - Spend 2 hours making the next race page complete and correct.
   - Spend 1.5 hours reordering the home page and navigation.
2. **Week 2:** Triage the blog. Pick the evergreen keepers and move or archive the rest.
3. **Week 3:** Merge the sponsor pages and tidy the volunteer page.
4. **After that:** Plan on roughly 1 to 2 hours a week of upkeep, more in the month before each race. Update the race page within a set time after each race, and roll the dates forward each year.

Two notes:
- I assumed one of the three races is coming up soon. If none is, do the inventory and the blog triage first.
- I haven't seen the site or any analytics. Before archiving blog posts, check where your entries actually come from, using your registration platform or analytics. That tells you whether any blog post earns its place.
