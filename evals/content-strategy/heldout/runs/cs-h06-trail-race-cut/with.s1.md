**Direction:** the site exists to get people into races and volunteer slots, and to keep landowners and permit holders confident in you. Everything else is secondary. Right now the blog gets the same prominence as registration, so that's what to change first.

**Who matters most**

You're probably right that runners come first, since entries pay for the races. But I'd rank landowners and permit people second, even though they'll almost never visit. Losing access ends a race, and losing a sponsor only shrinks one. Treat the land page as a small, accurate, always-current page, not a prominent one.

| Audience | What they need from the site | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Runners | Date, distances, cost, register button, course, logistics, results | 1 |
| Landowners and permit people | Who you are, your commitments, rules, a contact | 2 (low traffic, high stakes) |
| Volunteers | What the jobs are, when, how to sign up | 3 |
| Sponsors | What you offer, who came before, who to email | 4 |

**What to have**

1. **Home page:** the next race, its date and a register button, plus the mailing list signup. Nothing else gets equal billing.
2. **Three race pages on one template:** date, location, distances, fee, cutoffs, course, parking and logistics, FAQ, results, and a volunteer section. Each race has one named owner.
3. **One volunteer page:** the general signup and the roles. Each race page links to it or shows its own open slots.
4. **One sponsor page:** what sponsors get, past sponsors, a downloadable one-pager and a contact. If you have per-sponsor pages, fold them into this page as logos and a line each.
5. **One "Land and access" page:** permits held, landowner thanks, trail rules, and a contact. It goes in the footer, not the main nav. Keep it even if nobody reads it.
6. **A results and photos archive:** one page per year, not one per race per year.

**What to fold or drop**

- **Blog:** stop publishing new posts. Keep the few training posts that people actually find, or that fit a specific race, and link them from that race's page. Move the rest out of the nav. Don't delete by traffic alone, because low traffic can just mean nobody can find a post. Archive first, and delete only what is wrong or outdated.
- **Separate sponsor pages and separate volunteer pages per race:** fold them into the single pages above.
- **Anything that duplicates the race page:** point it at the race page instead of maintaining two versions.

The mailing list is your real channel for runners who have raced before. Training tips that still matter are better as an occasional email than as a big archive nobody has time to tend.

**Do this first (about five hours)**

1. **One hour: find out who your runners are.** Pull where entrants say they heard about the race, your top pages, and which pages send people to registration. That will confirm or change my guess that runners come first.
2. **Two hours: fix the next race's page.** Get the date, fee, register link and logistics correct and at the top, and make it the template for the other two.
3. **One hour: reshape the nav and home page.** Race pages and register go first, then volunteer, sponsors and land. Remove the blog from the main nav.
4. **One hour: draft the land and access page** and have whoever holds the permits check it.

Leave the sponsor and volunteer merges and the blog triage for later weeks, at about an hour each.

- **Assumption:** I haven't seen the site, so this is based on the pages you listed. Your page list and traffic could change what stays in the blog.
- **To measure:** the entrant source numbers in step 1 are what would confirm runners really come first.
