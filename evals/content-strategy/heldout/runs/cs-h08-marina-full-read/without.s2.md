# Full-read audit plan: 280 pages, 120 hours

## Time budget
- **Week 1 (10h): Setup and calibration.**
- **Weeks 2–10 (90h): Reading.** That averages about 19 minutes per page.
- **Week 11 (10h): Owner confirmations and a consistency re-check.**
- **Week 12 (10h): Synthesis and report.**

Within the reading weeks, spend the time unevenly:
- **Weeks 2–6, slip and service pages (about 120 pages, 50h, about 25 min each).** These need the most care because the facts (rates, availability, rules) can be wrong.
- **Weeks 7–10, news, area, bios and events (about 160 pages, 40h, about 15 min each).** Many of these will be quick calls.

Reading by type keeps your scoring consistent. It also lets you batch questions for each owner.

## Week 1: Setup
1. **Build the spreadsheet from the sitemap or a crawl export.** Pre-fill everything you can get without reading, so reading time goes to judgment. That means URL, title, page type, owner, last-modified date, word count, and HTTP status.
2. **Add analytics columns if you have access.** Use 12 months of views and entrances. You're reading every page anyway, but traffic helps you prioritize the fixes afterward.
3. **Write the rubric (below) and agree on it with your boss.** Do this before you score anything.
4. **Calibrate.** Score 10 mixed pages, then wait a day and re-score them. Where your scores moved, tighten the rubric wording.
5. **Tell the harbormaster and the events coordinator what's coming.** Agree how they'll receive questions (one batched sheet per week is easiest) and how fast they can answer.

## What to write down for each page
**Pre-filled:**
- Page ID, URL, title
- Page type (slip/service, news, area, bio, event, other)
- Owner (you, harbormaster, events coordinator)
- Last-modified date
- Monthly views, if available

**While reading:**
- **Purpose and audience.** One line on who this page is for and what they should be able to do. If you can't say, that's a finding.
- **Scores** (see rubric).
- **Issues found.** Use a fixed tag list so you can count them later: `outdated`, `wrong-fact`, `unverified-fact`, `broken-link`, `duplicate`, `thin`, `unclear`, `off-brand-tone`, `orphan`, `missing-CTA`, `formatting`, `accessibility`.
- **Evidence.** One short note or quote, such as "rates table says 2023" or "links to the 2022 form". Don't write more than a sentence or two.
- **Recommended action.** Pick one of Keep, Edit, Rewrite, Merge into [ID], Redirect, or Remove.
- **Priority.** High, Medium, or Low, based on risk and traffic.
- **Needs owner check.** Yes or no, plus the specific question.
- **Reviewed date and minutes spent.** The minutes tell you whether you're on pace.

## Scoring rubric
Use a 1–4 scale with no midpoint, so you have to commit. Score each criterion separately and don't average them into one number. Keep the average only as a sort aid.

| Criterion | 4 | 1 |
|---|---|---|
| **Accuracy** | Everything checks out | Contains wrong facts |
| **Currency** | Current, with nothing dated | Stale or refers to past events as upcoming |
| **Usefulness** | Clearly serves a real audience need | No clear audience or purpose |
| **Clarity and quality** | Easy to read and complete | Confusing, thin, or sloppy |
| **Findability and links** | Linked from sensible places, links work | Orphaned or full of dead links |

Give "Accuracy" a score of 2 if you can't verify a fact yourself. Also flag it "needs owner check" rather than guessing.

## Working rules
- **Cap sessions at about 2 hours.** Scoring quality drops with fatigue, and you'll feel it around page 15.
- **Time-box each page.** If a page is taking more than twice its allotment, score what you have, flag it, and move on.
- **Don't fix while you audit.** Record the problem and keep reading.
- **Keep a separate "Owner questions" tab.** Log the page ID, the question, who it's for, and the answer. Send it weekly, so you aren't waiting on answers in week 11.
- **Log duplicates as you see them.** On the later page, note "duplicate of [ID]". Cross-page patterns are easy to miss across 12 weeks.

## Week 11: Quality check
- Close out the owner questions and update the affected scores.
- Re-score 15–20 pages you read in the first weeks, and compare. If your scores drifted, re-read the affected category.
- Make sure no rows have blanks in the required fields.

## Week 12: Output
- **Summary:** score distribution by page type and by criterion, plus the top issue tags with counts.
- **Action list:** pages grouped by action (Remove, Merge, Rewrite, Edit), sorted by priority.
- **Owner handoff lists:** one for the harbormaster's slip and rate pages, one for the events coordinator's pages, each with specific fixes.
- **Appendix:** the full spreadsheet and the rubric.

## Pacing check
You need about 23 pages per week in slip weeks, and about 40 per week in the later weeks. Check the minutes column at the end of each week. If you fall behind, tell your boss early, and shorten the notes on the low-traffic old event pages rather than cutting the number of pages.
