# Full-site audit plan: 280 pages, 12 weeks, about 10 hours a week

## Time budget (about 120 hours)

| Phase | Weeks | Hours | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup and pilot | 1 | 10 | Build the spreadsheet and rubric. Score 15 pages from mixed types to test it. Fix the rubric, then re-score those 15. |
| Reading | 2–11 | 100 | About 28 pages a week. |
| Synthesis and report | 12 | 10 | Summaries, patterns, recommendations, owner sign-offs. |

At 28 pages a week you have about 21 minutes per page on average. That's enough to read carefully. The slip and service pages are probably templated, so budget about 15 minutes each (30 hours). Budget about 22 minutes for the other 160 pages (about 59 hours). That leaves about 11 hours of slack for slow weeks and owner follow-ups.

## Reading order

1. Start with a unique ID for each page, taken from the sitemap (URL plus a number). Add a page-type column: slip/service, news, local area, staff bio, event, other.
2. **Batch by owner and type, not by URL.**
   - Do all slip, rate and service pages in weeks 2–5. Send the harbormaster one consolidated list of questions at the end of week 3 and another at the end of week 5, so you aren't pinging them page by page.
   - Do events in one block, then send the events coordinator the list of pages that look expired or stale.
   - Do the news, local area and bio pages in the remaining weeks.
3. Set a weekly quota (28 pages) and note in the spreadsheet where you stopped each session. If you fall behind, you'll see it by week 3 and not week 11.

## What to write down for each page

**Identity (fill in from the sitemap before you start reading)**
- ID, URL, page title, page type, content owner (you, harbormaster, events coordinator)
- Date audited and a "needs owner input?" flag

**Facts**
- Last updated or published date (as shown on the page, or "none shown")
- Word count (rough is fine)
- Primary purpose of the page, in one phrase
- Intended audience (boater, prospective slip renter, local visitor, member, etc.)
- Internal links in and out, and any broken links you hit
- Any prices, dates, phone numbers, hours or names on the page. Mark each one verified, wrong or unverifiable.

**Scores (1–4 each, with a one-line anchor for each number written in your rubric tab)**
- **Accuracy:** Is the information correct today? 1 means it is wrong or contradicts another page. 4 means you verified it.
- **Currency:** Is it up to date? Old event pages and old news will score low here even when they were accurate when posted.
- **Usefulness:** Does it serve a real visitor need, and does it help someone do something (book, call, find)?
- **Clarity and writing:** Is it readable, free of errors and clear about what to do next?
- **Findability and structure:** Does it have a clear title and headings, and do other pages link to it? Could you find it from the navigation?
- **Consistency:** Do the terms, rates, branding and tone match the rest of the site?

Don't add up the scores into a single number at the page level. Keep them separate so a page with accurate content and bad structure doesn't average out to "fine."

**Decision**
- Disposition: keep as is, edit, merge into [page ID], rewrite, archive or delete, or needs owner decision
- Priority: high, medium or low (based on traffic or business importance and how bad the problems are)
- Notes: one to three sentences on the specific problems, with exact quotes where useful
- Owner question: the text of any question you need to send

## Keeping 280 pages consistent

- **Write the anchors down.** Your rubric tab should say what a 1, 2, 3 and 4 mean for each criterion. By week 8 you'll have forgotten your week 2 standards unless they're written.
- **Calibrate.** Re-score 10 pages from weeks 2–3 at the start of week 7 without looking at your earlier scores. If any score moves by more than one point, find out why and adjust the rubric. Then spot-check the pages scored in between.
- **Use dropdowns** for page type, disposition, priority and the scores, so the data stays filterable.
- **Log cross-page issues in a second tab** when you see them (for example "two different rates for a 30-foot slip" or "same bio on two pages"), with the page IDs involved. These are easy to miss when you read a page at a time, and they're often the most useful findings.

## Using the spreadsheet in weeks 11–12

- Pivot by page type and criterion to see where the weak spots are.
- Filter on high priority plus an accuracy score of 1–2. Those are the pages with the most risk, and the harbormaster should see their list first.
- Count dispositions by type. For example, "N event pages recommended for archiving" is a clear headline for your boss.
- Have each owner confirm the dispositions on their pages before the report goes out.
