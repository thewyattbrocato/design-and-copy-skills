# Gridwalk offline mode: case study plan

## What leads, what supports, what goes

**Lead (first screen and spine)**
- **Before and after screenshots.** They are your strongest visual and the first screen. Show them side by side at the same width, with the faults pinned on the old one: the tiny grey icon, and no sign of what has synced.
- **Usability result (1 of 5 → 4 of 5 understood what had synced).** This is your only outcome, so put it right next to the decision it came from. Label it honestly as a 5-person test. Don't dress it up as a business metric.

**Support (inside the sections, one or two items each)**
- **Interview notes.** Pull 2 or 3 of the sharpest findings from the 6 inspectors, ideally one quote that explains why the grey icon failed. Use these as the "why" inside a decision, not as a research section.
- **Sync conflicts (the Slack thread).** The engineer calling this the hardest part is your best candidate for the hard-problem section. But you haven't told me what you designed for conflicts, so I can't write that section. Fill it in with a before/after of the conflict screen and the trade-off you made. If no design came out of it, mention it briefly under limits.
- **Figma frames.** Pick 3 to 5 that show a decision changing between versions, for example v1 → v3 on the sync state. Use them as evidence of a decision changing, not as a gallery.
- **Competitor teardown.** One or two lines, or a single comparison figure, only where it explains a decision, such as how the other apps signal offline.

**Drop**
- The other 35 or so Figma frames, including every intermediate version that didn't change a decision.
- A separate research, personas, ideation or process-timeline section.
- The full teardown write-up and the full interview notes. Link them or offer them on request.
- Any business impact claim. You have none, so don't imply one.

## Outline

**First screen: Gridwalk offline mode**
- One sentence: a redesign of offline mode in a field-inspection app for utility workers, [platform], [your role, scope]. Add 2 to 4 facts, for example 6 inspectors interviewed, 3 design iterations, 5-person usability test.
- Figure: before and after, same width, with the faults pinned on the old one.
- Caption: what the inspector sees in each version, and in what condition (for example, in the field with no signal).
- Then one short paragraph on why: inspectors couldn't tell what had reached the server. Use one quote here.

**1. A persistent banner replaces the tiny grey icon**
- Claim: the old icon was easy to miss, so inspectors didn't know they were offline.
- Proof: the old header crop and the new banner, plus an interview quote.
- Caption: [what the banner says in each state, and when it appears].

**2. Per-item sync states show what has and hasn't synced**
- Claim: inspectors need to know the state of each record, not just the connection.
- Proof: the item list with sync states, and two or three Figma frames showing how the states evolved across versions.
- Test result goes here: 1 of 5 → 4 of 5 understood what had synced. Caption: 5 participants, [task used], same task before and after.
- Optional: one competitor line if it explains the choice.

**3. [Decision about sync conflicts]**
- Claim: this was the hardest part (cite the engineer's view, paraphrased or quoted with permission).
- Proof: [the conflict screen and the rule you designed, plus what you traded off].
- Cut this section if you didn't design anything for conflicts.

**End**
- Limits:
