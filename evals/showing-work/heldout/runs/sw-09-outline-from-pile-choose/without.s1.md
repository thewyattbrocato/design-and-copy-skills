# Gridwalk offline mode case study

## Lead with these (the spine)

1. **The before/after pair.** The tiny grey icon next to the persistent banner with per-item sync states is your hook. A hiring manager gets the whole project in five seconds.
2. **The usability result (1/5 → 4/5).** This is your only outcome evidence, so put it near the top. Report it as it is: "5 participants, directional, not statistically significant." Stating the limits yourself makes you more credible.
3. **The inspector insight.** Pull one or two sharp findings from the 6 interviews that explain why the grey icon failed. Something like "inspectors couldn't tell if their work was safe" works better than a summary of all the notes. Include a quote or two.
4. **Your design decision-making around sync conflicts.** The engineer's comment that this was the hardest part is the best "design under real constraints" story you have. How you handled it matters more than the final screens. If you can, show how you decided what the user sees when a conflict happens.

## Support (include, but keep small)

- **Figma frames:** pick 6–8 that show the evolution. Show one pivotal change between versions, ideally one that came from the interviews or a test, and explain why you made it. Don't show all three versions evenly.
- **Competitor teardown:** one short section or callout. Say what the two apps did and what gap you found. Skip the full teardown.
- **Slack thread:** don't screenshot it. Paraphrase or quote the engineer in one line as evidence of the technical constraint. Check whether you're allowed to share it.
- **Usability test details:** the task, what "understood" meant, and what the one miss in the after-test looked like. That miss makes a good "what I'd do next" item.

## Drop

- The remaining ~30 frames, including dead ends that don't teach anything.
- Interview notes that don't connect to a design decision.
- The full competitor analysis.
- Any account of process for its own sake ("I did affinity mapping...") unless it produced a finding.

## Outline

1. **Title and one-line summary.** Include your role, the team, the timeframe, and the result. For example: "Redesigned offline mode for Gridwalk so field inspectors can tell what's synced. Comprehension in testing went from 1/5 to 4/5."
2. **Context and problem (short).** Who the inspectors are, why they work offline (utility sites, poor signal), and the stakes of not knowing whether data saved. Put the before screenshot here.
3. **What I learned.** The 2–3 key interview insights, with quotes, plus the one-line competitor gap.
4. **The hard part: sync conflicts.** The constraint, the options you considered, and why you chose what you did. Mention the engineering collaboration.
5. **The design.** The persistent banner and per-item sync states, with the 6–8 curated frames. Show one key iteration and the reason for it.
6. **Results.** The 1/5 → 4/5 comparison, the test method, and the caveats. Say plainly that business metrics aren't available yet.
7. **Reflection and next steps.** What you'd measure once it ships (sync-related support tickets, data-loss reports, duplicate submissions) and what you'd fix based on the one participant who still got it wrong.

## Two notes

- **Be honest about the metrics gap.** "No business metrics yet" is fine if you name what you'd track. Don't imply impact you can't show.
- **Be clear about your role.** Say what you did versus what the team did, since hiring managers look for that.
