## Findings, in order of cost to the reader

1. **The first screen shows no work.** A title, then "About me," then company history means a reviewer skimming for 30 seconds sees nothing you designed. Open with the title, one plain sentence saying what the dashboard is, two to four concrete facts, and your single strongest screen.

2. **The sections follow the calendar, not the decisions.** "Research → Wireframes → Final design" is the order everyone's case study uses, so it doesn't distinguish you. Seniority shows in which two or three decisions you made and why. Each section title should state a decision, such as "Pause and skip without calling support."

3. **"Users were frustrated" is the weakest line in the outline.** It's a conclusion with no evidence. Replace it with what people said or did, and what that changed in the design. Pair each finding with the screen it produced.

4. **The five-phase diagram is filler.** It says nothing about this project and can come out. If your process was unusual, show that inside the decision it explains.

5. **Three paragraphs of company history are backstory.** Cut them to one sentence of context. The reader needs why the project mattered, not when Orchard & Vine was founded.

6. **"Lots of wireframes" and "6 screenshots in a gallery" are runs of images with no claims.** Every figure needs a claim right before it and a caption that adds a fact, such as the condition or the platform. Keep only the wireframes that show a decision being made, such as a rejected option next to the chosen one. The final screens should be a before/after, with each fault pinned on the old version.

7. **"I learned the importance of user-centered design" is generic.** At senior level, readers want tradeoffs, what you'd do differently, what's still limited, and how you worked with PM, engineering, and stakeholders. Put your role and scope in the case study too, since the outline never says what you owned.

8. **"Thank you" wastes the ending.** Replace it with limits, credits, and one action, such as getting in touch.

9. **"About me" doesn't belong in the case study.** Put a one-line version in the footer or on your portfolio index. The wine-love detail is fine as a brief human note there.

10. **"Elevating the Wine Club Experience" is vague.** Name what changed, for example "Orchard & Vine wine club dashboard: [what members can now do]."
