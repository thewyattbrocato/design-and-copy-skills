**Arrangement**

Put the brief or problem in one line above the figure. Then use two figures, each a full 720px column. Side by side would give you about 350px each, which is too small to read form fields.

1. **Before, as a 3×2 grid of the six steps.**
   - Each cell is about 230px wide, so the six screens read as one sequence.
   - Crop each step to its top screenful instead of the full-page height. Mark the cut with a torn or faded bottom edge.
   - Use the same crop height for all six, with a small step number ("1 of 6") in each corner. Tall strips would make the reader scroll and lose the count.
   - Keep the sidebar in the crop, since it's one of the things that changed.
2. **After, as one image at the full 720px.**
   - Crop it to the three parts: the date strip, the time list and the short form. Trim any empty space between them.
   - This image is wider than each before cell, which is fine. The contrast you want the reader to see is six pages against one, not pixel scale.
   - Put a thin "Before" or "After" label on each figure, in the same style and position.

Leave more space below each figure than above it, and reserve the image boxes in the page so nothing jumps as they load.

**What to annotate**

Use a few words per note, pinned to the spot on the screenshot, with numbered dots or short leader lines. Annotate the old version more heavily, because that's where the reader has to find the faults.

- **Before:**
  - Note the count itself ("6 pages to book").
  - Note where the sidebar promos sit relative to the main action.
  - Pin down which step asks for what, for example "date on step [n], time on step [n], details on step [n]". Fill these in from your own screens.
  - Mark any place where the user can't see what they chose earlier, if that's true.
- **After:**
  - Label the three regions: date strip, time list, details form.
  - Add one note where the choices stay visible together on the page, if they do.
  - Note that the promos are gone, only if that's a deliberate decision.

Write the captions as plain observations: "Booking is one page; the six steps and the sidebar are gone." Stick to what a reader can verify by looking.

**What to leave out**

- Any claim of improvement: faster, easier, higher conversion, fewer drop-offs. You have no data, so nothing in the labels, captions or headings should imply it. If you want a slot for it later, use a bracket like `[result: how it was measured, and the number]`.
- Browser chrome, device frames, and the site header and footer. They add height and no information.
- The full-length tall screenshots. Link to them or put them in an appendix if you want them available.
- Any real patient names or details in the forms. Swap in dummy data.
- Decorative arrows, "vs" graphics, and annotations on parts that didn't change.
- Steps you can't annotate. If a before screen has no fault worth pinning, show it without a note rather than inventing one.

The reader should be able to count six, see one, and spot the sidebar, without a number from you.
