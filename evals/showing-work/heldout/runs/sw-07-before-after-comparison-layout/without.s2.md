**Stack Before above After, and give the After the full 720px. Don't put them side by side.** Two columns would leave about 350px each, which is too small to read a form or a date strip.

## Arrangement

1. **Before: a 3×2 grid of the six steps.**
   - Each tile is about 225px wide with a small gutter.
   - Crop every screen to its first viewport-height (roughly the top 600–800px of the page), not the full-page capture.
   - Keep them in order, with a number badge on each tile: 1 to 6.
   - The grid shows "six separate pages" at a glance. The text won't be readable at this size, which is fine because the grid only needs to show the structure.
2. **After: one image at full 720px width.**
   - If the page is only moderately tall, show all of it.
   - If it's very tall, crop to date strip, time list and form, and drop anything below that.
   - A single large image against a grid of small ones also shows the change in scale.
3. **One readable Before close-up (optional).**
   - Pick the step that best shows the sidebar promos and show it at full width, cropped to about 500px tall.
   - This is the only place the reader needs to see the Before in detail.
   - Make each grid tile click through to its full-size image.

## What to annotate

- **Before:**
  - The step numbers on the grid.
  - A bracket or outline around the promo sidebar on the close-up, with a label such as "Promo sidebar, present on every step". Check that it really is on every step before you write that.
  - A short caption per tile, such as "Step 3: choose time". This tells the reader what each page was for.
- **After:**
  - Three labels for the regions: "Date strip", "Time list" and "Details form".
  - Small tags showing where each old step's content now lives, for example "was steps 1–2" on the date strip. This is what lets the reader see what changed, so it matters more than any other annotation.
  - A note where the sidebar used to be, such as "Promo sidebar removed".
- **Annotation style:** use one accent color for all annotations and keep it separate from the UI's own colors. Use outlines and short labels, not heavy arrows.
- **Headline facts:** you can state "6 pages → 1 page" and "promo sidebar removed". These are structural and don't need data.

## What to leave out

- Full-length scrolling captures of any screen. They shrink to unreadable slivers at 720px.
- Browser chrome, and repeated headers and footers.
- Any claim of faster booking, fewer drop-offs or better conversion. Without data, describe what changed and let the reader judge. You can add a placeholder note like "results to follow" if you plan to measure later.
- Annotations on every element. Label only the regions that carry the story.
- Differences in test content. Use the same sample date, service and viewport width in both so the comparison is fair.
