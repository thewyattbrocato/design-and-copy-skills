# Before/after slide: Parcelbox collect screen

## Title (states the takeaway)
**One screen, one obvious action: we turned a two-way choice into a single next step**

Alternative: *"Collecting a parcel went from 'which option do I use?' to 'scan your code.'"*

## Layout
Two screenshots side by side, with "Before" on the left and "After" on the right. Give them equal size so the comparison is fair. Put a short label above each and one line of caption below.

**Before** (caption: *Two competing methods, equal weight*)
- 12-digit code pad
- QR scanner option
- Users had to decide which to use

**After** (caption: *One primary action, one fallback*)
- Large "Scan your QR code" target
- Small "Enter code instead" link

## Annotations
Add two or three callouts on the screenshots, with arrows or circles:
1. On Before: circle the code pad and the QR option together, labeled **"Two equal choices."**
2. On After: circle the big scan target, labeled **"Primary action is unmistakable."**
3. On After: circle the small link, labeled **"Code entry kept as a fallback."**

The third callout matters. It shows you didn't remove a capability, only demoted it, and design leads will likely ask about that.

## Footer line (small text)
*Kiosk usage data not yet available. We'll measure scan vs. manual-entry rate and time-to-collect after launch.*

This is better than leaving the gap unmentioned. It shows you know what success looks like, and it answers the "did it work?" question before someone asks it.

## Speaker notes
> "This is the collect-your-parcel screen on the Parcelbox kiosk touchscreen. Before, we showed a 12-digit code pad and a QR scanner option side by side. Both were presented as equals, and people often didn't know which one to use. That hesitation happens at the exact moment someone wants to grab their parcel and go.
>
> The redesign makes one decision for them. The screen is now a big 'Scan your QR code' target. Manual entry is still there as a small 'Enter code instead' link, so nobody who can't scan is stuck, but it no longer competes for attention.
>
> The principle is to reduce choice at the point of action and keep the fallback available but quiet.
>
> I don't have kiosk usage data yet, so I'm not claiming a result. Once it's live, I'll look at the share of people who scan vs. enter a code, how long collection takes, and how often people abandon or back out. If the design works, scanning should dominate and time-to-collect should drop. I'm happy to talk through what would change my mind."

## Things to check before Thursday
- **Where does "people often didn't know which to use" come from?** If it came from observation, support tickets, or user testing, add a short source tag on the slide, such as "Observed in usability sessions." If it's your own judgment, say "we believed" in the notes. Leads will probe this, and a named source is stronger than an unsourced claim.
- **Keep the screenshots uncluttered.** Crop out any device bezels or irrelevant UI so the difference reads in about three seconds.
- **Expect this question:** "What about people who can't or won't scan?" Your answer is the fallback link. Have a sentence ready on its size and tap-target accessibility.
