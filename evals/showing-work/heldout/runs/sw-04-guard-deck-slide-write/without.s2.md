## Speaker notes (about 60-90 seconds)

"This is the collect-your-parcel screen on the Parcelbox kiosk, before and after the redesign.

Before, the screen offered two ways to collect: a 12-digit code pad and a QR scanner. Both had equal visual weight, so people often didn't know which one to use. That hesitation happens at the start of the task, at a kiosk with a queue behind them.

The redesign makes one decision for them. The screen now shows a single large 'Scan your QR code' target, because that's the fastest path for most people. Manual entry is still there as a small 'Enter code instead' link, so nobody is locked out if their phone is dead or the QR isn't available.

The principle was to give people one obvious next step and keep the alternative reachable but quiet.

I don't have kiosk usage data yet, so I'm not claiming results today. The next step is to measure it: how often people use the fallback, how long it takes to start collecting, and where they abandon the task."

## Tips
- **Don't imply results.** Words like "improved," "faster," or "fixed" will invite the question "what's the data?" "Clearer" and "simplified" describe the design intent, which is what you can support.
- **Check the "most people" claim.** The notes say QR is the fastest path for most people. If that's your assumption and not something you know, say "we're betting QR is the common path" instead.
- **Keep the callouts short.** Design leads will look at the screenshots first, so the text should only point at what to notice.
- **Be ready for the likely questions.** Expect "What about people without a smartphone?" (the fallback link) and "How will you know it worked?" (the metrics in your last paragraph).
