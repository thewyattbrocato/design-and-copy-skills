Returns that start from the order

I redesigned the returns flow for Kestrel Outfitters, an online camping gear shop. Customers can now start a return from their account and know up front whether an item is eligible. Return-request form completion went from 37% to 68%.

  • Web, online retail
  • 10 weeks, 2025
  • Sole designer, with 1 PM and 2 engineers
  • Replaced an email-to-support process

Why

Returns began with an email, and customers couldn't tell if they qualified

To start a return, customers had to find a "Contact us" form in the footer and email support. In the support tickets I read, the most common complaint was not knowing whether an item was eligible. Everything below follows from that.

Decision 1

Start returns from each order, and check eligibility per item up front

I put "Return an item" on every order in the account page. Eligibility is checked per item before the customer fills anything in, instead of at the end. The customer sees the answer they were missing before they spend any effort.

Decision 2

One tap plus an optional note, replacing a nine-option dropdown

The old reason dropdown had nine options, and most answers were "Other". A field that mostly collects "Other" asks for effort and returns almost nothing. I cut it to a single tap, with a free-text note for anyone who has more to say.

Decision 3

A prepaid label and drop-off map on the confirmation screen, not in a later email

Once a return is requested, the customer wants to know what to do next. The confirmation screen now shows the prepaid label and a drop-off map immediately, so they don't have to wait for an email to take the next step.

Result

Return-request form completion rose from 37% to 68%

More than half of customers who start the return-request form now finish it, up from about a third. I measured this over the six weeks after launch, using the PM's dashboard.

I can't separate the three decisions from each other. They shipped together, so I can't say how much of the gain came from each.
