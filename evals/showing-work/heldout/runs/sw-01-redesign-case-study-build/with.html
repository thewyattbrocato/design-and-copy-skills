Case study · Kestrel Outfitters · 2025

A returns flow customers can finish without emailing anyone

I redesigned how customers start a return at Kestrel Outfitters, an online camping gear shop. Returns moved from a footer contact form into each order in the account page, with eligibility shown per item before the customer fills anything in.

  • Sole designer, with a PM (Daria) and two engineers
  • About 10 weeks, 2025
  • Web, mid-size shop
  • Form completion 37% → 68%

Customers couldn't tell if they could return something

To start a return, customers had to email support. The way in was a "Contact us" form buried in the footer. In the support tickets I read, the most common complaint was not knowing whether an item was eligible at all.

That complaint shaped all three decisions below: tell people early, ask for less, and hand over everything they need in one place.

1Put returns on each order and check eligibility per item up front

A return belongs to an order, so the action lives there. Eligibility is checked for every item before the customer starts, not at the end, so nobody fills in a form only to be told no.

2Replace the nine-option reason dropdown with one tap and an optional note

The old dropdown had nine options, and most answers were "Other", so the field cost effort and told us little. One tap on a short list, plus an optional note, keeps the step quick and still lets people explain.

3Show the prepaid label and drop-off map on the confirmation screen

Previously the label came later by email. Now the confirmation screen is the last step: the customer sees the prepaid label and where to drop the parcel immediately, with nothing left to wait for.

The return-request form was completed far more often

In the six weeks after launch, completion of the return-request form rose from 37% to 68%. The figures come from the PM's dashboard.

This is the one number I have. I can't say which of the three decisions moved it most, since they shipped together.
