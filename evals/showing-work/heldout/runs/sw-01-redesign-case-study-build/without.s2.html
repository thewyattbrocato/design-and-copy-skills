Case study · E-commerce UX

Making returns something customers can start on their own

I redesigned the returns flow for Kestrel Outfitters, a mid-size online camping gear shop. Return-request form completion went from 37% to 68%.

Role
Sole product designer
Team
Daria (PM), two engineers
Timeline
About 10 weeks, 2025
Client
Kestrel Outfitters

The problem

To start a return at Kestrel, customers had to email support. The way in was a “Contact us” form buried in the site footer, so the process started with a hunt for the right page and ended in a wait for a reply.

I read through support tickets to see what was going wrong. The most common complaint was that customers didn't know whether an item was eligible for return. They were committing to a process without knowing if it would work for them.

Before

  • Find footer
  • “Contact us” form
  • Email support
  • Learn eligibility

After

  • Account page
  • “Return an item” on the order
  • Eligibility shown per item
  • One-tap reason
  • Label + map

Three design decisions

Each decision targeted a specific point where the old flow lost people or made them wait.

1
Entry point & eligibility

Put “Return an item” on every order, and check eligibility up front

The return action now lives on each order in the account page, where customers already look when something needs sorting out. Eligibility is checked per item at the start, so customers learn whether a return is possible before they invest any effort. This addresses the top complaint in the tickets directly.

BeforeFooter form; eligibility unclear until support replied
AfterAction on each order; eligibility checked per item at the start
2
Reducing effort

Replace the 9-option reason dropdown with one tap and an optional note

The old dropdown had nine options, and most responses were “Other”. That meant the field cost customers effort without producing useful categories. A single tap, plus an optional note for anyone who wants to explain, keeps the step quick and lets people say it in their own words.

Before9-option dropdown, mostly “Other”
AfterOne tap, plus an optional note
3
Closing the loop

Show the prepaid label and drop-off map on the confirmation screen

Rather than emailing the label later, the confirmation screen gives customers what they need to finish the return right away: a prepaid label and a map of drop-off locations. The flow ends with a next step instead of a wait.

BeforeLabel sent by email afterward
AfterLabel and drop-off map on the confirmation screen

Result

In the six weeks after launch, the completion rate of the return-request form rose from 37% to 68%.
