Case study · Product design · 2025

Returns that start in the account page

I redesigned how Kestrel Outfitters customers start a return, replacing an emailed support request with a three-step flow built into each order.

  • Online camping gear shop, mid-size
  • ~10 weeks, 2025
  • Sole designer, with 1 PM and 2 engineers
  • Form completion 37% → 68%

Before this, a customer who wanted to return something had to find a “Contact us” form in the footer and wait for support to reply. Reading support tickets, I found the most common complaint wasn’t the wait itself but not knowing whether an item was eligible at all. The three decisions below follow from that.

Decision 1

Return from the order, and check eligibility per item first

The old path started in the footer, so customers began a request without knowing whether it could succeed. I put “Return an item” on each order in the account page and moved the eligibility check to the start, per item, instead of the end.

Decision 2

One tap for the reason, with an optional note

The old form asked for a reason from a nine-option dropdown, and most people picked “Other.” A field that mostly returns one answer isn’t collecting information, so I replaced it with a single tap and left room for a short note.

Decision 3

Prepaid label and drop-off map on the confirmation screen

Previously the label arrived by email after the request, which left a gap between asking and being able to act. Now the confirmation screen is the finish line: the label and a map of drop-off points appear as soon as the request is submitted.

Result

Form completion rose from 37% to 68%

In the six weeks after launch, completion of the return-request form went from 37% to 68%. I can’t separate the three decisions from each other, so I treat this as the result of the flow as a whole.

What this doesn’t tell you
