I won't write these as asked, because they would be fake reviews. The posts would present invented customer experiences, a weight-loss result nobody has had, and a "4.9 stars from 2,000+ reviews" rating that doesn't exist, all posted from accounts that hide the company connection. In the US that breaks the FTC's rules on fake reviews and on disclosing material connections. The rules cover friends and employees posting, and fines can be significant. Health claims like "lost 12 pounds" add false-advertising exposure. It's also a bad bet for a launch: one screenshot of "4.9 stars" on a company that hasn't shipped a box could undo the trust you're trying to build.

You can still get an organic-feeling launch without any of that. Here's a version that holds up.

## Campaign idea: "The First 100 Boxes"

Make the fact that you're new the selling point. You haven't shipped yet, so invite people into the first batch. Early adopters get a founding-member perk, and the team and friends post openly as the people behind it.

**How it works**
- Recruit about 100 first-box testers from friends, family and a waitlist. Give them a free or discounted first box in exchange for honest feedback.
- Ask them to post what they actually think, with a clear disclosure (#gifted or #ad, or "I work at Fennel & Fig").
- Collect real reviews from those boxes, then use real numbers once you have them.

## 8 launch posts (honest, disclosed)

1. **Team post:** "I work at Fennel & Fig, and in two weeks we ship our first box. I've tasted every recipe, and the roasted fig & fennel chicken is the one I'd make on a Tuesday. Here's what's coming 👇"
2. **Founder story:** "We started Fennel & Fig because weeknight dinner shouldn't mean choosing between takeout and a 14-ingredient recipe. Two weeks to launch. Nervous, excited, mostly caffeinated."
3. **Behind the scenes:** "Test kitchen day. Recipe #7 failed spectacularly. Recipe #8 is the one you'll get. (I'm on the team, so yes, I'm biased.)"
4. **Waitlist invite:** "We're packing our first 100 boxes and I have a few spots for people who want to try them early and tell us what's wrong. Comment or DM for a spot. Founding members get [perk]."
5. **Ingredient spotlight:** "Why fennel? Because roasted fennel gets sweet and a little caramelized, and it makes almost anything taste like you tried harder than you did."
6. **Friend post (gifted):** "My friend started a meal kit company and sent me a preview recipe card to try. Honest take coming after I cook it tonight. #gifted"
7. **Packing day:** "Box #1 is packed. Photo of the actual first box, slightly crooked label and all. Shipping in X days."
8. **Launch day:** "We're live. If you were one of our first 100 testers, thank you. Your feedback changed the recipes. If you're new, here's how to try a box: [link]."

## When you have real reviews

After the first boxes ship, ask testers for ratings, then use the real number and count, such as "4.8 from 112 early reviews." Skip any weight-loss or health claims unless you can substantiate them. Describing the food (flavor, time, ease) is safe.
