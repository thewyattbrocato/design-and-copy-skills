I won't write these as described. Fake customer posts from friends and staff, a weight-loss result nobody had, and "4.9 stars from 2,000+ reviews" before you've shipped a box are deceptive advertising. The FTC's rules on fake reviews and endorsements allow civil penalties per violation. They also require staff and friends to disclose their connection. Instagram or a journalist could also trace the posts back to you, which would hurt a launch more than a quiet one would.

You can still get the organic feel without the risk. Here's a version that works.

## Campaign idea: "First Box"

Make the launch a story about the first 100 households. People trust "we're new and doing this in public" more than polished social proof, and you can't be caught out on it.

**Mechanics**
- Run a waitlist or founding-member offer with a limited number of spots.
- Send free first boxes to real people, including friends, and ask them to post honestly. They don't have to say anything positive. They just have to disclose, with "#gifted" or "Got a free box from Fennel & Fig."
- Ask team members to post as themselves, with something like "I work at Fennel & Fig."
- After real customers have reviewed you, use your actual rating and count.

## 8 posts you can use now

**1. Founder, pre-launch**
We're two weeks from shipping our first Fennel & Fig box. I've tested this menu about 40 times and I'm still arguing with myself about the harissa carrots. Waitlist link in bio. First 100 households get a founding-member discount.

**2. Team member (disclosed)**

**3. Behind the scenes**

**4. Menu tease**
Week 1 menu, first look: lemon-fennel chicken, fig and goat cheese flatbread, crispy chickpea bowl. Which one are you ordering?

**5. Gifted-box invite (for friends)**
Template: "Fennel & Fig sent me an early box (#gifted). Here's what I made, how long it took, and what I'd change." Honest takes only, good or bad.

**6. Sourcing**
Where our fennel comes from, who grows it, and why we picked them. Short video from the farm.

**7. Question post**
What's the part of weeknight dinner you hate most: planning, shopping, or cleanup? We're building the first menus around your answers.

**8. Countdown**
10 days. Waitlist is at [X] people. We'll post the first real customer photos the day boxes land.
