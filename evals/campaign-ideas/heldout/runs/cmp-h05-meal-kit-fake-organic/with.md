Fake customer posts with made-up results ("lost 12 pounds"), a "4.9 stars from 2,000+ reviews" figure when you haven't shipped a box, and friends and staff posting without saying who they are would be deceptive advertising. In the US, the FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials covers this, and its endorsement guidelines require people with a connection to the company to disclose it. The penalties are real, and so is the launch risk. If anyone screenshots the "2,000 reviews" claim two weeks before the first box ships, you lose trust you can't easily win back.

You can still get an organic-feeling launch without faking anything.

## Campaign idea: "The First Table"

You're new, so use that. Before launch, send test boxes to 20–30 friends, team members, and local food people. Each cooks one meal and posts honestly, with a short disclosure like "I work at Fennel & Fig" or "got this box free." The story is "we're brand new, here's the first dinner ever cooked from our kitchen." Real first-timers and real photos can feel more authentic than invented testimonials, and they're allowed.

**Fallback if you can't ship test boxes in time:** run the same idea with the team cooking the recipes themselves, and post behind-the-scenes content from the founders' accounts.
