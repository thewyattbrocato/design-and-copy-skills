# Part 1: Four Numbers, One Practice

## Email version

**Subject: Your practice has four "revenue" numbers. Which one are you watching?**

Ask a dentist how the practice did last month and you'll usually hear one number. Sometimes it's production. Sometimes it's what hit the bank. Sometimes it's a feeling.

The trouble is that a dental practice has four different numbers that all sound like "revenue," and they can be far apart.

**1. Gross production**
This is everything the practice billed at its full fee schedule. It's the number on the scheduling software's dashboard, and the biggest and most flattering one.

**2. Adjusted (net) production**
This is gross production minus the contractual write-downs. If your fee for a crown is $1,400 but the PPO has agreed to $950, you will never collect that other $450. It was never really revenue.

**3. Collections**
This is the money actually received from patients and insurers. It trails production because of timing, outstanding claims, and unpaid patient balances.

**4. Take-home**
This is what's left after supplies, lab fees, payroll, rent, and everything else, and it's what the owner can actually spend or reinvest.

Here's an illustrative month (made-up numbers, but the shape is typical):

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross production | $120,000 |
| Less insurance write-downs | –$28,000 |
| **Adjusted production** | **$92,000** |
| Collections | $88,000 |
| Overhead | –$61,000 |
| **Take-home** | **$27,000** |

The practice "produced" $120,000, and the owner kept $27,000. Neither number is wrong. They answer different questions.

**Why this matters**

- **Production tells you how busy you are.** It's a good measure of the schedule, but a poor measure of income.
- **The write-down gap tells you what your insurance contracts cost.** If it grows from 20% to 25% of gross production, you've lost money without seeing a single slow day.
- **The gap between adjusted production and collections tells you about your front desk.** If it keeps widening, money is getting stuck in claims or patient balances.
- **Take-home is the only number that pays your mortgage.**

**What to do this week**

Pull last month's figures for all four numbers. Most practice management software will give you the first three in a standard report, and your books have the fourth. Write them down in one column.

Then do the same for the previous two months. You aren't looking for a perfect answer, just for which gap moved.

→ Production to adjusted: what your insurance contracts cost you
→ Adjusted to collections: how well money is getting collected
