**The idea I'd pick:** Everyone tells us they're not creative before they sign up. 31 of 46 came back for more.

It starts from what you said people email you, and the retention number is the proof. It doesn't promise they'll become creative. It only says what happened.

**Other ways to go**
- **Fast kiln:** "Back in a week, not three." The new kiln means you see what you made while you still care about it. This is the strongest practical hook.
- **Eight chairs:** The campaign is the eight seats, with a visible count of how many are taken. It only works if you update the count honestly.
- **Six Tuesdays:** Sell the weekly ritual, 6:30 to 9, as time that isn't a screen or a commute.
- **Four pieces:** "You'll leave with four things you made," with the price, clay and firing included as the whole story.
- **Above the hardware store:** Lean on the Dunmore Street location, with parking behind the building, as the odd, findable place.

**The pick in three forms**
1. **Post or flyer:** The line "Most of our students start by telling us they're not creative," then "31 of 46 signed up again." Below it: six Tuesdays, 8 seats, $240 with clay and 4 fired pieces, starts Oct 21.
2. **Street sign:** A sidewalk sign or door card at the street entrance: "Not creative? Come up." [Check that the entrance suits this.]
3. **Registration form:** An optional field: "Finish this sentence: I'm not creative because ___." With the writer's consent, the answers could become later posts, credited or anonymous as they choose. [Add: consent wording.]

Each form works alone. The second and third need nothing but your sign and your form.

**Plain fallback:** A facts-first post: six Tuesday evenings, 8 seats, $240 including clay and 4 fired pieces, a second kiln so pieces come back in about a week, and the location and parking.

**Ask the owner:**
- Is "31 of 46" the right way to describe spring? I assumed it means students who signed up for the next session.
- Do you have permission to use any student's words? I haven't used any.
