**My pick: answer the email everyone sends.** Most people write "I'm not creative" before they sign up, so the campaign is your reply to that sentence.

**The idea:** "You're not creative? Good. Here's what happens on Tuesday." Every piece answers the objection people already say out loud. Most skill ads skip it and promise creativity.

**Three forms**
- **A reply post or email to the list.** It opens with the line you hear most, anonymous and unattributed, and then answers it plainly. Sample: *"A lot of you write to say you're not creative. Nobody has to be. Six Tuesday evenings, 8 seats, clay and firing included."* It ends with the date, price and sign-up link.
- **A sign at the street door** under the hardware store: *"Upstairs: 8 people who said they weren't creative, making bowls."* This only works if it's true when you put it up. Reword it if it isn't.
- **A week-two message to students:** *"Your first pieces are back from the kiln."* Pieces now return in about a week, not three, so students hold something finished early in the class. Use that message as the post for the next session's registration.

**Ask the owner:**
- Are you comfortable paraphrasing the emails without names? Get written permission before quoting anyone verbatim.
- Is the "8 people who said they weren't creative" sign true? Check with this fall's sign-ups.

**Other ways to go**
- **"Back in a week."** The second kiln means finished pieces in about a week instead of three. It's the most practical angle, and it works well as a supporting message.
- **"31 of 46 came back."** Two-thirds of last spring's students re-enrolled. It's honest proof and works as a one-line closer. On its own it reads as a stat, not an idea.
- **"Hardware downstairs, hands upstairs."** This is a local angle built on Dunmore Street, the building and the parking behind it. It's charming but doesn't address hesitation.
- **"Eight seats."** A minimalist poster with eight stools or eight bowls and the dates. It's clean, but it relies on scarcity and says little else.

**Plain fallback:** A straightforward announcement of dates, Tuesdays 6:30 to 9 from Oct 21, $240, 8 seats, clay and up to 4 fired pieces included, and the new kiln. Add "31 of 46 students re-enrolled last spring" as the proof line.

**Two notes**
- By my calendar Oct 21 has already passed. I used it as you gave it, so double-check the start date before anything goes out.
- Avoid promising that anyone "will discover their creativity." You haven't told me that, and the reply works better if it promises only what you can verify.
