# Kilnside fall wheel-throwing campaign: three directions

## A. "Not creative? Good." (my pick)
Most of your students start with the same objection, and the typical ad ignores it. This campaign says it out loud.

- **Headline:** "You're not creative. Come throw anyway."
- **Alternate:** "'I'm not creative' is the most common email we get. They sign up anyway."
- **Format:** Short posts or posters built from the real wording of those emails, with names removed or permission obtained. Each one ends with the same detail line: *Six Tuesdays, 6:30–9. 8 seats. $240, clay and firing included.*
- **Proof line:** "31 of last spring's 46 students signed up again."
- **Why it works:** It's true to what you actually hear, so it won't read like generic "learn a new skill" copy. It also speaks to the hesitation people have before they email you.
- **Risk:** The tone can sound like a dare. Keep it warm.

## B. "Back in a week"
This one leads with the second kiln. Pottery's usual complaint is waiting about three weeks to see your work. Now it's about one.

- **Headline:** "Throw it Tuesday. Hold it next week."
- **Format:** A simple before-and-after image, such as a wet pot and then the finished one, with "about a week, not three" as the caption.
- **Why it works:** It's concrete and new, since the kiln was added this summer. It also suits a six-week class, because students see results mid-course.
- **Risk:** It's a feature, not a feeling. It's also less compelling to someone who's never touched clay and doesn't yet know why the wait matters.

## C. "Eight seats"
This direction treats the class as a small, specific room rather than a product.

- **Headline:** "Eight people. Six Tuesdays. One room above the hardware store."
- **Format:** A quiet, local, almost-secret feel. Mention Dunmore Street and the parking behind the building.
- **Why it works:** The scarcity is real, because there's one class this fall with 8 seats. The location detail gives it character.
- **Risk:** Scarcity alone is the most common ad move. It works best as a supporting line, not the main idea.

## What I'd do
Run **A** as the main campaign, with **B** as a follow-up post or email once people are interested, and **C**'s seat count as the closing nudge ("8 seats, one class this fall"). The "not creative" line is the one thing in your notes a competitor couldn't copy, and the 31-of-46 return rate backs it up. People who thought they weren't creative mostly came back.

## Two things to check before launch
1. **The date.** Oct 21 falls on a Wednesday this year, but the class is on Tuesdays. Is the first class Oct 20 or Oct 21? Registration also opens next week, so the window before the start is short. The campaign will probably need to run as a tight 1–2 week push.
2. **Permission.** If you quote the emails in A, get each person's OK or paraphrase them.
