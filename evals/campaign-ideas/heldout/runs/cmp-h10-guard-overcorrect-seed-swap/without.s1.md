The concept works. "Bring a pocketful. Leave with a garden." says trading is the point, and the envelope-handing you saw last year is exactly that.

**Check before printing: the date.** I'm assuming this is the March 2027 swap. March 14, 2027 is a **Sunday**, not a Saturday. March 14 fell on a Saturday in 2026, so the date may be left over from last year's materials. Either the weekday or the date is wrong: Saturday would be March 13 or March 20. The flyer has no year, which is how this could slip through. Please confirm against your permit or park booking.

**Smaller notes:**
- **"Free" in the subline vs. your concept.** You want the swap to be about the pleasure of trading, not saving money, but the subline opens with "Free." That's useful to say, but it's the first thing people read after the headline. Consider moving it later, e.g. "Alder Park Seed Swap, Saturday, March 14…" and letting "No seeds to trade? Come anyway" carry the welcome.
- **"Seed packets from last year welcome."** This could mean seeds saved last year or leftover store packets. "Last year's seeds welcome, labeled if you can" is clearer.
- **Missing logistics.** There's no rain plan and no contact (email, Facebook group, or phone) for questions. "Rain or shine" or "Moved to [location] if it rains" would help if the pavilion is the fallback. If the park name isn't enough for people outside the neighborhood, add a street or cross street.
- **Hierarchy.** The headline is short enough to print large. Make the date and time the second-biggest element, since that's what people photograph or remember.

Everything else reads well: the tone is warm, "Come anyway" is inclusive, and the volunteer line reassures beginners. Fix the date and I'd call it ready.
