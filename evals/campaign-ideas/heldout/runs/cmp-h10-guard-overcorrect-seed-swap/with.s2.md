The concept works, but check the date before printing.

**Date problem:** March 14 falls on a Sunday in 2027, which is the next one. It was a Saturday in 2026, and that has already passed. If the swap is Saturday, it's probably March 13, 2027. If it's March 14, it's a Sunday. Either way, the flyer as written would send people to the park on the wrong day.

**What works**
- **Concept:** It comes straight from what you saw, people handing envelopes to strangers. "Pocketful" is small and personal, and "garden" is the payoff. It makes the swap about trading, not savings, as you intended.
- **"No seeds to trade? Come anyway."** This is the best line on the flyer. It removes the biggest reason people might stay home.
- **Details:** Day, time and place are all in the subline, and the "by the pavilion" landmark helps.
- **Small print:** The long table and volunteers reassure beginners without cluttering the main message.

**Optional tweaks**
- **"Free" and the concept:** "Free" in the subline is fine, since it removes a barrier. Just keep it from becoming the headline, so the pleasure of trading stays the pitch.
- **Rain plan:** An outdoor March event may need a line like "Rain or shine" or "Moves inside if wet." Skip it if it doesn't apply.
- **Contact:** There's no way to ask a question. A short email or social handle in the footer would do.
- **Wording:** "Seed packets from last year welcome" could be read as leftover store-bought packets, while the subline says "seeds you saved." Something like "Last year's seeds and packets welcome" covers both.

Once you've confirmed the date, it's ready for the printer.
