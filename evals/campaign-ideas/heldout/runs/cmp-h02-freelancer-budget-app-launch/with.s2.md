**Idea:** Budget what you've banked, not the month you're in.

The buyer truth is that every budgeting app assumes a paycheck on the 1st, and a freelancer's money arrives when a client finally pays. [assumed: check this against what your beta users said.] The campaign is about that mismatch, and Tally Nest is the app that stops pretending.

**Three forms** (each works without the others)

1. **A post series, "Net 30 (ha)."** Each post takes a line freelancers already say or hear, like "Net 30 means net 60" or "I'll pay it Friday," and sets it against the standard budget app's "Your monthly budget: $X." The caption is one line: *Your income doesn't run on a calendar. Your budget shouldn't either.* Any example numbers are labeled as illustrative.

2. **A product moment: the "Banked" number.** The app opens on one figure, what you've actually banked so far, and what it covers. Screenshot that screen for the App Store and the launch video. If you have time to build it, add a share card the user chooses to post. [add: confirm this matches the real home screen.]

3. **A launch-day note from the two of you.** Write it as a short founder message to your beta list, and post it publicly too. It says why you built it, who it's for, and what it costs: free for a month, then $6 a month. [add: your real reason for building it.] Put the price up front. Freelancers will trust it more if they don't have to hunt for it.

**Ask the owner:**
- Can you get two to five beta users to give a short quote you can credit? Get their consent first, and name them or label them as beta users. Without beta numbers, don't claim any results.
- Do you have time before Nov 3 to build the share card? If not, form 2 is only the screenshot.

**Plain fallback:** a straight launch post: *Tally Nest is a budgeting app for freelancers. It works from what you've actually banked, not a monthly paycheck. Free for your first month, then $6 a month.* Add the "Banked" screenshot and an App Store link.
