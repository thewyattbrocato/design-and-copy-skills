I'd split it into short steps with an account created at the start, and move the two things that make people leave (documents and bank details) to the end. That's 18 items by my count, since the terms checkbox makes one more than your 17.

**Order: easy facts first, hard-to-find things last**

1. **About you** has contact name (one field), email and phone. Print the reason beside the phone field, for example "so we can reach you about orders". Create the account here, so people can resume by email if they leave.
2. **Your catering** has cuisines as checkboxes, or a multi-select with search if the list is long, and delivery distance as a few preset radios ("Up to 10 miles" and so on). Vendors enjoy this step, and it makes them feel they've started.
3. **Your business** has legal business name, trading name (marked "optional", with the hint "if different from your legal name"), tax ID and address. Use address autocomplete, or let the postal code fill city and state. Give the tax ID a numeric keyboard, accept dashes and spaces, and say why you need it.
4. **Documents** has the food handler permit and insurance certificate. Allow phone camera and photo uploads, and show accepted file types and size up front. Add "I don't have this handy, finish later".
5. **Payouts** has account name prefilled from the legal name, plus routing and account number. Use numeric input modes, not spin-button number fields, and turn autofill off. Say that payouts are the reason for the request.
6. **Review and submit** shows everything with Edit links, bank numbers masked to the last 4 digits, then the terms checkbox and a primary button, "Submit application".

**Other points**
- Put the step in the heading ("Your business, step 3 of 6"), and keep Back from losing answers.
- Validate on submit of each step. Keep the Continue button always pressable. Show errors at the field with a summary at the top if there are several, and keep everything typed.
- Save progress per step, and email a resume link.
- Decide whether the permit, insurance and bank details must be in hand to apply, or only to go live. If only to go live, let people submit without them and prompt for them afterward. That is likely your biggest win, because vendors rarely have those documents and numbers to hand during sign-up.
- The final confirmation should say what happens next, how long review takes ([your timeline]), and what to do if they hear nothing.

I'm working from the field list alone. Before you build, check your analytics for which field or step people actually leave on. If most drop-off is at the uploads, steps 4 and 5 matter most.
