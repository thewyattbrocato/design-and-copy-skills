I'd split it into short steps, put the easy and interesting questions first, and let vendors save and come back. The fields that cause abandonment are probably the ones vendors don't have on hand: tax ID, bank details and two document uploads. A long list of text fields is less likely to be the problem.

**Layout (5 short steps, with a progress bar and autosave)**

1. **Create account:** contact name, email, phone. This is the only step that has to be done in one sitting. Once they have an account, you can email them a resume link if they leave.
2. **Your food:** cuisines (multi-select chips) and delivery distance (slider or presets like 5/10/25/50 mi). It's quick and relevant to what they signed up for, so it builds momentum.
3. **Your business:** legal name, trading name, and address. Add a "Same as legal name" checkbox for trading name, or make it optional. Use address autocomplete so street, city, state and postal code fill in from one entry.
4. **Compliance:** tax ID, food handler permit and insurance certificate. Tell them up front what they'll need ("Have your permit and insurance PDF ready"). Accept phone photos, and allow "I'll upload later" on the two documents.
5. **Payouts and terms:** bank account name, routing number, account number, then the terms checkbox and submit.

**Why this order**
- Asking for contact details first gives you a lead even if they quit.
- Easy steps come before the ones that need paperwork, so people have already invested effort when they hit the hard parts.
- Payouts go last because they feel the most sensitive. Add a short note like "Used only to pay you. Encrypted and never shown to customers."

**Reducing the work**
- **Defer what you can.** Ask whether you need bank details and documents at sign-up. If not, let vendors submit steps 1–3, then finish payouts and compliance from a dashboard checklist before they can go live or get paid. This probably helps abandonment the most.
- **Validate inline.** Check the routing number checksum and the postal code format as they type, not on submit.
- **Use the right inputs.** Use a phone input with formatting, email autofill, and a single-column layout. Label every field and say why you're asking for the sensitive ones, such as tax ID.
- **Use plain terms.** Show a short summary and link to the full text, not a long scrolling box.

You listed 17 items, but I count 18 including terms. Either way, the number of fields matters less than how they're grouped and when each is asked.

Once it's live, track drop-off per step. That will show whether the real problem is the documents, the bank step, or something earlier, and you can adjust from there.
