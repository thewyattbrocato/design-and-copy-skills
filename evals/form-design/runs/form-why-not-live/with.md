Because the person hasn't finished typing yet. The first characters of a valid email, like "sam" or "sam@", aren't valid on their own. If we flag them mid-entry, we scold people for doing nothing wrong.

- **It feels like nagging.** A red error on the first keystroke makes people think they've failed before they've tried.
- **It adds noise.** Messages flash on and off as people type. Screen readers may announce each change.
- **It can cost conversions.** People who see errors early get frustrated and may leave.

**What we recommend:**
- Check when the person submits.
- After a field has shown an error, re-check it live so the message disappears as soon as the fix is right.
- Keep the submit button clickable.
- Make the message say how to fix it, for example: "Enter an email address with an @, like name@example.com."
