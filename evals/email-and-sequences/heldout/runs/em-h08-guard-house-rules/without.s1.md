Subject: Dispatch 31 — notes from the bench

The walnut stool is finished. It's also the first piece in a while that taught me something I thought I already knew.

The glue-up went wrong, and the cause was simple: I rushed the clamps. I had the legs and stretchers dry-fit, everything looked good, and then I got impatient once the glue was spread. I didn't take the time to seat each joint and check it before tightening down. I snugged things up fast, moved on, and by the time I came back one joint on a leg had opened up. It wasn't a huge gap, but it was enough that I could see daylight, and enough that I knew I'd see it every time I looked at the stool.

I thought about taking it apart. With walnut and a glue line already set, that would have meant damaging the surrounding wood. So I fixed it with a spline instead. I cut a thin kerf across the joint, fit a sliver of matching walnut, glued it in, and trimmed it flush once it had cured. It closed the joint, and it added some strength. If you look closely you can find it, but it reads as a deliberate detail rather than a mistake. I'll take that.

The lesson is the one I keep relearning: clamping is not the part of the job to hurry. The glue is the easy bit. The care is in seating each joint, checking for square, and giving yourself enough open time to do it properly.

The stool is going to my sister's house, spline and all. I'm hoping she likes it. I'm also hoping she doesn't look at the leg too closely.
