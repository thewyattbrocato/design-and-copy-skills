Subject: Dispatch 31 — notes from the bench

The walnut stool is finished. It's also the stool that taught me a lesson I thought I'd already learned.

During the glue-up I rushed the clamps. I had the open time in my head and I wanted everything squared and tightened before the glue skinned over, so I went fast and sloppy. I didn't check that the pressure was even. By the time I came back to it, a joint on one leg had opened up. It was a hairline gap, but it was enough to see from across the shop, and enough that I knew it would keep bothering me every time I looked at the stool.

I could have started over with a new leg. Instead I cut a slot across the joint and fitted a spline. It's a thin strip of wood, glued in to bridge the gap and pull the joint back together. It took an afternoon. The repair is stronger than what I'd have had if I'd gotten the glue-up right the first time, and if you know where to look you can see it. I've decided I like that. A visible fix says something honest about how the piece was made.

The lesson is the same as always. Dry-fit, set out every clamp and caul before the glue comes out, and give yourself more time than you think you need. The clamps aren't the slow part. Rushing them is.

The stool is going to my sister's house. I'm glad it's going somewhere it will get used.
