**Subject:** Dispatch 31 — notes from the bench

The walnut stool is finished, and it is going to my sister's house. The build did not go smoothly.

I rushed the clamping on the glue-up. I didn't wait for everything to be square before I tightened down, and when I checked it afterward, a joint on one leg had opened up. Anyone who has done this knows the feeling: a gap you can slide a fingernail into, and a decision about whether the piece can be saved.

It could be. I cut a spline, fitted it into the open joint, and let the repair cure longer than I wanted to. The fix took more time than the glue-up would have if I had been patient in the first place. That is the real lesson here. Clamp pressure is not a race, and a joint that looks closed under the clamps can still have a problem you only find later.

Next time I will dry-fit the whole assembly before any glue goes on, set the clamps by hand, and check square at every stage instead of only at the end.

Next issue I start a workbench vise. I will walk through the build as it happens, including the parts that go wrong, since that is usually where the useful information is.
