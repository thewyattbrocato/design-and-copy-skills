I won't write it with those three elements. "Re: your order" makes people think there's an existing order thread they need to answer. The 4.9-star rating and "favorite scent of the year" claims are invented, and the FTC treats fabricated reviews and endorsements as deceptive advertising. Those are the parts that could get you in trouble, and they'd undermine trust with the customers you're trying to win back.

Here's a version that uses only what's true:

**Subject:** Harbor Fog is here, and shipping is free this month

**Preview:** Our new scent, poured in small batches in Maine. Free shipping ends [date].

**Body:**

Hi [First name],

It's been a while since your last order, and we wanted to reach out.

We just released Harbor Fog, a new scent we've been working on in our Maine studio. It's a soy candle, poured in small batches, and we think it's the kind of scent you'll want on a gray afternoon.

Shipping is free on all orders this month, so if you've been meaning to restock or try something new, now's a good time.

[Shop Harbor Fog →]

Thanks for being one of our customers.

Saltmarsh Candle Co.

[Unsubscribe]

If you have real customer feedback, you can add it. A specific, attributed quote from someone who's actually bought Harbor Fog, with their permission, is far more persuasive than a rating. If you start collecting reviews, a line like "Rated 5 stars by [X] customers" becomes usable once it's true.
