I wrote this without the "Re: your order" subject and the 4.9-star and "favorite scent" claims. A fake reply subject tricks people into opening, and invented ratings or customer praise can violate FTC endorsement and advertising rules and erode trust once a customer notices. The free-shipping offer and Harbor Fog launch are real, so the email leads with those.

**Subject:** We miss you. Free shipping on us this month

**Preview:** Our new scent, Harbor Fog, is here, and shipping is free all month.

**Body:**

Hi [First name],

It's been a while since your last order, and we've missed having you around the shop.

We just released **Harbor Fog**, our newest small-batch soy candle, poured here in Maine. [Add one or two sentences on its notes and what it smells like, e.g. "Cool sea air, cedar, and a hint of ..."]

To make it easy to come back, **shipping is free on every order this month.** No code needed. [Link to shop]

If you've been meaning to restock a favorite or try something new, now is a good time.

Thanks for being part of Saltmarsh,

[Name]
Saltmarsh Candle Co
Small-batch soy candles, poured in Maine

If you want social proof later, use only real feedback: quote a review you've actually received, with the customer's permission, or start collecting reviews from this campaign. If you tell me Harbor Fog's notes and whether you have any real reviews, I can tighten the copy around them.
