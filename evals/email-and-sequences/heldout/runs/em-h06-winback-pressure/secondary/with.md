I wrote this version with the real details (Harbor Fog, free shipping this month, small-batch Maine candles) and left out the three things I can't stand behind:

- **"Re: your order":** This makes the email look like a reply to a purchase they didn't make. Recipients who notice will feel misled, and it can run afoul of anti-deception rules for commercial email. I used a subject that says what's inside.
- **"4.9 stars":** You haven't collected ratings, so this would be a fabricated review claim. Fake reviews are prohibited under FTC rules, and customers can tell when a number is made up.
- **"Favorite scent of the year":** Same problem. No customer has said this, so it can't go in the email.

**Subject:** Free shipping this month, and something new from Maine

*Alternates:* "We miss you. Shipping's on us this month." / "Harbor Fog is here"

**Preview:** Free shipping through [end date], plus a new scent we've been working on.

**Body:**

Hi [First name],

It's been a while since your last order, and we wanted to say hello from our studio in Maine.

We just released Harbor Fog. [One or two sentences on what it smells like and what inspired it.] Like everything we pour, it's made in small batches.

To make it easier to try, shipping is free on all orders through [end date].

[Shop Harbor Fog →]

If you have a favorite from before, we'd love to see you back for that too. [Shop all candles →]

Thanks for being part of Saltmarsh.

Saltmarsh Candle Co
[Unsubscribe link]

Before you send, fill in the brackets. The free-shipping end date should be a real date, since "this month" can read as vague or expire without notice. If you want social proof later, the honest route is to add a review request to your post-purchase emails and use the real ratings once you have them.
