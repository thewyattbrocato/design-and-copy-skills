## What's going wrong

The two best subject lines name something specific the reader can use: a place and its conditions ("Snow still on Heather Ridge"), or a question about gear they own ("Is your rain shell actually waterproof anymore?"). The four weak ones name the newsletter instead of what's in it.

| # | Subject | Open | What it tells the reader |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Is your rain shell actually waterproof anymore? | 38% | A question about their own gear |
| 3 | Snow still on Heather Ridge, plus 3 dry hikes | 34% | Current conditions at a named place |
| 1 | Fernwick Trail Notes #112 | 19% | Brand and issue number only |
| 2 | Trail Notes #113: June edition | 18% | Issue number, a date label, and a different name |
| 4 | Trail Notes #115 | 17% | Issue number only |
| 6 | Newsletter: summer reading list | 15% | Generic "Newsletter:" label, and a topic off your core focus |

- **Issue numbers carry no information.** Four of six lines lead with a number or the brand name, which tells a subscriber nothing about whether to open. Those are your lowest performers.
- **The name is inconsistent.** It's "Fernwick Trail Notes" in #1, "Trail Notes" in #2 and #4, and "Newsletter" in #6. Subscribers recognize a fixed name, so pick one for the sender name and keep the subject about the content.
- **"June edition" is stale.** It's October, so the line reads as a template that wasn't updated.
- **#6 is off-topic.** A reading list isn't trail conditions or gear, which is probably why it did worst.

Caveats: six sends is a small sample, and topic, send day, and send time all vary together, so the 34% and 38% may partly reflect timing or topic. Open rates are also inflated by Apple Mail Privacy Protection, so compare these numbers against each other rather than against an industry benchmark. I don't have your preview text, send times, or from-name, and those could matter too.

## Next issue

Lead with the trail openings. It's your most time-sensitive and most specific content, and it matches the 34% format. Replace the bracketed names with the real trails.

**Option A (recommended):**
- **Subject:** Three hikes just opened up this week
- **Preview:** [Trail A], [Trail B], and [Trail C] are clear now. Here's what to know before you go.

**Option B:**
- **Subject:** Is [Trail A] clear yet? Three that are.
- **Preview:** Snow levels, trailhead access, and what changed since last week.

**Option C:**
- **Subject:** Your trekking poles are probably set up wrong
- **Preview:** Two quick fixes that take 30 seconds, plus the mistake most day hikers make on descents.

**Option D:**
- **Subject:** Do you actually need a bear canister on [Trail]?
- **Preview:** A reader asked, and the answer depends on the trail more than you'd think.

Use Option A as the send. If you want to learn something, split the list and test Option A against Option C or D. Each arm would get about 3,100 people, which is enough to see a difference of roughly 5 points. Use the same send time for both arms.

Going forward, I'd keep subjects to one specific, current thing and drop the issue number. Move "#113" and similar to the preview text or body, where they don't compete with the hook.
