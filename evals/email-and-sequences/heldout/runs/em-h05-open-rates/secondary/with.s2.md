**What's going wrong:** The two best subject lines name something specific a hiker can act on: snow on a named ridge (34%) and whether a rain shell still works (38%). The four weak ones lead with the newsletter's name and an issue number ("Trail Notes #115") or a generic label ("Newsletter: summer reading list"). Those tell a reader nothing about what's inside, so they get skipped. "June edition" is also a dated label. Six sends is a small sample, and send time and topic also vary, so treat this as a strong pattern to act on rather than proof. Drop the issue number and lead with the most specific item in each issue.

**Subject lines and preview text for the next issue**

1. **Subject:** Three trails reopened this week
   **Preview:** [Trail name] is open again. Plus a bear canister question.

2. **Subject:** Trekking pole tips for your next hike
   **Preview:** Short, practical ones for day hikes.

3. **Subject:** Do you actually need a bear canister?
   **Preview:** A reader asks, and we answer for PNW day hikes.

I'd send option 1 as the issue subject, since the reopened trails are the most time-sensitive and specific news. Options 2 and 3 work as alternates for future issues or a test.

Two things to check before sending: fill in the trail names in the preview, and make sure the bear canister answer matches current rules for each area, since those requirements vary and change.
