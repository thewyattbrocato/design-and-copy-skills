## What's going wrong

The pattern is clear even with six data points. The two high performers (34%, 38%) say something specific about the content: a condition on a named trail, or a question about gear the reader might own. The four low performers are labels. They tell the reader what the email is, not what's in it.

| Subject | Open rate | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| "Is your rain shell actually waterproof anymore?" | 38% | Specific question about something the reader owns |
| "Snow still on Heather Ridge, plus 3 dry hikes" | 34% | Timely, named place, concrete payoff |
| "Fernwick Trail Notes #112" | 19% | Brand and issue number only |
| "Trail Notes #113: June edition" | 18% | Issue number, and "June" reads as stale |
| "Trail Notes #115" | 17% | Issue number only |
| "Newsletter: summer reading list" | 15% | Generic label, and the topic is weak for day hikers |

Specific problems:

1. **Issue numbers as subject lines.** #112, #115 and similar give a subscriber no reason to open. That's probably the biggest single cause.
2. **Inconsistent naming.** "Fernwick Trail Notes," "Trail Notes," and "Newsletter:" are three different names. Subscribers can't build recognition around a brand that changes every week.
3. **Topics without a hook.** "Summer reading list" is a generic subject, and a reading list isn't what your readers signed up for.
4. **You're not leading with the news.** Your strongest subjects are the ones with a live trail condition. Trail status is the most valuable thing you have, and it's buried.

Caveats: six sends can't prove causation. Send time, season, and the content itself also vary. Also, Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates opens for some subscribers, so treat the absolute numbers loosely and watch the trend.
