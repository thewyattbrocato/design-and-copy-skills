**What's going wrong:** your subject lines are labels instead of reasons to open. The four weak ones are issue numbers or generic titles, and they got 15-19%. The two that name something specific and useful got 34% and 38%, about double.

| Subject | Open rate | What it tells the reader |
|---|---|---|
| "Trail Notes #115" | 17% | Nothing |
| "Newsletter: summer reading list" | 15% | Nothing, and it's off-brand for a trail-conditions list |
| "Snow still on Heather Ridge, plus 3 dry hikes" | 34% | A place, a condition, a count |
| "Is your rain shell actually waterproof anymore?" | 38% | A question the reader wants answered |

Inbox placement looks fine, since the same list opened 34-38% on two sends. So I'd treat this as a subject line problem, not a deliverability or list-quality problem.

Six sends is a small sample, and I can't separate subject from content. Issues 3 and 5 may simply have had stronger content. But the pattern is consistent enough to act on.

**Fixes:**
- Take the issue number out of the subject. Put it in the preheader or footer.
- Lead with the most concrete, time-sensitive item. For you that's usually a trail condition.
- Use specifics: names, counts, conditions. Your best subject has all three.
- Don't leave preview text blank. It's currently probably showing the first line of the email or "View in browser."
