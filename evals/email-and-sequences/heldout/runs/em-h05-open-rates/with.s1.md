   Plus trekking pole tips and a reader's bear canister question.

   And a reader's bear canister question, answered.

I'd start with #1. The hikes are the most useful thing in the issue, and your two best subject lines (34% and 38%) both led with a specific, useful thing.

**What's going wrong**

The envelope doesn't say what's inside. Your four weak subjects ("Fernwick Trail Notes #112", "Trail Notes #113: June edition", "Trail Notes #115", "Newsletter: summer reading list") are labels. They read like a masthead or an issue number, and a subscriber can't tell what they'd get by opening. Your two strong ones name a concrete thing: snow on Heather Ridge, or a rain shell that may have stopped working. They also open at about double the rate (34% and 38% against 15–19%).

The strong subjects also use a different style. #3 gives specific conditions and a count of hikes, and #5 asks a question the reader probably cares about.

Two caveats:
- Six issues is a small sample, and topic, send time and season are mixed in with the subject style. The pattern is strong, but it isn't proof.
- Open rates are inflated by some email apps that preload messages, so watch clicks as well. A subject that gets opens but no clicks isn't working.

If you want to test, change one thing at a time. For example, keep the sender name and send time fixed for a few issues and vary only the subject style.

Fill in the real trail names or region in the hikes line if you want it more specific. "Heather Ridge" worked because it named a place.
