# Setting the Saltmarsh Kitchen menu

A menu looks like a spreadsheet when it has a name column, a price column, and rules or dot leaders joining them. Avoid that structure and the rest follows.

## Structure
- **Use one centred column**, about 9–10 cm wide, in the middle of the A4 page. Margins should be generous: at least 25 mm at the sides and 20 mm top and bottom. The white space will make it look more expensive.
- **Put the price at the end of the dish line**, or on its own line under the description. Don't push it to a right-hand column. Don't use dot leaders or table borders. In your word processor, use centred paragraphs, not a table.
- **Drop the £ signs.** Use the prices as you gave them: 4.5, 9, 12.5, 24. Keep this consistent throughout. Either leave every price as it is, or use .50 on every half price (4.50, 12.50) and whole numbers elsewhere. Don't mix styles.

## Type
- **Use one typeface family**, ideally a serif with real italics. Good options are Garamond (EB Garamond if you can install it), Georgia, Baskerville, or Caslon. If you want a contrast, use a clean sans only for the section headings, not for the dishes.
- **Dish names:** 12 pt regular, in sentence case. Don't use all caps and don't bold them.
- **Accompaniments:** 10.5 pt italic, or the same size in grey (about 60% black). Put them on a line under the dish name:

  > Smoked mackerel pâté
  > *pickled cucumber, rye toast* · 9

  Write "pâté" with the accent.
- **Section headings** (Starters, Mains, Puddings): 11 pt, all caps or small caps, with letter-spacing of about +10–15%. Add one thin short rule or a small ornament under each heading, if you want any decoration.
- **Restaurant name** at the top: 24–28 pt, spaced capitals or a refined serif. Add a small line under it such as "Seafood · [town]".
- **Footer note:** 8.5–9 pt italic, centred, at the bottom. Put the allergy sentence and the service charge sentence on separate lines.

## Spacing
- Line spacing should be about 120–130% of the type size.
- Leave 10–12 pt between dishes and 28–36 pt between sections.
- With only 10 dishes, the page will have plenty of room. Use it. Cramming the text into the top half would look worse than spacing it out.

## Details that make it look professional
- Use **real typographic characters**: curly quotes and a proper middle dot (·) or en dash. Hyphenation should be off, and avoid orphaned words on a line.
- Set "each" after the oyster price in italic, like this: 4.5 *each*.
- Turn on ligatures if the font supports them.
- Use **black text on white or cream paper** (a 100–120 gsm uncoated stock is ideal). Don't use more than one accent colour, and only for the headings if at all.
- Keep the dishes in order of what you want people to read. If the fish pie or bream are your best sellers, give no dish extra emphasis. Position alone will do the work.
- If you add any decoration, keep it to one small motif, such as a fish, a wave, or a reed, at the top or bottom.

## Before printing
Print one copy at 100% scale (no "fit to page") and read it from arm's length. If you have to squint at the italic lines or the footer, go up half a point. Before you commit to a large run, check the allergy wording against your local rules, since food businesses often have specific requirements.
