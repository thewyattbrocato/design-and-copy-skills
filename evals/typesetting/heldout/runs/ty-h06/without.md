# Setting the Saltmarsh Kitchen menu

A spreadsheet look comes from rules, columns, bold everywhere, and prices lined up in a neat column. A proper menu comes from whitespace, a small number of type sizes, and centered or left-aligned text with quiet prices.

## Page and layout
- **Size:** A4 portrait, with 20–25 mm margins on all sides. Generous margins make it feel considered.
- **Alignment:** Pick one. Centered works well for a 30-seat place with short dishes. Left-aligned also works. Don't justify the text.
- **Single column.** You only have 10 dishes, so don't split the page into columns.
- **Vertical rhythm:** Put about 3× the space between sections as between dishes. Let the page breathe, and don't stretch the content to fill it. Empty space at the bottom is fine.

## Fonts
Use **one family**, or at most two (a serif plus a plain sans for the small print).
- **Serif options:** Garamond (EB Garamond, free), Caslon, Baskerville, Cormorant, or Libre Baskerville. These suit seafood and look traditional.
- **Sans options:** Gill Sans, Lato, or Josefin Sans for a lighter coastal feel.
- Avoid script fonts, Papyrus, Comic Sans, and anything nautical-themed.

## Size and weight hierarchy
| Element | Size | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Restaurant name (top) | 24–28 pt | Small caps or letter-spaced capitals |
| Section heads (Starters, Mains, Puddings) | 12–13 pt | Capitals with wide letter-spacing (+100 to +200), regular or semibold |
| Dish name | 12 pt | Regular, or medium |
| Description (e.g. "shallot vinegar") | 10.5–11 pt | Italic or a lighter grey |
| Footer | 8.5–9 pt | Italic or small, in grey |

Don't use more than these levels. Avoid underlining, and don't use bold on every dish.

## Dish formatting
Put the dish name on one line, with the accompaniments below it in italic:

> **Whole grilled bream**
> *salsa verde, new potatoes*

Alternatively, run them on one line separated by commas, with the dish name in regular and the rest in italic. Choose one style and use it throughout.

Set line spacing to about 1.2–1.3× for the dish and description lines.

## Prices
This is where menus most often turn into spreadsheets.
- **Drop the currency symbols and decimals where you can.** Write `9`, `12.5` as `12.5` or `12.50`. Pick one style: either always two decimals (`4.50, 9, 12.50`) or none where whole. Mixed styles look sloppy, so I'd use `4.5 / 9 / 12.5 / 24 / 18 / 17 / 19.5 / 8 / 7.5 / 11` consistently as whole or half numbers.
- **Don't use dot leaders** (`......… 24`) or a right-aligned column of prices.
- **Centered layout:** put the price directly after the dish name or on the description line, separated by a space or an en-space.
- **Left-aligned layout:** put the price at the end of the dish name line, in the same font and size, not bold.
- For the oyster, write "4.5 each" in the same style as the other prices.

## Details that signal "proper menu"
- Use curly quotes and apostrophes, not straight ones.
- Use "pâté" with the accent, and use an en dash for ranges.
- Capitalise only the first word of each dish, and keep it consistent.
- Use a small ornament between sections, such as a short thin rule (about 20 mm) or a simple fleuron. Skip full-width lines.
- Use "and" spelled out, not "&", unless you want it as a deliberate design choice.
- Use text colour in black or very dark grey, on white or warm off-white paper. A single accent colour (deep navy or sea green) for section heads only is fine.

## Footer
Set the allergy and service charge line at the bottom in 8.5–9 pt italic, centered, separated from the menu by extra space. Keep it as two short sentences:

> *Please tell us about allergies before ordering.*
> *A discretionary 12.5% service charge is added to tables of six or more.*

## Word processor tips
- Set up **styles** (Title, Section, Dish, Description, Footer) rather than formatting each line by hand. This keeps spacing and fonts consistent.
- Use **space before/after paragraph** settings, not blank lines.
- Turn on **ligatures and kerning** if your software offers them. In Word, it's under Font > Advanced.
- Export to **PDF** with fonts embedded before sending to a printer. Check a test print on the actual paper, since 10.5 pt text can look different on screen.
- For paper, a 120–170 gsm uncoated stock feels better than thin office paper.
