# Setting the Saltmarsh Kitchen menu

A menu that looks like a spreadsheet usually has prices in a ruled column, every line at the same weight, and too much going on. A proper menu does the opposite: it uses one or two typefaces, only a few sizes, lots of white space, and prices that sit quietly with the text.

## Page and margins
- **A4 portrait**, with generous margins of **20–25 mm** on all sides, and a little more at the bottom.
- Use a **single centred column** about 100–120 mm wide, in the middle of the page. Don't run text edge to edge. The empty space is what makes it look considered.
- Use **centred alignment** for everything. It's traditional for menus and works well with this little content.

## Typefaces
Pick **one** pairing and stick to it:
- **Serif for everything**, such as Garamond, Caslon, Baskerville or Georgia. Use a small-caps or letterspaced version for headings. This is the safest option.
- Or **a serif for dishes and a light sans for headings and the footer** (for example, Garamond with Gill Sans or Lato Light).

Don't use more than two families. Avoid script fonts, Comic-style fonts and Papyrus.

## Sizes (for A4)
| Element | Size | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Restaurant name | 26–32 pt | Small caps or all caps, letterspaced (+100 to +200 tracking) |
| Section headings (Starters, Mains, Puddings) | 12–13 pt | All caps, letterspaced, maybe in a muted colour |
| Dish name | 12–13 pt | Regular, or italic for the description |
| Description / accompaniments | 10.5–11 pt | Regular or italic |
| Footer note | 8.5–9 pt | Italic, grey |

Keep line spacing at about **1.3–1.4×** the font size.

## Putting the dishes together
Use a **two-line structure** for each dish:

> **Whole grilled bream**
> *salsa verde, new potatoes*
> 24

The dish name goes first and the components go below it in italic or a lighter weight. This is much easier to scan than a long comma-separated line.

For example:

> Smoked mackerel pâté
> *pickled cucumber, rye toast*

## Prices
- **Drop the £ signs and the trailing ".0"** to keep it calm: `9`, `12.5` and `19.5`. Better yet, show consistent decimals (`12.50`, `19.50`) if you write any of them with a decimal. Choose one style and apply it to every price. I'd go with whole numbers where possible and `.5` as `.50`.
- **Don't put the price in a right-aligned column joined by dot leaders.** That's the main thing that makes a menu look like a spreadsheet.
- Instead, put the price **centred under the dish description**, or **on the same line directly after the dish name**, separated by a space or an en dash.
- Oysters are priced each, so write that as `4.50 each`.

## Spacing and hierarchy
- Leave about **4–5 mm** between dishes and **12–15 mm** before each new section heading.
- Put a short **hairline rule or a small ornament** (a thin 20 mm line, or a small ✦) between sections, rather than boxes or table borders.
- Don't use bold for everything. One bold level, for dish names or headings but not both, is enough.
- No clip art, no borders around the whole page. If you want a decorative touch, a single thin line or a small fish or wave mark at the top is enough.

## Don't use a table for this
If you laid the content out in a Word or Pages table, turn the borders off and switch to centred paragraphs. A table is where the spreadsheet look comes from. Use **paragraph styles** (Title, Heading, Dish, Description, Footer) so you can change sizes in one place.

## Footer
Put the allergy and service charge note at the bottom in small italic grey, centred, and separated from the pudding by extra space:

> *Please tell us about allergies before ordering.*
> *A discretionary 12.5% service charge is added to tables of six or more.*

## Colour and print
- Use **black text on white or cream paper**, with at most **one accent colour** (a deep navy or sea green) for the name and section headings.
- Print on **uncoated paper of 120–170 gsm**. Cream or off-white stock suits a seafood restaurant.
- Before you print a run, **print one test copy at 100%** (not "fit to page") and check that the text is readable at arm's length in dim light.
- Export to **PDF** with fonts embedded if you're sending it to a printer.

## Content tweaks
- Write "pâté" with the accent if your font has it.
- Capitalise the first word of each dish only, and keep the descriptions lowercase.
- Consider ordering mains by price or by the way you'd like to sell them, rather than the order you listed them in. Putting the bream first is a good choice.
