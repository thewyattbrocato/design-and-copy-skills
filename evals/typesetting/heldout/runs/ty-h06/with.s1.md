# Setting the Saltmarsh Kitchen menu in a word processor

The "spreadsheet" look comes from too many sizes, bold everywhere, and every item crammed into one line of text. Fix it with one typeface, a few clear sizes, and generous space. Don't add borders or tables.

## Page
- A4 portrait, with margins of 25 mm at the sides and 22 mm top and bottom.
- Set a single column with the dishes left-aligned. Keep the menu title centred.
- The ten dishes are short, so you'll have spare room. Use it as white space between dishes instead of enlarging the type.

## Typeface
- Use one family with real italic and semibold styles. Use a serif such as Garamond, Caslon or Georgia, or whichever of these you have.
- Don't use faux bold or italic. Choose the actual Semibold or Italic style from the font list.
- Print in black or a very dark ink. A mid-grey for descriptions is fine, but nothing paler.

## Sizes (five roles)
| Role | Size | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Restaurant name | 28 pt | Regular, spacing +1 pt |
| Section heads (STARTERS, MAINS, PUDDINGS) | 11 pt | Caps, letter-spacing +1.5 pt, semibold |
| Dish name and price | 12 pt | Semibold name, regular price |
| Description | 10.5 pt | Regular or italic |
| Footer | 9 pt | Italic, centred |

- **Line spacing:** use "Exactly" 15 pt for the 12 pt dish names and 14 pt for the descriptions. Single spacing is too tight.
- **Spacing between items:**
  - Put 28 pt before each section head and 10 pt after it.
  - Put 10–12 pt between dishes, using Paragraph → Spacing After. Never press Enter for blank lines.
- Only the section heads are all caps. Everything else is sentence case.

## Dish layout
Split each dish onto two lines instead of running it together:

> **Whole grilled bream**  → → → 24
> salsa verde, new potatoes

- The name is on the first line and the accompaniments are on the second.
- Put the price on the first line, at a right-aligned tab at the column edge.
- Skip dot leaders and £ signs, which are the most spreadsheet-like elements.
- Put "each" on the oyster's description line ("shallot vinegar, each"). That keeps the price column clean.

## Prices
- Use a **decimal tab** for the price column so 9, 12.5 and 24 line up on the point. Use the same tab stop for all three sections.
- Turn on tabular (fixed-width) figures if your word processor offers them. In Word, go to Font → Advanced → Number spacing → Tabular.
- Spell the starter as **pâté**, with the accent. Use curly apostrophes and quotes.

## Footer
- Add a thin rule (0.5 pt) above it.
- Set both sentences in 9 pt italic, centred, with 24 pt of space above.
- Don't go smaller than 9 pt. The allergy line is important, so keep it readable.

## Quick check before printing
Print a draft at 100% and hold it at arm's length. You should see three clear blocks, with the section heads and dish names standing out. If anything looks crowded, add space before reducing the type.

My assumptions: you're printing on a normal office or print-shop printer on white paper. I also assumed no currency symbol, since you didn't give one.
