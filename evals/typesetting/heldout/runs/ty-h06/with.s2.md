# Setting the Saltmarsh Kitchen menu (A4, word processor)

**Page and structure**
- Margins are 25 mm all round. Make the text column narrower than the page, about 110 mm, and centre it. Keep the empty space. A menu with a lot of white around it looks like a restaurant menu.
- Use one column, left-aligned, with no justified text and no table with borders. A spreadsheet look usually comes from gridlines, a price column far from the dish, and every line at the same size and weight.
- Use paragraph styles (Title, Section, Dish, Description, Footnote). Don't use blank lines or spaces to create gaps.

**Typeface**
- Use one serif family with a real italic, such as Garamond, Palatino, Baskerville or Georgia. Avoid Times New Roman, and avoid any script or novelty face.
- Don't use the bold or italic buttons on a font that lacks those styles. Word will fake them, and they look smeared.

**Sizes and roles** (five sizes in all)

| Role | Size | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Saltmarsh Kitchen | 30 pt | Regular, slight tracking (+10) |
| STARTERS / MAINS / PUDDINGS | 11 pt | Caps, tracked +100 (Font → Advanced → Expanded) |
| Dish name | 12 pt | Regular |
| Description (shallot vinegar, pickled cucumber…) | 10.5 pt | Italic |
| Allergy and service line | 9 pt | Regular, centred |

**Dish layout**
- Put the dish name on one line and its accompaniments in italics on the line beneath:

  Whole grilled bream  **24**
  *salsa verde, new potatoes*

- Put the price at the end of the dish-name line, using a right-aligned tab stop at the column's edge. Don't use dot leaders, which look like a ledger.
- Leave off £ signs. Your prices are short, so the numbers alone are cleaner.
- Make price formats consistent: either 4.50, 9.00, 12.50, 24.00 or whole numbers where possible. Mixing "9" with "12.5" looks careless. Two decimals is the safest.
- Use lining, tabular figures if your font offers them (Font → Advanced → Number forms/spacing). This makes the prices stack in a neat column.

**Spacing**
- Line spacing: Exactly 14 pt for 10.5 pt text, and 15–16 pt for dish names.
- Between dishes: 10 pt after each description.
- Section headings: 28 pt before and 10 pt after. The heading should sit closer to its own dishes than to the section above. Add a short, thin rule or a small gap under each heading if you want a little more structure.
- Keep each section together on the page. Set "Keep with next" on headings and dish names.

**Details**
- Use *pâté* with the accent, and curly apostrophes and quotes.
- Set "each" after the oyster price in italics or in the description line (*shallot vinegar, each*), so the price column stays clean.
- Keep the footer line at 9 pt or larger, and put it at the bottom with some space above it. Keep it short, in sentence case, and never in all caps. Print it in dark grey or black.

**Colour**
- Use black, or a single dark navy or deep green for the name and section headings. Don't use more than one accent colour. Print a proof on the actual printer first, because pale greys often vanish on paper.
