Here's a setup for a one-page A4 menu. It's printed, so sizes are in points.

**Page and layout**
- Margins: 20–25 mm all round. A 30-seat place can afford generous white space, which is much of what makes a menu look considered.
- One centred column about 100–110 mm wide, centred on the page. Don't run text across the full A4 width.
- Centre-align the dish names and descriptions. That's the traditional menu look and it works for a short list. Don't use tab stops with dot leaders to the prices. That's the spreadsheet look.
- Put the price on the end of the dish's last line, or on its own right-aligned line. For a centred menu, the cleanest option is the price directly under or after the description in the same line, separated by a space.
- Leave out currency signs. Write `4.5`, `9`, `12.5`. Use one decimal convention: either `9` and `12.5`, or `9.0` and `12.5` throughout. I'd keep your numbers as given.

**Type sizes (four roles)**
| Role | Size | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Restaurant name | 28–32 pt | Serif, regular, tracked slightly (+20 to +40) |
| Section heads (Starters, Mains, Puddings) | 11 pt | Small caps or all caps, tracked +100 (about 0.1 em) |
| Dish name | 12 pt | Regular or medium |
| Description | 10.5 pt | Regular, same family, grey-black rather than pure black |
| Footer note | 9 pt | Italic or regular, centred |

Use real small caps or capitals typed in caps. Don't use the word processor's bold-to-fake-it button on a face with no bold. Keep to one typeface family: a good serif (Garamond, Caslon, Baskerville, Georgia if that's all you have) is the safe choice for seafood.

**Dish structure**
- Dish name on the first line (12 pt), then the accompaniments on the next line in 10.5 pt, e.g.:
  - **Whole grilled bream** / salsa verde, new potatoes · 24
- Or put name and price on one line and the description beneath. Pick one and use it throughout.
- Set the description in lower case. Don't capitalise every word.
- Use "pâté" with its accent if you want to be correct.

**Spacing**
- Line spacing: about 1.3× for descriptions (13.5–14 pt on 10.5 pt). Use exact or "multiple" spacing, not single.
- Between dishes: 10–12 pt space after each.
- Before a section heading: about 28–36 pt. After it: about 12–14 pt. Roughly twice as much above as below, so the heading belongs to the dishes beneath it.
- Name at the top: about 36 pt of space below it.
- Footer: leave about 30–40 pt above it, and consider a short thin rule (a 30 mm hairline) or just white space to separate it.

**Prices**
- Use a font with tabular (lining) figures if the prices will line up in a column. Since you're centring, this matters less. If you right-align instead, use the same number of decimals and align on the decimal point.
- Don't bold the prices or make them larger. Same size as the dish name, same weight, is the polished look.
- Don't use a £ column of prices.

**Footer note**
- "Please tell us about allergies before ordering. A discretionary 12.5% service charge is added to tables of six or more."
- Set at 9 pt, centred, in two short lines. Break after "ordering." so it doesn't leave a single word hanging. Keep it legible. Allergy information shouldn't be tiny or pale.

**Colour and finish**
- Black or very dark grey on white or cream paper. One accent colour at most (a deep sea blue or green) for section heads only.
- Print a test page at 100% before the full run. Check that 10.5 pt descriptions read comfortably at arm's length in dim light.
- Export as PDF for the printer so the fonts don't shift.

Two assumptions: this is for printing on white or cream stock, and the menu is a single column. If you'd rather have two columns, tell me and I'll adjust the widths.
