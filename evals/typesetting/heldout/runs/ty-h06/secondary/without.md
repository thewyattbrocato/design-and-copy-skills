For a menu that reads as a menu rather than a price list, the main tools are **a clear hierarchy, generous spacing, and dot leaders for prices** instead of a table.

**Page setup**
- A4 portrait, margins of about 20 mm on the sides and 15 to 18 mm top and bottom. Keep everything inside a single centred text column.
- Set the whole page to centred alignment for headings, and left-align dish text. Centred dish lines look more like a card than a list, but they're harder to scan when there are several per section.

**Type**
- **Section headings** (STARTERS, MAINS, PUDDINGS): a serif or display face, all caps, letter-spaced out (+100 to +200 tracking in Word's Character Spacing), around 16 to 18 pt. Add a thin rule or a small ornament under each.
- **Dish names**: the body font, regular or semi-bold, around 11 to 12 pt.
- **Descriptions**: the same font in italic or a lighter grey, about 9.5 to 10 pt, on their own line under the dish name. Don't set descriptions in bold.
- **Restaurant name**: the largest item on the page, around 28 to 36 pt.
- **Pairing**: a classic serif for headings and a readable serif or humanist sans for body text works well. Garamond, Baskerville, Cormorant, or Gill Sans all suit a seafood place. Use no more than two typefaces.
- **Allergy and service notes**: small, around 8.5 to 9 pt, at the bottom, separated by a rule.

**Prices: the spreadsheet problem**
Don't put prices in a table column or separate them with tabs at random positions. Instead:
1. Put the price on the same line as the dish name, after a right-aligned tab stop with a dot leader.
   - In Word: Home > Paragraph > Tabs (or Format > Tabs). Set the position to your text width (A4 minus both margins, e.g. 170 mm with 20 mm margins), alignment **Right**, leader **2 (dots)**. Then type the name, press Tab, and type the price.
   - In Google Docs and Pages, you can do the same with a right tab and a dot leader if your version supports leaders. If not, use a right-aligned tab with no leader, which still looks clean.
2. Keep prices consistent: two decimals and the currency symbol, e.g. **£4.50**, **£12.50**, **£7.50**. Your draft uses "4.5", "7.5", and "12.5" in places, so I've used those as £4.50, £7.50, and £12.50 below.
3. Put a little space between items (6 to 8 pt before each dish) and more space between sections (18 to 24 pt).

**Printing**
- Use a PDF export with embedded fonts, and print from the PDF rather than the word processor so the layout doesn't shift.
- If you use a coloured background or a dark paper, add 3 mm bleed or check with the printer first.
- Print one test copy before the full run to check the dot leaders and the bottom margin.
