Set it up as a single-column, centred layout with a clear hierarchy, and align the prices to a right-hand tab stop rather than typing them in a table.

**Page setup**
- A4, margins of about 20 to 25 mm all round, with the content centred.
- One column. Restaurant menus read top to bottom, so avoid tables.

**Type**
- Pick one serif or one elegant sans-serif for dish names and a second for headings if you want contrast. Two families at most. Good options that ship with Word or are free on Google Fonts: Garamond, Cormorant Garamond, Lora, Playfair Display for headings, or Josefin Sans, Montserrat for small caps.
- Section headings (STARTERS, MAINS, PUDDINGS): 14 to 18 pt, all caps, letter-spaced (Font > Advanced > Spacing: Expanded 1 to 2 pt).
- Dish names: 11 to 13 pt, regular or medium weight.
- Descriptions: 9 to 10 pt, italic or a lighter grey (around 60 to 70% black).
- Footer lines: 8 to 9 pt.

**Prices (the part that makes it look like a spreadsheet if done wrong)**
- Don't use a table or spaces to line them up. Set a right-aligned tab stop at the right margin (Paragraph > Tabs, Alignment: Right, Leader: dots optional).
- Put the name, a tab, then the price on the same line: `Burnt honey tart<TAB>8.00`.
- Use two decimals consistently (9.00, not 9), and add the currency symbol once in the heading or footer if the menu needs it.

**Spacing**
- Space before each section heading: about 18 to 24 pt. Space after each dish: about 6 to 8 pt. Space between a dish name and its description: 0 to 2 pt, so each description sits tight under its dish.
- Keep each section on the page with "Keep with next" on headings.

**Ornament**
- A thin rule or a small ornament under each section heading is enough. Don't add more than that.
