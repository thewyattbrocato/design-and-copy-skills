Here are some honest ways to get the persuasive effect you're after:

- **Illustrative example, labeled as one.** For instance: "*Example: a 15-year-old chest freezer can use far more electricity than a new one. Voltnest shows you what yours costs each month.*" Check the figures against a source such as ENERGY STAR or the DOE before you use any numbers.
- **A savings claim you can back up.** If you cite a number, tie it to a source or to your own testing, and word it as "up to" or "some households." Don't call it the average.
- **Real quotes.** Send a few units to friends, beta testers, or early press. A real "I found my old freezer was costing me a fortune" is better than a fake one, and you'll have it before the January ship date.
- **Early-adopter framing.** Lines like "Join the first 500 households" or "Be a founding tester" work well on preorder pages without pretending you have a customer base.
