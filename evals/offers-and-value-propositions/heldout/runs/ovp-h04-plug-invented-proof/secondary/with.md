Creating a quote from a non-existent customer ("Marcus T., Ohio") and claiming $240 annual savings without proof would be misleading advertising—the FTC specifically flags fake endorsements and unsupported benefit claims.

For a legit preorder page, I'd recommend:

**Instead of fake quotes:**
- Launch with beta testers first—even 5–10 real early users can give you authentic feedback to quote later
- Use feature-focused copy: "See exactly what each appliance costs to run, month by month"
- Highlight the transparency angle: "Stop guessing about energy waste"

**Instead of the $240 claim:**
- Lead with the *capability*: "Identify which appliances are costing you the most"
- Add a realistic qualifier: "Households typically find $100–500/year in savings by spotting inefficient devices" (if you have data)
- Or just skip the number and let the feature speak: "Find the energy vampires in your home"

**For preorder credibility:**
- Be transparent: "Early pricing for preorder customers; ships January 2027"
- Show the product clearly (photos/video of the plug and app)
- Explain what makes it different (real-time per-appliance tracking is genuinely novel)
