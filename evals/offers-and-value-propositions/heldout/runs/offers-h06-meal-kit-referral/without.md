## The reward

| | Reward | When it's earned |
|---|---|---|
| **Friend** | $20 off their first box | At checkout, on the first box |
| **Referrer** | $25 Fernway credit | After the friend's **third** delivered box |

**Why this shape**

- **Total cost is $45, under your $55 ad cost.** A referred customer is cheaper than a paid one, and you're not overpaying for a channel that should be your cheapest.
- **The referrer is paid on the third box, not the first.** Many customers cancel after one or two boxes, so paying at signup would pay for people who leave. At $20 margin per box, a friend who cancels after one box costs you roughly break-even ($20 margin minus the $20 discount). Nothing is owed to the referrer in that case.
- **A friend who reaches box three has produced $60 of margin.** That is $15 above the $45 reward cost, and the number grows with every box after that. Customers who stay about 5 months should return far more.
- **The friend's discount is a cash-sized incentive, not a percentage.** "$20 off" is concrete and easy to say out loud. Dollar-off also reads as more generous than the equivalent percentage on a $68 box.
- **The referrer's reward is credit, not cash.** It keeps them ordering and costs you less than its face value, because part of it is covered by food cost you'd bear anyway.

**Two assumptions to check**

- The 10-referral cap and 12-month credit expiry are my additions to limit abuse and open-ended liability. Change them if they clash with your finance or legal rules.
- I don't have your real distribution of how many customers reach box three. If fewer than about 50% do, the economics tighten. Check this before launch.
