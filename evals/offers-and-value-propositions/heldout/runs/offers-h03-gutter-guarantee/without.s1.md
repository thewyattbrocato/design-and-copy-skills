## Recommended: The Halyard Promise

**If you're not happy, we make it right. Free.**

- **Clean-out guarantee.** If your gutters aren't flowing freely when we leave, we come back and fix it at no charge. We'll also run water through them with you to prove it.
- **Re-visit window.** If a clog or overflow shows up within **14 days** of a cleaning, call us and we'll return at no charge.
- **Price locked before we start.** You get the quote up front, and you never pay more than that without your OK.
- **Pay after the work.** You inspect it first, and you pay once you're satisfied.
- **Your property is protected.** If we damage something, we fix it or pay to have it fixed.

*Not satisfied after we've tried to make it right? You don't pay for that visit.*

## Why this one
- **"Pay after you're satisfied"** is the biggest trust-builder for an unknown business. It moves the risk from the homeowner to you.
- **"Run water through them with you"** is concrete proof, and it makes the guarantee feel real.
- **A short, specific window (14 days)** reads as more credible than "lifetime" or "100% satisfaction forever." Over-the-top promises make nervous people more suspicious.

## Before you publish, check these
1. **Damage line.** Only include it if you can honor it. If you have liability insurance, say so ("Fully insured"), and only if it's true. If you don't have it, get it before you advertise this, or drop that bullet.
2. **Re-visit window.** 14 days is a guess. Use a length you can afford, since each free return costs you a trip. Heavy leaf-drop weeks may need a note like "excluding new debris from storms or overhanging trees."
3. **Small repairs.** I limited the strong guarantee to cleanings. For repairs, I'd offer something like "workmanship guaranteed for 90 days," which is easier to honor than an open-ended promise. Pick a period you're comfortable with.
4. **Refund wording.** "You don't pay" is the strongest option. If a full refund on a big job worries you, say "we'll refund the visit" for cleanings only.
