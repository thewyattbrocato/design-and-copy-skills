Going from your description alone, the design has a few problems that will matter on each of your three uses. The biggest is the color.

**1. Contrast is too low for signage and printing.** #c9a227 on white is about **2.4:1**. Body text needs 4.5:1, and even large text needs 3:1. Thin hairlines drop the effective contrast further, because antialiasing blends them into the white. On a shop sign seen from across the street, and on a paper cup, the wordmark may look faded or disappear. A darker gold keeps the premium feel and reads much better. For example, **#8a6b16** is about **5:1** on white. Test a few values by viewing the logo at a distance or squinting at it.

**2. The favicon won't work with this design.** At 32px, hairlines and a tiny leaf will render as gray noise or vanish, and a script wordmark becomes unreadable. Make a separate mark for small sizes: a bold "P" monogram, or the leaf alone with heavier strokes and a simple shape. Keep the full wordmark for the sign and cups.

**3. Hairlines are risky in print.** Paper cups are printed or foil-stamped, and very fine strokes can fill in, break up, or fail to register. Most printers want a minimum stroke width around 0.5pt for ink, and foil needs more. Ask your printer for their minimum, and check whether the cup will be printed in gold ink or foil. Gold ink on kraft or colored cups will look different from gold on white.

**4. The leaf will disappear and may be misread.** A tiny leaf over the "i" will shrink to nothing at small sizes, and at a glance it can look like a stray accent mark. Either enlarge it so it's a clear element or drop it from small versions.

**5. Check script legibility at small sizes.** Script letterforms like the loops in "P," "b," and "y" are often the first thing to break down. View "Pemberly Tea" at about 16px and 1 inch wide. If the name isn't readable there, add weight or simplify the letterforms.

**Suggested next steps:**
- Build a darker-gold version of the full wordmark for the sign and cups.
- Make a simplified mark (monogram or leaf) with heavier strokes for the favicon.
- Confirm the minimum stroke and print method with your cup supplier.
