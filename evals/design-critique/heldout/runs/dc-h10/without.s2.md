Going on your description alone, the concept suits a tea shop. But the thin gold hairlines on white will cause problems at the sizes you listed.

**Main concerns**

1. **Contrast is low.** #c9a227 on white is about 2.4:1. Even a bold weight would look faint, and hairlines make it worse. On a sign viewed from across the street, it will wash out in daylight.
2. **Hairlines don't survive small sizes or printing.**
   - On paper cups, ink spread and printing tolerances can break up very fine strokes. Cups are also curved and often seen at an angle.
   - At 32px, the hairlines will drop below a pixel and render as faint, broken gray-gold smudges.
3. **The leaf over the "i" will be lost.** It's the most distinctive detail, but at small sizes it will vanish or turn into a blob. It also competes with the i-dot, so check that it doesn't read as an accent mark or a smudge.
4. **Script legibility.** Thin connecting strokes in a script are usually the first thing to disappear. "Pemberly" has several ascending and descending letters, so at reduced size it could read as a squiggle.

**What I'd do**

- **Thicken the strokes.** Keep the contrast between thick and thin strokes, but raise the thinnest weight enough that it holds up when the logo is shrunk to the smallest size you'll use. Test by shrinking it to about 1 inch wide and checking that every stroke is still visible.
- **Make a darker gold for use on white.** Something around #8a6d00 to #9a7b0c gets you much closer to readable contrast and still looks gold. Keep #c9a227 for use on dark backgrounds like deep green, charcoal, or navy, where it will look much better.
- **Don't use the wordmark as the favicon.** Make a separate mark, such as a bold "P" or just the leaf, on a solid background with a heavy stroke. Check it at 16px and 32px.
- **Prepare a simplified small-size version.** Give it a heavier weight, looser spacing, and a bigger leaf. Use it on cups, stickers, and anywhere the wordmark is under about 1.5 inches wide.
- **Make a one-color version.** Cups and signs often print in a single color, or use foil or paint. If the logo only works as a gold color with fine detail, it will be fragile in production.
- **Check the background.** White is the worst case for this gold. A cream, kraft, or dark cup would flatter it much more.

**Questions that would help me give better advice**

- What's the sign made of, and is it lit at night?
- Will the cups be printed or foil-stamped?
- Is there a tagline or icon alongside the wordmark?
