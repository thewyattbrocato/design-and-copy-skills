**The page feels amateur because nothing is ranked: everything is loud, so nothing stands out.** The page has several bold sizes, one color, and boxes around every section. Nothing tells the reader what matters most.

Assumptions: a visitor deciding whether to come and buy tickets, and a high-fidelity page. I haven't seen it, so I haven't checked contrast, spacing or the photos.

**Findings, in fix order**

1. **Three near-identical bold sizes flatten the hierarchy (major).**
   - The headline is 28px bold, the date is 26px bold and the intro's first sentence is 24px bold. They span only 4px, so they read as one heavy block rather than title, then date, then lede.
   - Bolding one sentence at 24px, then dropping to 16px mid-paragraph, makes the paragraph look like a mistake.
   - Fix: make the headline clearly dominant (about 40px+). Set the date at about 20px in a medium weight. Make the intro a plain 18px paragraph.

2. **Four buttons use four styles, with no primary action (major).**
   - "Get tickets" is outlined, "Vendor list" is filled, "Map" is a text link in a pill, and "Subscribe" is filled in a different red.
   - The outlined button is the weakest-looking one, yet it's the most important action. The filled "Vendor list" button beats it.
   - Fix: pick one primary style (filled) for "Get tickets" only. Use one secondary style (outline) for "Vendor list" and "Map". Make "Subscribe" a quiet link or secondary button in the same red as everything else.

3. **Five identical boxes make the sections look equally important (major).**
   - About, vendors, food, parking and tickets each get a 1px border plus a drop shadow. Tickets, the goal of the page, looks the same as parking.
   - The border and the shadow are also redundant with each other. Together they make the page look like a stack of default cards.
   - Fix: remove the boxes and separate sections with spacing and headings. Keep one emphasized treatment for tickets.

4. **A single dark red on pale pink gives the page no emphasis tools (moderate).**
   - Text, headings, buttons and borders are all #8b0000. Because color can't signal "this is the action" or "this is secondary", size and boxes end up doing all the work.
   - Fix: use near-black or a dark warm gray for body text. Reserve the red for the primary button and key accents. Put the background on a neutral warm off-white or a lighter tint.
   - Taste note: the red and pink palette itself isn't the problem. The lack of a neutral is.

5. **Vendor photos of different sizes (moderate).** Ragged edges are one of the clearest signs of a page that wasn't designed. Fix: crop all of them to one aspect ratio (for example 4:3) and show them in an even grid with a consistent gap.

6. **Everything is centered (minor to moderate).** Centered headings are fine, but centered multi-line paragraphs and lists have a ragged left edge, which makes them harder to scan. Fix: center only the hero. Left-align body text, with a line length of about 60–75 characters.

**What works and should stay**
- **Page structure:** the five sections (about, vendors, food, parking, tickets) cover what a visitor needs to know.
- **Dark red on pale pink:** it feels warm and seasonal, which suits a winter market. Keep the mood and fix how it's applied.
- **Date line as its own element:** the date is easy to find. Only its size and weight need to change.

Not checked: contrast of #8b0000 on your pink (the exact pink wasn't given), mobile layout, and the photo content.
