**The page reads as a generic SaaS template, not a boatyard product.** Nothing on it says haul-outs, launches, or travel lift, so a boatyard owner can't tell in five seconds that Slipway is for them. That gap is most likely what your cofounder is sensing.

**Findings, ranked**

1. **Blocker: the hero doesn't say what the product does.** "Work smarter, not harder" and "all-in-one platform that helps teams streamline their workflow and unlock their full potential" could describe any tool. Fix: name the job and the user, e.g. "Know who's on the travel lift today. Schedule haul-outs, launches, and lift time for your boatyard in one place."

2. **Blocker: the testimonial looks fake.** "John Doe, CEO at Acme Inc." is a placeholder. If it ships, it damages trust more than having no testimonial. Fix: use a real quote from a real yard with their name, yard, and location, or remove the section until you have one.

3. **Major: "Join thousands of happy customers" is an unsupported claim.** If you don't have thousands of customers yet, it's a false claim on a public page. Fix: state only what's true ("Used by N yards" or a specific result), or cut it.

4. **Major: the features are generic and interchangeable.** "Powerful / Simple / Secure" with "cutting-edge technology" and "anything you throw at it" says nothing a boatyard would recognize. Fix: make each card a yard task. For example: "Travel lift conflicts caught before they happen," "Haul-out and launch dates on one shared calendar," "Crew and customer access without giving away the whole schedule." Those also differentiate you from general scheduling tools.

5. **Major: no product visible.** There's no screenshot or short demo of the schedule. For a scheduling tool, seeing the calendar with a lift booked is the fastest proof. Fix: add a screenshot of a real-looking day schedule under the hero.

6. **Minor: three different calls to action.** "Get Started" (nav and hero), "Learn More," and "Sign Up Free" (bottom CTA). Pick one primary action and use it consistently. Keep "Learn More" only if it goes somewhere specific.

7. **Minor: the bottom CTA repeats the hero.** "Ready to get started?" adds nothing new. Use it to restate the concrete benefit, or to reduce risk ("Free for your first season, cancel anytime" if that's true).

8. **Minor: "Pricing" and "About" are in the nav, but the page never addresses price.** If small yards are price-sensitive, a line near the CTA ("Pricing per yard, no per-seat fees") would help. Only add it if it's true.

**What works and should stay**
- The section order (promise, features, proof, call to action) is a sound conversion flow.
- The copy is short, so the page is quick to scan.
- The footer is minimal and correct.

**Not checked:** visual rendering and CSS, since you excluded them. Taste-level points like tone are my judgment, not measured.

**Suggested order:** fix the hero first (it's the first thing anyone reads), then replace the placeholder testimonial, then rewrite the three feature cards around yard tasks. The rest can follow.
