**Verdict:** The page feels off because it reads as a generic SaaS template, and nothing on it mentions boatyards. A yard manager would not know within five seconds that this is for them. The placeholder testimonial also makes it look unfinished.

**Findings**

1. **Blocker: the page never says what Slipway does for a boatyard.** The hero ("Work smarter, not harder", "all-in-one platform… unlock their full potential") could describe project management, accounting, or a CRM. Haul-outs, launches, and travel lift never appear. *Fix:* Put the job in the H1, e.g. "Know who's on the travel lift today." Use the subhead to name the work: haul-outs, launches, and lift time across the yard.

2. **Blocker: placeholder testimonial.** "John Doe, CEO at Acme Inc." is visible template text. Anyone who sees it will assume the product isn't real yet. *Fix:* Replace it with a real quote from a yard owner or manager, with their name and yard. If you don't have one yet, remove the section until you do.

3. **High: features are adjectives, not features.** "Powerful: built with cutting-edge technology," "Simple," and "Secure" are claims with no content. A yard owner can't tell whether any of them solves their problem. *Fix:* Replace the three cards with the actual jobs, such as lift scheduling, haul-out and launch calendar, and conflict alerts. Confirm these match what the product does.

4. **High: unsupported social proof.** "Join thousands of happy customers" is a claim you need to be able to back up. If it isn't true yet, it damages trust more than it helps. *Fix:* Use a number you can verify ("Used by 14 yards on the Gulf Coast"), or drop the line.

5. **Medium: no product visual.** The page has no screenshot or schedule view, so nothing shows what the product looks like. For a scheduling tool, the schedule is the proof. *Fix:* Put a real screenshot of the lift/haul-out calendar in the hero.

6. **Medium: inconsistent calls to action.** The page uses "Get Started," "Learn More," and "Sign Up Free" for the same action. "Learn More" has no destination in the markup. *Fix:* Use one primary verb throughout, and point "Learn More" at a real section or page.

7. **Medium: "all-in-one" overpromises.** It implies breadth you may not have and hides the specific niche, which is the strength. *Fix:* Drop it and name the niche instead.

8. **Low: footer and contact.** For a B2B tool sold to people who may prefer a phone call, there's no contact path. *Fix:* Add an email, phone, or "Book a demo" link.

9. **Low (markup): links without `href`.** The nav items are `<a>` tags with no `href`, so they aren't focusable or keyboard-accessible. *Fix:* Add real `href` values to each link.

**Fix order:** 1 and 2 before anything goes live. Then 3, 4, and 5, which together make the page specific. Then 6 through 9.

**What to keep**
- The nav structure (Features, Pricing, About, Get Started) is familiar and easy to scan.
- The section rhythm (hero, features, proof, CTA, footer) is a sound skeleton. The problem is the content inside it.
- The primary and secondary button pair in the hero gives a clear first choice.
- Privacy and Terms are in the footer where visitors expect them.
