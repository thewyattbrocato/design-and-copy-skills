**Verdict:** The page could sell almost any B2B software. Nothing on it says boatyard, haul-out, travel lift, or launch, so a yard manager can't tell in five seconds what Slipway does or whether it's for them. That generic feel is probably what your cofounder is sensing. Fixing the copy will matter more than any layout change.

## Findings

**1. Blocker: The hero doesn't name the product or the customer.** "Slipway is the all-in-one platform that helps teams streamline their workflow and unlock their full potential" is a template sentence. Swap in "teams" and "workflow" and it describes project management software. Your audience is a yard owner or dock manager who needs to know the lift schedule is handled. Rewrite it around the specific job, for example: "Know who's on the travel lift today." Subhead: "Slipway schedules haul-outs, launches, and lift time for small boatyards, so nobody double-books the lift or leaves a boat on the hard." These are drafts. Every claim has to match what the product actually does.

**2. Blocker: The social proof will read as fake.** "John Doe, CEO at Acme Inc." is an obvious placeholder, and a visitor will notice it. "Join thousands of happy customers" is unverifiable, and for a new product it's probably untrue. Either use a real quote from a named yard with a real name and yard, or remove both. If you have a real number, such as yards or launches scheduled, state it specifically. Don't ship the placeholder.

**3. High: The features are adjectives, not jobs.** "Powerful," "Simple," and "Secure" describe the product in general terms, and "Built with cutting-edge technology" says nothing a yard owner cares about. Each card should name a problem the yard actually has, for example "Lift conflicts caught before the crew shows up," "Customers get launch-day updates without a phone call," and "Haul-out schedule on one screen, not a whiteboard." Confirm each one is a real feature before publishing. "Enterprise-grade security" is also wrong for this audience and should go.

**4. High: The hero has no product on it.** A scheduling tool is best shown, and the HTML has no image, screenshot, or demo of a schedule. Add a screenshot of the lift and yard board next to the headline. I can't see the visuals, so I'm judging from the markup alone.

**5. Medium: Three CTA labels for one action.** The nav says "Get Started," the hero says "Get Started," and the closing section says "Sign Up Free." Pick one. "Sign Up Free" is the most specific, but only use it if there is actually a free tier. "Learn More" is vague. "See a launch day" would tell them what they're clicking into.

**6. Medium: Filler language throughout.** "Work smarter, not harder," "unlock their full potential," "Everything you need," and "cutting-edge" are stock phrases. Each one makes the page feel generic, and cutting them costs nothing.

**7. Low: Navigation and footer gaps.** Pricing and About are linked, so confirm those pages exist. There's no "Log in" link for existing customers, and no Contact link, which matters for a sales-led tool with an on-site audience. The footer is fine as it is, apart from the placeholder copyright line, which is correct as written.

## Fix order

1. Rewrite the hero headline and subhead around haul-outs and lift scheduling (finding 1).
2. Remove the placeholder testimonial and "thousands" claim, or replace them with real, verifiable proof (finding 2).
3. Rewrite the three feature cards as yard problems you've confirmed the product solves (finding 3).
4. Add a real product screenshot to the hero (finding 4).
5. Settle on one CTA label and use it everywhere (finding 5).
6. Strip the filler phrases (finding 6), then add Log in and Contact (finding 7).

## What to keep

- **The section order** (hero, features, proof, closing CTA) is a sound skeleton. Keep it.
- **One primary action repeated down the page** is the right approach. Only the label needs fixing.
- **Three short feature cards** are a reasonable size for this content. Keep the structure and change the text.
- **The footer legal links** (Privacy, Terms) are required and correctly placed.

## Not checked

I didn't assess the visuals, the CSS (out of scope), mobile layout, or whether the features and claims are accurate. The copy suggestions assume what Slipway does, so verify them against the product before you publish.
