**Verdict:** The page reads as a template for any SaaS product. Nothing on it mentions boats, yards, haul-outs, launches, or the travel lift, so a boatyard manager can't tell in the first screen that this is for them. That gap is probably what your cofounder is sensing. The fake testimonial and unverified customer count are the most urgent problems.

**Findings, ranked**

1. **Blocker: placeholder social proof.** "John Doe, CEO at Acme Inc." is obviously a placeholder. If it ships, it's a fabricated testimonial. "Join thousands of happy customers" is an unverifiable claim, and it's false if you're pre-launch or have fewer customers. Remove both until you have real quotes and a real count you can state.

2. **Major: the hero names no product or job.** "Work smarter, not harder" and "all-in-one platform that helps teams streamline their workflow and unlock their full potential" could describe any tool. A draft that names the job: "Schedule every haul-out, launch, and travel-lift slot in one place." Test it against your actual buyer's words.

3. **Major: feature cards are adjectives with no content.** "Powerful: cutting-edge technology," "Simple: intuitive interface," and "Secure: enterprise-grade" give a yard owner nothing to check. "Enterprise-grade" also signals the wrong buyer for a small yard. Replace each with a concrete capability: for example, travel-lift conflict checks, a launch calendar the crew can see, or haul-out history per boat.

4. **Major: no product visual.** A scheduling tool sells on seeing the schedule. A screenshot of a real haul-out day would do more than any copy here.

5. **Moderate: CTAs are inconsistent and dead-end.** There are three labels for one action ("Get Started," "Sign Up Free," "Learn More"). "Learn More" goes nowhere specific. Pick one primary action and give the secondary link a real destination, such as "See a sample yard schedule."

6. **Minor: nav and footer.** There's no "Log in" for existing customers, and no contact or demo link for a buyer who wants to talk before signing up. "Everything you need" overpromises for a v1 product.

**Fix order:** 1 (remove unverifiable claims), 2 and 3 together (they're the core of the rewrite), 4 (screenshot), 5 (CTAs), 6 (nav and footer).

**Keep**
- The section order (hero, features, proof, CTA) is a sound skeleton for a short page.
- "Get Started" in the nav is in the right place.
- Three cards is a fine count if each card carries real content.

**Notes**
- I judged only the markup and copy. CSS and rendering were out of scope, so I didn't check layout or contrast.
- The copy suggestions are drafts. They need to be checked against how your buyers actually describe their work.
