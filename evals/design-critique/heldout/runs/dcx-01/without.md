Your cofounder's "off" is that the page could be for any software product. Nothing on it says boatyard, except the name. Your customers are yard managers who think in terms of haul-outs, the travel lift, tides, and a September rush of boats coming out of the water. The page never mentions any of that, so they'd have no reason to think it was built for them.

**What's hurting it, in order of importance**

1. **The hero says nothing.** "Work smarter, not harder" and "streamline their workflow and unlock their full potential" could sit on a thousand sites. A visitor should know within five seconds what Slipway does and who it's for. Right now they can't tell it's about boats.

2. **The feature cards are adjectives, not features.** "Powerful / Simple / Secure" tells a yard manager nothing. They want to know whether it stops two boats being booked for the same lift slot, or whether it handles a haul-out that gets pushed back by weather. Your real features are much more interesting than these cards.

3. **The social proof looks fake, and it may be hurting you.** "John Doe, CEO at Acme Inc." reads as a placeholder, and "Join thousands of happy customers" is a claim you probably can't back up. Small yard owners know each other, and a made-up claim costs you trust. One real quote from one real yard, with the name and the yard, is worth more than any of this. If you don't have one yet, cut the section.

4. **"Enterprise-grade security" is the wrong audience.** A 30-slip yard isn't buying enterprise anything. It signals that you don't know who you're selling to.

5. **There's nothing to see.** A scheduling tool needs a screenshot of the schedule, such as a week view with the travel lift, the haul-outs, and the launches. That one image would do more than all the copy.

6. **The calls to action are inconsistent.** "Get Started," "Learn More," and "Sign Up Free" are three different asks. Pick one primary action. For a niche B2B tool, "Book a 15-minute demo" or "Start a free season" often beats generic signup.

**What I'd do instead**

- **Hero:** state the pain directly. For example, "Know who's on the travel lift, and when." Add a subhead like "Scheduling for haul-outs, launches, and yard work, built for small boatyards." Put a real schedule screenshot beside it.
- **Features:** replace the cards with three or four concrete jobs, such as:
  - "Book the lift without double-booking it."
  - "Reschedule a whole day when the weather turns."
  - "Tell owners when their boat is going in."
  - "See the week's haul-outs and launches at a glance."

  Use your actual features here. Mine are guesses.
- **Proof:** use a real quote, or a line like "Built with [yard name]" if you have a design partner. If you have neither, be honest and say you're onboarding your first yards.
- **Closing CTA:** make it specific, like "Get your yard off the whiteboard." Whiteboards and spreadsheets are your real competition, so name them.
- **Tone:** write the way a yard manager talks. Plain and practical, with no "unlock" or "streamline."
