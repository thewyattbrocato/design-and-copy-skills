Your cofounder's instinct is right. The page could be for any SaaS product. Nothing on it says boats, yards, or a travel lift, and the name "Slipway" is the only clue. Small boatyard owners are practical, skeptical, and busy, so they'll bounce from this page in about five seconds.

**What's making it feel off**

1. **The hero doesn't say what the product does.** "Work smarter, not harder" and "streamline their workflow and unlock their full potential" are filler. A yard manager should know in one line that this is for scheduling haul-outs, launches, and the lift.
2. **The feature cards are adjectives, not features.** "Powerful / Simple / Secure" tells them nothing. "Cutting-edge technology" and "enterprise-grade security" are the wrong register for a 6-person yard. They want to know what it does on a Tuesday in April.
3. **The testimonial reads as fake.** "John Doe, CEO at Acme Inc." is obviously a placeholder, and "Highly recommend!" says nothing specific. If it ships like this, it hurts trust more than having no testimonial.
4. **"Join thousands of happy customers" is probably not true yet.** If you're early, this is the biggest trust risk on the page. Boatyards are a small, tight-knit industry where people talk, so a claim like that will get noticed.
5. **The CTAs are vague.** "Get Started" and "Learn More" don't say what happens next. A booked demo, a free trial, or "see a sample schedule" would all be clearer.

**What I'd do instead**

- **Hero:** Lead with the real problem. For example: *"Know who's on the lift, and when."* Follow with: *"Slipway schedules haul-outs, launches, and yard work for small boatyards, so the whiteboard, the texts, and the double-bookings go away."* Use a real screenshot of a schedule view next to it, not an abstract graphic.
- **Features:** Replace the three cards with the actual jobs the product does. These are guesses, so swap in what's true:
  - *Lift and crew calendar:* see the travel lift, the crew, and the tide window in one view.
  - *Customer notifications:* boaters get a text when their haul-out is confirmed or moves.
  - *Work-order tracking:* know which boats are on the hard, what's being done, and when each is ready to launch.
- **Testimonial:** Use a real quote from a real yard, with a name, yard name, and location. If you have a pilot yard, one specific line ("We cut our scheduling calls in half") beats any generic praise. If you have none yet, cut the section rather than fake it.
- **Social proof:** Replace "thousands of customers" with something true, such as "Used by 4 yards on the Chesapeake" or "Built with input from yard managers." Small and honest works well in a niche industry.
- **CTA:** Make it concrete: *"Book a 15-minute walkthrough"* or *"Start a free 30-day trial"*. Keep the same wording in the nav button, hero, and bottom CTA.
- **Voice:** Write like someone who has stood in a yard. Specific nouns (travel lift, haul-out, blocking, launch window, the hard) do more for credibility than any claim about the product.

**What's fine as is**

The structure of nav, hero, features, proof, CTA, and footer is a standard layout and works. The problem is the content, not the layout.
