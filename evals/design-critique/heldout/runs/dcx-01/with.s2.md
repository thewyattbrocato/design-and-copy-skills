**Findings, ranked**

1. **Blocker: the hero doesn't say what the product is.** "Work smarter, not harder" and "streamline their workflow and unlock their full potential" describe no product. The words boat, yard, haul-out, launch and travel lift appear nowhere on the page. Your actual offer is concrete and good: who has the lift, when, and what's coming out of the water Thursday. Lead with that. For example, the headline could name the job ("Know who's on the travel lift, and when"), and the subhead could name the pain it removes. Use whatever pain your customers actually describe, such as whiteboards, phone tags or double-booked lifts.

2. **Major: the feature cards are adjectives, not features.** "Powerful," "Simple" and "Secure" have 0 product nouns between them. "Enterprise-grade security" is also the wrong register for a small yard. Replace them with the three things the tool does, such as a lift schedule, haul-out and launch queues, and notifying owners. Each card should describe a task, not a quality.

3. **Major: the proof is placeholder-grade.** "John Doe, CEO at Acme Inc." reads as a template. "Thousands of happy customers" is hard to believe in a niche where the whole market is a few thousand yards. One real quote from a yard manager, with a name and yard, will do more than everything else in that section. If you have no customers yet, cut the testimonial and the "thousands" line rather than fake them. Say "built with yards in [region]" if that's true.

4. **Moderate: there's no product visual.** The page has no screenshot of a schedule. For a scheduling tool, the calendar view is the most persuasive thing you have, and a yard manager will understand it in seconds.

5. **Moderate: the calls to action are inconsistent.** The same action is labeled "Get Started" (twice) and "Sign Up Free," and "Learn More" says nothing about where it goes. Pick one label that says what happens next ("Start free" or "See the schedule"), and make the secondary link specific ("See how a haul-out is scheduled"). If there's a pricing page, the free claim should match it.
