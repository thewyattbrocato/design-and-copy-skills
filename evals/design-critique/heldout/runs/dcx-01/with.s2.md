**Your cofounder is right, and the cause is that nothing on the page says boatyard.** Replace "Slipway" with any other product name and every line still works. Someone who runs a yard can't tell this is for them.

**Findings, ranked**

1. **Blocker: the hero doesn't name the offer.** "Work smarter, not harder" and "streamline their workflow and unlock their full potential" say nothing about what the product does or who it's for. The words haul-out, launch, travel lift, yard and marina appear nowhere on the page. A yard manager lands here and has to guess.
   - Fix: say the job in the H1, for example "Know who's hauling out Thursday and who has the travel lift." Make the subhead one concrete sentence about the yard's day. Add a product screenshot of the schedule, since the page has no visual of the product at all.

2. **Major: the feature cards are adjectives, not features.** "Powerful / Simple / Secure" with "cutting-edge technology" and "enterprise-grade security" are claims every SaaS page makes, and none can be checked. They also don't match your buyer, a small yard with a few staff, where "enterprise-grade" may read as expensive or complicated.
   - Fix: replace them with three things the tool does. These could be a drag-and-drop haul-out and launch calendar, conflict warnings when two boats need the lift at once, and customer notifications when their boat is going in. Use your real features if they differ.

3. **Major: the proof looks fake, and one claim may be false.** "John Doe, CEO at Acme Inc." reads as placeholder text. "Join thousands of happy customers" is probably untrue for a niche tool. If it is, it costs you trust with a small, word-of-mouth industry.
   - Fix: use one real quote from a named yard manager, with the yard name and ideally a photo. If you have no customers yet, drop the testimonial and the "thousands" line. Say something true instead, such as "Now taking on founding yards."

4. **Moderate: the calls to action are inconsistent and vague.** The page has three labels for the same action: "Get Started", "Sign Up Free" and "Learn More". The secondary button promises nothing. Your goal may be a demo, a trial or a waitlist, and the page doesn't say which.
   - Fix: pick one primary action and label it with what happens ("Start free trial" or "Book a 15-minute demo"). Use that same label in the nav, hero and bottom CTA. Make the secondary button something specific, like "See the schedule view".

5. **Moderate: the page gives a yard owner nothing to size you up.** There is no mention of price range, setup effort, or whether it works on a phone on the dock or replaces the whiteboard. These are the questions a small-yard buyer has.
   - Fix: add a short "how it works" or FAQ block covering them.

6. **Minor: the markup has no `href` on any `<a>`.** Without `href`, they aren't keyboard-focusable and aren't real links. I assume this is mockup shorthand.

**What works and should stay**
- **Page order:** hero, features, proof, CTA is a sound, familiar sequence. The problem is the words, not the structure.
- **Navigation:** it's short, with one visually distinct action button.
- **Repeated CTA:** the sign-up button at the bottom gives scrollers a second chance without a new decision.
- **Footer:** it's minimal and has the legal links you need.
