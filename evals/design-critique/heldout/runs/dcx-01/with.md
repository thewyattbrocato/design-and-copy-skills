**Your cofounder is right, and the cause is the copy. Nothing on this page says Slipway is for boatyards.** Swap the name and you could paste it onto any SaaS product. A yard manager landing here can't tell what it does, who it's for, or why it beats the whiteboard or spreadsheet they use now.

I'm assuming the visitor is a yard owner or manager deciding whether to try it. I judged the markup and copy only, since you said the CSS is fine.

## Findings, in fix order

**1. Blocker: the hero doesn't name the offer.**
- "Work smarter, not harder" and "all-in-one platform that helps teams streamline their workflow and unlock their full potential" contain no noun a boatyard would recognize. There's no boat, haul-out, launch, travel lift, or schedule.
- The word "scheduling" appears nowhere on the page.
- Fix: say the job in the headline, for example "Know who's on the travel lift, and when." Follow it with one sentence on haul-outs, launches, and conflicts. Replace "Learn More" with something concrete, like "See a sample yard schedule."

**2. Major: the feature cards are generic, and the "Enterprise-grade security" card is wrong for the audience.**
- "Powerful / Simple / Secure" describes no actual feature, and each card fits any product. "Built with cutting-edge technology" and "Enterprise-grade" are claims nobody believes.
- A small yard isn't buying enterprise anything. That phrase signals the tool isn't for them.
- Fix: write three cards about real problems. Examples are double-booked lift slots, a haul-out that depends on tide or crane availability, and texting customers when their boat is launching. Use only features you actually have. A screenshot of the schedule view would do more than all three cards.

**3. Major: the testimonial and social proof look fake.**
- "John Doe, CEO at Acme Inc." reads as placeholder text, and "Join thousands of happy customers" is a claim a new small-yard tool can't back up. Visitors will discount the whole page.
- Fix: use a real quote from a real yard manager, with the yard's name and location, or cut the section. If you have a few pilot yards, "Used at 4 yards on Lake X" is more credible than "thousands." If you have no users yet, say you're onboarding early yards.

**4. Moderate: the calls to action don't match the product.**
- "Get Started" appears twice and "Sign Up Free" once, with no hint of what happens next. If there's a trial, a demo, or a call, say which.
- Boatyards are small and relationship-driven, so "Book a 15-minute demo" or "Try it on your next haul-out" may convert better than a self-serve signup. That's a hypothesis, not a fact.
- The nav's "Get Started" and the hero's should be the same action.

**5. Minor: no pricing or "about" content is visible here.**
- Nav links to Pricing and About are fine, but a small business wants to know the price quickly. If it isn't ready, say "Pricing: early-access, contact us" instead of leaving a dead end.

## What works and should stay
- **Structure:** hero, features, proof, CTA is a clear order, and the page is short. Keep it that way.
- **Name:** "Slipway" is specific and nautical. The page just doesn't use it.
- **Single primary action** per section, with the secondary style kept distinct.

## Fastest fix
Rewrite the hero and the three cards in boatyard language, add one real screenshot, and replace the testimonial. That likely removes most of the "off" feeling. To confirm, show the new hero to 3–5 yard people for five seconds and ask what the product does.
