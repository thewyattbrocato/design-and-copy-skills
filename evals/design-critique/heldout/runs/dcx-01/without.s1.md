Your cofounder's instinct is right. Nothing on the page says it's for boatyards. Swap "Slipway" for any other name and every line still works, so a yard manager lands on it and sees a generic SaaS template. The word "boat" never appears.

**What's off**

1. **The hero says nothing.** "Work smarter, not harder" and "streamline their workflow and unlock their full potential" could describe any product. The visitor has to guess what Slipway does. Name the job: who's hauling out, when, and who has the travel lift.

2. **The features are interchangeable.** "Powerful," "Simple," and "Secure" are filler. "Enterprise-grade security" is the worst of them, because a 6-person yard isn't shopping for that. Describe what the tool does for them, such as:
   - Travel lift and crew booked without conflicts
   - Haul-out and launch windows tied to tides and weather
   - Spring launch and fall haul-out rushes
   - Customers who get a text when their boat is going in

3. **The testimonial reads as fake.** "John Doe, CEO at Acme Inc." is obviously a placeholder, and it undermines everything around it. If you don't have a real quote yet, cut the section. A made-up one is worse than none. One real line from a yard owner beats everything else on the page.

4. **The CTA claims things you probably can't back up.** "Join thousands of happy customers" is a stretch for a niche product. Visitors will notice, and it costs you trust. Either use a real number, even a small one ("Running at 12 yards in New England"), or drop the claim.

5. **The calls to action are muddled.** The nav and hero say "Get Started," the bottom says "Sign Up Free," and "Learn More" leads nowhere specific. Pick one primary action and use the same wording everywhere. For a small-yard tool, "Book a 15-minute demo" or "Try it free" will probably do better than "Get Started." Both need Pricing to be clear.

6. **There's no product on the page.** A scheduling tool needs a screenshot of the schedule, even a rough one. A visual of the lift calendar will explain more than any paragraph.

**A rewrite of the top to show the direction**

> **Stop double-booking the travel lift.**
> Slipway is scheduling for boatyards: haul-outs, launches, crew, and the lift, all on one board your whole yard can see.
> [Book a demo] [See how it works]

Then three feature cards with concrete outcomes, a screenshot, one real testimonial, and a CTA that says something specific ("Get your spring launch season off the whiteboard").

**Underlying problem:** the copy is written for "teams" in general. Write it for one person, the yard manager juggling a whiteboard, a spreadsheet, and a ringing phone. Use their words, like haul-out, launch, lift, blocking, and yard slots. That will fix the "off" feeling more than any layout change.
