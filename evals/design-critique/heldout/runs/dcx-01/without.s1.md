Your cofounder's instinct is right. The page is generic. Nothing on it says boatyard. Swap "Slipway" for any SaaS name and every line still works, which is why it feels off.

**What's wrong**

1. **The hero says nothing.** "Work smarter, not harder" and "streamline their workflow and unlock their full potential" could describe any product. A yard manager landing here can't tell what Slipway does or whether it's for them. Your best material is specific and you haven't used it: haul-outs, launches, the travel lift, who's where and when.

2. **The feature cards are filler.** "Powerful / Simple / Secure" are adjectives, not features. "Cutting-edge technology" and "enterprise-grade security" are especially mismatched for a small yard. Nobody running a 40-slip yard is shopping for enterprise anything.

3. **The testimonial reads as fake.** "John Doe, CEO at Acme Inc." is a placeholder, and the quote is generic anyway. If you don't have a real one yet, remove the section. A fake one costs you trust.

4. **"Join thousands of happy customers" is probably untrue.** If it is, that's a credibility problem. Small boatyard owners talk to each other, and a claim like that will sound wrong to them.

5. **The CTAs are vague.** "Get Started" and "Learn More" don't say what happens next. The CTA section also says "Sign Up Free" while the others say "Get Started". Pick one, and make sure it matches your real pricing.

6. **"Teams" and "workflow" are the wrong audience language.** Your users think in boats, slots, tides, crew, and the lift. Use their words.

**What to do instead**

- **Hero:** state the job plainly. For example: *"Know where every boat is and when the travel lift is free."* Follow with one sentence on haul-outs, launches, and scheduling for small yards. Add a real screenshot of the schedule view if you can.
- **Features:** replace the three adjectives with three concrete problems you solve. For example:
  - Schedule the travel lift without double-booking it.
  - Tell customers when their boat is going in or coming out.
  - See the week's haul-outs and launches at a glance.
  
  Use whatever is actually true of your product.
- **Proof:** use a real quote from a real yard, with name and yard. If you have none yet, use a short founder note on why you built this, or a line like "Built with [yard name]". That's more convincing than a fake endorsement.
- **CTA:** make it specific, such as "Book a 15-minute demo" or "Start a free trial", whichever matches how you actually sell.
- **Also missing:** a short "how it works", pricing basics or a hint of them, and some indication of who's behind it. Small-yard owners buy from people they trust.

**The underlying cause:** the page was written as if the product were generic software. Your advantage is that you understand a very specific business. Every section should sound like it was written by someone who has stood on a yard's hardstand.
