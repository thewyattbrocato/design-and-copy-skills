It's not fine yet. It's clean and nothing in it is wrong, but it could be the pricing section for any SaaS product. Nothing says pottery, wheels, kilns, or studios, and that's the biggest problem.

## Biggest issues

**1. "Up to 100 items" means nothing to your buyer.**
A studio owner doesn't know what an "item" is. Is it a member, a booking, a wheel, a piece in a firing? Pricing tiers should be measured in something the owner already counts, like members, wheels and kilns, or locations. This is also the main thing that decides which plan someone picks, so it needs to be clear.

**2. The tiers don't show what each plan is for.**
- "Perfect for getting started" and "Everything in Basic, plus more" are filler. "Plus more" tells me nothing.
- "Advanced analytics" is the only Pro feature that's a real feature, and it's vague. Do you mean revenue per kiln firing, studio utilization, or member retention?
- Think about what studios care about: booking wheel and kiln time, member billing, waitlists, class sign-ups, firing fees, and a member-facing mobile view. Put the ones that apply into the tiers.

**3. Enterprise probably doesn't fit your market.**
Pottery studios rarely call themselves "large organizations." If the real top tier is multi-location studios or ceramics programs at colleges and community centers, say that. "For studios with multiple locations" will get clicks that "advanced needs" won't.

**4. The CTAs ignore your best selling point.**
Both paid plans say "Select," but your offer is a free trial with no card. Use "Start free trial" on Basic and Pro. Move "14-day free trial, no credit card required" up near the heading. It's currently the smallest, last line, and it's the strongest line on the page.

**5. The heading and subheading are filler.**
"Simple, transparent pricing" is on thousands of pricing pages, and "Choose the plan that's right for you" says nothing. Try something specific, like "Plans that grow with your studio," or state the benefit: "Less time on the booking spreadsheet, more time at the wheel."

## FAQ

All four answers are non-answers. They read as if the questions were written to fill space.

- **"How does it work?" / "Simply sign up…"** repeats the CTA and explains nothing. Describe what a studio owner actually does: add wheels and kilns, set member rates, open booking.
- **"Is my data secure? Absolutely. We take security very seriously."** This is a red flag for anyone who cares about security. Either give specifics (where data is stored, how payments are handled, whether you use Stripe) or cut the question.
- **"Can I cancel anytime?"** Add what happens next: Is there a refund? Can they export member data? Does access last until the end of the billing period?
- **"Do you offer support?"** It repeats the pricing table. Say what channels you have and how fast you reply.

**Questions I'd add instead** (these are what studio owners actually ask):
- What happens when the trial ends?
- Can I bring over my existing member list?
- Do you handle member payments, and are there processing fees?
- Can members book from their phones?
- Can I switch plans later?
- What counts as an "item" (or whatever replaces it)?

## Smaller notes
- Tone drifts. "Yes!" and "Absolutely" are enthusiastic, while the rest is flat. Pick one voice.
- There's no annual option or discount. If you plan to offer one, add it now, because it's awkward to bolt on later.
- The $9 to $29 jump is big, with only "unlimited" and "analytics" to justify it. Once the tiers are measured in real units, this should be easier to defend.

## What to keep
The three-tier structure, the "Most Popular" badge on Pro, "Contact Sales" for the custom tier, and the no-credit-card trial are all right. Keep them.
