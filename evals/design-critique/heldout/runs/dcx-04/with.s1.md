Your structure is fine: three tiers, a highlighted middle plan, and a trial line. The problem is that the copy is generic. Swap "Kilnbook" for any other SaaS name and every line still works. Nothing says pottery, wheels, kilns, or members, which are the things that would make a studio owner feel you understand their problem.

## Biggest issues

**1. "Items" is undefined.**
This is the most important fix. A studio owner can't tell if an item is a member, a booking, a wheel, or a kiln load. The limit is the main thing separating Basic from Pro, so they can't judge whether they'd outgrow Basic. Use a unit they already think in, like members, bookable resources (wheels and kilns), or bookings per month.

**2. Tier descriptions are filler.**
- "Perfect for getting started" and "Everything in Basic, plus more" tell the reader nothing.
- "Advanced analytics" is vague. Say what it shows, such as kiln utilization, peak wheel hours, or revenue per member.
- "Priority support" and "Email support" would be stronger with a response time.

**3. Heading and subheading are boilerplate.**
"Simple, transparent pricing" is on thousands of pricing pages. "Choose the plan that's right for you" is a placeholder. This is a spot for something specific, like "Plans for studios of every size" with a line about the trial or about what's included.

**4. The CTAs undersell your best offer.**
Your strongest point is the free trial with no card, but the buttons say "Select". Use "Start free trial" on Basic and Pro. Also clarify whether Enterprise gets a trial, since "All plans include" conflicts with "Contact Sales".

**5. Enterprise may not fit your market.**
Most pottery studios are small, so "large organizations with advanced needs" may not match anyone you sell to. If you do have multi-location studios, schools, or co-ops, name them ("Multiple locations") instead.

## The FAQ

All four answers are non-answers. "Simply sign up and start using it" and "We take security very seriously" are the kind of lines people skim past, and the security one can read as evasive. Specifics build trust:

- **Security:** say how payments are handled (for example, through Stripe, with no card data stored by you) and how member data is protected and backed up.
- **Cancel:** can they export their data, and what happens to existing bookings and members?
- **Support:** "friendly team" is a claim. Hours and response time are facts.
- **How does it work:** cut it, or turn it into something concrete like "How long does setup take?" or "Can I import my member list?"

**Questions to add** (the ones I'd expect studio owners to ask):
- What happens when the trial ends?
- What happens if I go over my plan's limit?
- Can I switch plans later?
- Can members book and pay online?
- Does it handle firing fees, class vs. open-studio time, or waitlists?
- Are there transaction fees?

## Smaller notes
- "Simply" in the first answer can come across as dismissive when someone is struggling with setup.
- Consider annual pricing, or say outright that it isn't offered.
- Keep "No credit card required". It's concrete and useful.
