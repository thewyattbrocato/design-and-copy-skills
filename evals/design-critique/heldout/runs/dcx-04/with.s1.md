*Assuming the audience is a studio owner or manager evaluating the software, and that this is the final copy with no visuals to judge.*

## Findings, ranked

**1. Major: the tiers don't map to a studio's world.**
- "Up to 100 items" and "Unlimited items" never say what an item is. A bookable wheel? A member? A kiln firing? An owner can't tell whether they'd hit the cap.
- Studios usually size themselves by members, wheels or kilns, or bookings per month. Pick the real unit and name it, e.g. "Up to 40 members" or "Up to 12 wheels and kilns".
- "Advanced analytics" is the only thing that justifies paying 3× more, and it's undefined. Say what it tells them, e.g. "See which wheels and kiln slots sit empty."
- "Everything in Basic, plus more" is filler. List what the extra is.

**2. Major: the FAQ answers aren't answers.**
- "How does it work?" gets "sign up and start using it," which says nothing. The real answer is something like "Add your wheels and kilns, set member rules, and members book slots online."
- "Is my data secure?" gets "Absolutely. We take security very seriously." That is the standard line that makes people more suspicious. Give one concrete fact, such as payment handling, backups or who owns the data.
- "Do you offer support?" repeats the pricing table without adding hours, channel or response time.
- "Can I cancel anytime?" is the best of the four. It still skips the questions people actually have: what happens to data after cancelling, and whether there's a refund on the current month.

**3. Major: the FAQ is missing the questions a studio owner will have.** This is my guess at what they'd ask, not tested.
- Do members pay through it, or only book?
- Can I limit bookings by membership level?
- Can it handle kiln firing schedules and glaze-load fees?
- Can I import my existing members?
- What happens when I exceed the Basic limit?

**4. Moderate: the headings and subheads say nothing.** "Simple, transparent pricing" and "Choose the plan that's right for you" are what every pricing page says. The pricing is simple, so you don't need to claim it. Use the space for something specific, e.g. "Booking software for studios that rent wheel and kiln time."

**5. Moderate: Enterprise is a weak fit for this market.** "Large organizations" and "account manager" are unlikely to match many pottery studios. If you do have multi-location studios or ceramics programs at colleges, say so ("Multiple studios or locations"). If you don't, test whether the tier is worth the clutter.

**6. Minor: the trial line works, but there's a gap.** "14-day free trial. No credit card required" is clear and I'd keep it. Say what happens at day 15, such as "then choose a plan or your account pauses." Also, the Select button on a no-card trial is ambiguous. "Start free trial" says what it does.

**7. Minor: the "Most Popular" badge is unsupported.** If it's true, fine. If you have no customer base yet, the claim is unearned and a savvy buyer may notice. "Best for most studios" is a safer way to point at the middle plan.

## What works, keep it

- **Three tiers with the middle one highlighted.** It's a familiar layout that guides the eye to Pro.
- **Prices shown for the two self-serve plans.** Hiding them would be worse.
- **The trial and no-card line.** It removes the biggest worry before signup.
- **Short copy.** The structure is easy to scan. Specifics will fill it out without making it long.
