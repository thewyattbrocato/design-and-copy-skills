Your structure works. Three tiers, a clear "most popular" marker, and a no-card trial are all standard and effective. The main problem is that the copy could describe almost any SaaS product. Pottery studio owners need to see their own world in it: members, wheel and kiln time, bookings.

## Pricing

- **"Up to 100 items" is the biggest clarity problem.** What's an item? A wheel? A kiln? A member? A booking slot? Studio owners will stop and wonder, and some will leave. Say what it counts, e.g. "Up to 100 members" or "Up to 4 wheels and 2 kilns."
- **"Everything in Basic, plus more" is filler.** Name the features: "Everything in Basic, plus unlimited members, automated reminders, and advanced analytics."
- **"Enterprise: For large organizations"** probably doesn't match your buyer. A pottery studio is rarely a large organization. Try "For multi-location studios or co-ops" or whatever Enterprise actually means for you. "Custom integrations" should name the systems (Stripe, QuickBooks, Square?) or it's an empty promise.
- **"Select" is a weak CTA.** Since you offer a free trial, "Start free trial" is clearer and more motivating. Enterprise's "Contact Sales" is fine.
- **Heading and subheading are generic.** "Simple, transparent pricing" is standard. You could try "Pricing that fits your studio" or drop the subheading, which says nothing. "Choose the plan that's right for you" is filler.
- **"Perfect for getting started"** is vague. Say who it's for: "For solo studios with a small membership."
- **Clarify billing:** monthly only or annual too? Does the trial apply to Enterprise? Are prices in USD and do they exclude tax?

## FAQ

- **"How does it work?"** doesn't answer the question. Explain the flow for your users: "Members book wheel and kiln time online. You set rates and hours, and Kilnbook handles reminders and payments." Drop "Simply," which is filler, and "right away," which conflicts with the setup step.
- **"Can I cancel anytime?"** The answer should cover what happens next: when access ends, whether there are refunds, and whether members' data can be exported. The exclamation point reads as salesy.
- **"Is my data secure?"** is the weakest answer. "Absolutely. We take security very seriously" gives a skeptical buyer nothing, and it makes them trust you less. Name real facts: where data is hosted, how it's encrypted, how payments are processed, and who can see member information. If you don't have these yet, that's worth knowing before launch.
- **"Do you offer support?"** Say what's included and tie it to the tiers. Basic gets email support, Pro gets priority support. Add hours or response time if you can. "Our friendly team" describes you rather than the support you provide.

## Missing questions

Your buyers will likely ask these, and the answers are probably the ones that close the sale:
- Can members book themselves, or does the studio book for them?
- Do members pay through Kilnbook? What are the fees?
- Can I import my existing member list and schedule?
- What happens when the trial ends? Do I get charged automatically?
- Can I switch plans later?
- Does it work on phones for members?

## Overall

Replace the generic phrases with studio-specific ones, and make the FAQ answers concrete instead of reassuring. Most of your copy's weakness is that it promises things without saying what they are. Specifics will do more to build trust here than enthusiasm.
