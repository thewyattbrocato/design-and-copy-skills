**Ship B, with its content and a visual pass from A.** I'm fairly confident, because the difference is in the copy and the next action, and polish can't make up for that.

**Why B wins**
- **A doesn't say what to do.** "Get Started" could mean book a walk, add a dog, or finish onboarding. "Nothing here yet" and "your journey" would fit any app. A first-time owner can't tell what the button does.
- **B names a person, a place, a time and a payoff.** "Marta," "Tuesday and Thursday mornings around Pell Park," and "a photo halfway through" make the first booking feel concrete. "Book Marta for Tuesday" is one tap to the goal.
- **B answers the trust question.** The "vetted and local" link addresses the main worry of anyone handing over a dog.

**What I'd change in B (in order)**
1. **Fix the heading, which is medium severity.** "No walks booked this week" is wrong for a brand-new user with no history, and it reads as a failure. Use a branch: new users get something like "Book your first walk," and returning users get B's current wording.
2. **Make one button primary, which is medium severity.** The two buttons look like the same default web buttons, so they compete. Fill "Book Marta for Tuesday" and make "See other walkers" a text or outline button. Use A's full-width filled style for the primary.
3. **Handle Marta being unavailable, which is high severity if it's hard-coded.** The copy depends on her actual availability and on her being a good match for this owner. Generate it from real data, and fall back to a generic "Browse walkers near you" state when no walker fits. Otherwise the button can offer a slot that doesn't exist.
4. **Fix the trust link, which is low to medium severity.** "Why only a few walkers?" raises a doubt the user may not have had. Reword it to the reassurance, such as "Every walker is vetted and local," and make it a clear secondary link.
5. **Replace the emoji and the plain styling, which is low severity and partly taste.** The default-looking buttons and the small emoji read as unfinished. Use A's spacing, type scale and button style, and swap the emoji for a small illustration, such as A's paw or a dog, but keep it left-aligned or modest so the text stays dominant.
