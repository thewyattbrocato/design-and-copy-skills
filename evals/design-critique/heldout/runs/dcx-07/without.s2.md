**Ship Version B, with edits.**

**Why B wins**

- **It's specific.** A names nothing: no walker, no time, no next step. B tells the user who's available, when, and where, so the empty state becomes a prompt to act.
- **It sells the product.** "She'll send you a photo halfway through" is the emotional hook of a dog-walking app, and A has nothing like it.
- **Its CTA is concrete.** "Book Marta for Tuesday" is a one-tap path to a booking. "Get Started" is vague, and the user is already in the app, so it's unclear what it starts.
- **A's copy is filler.** "Nothing here yet" and "We can't wait to see where your journey takes you!" could be on any app. "Journey" is especially off for a dog walk.

A's real strength is visual polish. That's the cheaper problem to fix.

**What I'd change in B**

1. **Apply A's visual system.** Keep B's words, but use the design team's spacing, type scale, and button styles. Default-looking buttons will undercut trust in an app where people hand over their dog and their home address. Replace the emoji with a small illustration, perhaps A's paw tile.
2. **Make one button primary.** Make "Book Marta for Tuesday" filled and "See other walkers" a secondary or text style. Two equal buttons make users choose.
3. **Handle the Marta dependency.** This copy assumes the user has a known walker with a known schedule. For brand-new users with no history, fall back to something like "Find a walker near [neighborhood]" with a nearby walker featured. Check whether B's copy would show up for someone who has never booked, because "Marta" would mean nothing to them.
4. **Make the availability real.** Pull Marta's slots from live data. If the Tuesday slot is gone or has passed, the CTA must update. A stale "Book Marta for Tuesday" would be worse than A's generic copy.
5. **Reconsider the heading.** "No walks booked this week" is accurate but flat. Something like "No walks this week yet" is slightly warmer. This is minor.
6. **Move or reword the vetting link.** "Why only a few walkers?" reads defensively and raises a doubt the user didn't have. Reframe it as reassurance, such as "All walkers are vetted and local," and make it a quiet footnote or a link to the safety info.
7. **Check the tab's other states.** If the Walks tab also shows past walks, "this week" may mislead someone who walked last week. Make sure the heading fits that case.

**Before Friday**

Items 1, 2, 5, and 6 are quick. Items 3 and 4 are the real work. If they won't fit in the timeline, ship B with a generic fallback (for example, "Book a walk" and "See walkers") for users without a Marta match. That still beats A.
