**Ship Version B**, with a few fixes before Friday.

Version A is a generic empty state. "Nothing here yet" and "where your journey takes you" don't tell the user anything they can act on, and "Get Started" is vague about what it starts. Version B tells the user what's happening this week, gives one specific next step, and answers the obvious trust question. The founder's copy reads like she knows the customer.

**Changes to make to B:**

1. **Make the Marta line real or cut it.** It only works if it's generated from actual availability. If no walker is free, show the generic version instead of a fake suggestion. A wrong "Marta's free Tuesday" will hurt more than a plain empty state.
2. **Verify the photo promise.** "She'll send you a photo halfway through" is a product claim. If it isn't built, remove it. Don't ship a promise the app doesn't keep.
3. **Handle first-time users.** "No walks booked this week" fits someone with history. A brand-new user with no walks and no local walkers yet needs a different state. Keep A's structure for that case, but use B's voice: say what Brindle does and give one next step.
4. **Replace the emoji** with the brand's own icon or a small illustration in the same style as the rest of the app. Keep the restraint. The emoji is the one part that looks unfinished.
5. **Restyle the buttons** to the design system's primary and secondary buttons. "Book Marta for Tuesday" should be primary and "See other walkers" secondary. The function is right; the default web look isn't.
6. **Rewrite the trust link.** "Why only a few walkers?" reads like a question the app anticipated because it's worried. Try a plain line instead: "All walkers are vetted and local." Or open a short sheet explaining the vetting process.

**Test before Friday:** show B to two or three people who've never used Brindle, and ask what they think happens next. If they can say "book Marta" without prompting, the copy is working.
