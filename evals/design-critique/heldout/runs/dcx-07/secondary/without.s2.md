**Ship Version B, but rebuild its visual layer with A's polish.** B tells the user what to do next, in specific terms. A tells them nothing except that the screen is empty, and its "Get Started" button doesn't say what it starts. A's visual craft is worth keeping, but a clean empty state with no next step is the weaker product choice.

Changes I'd make before it ships:

1. **Make the recommendation data-driven, with a fallback.** "Marta" and "Tuesday and Thursday mornings" can't be hardcoded. Show a real walker and slot only when the data exists. Otherwise fall back to a single "See walkers near you" button.
2. **Split new users from returning users.** "No walks booked this week" fits someone who has booked before. A new user with zero history needs something like "Your walks will appear here. Find a walker to get started," with one button.
3. **Confirm the photo promise is real.** "She'll send you a photo halfway through" is a product commitment. If walkers don't reliably do this, cut the line.
4. **Reword the trust link.** "Why only a few walkers?" puts the scarcity first. Try "How we vet walkers" or "Every walker is vetted and local. Learn more." It should answer the doubt without raising it.
5. **Restyle the buttons.** Use one filled primary button in the brand style and a quieter secondary, not default web buttons. Replace the emoji with A's paw-print mark or drop it. Keep B's left alignment and short copy.

Keep "See other walkers" as the secondary action. It gives people a way out when the suggested walker or time doesn't fit, and that matters more than the illustration.

I'm judging from the copy and mockups only, so check the walker-availability and photo-sending behavior against the real product before Friday.
