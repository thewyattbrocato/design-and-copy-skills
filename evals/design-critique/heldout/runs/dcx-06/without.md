I think the problem is that no page is wrong, but the pages don't agree with each other. Here are the gaps, most important first.

**1. The cancellation policy comes too late and is easy to miss.**
- It first appears as small print under the pay button on page 3, after the person has chosen a court and entered their details.
- "Late cancellations are not refunded" is the most consequential line in the flow. It should show on page 1 next to the price, or at least be a visible line above the button on page 3.
- It also never says *how* to cancel, and the confirmation page doesn't mention it either. A policy with no way to act on it feels like a trap.

**2. The price changes between pages.**
- Page 1 shows $24, and the person pays $25.92. The tax only appears on page 3.
- That's a small jump, but it's the classic "surprise at checkout" pattern. Show "$24 + tax" or the total up front.

**3. Guests are collected and then vanish.**
- Page 2 asks for the number of guests, but nothing afterward uses it. The price doesn't change, and neither the payment summary nor the confirmation mentions it.
- Either it matters (capacity, per-guest fees, a guest policy) and should be shown back to the user, or the field is unnecessary.

**4. The details don't carry through.**
- Sport is a filter on page 1 but never appears again. A pickleball player never sees "Pickleball" confirmed.
- The name and email from page 2 aren't shown on the payment page.
- The confirmation page, the one people screenshot or return to, shows none of the booking details: no court, date, time, amount paid or email address. It says only "#HC-2231 is on its way to your inbox", with no way to tell *which* inbox.

**5. The format and wording drift.**
- Time is "18:00-19:00" on page 1 and "6:00 PM to 7:00 PM" on page 3.
- Date is "Sat 14 Nov" on page 1 and "Sat 14 November" on page 3.
- The vocabulary is split between "reserve", "booking", "reservation", "purchase" and "order". The first and last pages use the club's language, while the payment and confirmation pages ("Complete purchase", "Order confirmed", "Thanks for your purchase!") use generic retail wording.

That last point is probably what the manager is sensing. The first half feels like a neighborhood club, and the second half feels like a template web store. Nothing tells the customer they're now holding a court at Hollis Court, so the end of the flow feels cold and unfinished.

**Suggested fixes, in order**
1. Surface the cancellation policy on page 1, and put it in readable text on page 3.
2. Show the total, or "+ tax", on page 1.
3. Add a booking summary (sport, court, date/time, guests, name) to pages 3 and 4.
4. Pick one time and date format. I'd use 12-hour time if your members do.
5. Standardize the wording on "booking" or "reservation", for example "Confirm and pay" and "You're booked!".
6. On the confirmation page, add the email address, a "how to cancel or change" link, and "Add to calendar".
