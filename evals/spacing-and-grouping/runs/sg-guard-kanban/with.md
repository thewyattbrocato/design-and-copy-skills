For this board, the reviewer's general point is right, but it doesn't apply to the task cards.

**Keep the cards.** Each task is a discrete object that people pick up, drag between columns, open, and compare. That is the case where a card is the right grouping device. The surface tells users "this whole thing moves as one unit" and gives them a drag target. It also separates the task from the column behind it. Plain space can't do those jobs well, especially in a dense column where every task is a short block of text.

**Where the reviewer is probably right.** Overuse tends to show up around the cards, not in the cards themselves:
- **Column containers.** If each column is also a bordered, shadowed box, you have cards inside cards. Use a light tint or just space and a header for the column, and leave the task cards as the only raised surfaces.
- **Stacked treatments.** Border, shadow, and fill together is too much. Keep the border or a very soft shadow, plus the fill. The shadow can then appear only on hover or while dragging, which also reads as "lifted".
- **Inside the card.** Don't wrap labels, assignee, or due date in their own boxes. Group them with space.
- **Rest of the product.** Settings sections, summary panels, and similar areas that aren't draggable objects are good candidates for headings and space instead of boxes. That is likely what the reviewer is reacting to.

**Spacing check.** The gap between cards should be at least as large as the card padding, or the cards blur into one mass. Padding inside a card should be smaller than the gap between columns.

Summing up: don't remove the cards, flatten everything around them, and apply the reviewer's advice to the non-object areas of the product.
