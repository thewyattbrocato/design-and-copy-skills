**Use extra space and headings only.** Skip the cards and the horizontal lines.

- **Cards are for objects.** A card suits something users open, select, or act on as a whole. Your sections are just groups of fields. Boxing three short sections adds borders and padding that compete with the inputs, and the form ends up looking heavier than it is.
- **Space can separate three sections.** The form is short: 4, 5, and 3 controls. Rules help in dense lists or tables, and this form isn't one.
- **Make spacing within a section clearly smaller than spacing between sections.**
  - Within a section, the gap between fields should be about 16 px.
  - Between sections, leave about 40 to 48 px, so roughly 2 to 3 times the within-section gap.
  - Put more space above each heading than below it, about 2:1. A section heading should sit closer to its own fields than to the section above it.
  - Pick every value from one small scale (for example 4, 8, 16, 24, 40) and avoid one-off numbers.
  - Set the gaps on the container (`gap`, or a stack) instead of putting a bottom margin on every child. That way the last item doesn't add an extra gap.

**When to add something**
- If the headings still don't separate the sections enough, add a single quiet hairline above each section heading, with space on both sides. Don't add a line between the sections and also wrap them in boxes.
- The Preferences toggles are the one place a rule might help. If each toggle has a description and the rows feel loose, thin dividers between the rows can help the scanning. That is a choice for that list, not for the whole form.
- Use cards only if the sections become independently saveable or collapsible. Then each one is a real object, and every section should get the same surface.

**Check:** Blur or squint at the form. You should see exactly three clusters. If two sections merge, increase the space between sections instead of adding a device.
