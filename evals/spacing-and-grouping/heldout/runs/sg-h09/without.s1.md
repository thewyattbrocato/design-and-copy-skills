**Recommendation: headings plus extra space, with no cards or lines.**

For 12 fields in three short sections, whitespace and headings are enough to group them. Cards and rules add visual weight the form doesn't need.

- **Cards** suit sections that are long, independently editable, or saved separately. Nesting fields inside boxes adds borders and padding, and with only 3 to 5 fields per section it makes the form look busier than it is.
- **Horizontal lines** separate sections but don't label them. You still need headings, so the lines are redundant. They also tend to look like "end of form" markers.
- **Space and headings** keep the form light and scannable. Proximity does the grouping: put about 12 to 16px between fields in a section and about 32 to 40px between sections. Make each heading clearly stronger than the field labels.

**When I'd change this:**
- If each section has its own Save button, or users edit them separately (a settings page), use cards.
- If the form grows to 6 or more sections or gets long, add thin dividers or cards to help people keep their place.
- If the page background is busy or the form sits among other content, a card around the whole form (not each section) helps it stand out.

**Practical notes:**
- Add a one-line description under a heading only if the section's purpose isn't obvious. Preferences might need one. Personal info probably doesn't.
- Toggles read differently from text inputs. Space them as a list, with the label on the left and the switch on the right, so Preferences looks like a different kind of section without a card.
- Check the grouping by squinting at the page or blurring it. If you can still see three clusters, the spacing is working.
