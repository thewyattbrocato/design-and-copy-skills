**How I read it**
1. **"Q1" is a drift, not a plan.** It has no date, no named event they're waiting on, and no step on either side. A real postponement sounds like "budget resets Jan 6" or "we're locked until the fuel contract renews."
2. **Liking the demo isn't a buying step.** The quote went out with a felt problem but, as far as I can see from your account, no sized figure, no confirmed signer, and nothing the buyer had to do. Silence after that is the usual result. The cost of staying put is low for Priya and the cost of choosing wrong lands on her.
3. **The likeliest causes, in rough order:**
   - It's not a priority against the daily work, since "things got busy" is probably literal.
   - Priya is afraid of recommending the wrong thing, or she can't get the VP or finance to a decision.
   - The price or the change effort looked bigger than the pain, and she didn't say so.
   - Least likely: they're comparing you against a competitor or a spreadsheet.

I can't rank these without hearing from her. So the aim of your next move is to find out which one it is, not to push.

**Is it worth doing anything?** Yes, once, and cheaply. The pain was real and stated, which most dead deals lack. Don't send more nudges, urgency, or a discount. Don't remind her what waiting costs, because she already knows. Here's what I'd do:

1. **Send one message to Priya, not a "just checking in."** Name the likely fear, offer one recommendation and a smaller step, and give her both exits. For example:

   > Priya, I don't want to keep nudging you. You said fuel and overtime were the pain, and I'd rather make this easy to decide than keep it hanging. My suggestion: instead of the full rollout in the quote, we run it on [2–3 trucks / one route group] for [4 weeks] and judge it on [fuel per route, overtime hours]. If it doesn't move those numbers, you stop and owe nothing [only say this if it's true]. If now is simply the wrong time, tell me and I'll close this out and reach back in [January]. Which is closer to where you are, and could we take 15 minutes on [specific day/time] either way?

   Fill in the pilot terms only if you can actually offer them. Don't promise a no-cost exit you can't deliver.

2. **Ask the question that tells you what happened.** If she replies, ask "Where did this land after you took it internally?" and "Who else has to say yes?" That shows whether it's priority, fear, or an unnamed blocker.

3. **Get the numbers if she'll talk.** Ask for last quarter's fuel spend and overtime hours. If she'll pull them, that's real intent. If she won't, it tells you where the deal stands.

4. **Stop after that.** If there's no reply to this message, send one final close-out note ("I'll assume this isn't a priority right now, and I'm glad to pick it up in Q1 if you want to") and move it to a low-priority follow-up. Don't forecast it as late-stage. A "no" or a dated "Q1" reply is a fine outcome: put the date on both calendars and drop it.

**Going forward:** you can sharpen this by checking before the next quote that you have a sized figure, a named signer, and something the buyer commits to (data, a second stakeholder's time). Send the quote after that, not as a way to get them there.
