**Short version:** this deal probably isn't dead, but it has stalled, and you shouldn't forecast it as late-stage. "We'll circle back in Q1" has no date, no owner and no named event behind it. That's drifting, not a dated wait.

**What you know and what you don't**
- Known: Priya feels the pain (fuel, overtime), and she and the VP of ops both liked the demo. You sent a quote and then got five weeks of silence, a vague call, and no reply to two emails.
- Unknown: who signs and whether there's budget. Also unknown are the actual fuel and overtime numbers, and whether anyone besides those two has weighed in, such as finance, the owner, or the dispatchers and drivers.

**Likely reads, most to least probable (all guesses)**
1. **No clear route to a signature.** Neither Priya nor the VP may be the person who approves spend. If so, "Q1" may just mean the next budget cycle, and nobody is pushing the purchase through.
2. **Fear of choosing wrong.** The pain is real, but switching how 11 trucks get routed is visible and risky, while doing nothing costs them nothing visible. This is common after a demo everyone loved.
3. **The problem was never sized in their numbers.** "Fuel and overtime are killing us" is a feeling until someone says what it costs per month. A quote with no business case is easy to set aside.
4. **A real priority shift,** like a busy season, a different fire, or a competing project. "Things got busy" may be literally true.

You can't tell these apart from where you sit. Your next move should find out which one it is, not argue against all of them.

**What's worth doing**
- Stop sending check-ins. A third "just following up" adds nothing. Don't add urgency, a deadline or a discount either. She already knows the cost, and pressure makes choosing feel riskier.
- Send one message to Priya that asks something real and names both exits. It could say:

  > "Priya, you mentioned Q1. Two quick questions so I don't guess: who else needs to sign off on something like this, and what happens in Q1 that makes it a better time? If it'd help, I can pull together a one-page view of your fuel and overtime numbers to take to them. If this has moved off the list, tell me and I'll close it out on my side. Would a 15-minute call the week of [date] work?"

- Her answers will tell you which read is right:
  - **A name or a budget event** means it's read 1, and you now have a real next step.
  - **Silence again, or vague again,** means it's low priority. Move it down your forecast and take the polite close.
  - **Worry about rollout or risk** means it's read 2. Then recommend one smaller, reversible start, with a reason and the condition that would change your advice. Do this only if your product supports it, and don't offer a pilot until you know who signs.
- If she'll give you last quarter's fuel and overtime figures, build the business case from her numbers and let her edit it. That gives her something to forward to whoever holds the budget, without making her look like your advocate.
- If she goes quiet again after that, close it out kindly and set a dated return only if she welcomes one.
