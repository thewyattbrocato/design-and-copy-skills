**How to read it:** This is probably a soft no, or at least not a priority. Three things point that way:

- **"Still evaluating" with no next step.** Real evaluations have a decision date, a criteria list, or a named person who's deciding. None was mentioned.
- **"Circle back in Q1."** It's late October, so Q1 means roughly 2 to 4 months of waiting. That's usually how people say "not this budget cycle" without saying no.
- **Silence after the quote, then two unanswered emails.** Priya liked the product, but the pain was probably not urgent enough to beat other priorities. The deal may also be stuck on budget or on the VP, who may not have been as sold as Priya.

Some things I can't tell from here: whether the VP actually approved the spend, whether the budget is already set for next year, whether they're comparing you to a competitor or to doing nothing, and whether the price was a surprise. Priya's "things got busy" may be accurate, or it may be a polite way to end it.

**Worth doing:**

1. **Stop sending "just checking in" emails.** Two unanswered follow-ups are a signal. Send one direct message instead.
2. **Ask the direct question.** Something like: *"Is this still a priority for your team this year? If the timing isn't right, I'd rather know so I can stop taking your time. If it is, what would need to happen to move it forward?"* This gets you a real answer, and it's the kind of question that often produces a yes or a clear no.
3. **Find out who controls the budget and whether the VP is on board.** Ask Priya directly: "Who else needs to sign off, and what does their decision process look like?" If the VP never saw the quote, ask for 20 minutes with both of them to walk through it.
4. **Build the ROI for them.** They said fuel and overtime were the problem. Put a one-page estimate together using their numbers (monthly fuel spend, overtime hours, expected reduction) so Priya has something to forward internally. That gives her a reason to push, and it makes the value concrete for the VP.
5. **Offer a smaller first step.** A pilot on 2 to 3 trucks, or a 30-day trial, can be easier to approve than a full 11-truck contract, especially late in the year.
6. **If they still say Q1, lock in the date.** Ask when their budget gets set, put a calendar hold for early January, and move the deal in your CRM from active pipeline to a dated nurture item.
