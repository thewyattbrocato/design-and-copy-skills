Because she didn't say no, the deal isn't dead yet, but it has no next step, and nothing you've done so far has asked her for one.

**What you know:** Priya named a cost problem (fuel and overtime) and a demo landed with her and the VP of ops. You don't know these:
- Whether the VP has seen the quote, or whether Priya is the one who signs.
- Whether there's a budget line for this, or whether it competes with other spend.

Her "we're still evaluating" with no date and no mention of a decision means the problem was never sized in dollars and no one has owned a decision date. The five weeks of silence after the quote suggest the quote went into review and stopped there.

**Is it worth doing anything?** Yes, but not more "checking in." Your two emails probably gave her nothing to answer. Make one specific, low-pressure ask:

> Hi Priya, thanks for being straight with me on the call. Q1 works if that's the real timing. Before then, two quick things would help me keep this useful for you: did the quote get to [VP name], and is there a budget for this in Q1, or does it need to come from a different line? If it's not a fit right now, just tell me and I'll stop following up.
>
> Would a 30-minute call on [date] work to go over the fuel and overtime numbers with [VP], or should we close this out and reconnect in January?

This does three things. It asks about the quote and the signer, which are the actual unknowns. It names both exits, so she can say no without awkwardness. And it puts a date on the table. If she doesn't reply in about a week, don't send a third "checking in." Accept the silence as a no for now and put a reminder in your calendar for early January.

**If she says yes to the call:** agree one number they care about (the fuel or overtime cost per month, in their figures) and ask Priya who else needs to see it. Don't re-run the demo. The aim is to get the VP to say that cost in their own words.

**If she says not a fit or not now:** confirm it, ask for the reason in her words, and stop. Set one dated return only if she invited it.

Two things I can't tell from here: whether the quote's price is close to what they expected, and whether the VP has already decided. Either one changes the next move. The quote's price, and whether a smaller first step (one truck or one route) would fit their budget, are the next things to check.
