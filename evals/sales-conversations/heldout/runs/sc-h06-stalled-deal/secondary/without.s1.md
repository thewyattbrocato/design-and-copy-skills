**How to read it:** "Still evaluating... we'll circle back in Q1" is a soft no. Nobody is actively comparing you against an alternative right now, and the deal has lost its urgency. Five weeks of silence after a quote, followed by a vague timeline, usually means one of these:

- **The pain isn't urgent enough to fund this year.** Fuel and overtime hurt, but maybe not enough to justify a purchase before budget resets.
- **Priya is a champion without the authority.** She liked it, but the VP's enthusiasm in the demo didn't turn into ownership. Nobody on their side is pushing it internally.
- **Something else took priority.** Busy season, a truck or driver issue, or another project. This is the most benign reading, but it still means you aren't the priority.
- **There's no decision process.** You never found out who signs, what the budget is, or what the timeline is, so the deal drifted.

Your two follow-ups without a reply suggest it's stalled, not that they're mid-decision. Her "things got busy" was the polite version of "not now."

**Is it worth doing anything? Yes, but change the approach. More "just checking in" emails won't help.**

1. **Get specific about what's blocking them.** Send one short, direct email: "I know timing is tight. Before Q1, is there anything holding this back, like budget, priorities, or a different option you're considering? Happy to adjust scope or pricing if that's the issue." Asking for the loss reason directly often gets a real answer.

2. **Build the ROI with her numbers.** She said fuel and overtime were the problem, so ask for the figures: monthly fuel spend, overtime hours or dollars, and roughly how much routing inefficiency costs. Then show the VP what it's worth in dollars against your price. A VP who "loved the demo" may need a business case to sign off.

3. **Find the path to a decision.** Ask Priya: "Who else needs to be part of the decision, and how does your budget get set for next year?" If budget gets set in Q1, a Q1 revisit may be real and you can plan for it. If she doesn't know or dodges, that's a warning sign.

4. **Offer a smaller first step.** A pilot on 2 to 3 trucks, a phased rollout, or a 30-day trial can get past "not now" by lowering the stakes. A quote with an expiration date can also create a reason to decide.

5. **Set the Q1 follow-up now.** Get a specific date on the calendar for January, and put it in your CRM. Then send one light note in early January, not a chase.

6. **Let it go if it doesn't move.** If you get no reply to the direct question, treat the deal as lost for now, move it to a nurture list, and spend your time on prospects who have an active problem.

**One thing to check:** what did the quote look like relative to what they said the problem was worth? If the price is high compared to the savings they described, that might be the real objection, and it's worth knowing before you invest more time.
