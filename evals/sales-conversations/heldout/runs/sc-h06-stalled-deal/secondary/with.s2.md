**Short read:** This is a soft no, or at best a deprioritized deal. "Still evaluating" and "circle back in Q1" with no reason and no date usually means the purchase has no urgent owner and no sized case. Priya likes the product and the problem is real to her, but nobody has yet shown that solving it now beats doing nothing. The VP's enthusiasm in the demo doesn't change that unless the VP is actively pushing it internally, which you haven't confirmed.

**What I can't tell from what you've given me:**
- Whether Priya can sign, or what the path to a signature is
- Whether the VP is still engaged or has moved on
- Whether the quote's price was ever discussed
- Whether anyone has put a number on fuel and overtime in their own books
- Whether Q1 is a real budget cycle or just a polite date

**Is anything worth doing? Yes, but not more follow-ups.** Two generic "checking in" emails won't move a buyer who has already deferred. Do this instead:

1. **Ask one direct question on a short call.** Priya is the only confirmed contact, so use her. Something like: "Before Q1, I want to make sure we're not wasting your time. What would need to be true for this to be worth acting on sooner? And who else needs to be comfortable before it moves?" Ask her to pick the date. This gets you the missing reason and the missing signer.

2. **Name both exits, as the skill suggests.** "Should we keep going on a small first step, or close this out and pick it back up in January?" Buyers who are stalling often say yes to a clear choice more readily than to a vague wait. If the answer is "close it out," accept it and record the reason in her words.

3. **If she's open, size the problem in her numbers, not yours.** Ask for last quarter's fuel spend, overtime hours, and miles across the 11 trucks. Build a return estimate from her figures and label every assumption as hers. If she won't share numbers, that tells you the problem isn't sized yet, and that's the real blocker.

4. **Only propose a pilot once the gate is met.** A problem she can size, a named signer, and what she'll put in (time, route data). A pilot on two or three trucks for a month is a reasonable offer if all three are there. Without them, a pilot just adds work for her.

5. **Put the Q1 check-in on both calendars now** with a specific date and owner, instead of waiting for her to call. If she won't pick a date, that's your answer about priority.

**Lesson for next time:** the quote went out before the return was sized in the buyer's own numbers. The enthusiasm came from the demo, but the business case never got built with Priya. Next deal, get the fuel and overtime figures before the demo, and don't send a quote until someone has said who signs and when they'd decide.
