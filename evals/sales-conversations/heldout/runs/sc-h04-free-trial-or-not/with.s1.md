**Recommendation: offer trials, but don't say yes to everyone. Make the trial a short, scoped proof that you help set up. A blanket 30-day trial is the wrong version of "yes".**

The real cost isn't the 30 days. It's that setup takes about a day of the office manager's time. A cold self-serve trial therefore fails in one of two ways. Either the manager never finds the day, the trial dies quietly, and you count it as "didn't convert". Or they spend the day and find the product doesn't fit their messy rotas, which you could have learned in 20 minutes. Your cofounder is right that you shouldn't gatekeep a self-serve product. But this one has a one-day entry fee, so it isn't frictionless for the buyer.

**What I'd do instead**

1. **Ask what they want to be sure of.** A trial request is a request for proof. On a 15-minute call, ask:
   - What does a swap or time-off request look like today (texts, a spreadsheet, the manager as go-between)?
   - How many a week, and how long does each take to resolve? `[to learn]`
   - What would make them confident enough to switch?

2. **Check three things before you start a trial:**
   - **A felt problem they can size.** For example, swap requests per week × minutes each. `[to learn]`
   - **A signer.** Is it the office manager, the practice owner, or someone else? Name them, because the person doing setup is often not the person paying.
   - **What they'll put in.** Schedule data, plus a few staff willing to use it.

   If one is missing, the next step is to get it, not to start a trial. A prospect who won't answer these questions would probably not have finished setup either.

3. **Take the setup day off their plate.** Ask for their current schedule as a spreadsheet, photo or export, and load it yourselves, or at least do the first week with them on a screen share. That removes the biggest reason trials stall. It's also a good discovery session, because their rota quirks (split shifts, on-call, cross-site coverage) are exactly what you need to learn.

4. **Agree a success test in writing before day one.** It should use numbers they helped choose. For example: "By [date], X% of swaps handled in-app, and the manager no longer relays them by text." Also agree:
   - a start and end date, plus one mid-point check-in;
   - who does what on each side;
   - the price they'll pay if they continue, and the date they decide.

5. **Let the trial end on its date.** Don't pull access to push a signature. If they need more time, offer a stated extension or a read-only period. Continuing should be opt-in, with the price already known and nothing charged until they say yes.

**Two decisions are yours, not mine**
- **Length.** I'd count the trial from the day their schedule is loaded, not from signup, since setup eats the early days. Whether that's 14 or 30 days depends on your swap cadence: they need to see a few real swap cycles. I don't have your data on that, so check how long it takes an active clinic to reach its first handful of swaps.
- **Self-serve for tiny accounts.** If a 10-person clinic with a simple rota just wants to click around, let them. That's the "small, self-serve purchase" case, so skip the gate and give them a sandbox with sample data. Save the structured trial for accounts where setup is a real commitment.

**What to tell your cofounder**
"Yes to trials, no to blind trials. Every trial that dies in week one because nobody had a day to set up is a lost prospect we labelled a free trial." You can test this cheaply. Run the next three requests this way, track how many reach the success test, and compare with how the unguided ones go. `[to learn]`, since I'm not going to guess your conversion numbers.
