Offer trials, but don't say yes to everyone, and don't call it self-serve while setup costs a day of the office manager's time.

**Why not a blanket yes**
- The office manager is the scarce resource. Many of them will quietly decide a day of data entry isn't worth it, and the trial will die at setup, not at the product. That looks like churn but is really a setup failure.
- Your product only shows value once the schedule is loaded and staff are invited. An empty trial teaches the prospect nothing, and a 30-day clock burns while it sits empty.
- Unqualified trials also make your conversion data useless, because you can't tell "bad product" from "never set up."

**What I'd do instead**
1. **Remove the setup cost.** Offer to import their schedule for them from a spreadsheet, PDF, or photo of the rota. For 10–60 staff that's an hour or two of your time, and it's your best sales tool. You can drop this once you've built a decent CSV import.
2. **Start the clock at activation, not signup.** Make it 14–30 days from when the schedule is loaded and staff are invited. A full schedule cycle is the real test, so don't shrink it below about 2 weeks.
3. **Qualify lightly.** Ask for a named decision-maker (often the owner or practice manager, not just the office manager). Also ask for a 15-minute kickoff and agreement on what success looks like, e.g. "at least 10 swap or time-off requests handled in the app by the end of the trial."
4. **Schedule a mid-trial check-in** around day 10 and a decision call at the end. Without those, trials just fade out.
5. **Optionally, start with one team or location.** A single team makes setup smaller and the trial easier to say yes to.

**Your cofounder is right about one thing:** friction early in the funnel costs you deals. So don't gate by making people jump through hoops. Gate by being hands-on, so the prospect does less work and you filter for people who'll actually use it.

For these first few prospects, run it by hand and watch where they stall. Whatever you end up doing manually (importing, inviting staff, nudging) tells you what to build before you open up real self-serve.

If the trials still don't convert after you've removed setup friction, consider a paid pilot (e.g. one month at a discount with a money-back guarantee). It gets more commitment than a free trial, and small clinics often accept it.
