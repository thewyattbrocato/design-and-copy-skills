Offer trials, but don't say yes to everyone, and don't call it self-serve. Your cofounder is right that you should say yes, but wrong about the reason.

**Why "self-serve" doesn't fit**
- A day of an office manager's time is the real cost of the trial, not the price. The most likely failure is a prospect who never finishes setup, so the trial dies and you learn nothing.
- Shift swapping only has value once staff use it. A manager who enters schedules but never gets staff to swap hasn't tested the product.
- At 10-60 staff per clinic, you're not running a funnel with thousands of signups. You can afford to be hands-on.

**What I'd do**
1. **Do the setup for them.** Ask for what they already have: a spreadsheet, a photo of the wall schedule, or a PDF. Load it yourself in 1-2 days. This cuts their effort from a day to about 30 minutes of review. It also teaches you what import tooling to build next.
2. **Start the clock when the schedule goes live.** Make it 30 days from the first published schedule, not from signup. One real scheduling cycle plus a few swap or time-off requests is the minimum needed to see value.
3. **Qualify before you commit.** Ask for 10 minutes on a call to confirm four things:
   - They have a real pain: swaps by text or group chat, or time-off conflicts.
   - The person who makes the schedule is the one trialing.
   - They'll invite staff.
   - They'll look at results with you at the end.
4. **Agree on success criteria up front.** For example: "If 70% of staff are active and at least 5 swaps or time-off requests go through the app, we move to paid." That turns the end of the trial into a decision, not a fade-out.
5. **Check in at day 3, day 14 and day 25.** Day 3 is to make sure staff got invited. Day 14 is to look at usage. Day 25 is the conversion conversation.

**Alternatives if you want less founder time**
- **A shorter trial (14 days) with the same white-glove setup.** This works if their schedule cycle is weekly.
- **A paid pilot with a money-back guarantee.** Charging for the first month, refundable, filters for seriousness and often converts better. I'd try this with prospects who decline the qualification call.

**How to settle it with your cofounder**
Say yes to all of them, but with the setup call as step one. Track how many prospects reach a live schedule, how many get staff active, and how many convert. After about 10 trials you'll know whether to open it up to true self-serve, tighten the filter, or move to paid pilots. The setup-completion rate is the number to watch. If it's low, the product needs better import before it needs a trial policy.
