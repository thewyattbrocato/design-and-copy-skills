**Offer a trial, but not the open-ended version your cofounder is describing.** Your setup cost is the real issue. A day of an office manager's time is a large cost for a prospect who hasn't decided to buy, and a trial that ends before they've loaded schedules shows them nothing. The trial itself isn't the problem. The problem is a 30-day clock that starts before the product can do anything useful.

I'd do this:

- **Start the trial clock at go-live, not at signup.** The 30 days begin once their schedules are loaded and the first real shift is live. Otherwise you're giving free time to a prospect who hasn't started.
- **Do the setup for them.** Offer a concierge import: they send you their existing roster and schedule sheets, and you load them, or you run a 45-minute session with their office manager. This turns a day of their time into an hour, and it tells you where your import flow is painful.
- **Require a card on file, or a paid setup fee that converts to credit.** This filters out people who only want free software and makes the trial a real commitment. A card on file with auto-convert is standard for B2B SaaS and doesn't hurt conversion much.
- **Ask for a named champion and a loading date before you start.** If they can't name someone who will own the rollout, they won't finish setup, and you'll get a churned trial and no data.
- **Cap it.** Take one or two pilot clinics at a time while you're still learning the import process. Saying yes to everyone means you're doing onboarding work you can't staff.
- **Define success up front**, such as "two weeks of real swap requests and time-off requests run through the app." Then you know whether to convert them, and so do they.

On your cofounder's point: "self-serve" is true for the product, but your prospects are buying a schedule migration, not just software. Trials are cheap for you in cash terms but expensive for the clinic in staff time, and that's what hurts conversion and goodwill.

To decide how much setup help to offer, compare the value of one day of office-manager time to what the clinic pays you per year. If the annual contract is several thousand dollars, a concierge import is cheap for you to give. If it's a few hundred, you'll need the card-on-file and self-serve import route instead.
