**Recommendation:** Don't say yes to everyone, and don't refuse. Offer a trial shape that fits the cost, and only to prospects who pass a short qualification check.

**Why your cofounder is partly right:** Self-serve means your support cost is low. But the real cost of a trial here is the office manager's day of data entry, and an open 30-day trial usually ends with "we never got round to setting it up" and no decision. Saying yes to everyone gives you a pile of trials that never load schedules, so you learn nothing.

**Three shapes, from cheapest to most committed:**

- **A. Self-serve trial with a faster start.** Offer 14 to 30 days, plus a sample-schedule import or a template that gets one team (say 8 people) live in an hour, not a day. Your cost is low. Use this for prospects who want to try it alone.
- **B. Paid guided setup.** A flat onboarding fee [price to set] that you spend the day doing, credited against the first invoice if they convert. This filters for serious buyers and covers the day you'd otherwise lose. Price it with pricing-and-packaging in mind.
- **C. Structured pilot for one team or site.** Only if the prospect passes the gate below. Write down a success test in their numbers (for example, time-off and swap requests handled in-app for two weeks, with approvals done within a day), a named signer, a checkpoint at week two, a decision meeting booked for day 30, and the price after the pilot. Access stays on through the end date. Do not pull access to speed up a signature.

**Gate check for the two prospects** (before you offer any trial). Ask:

1. Who signs, and what is their route to a yes? [to learn]
2. How many hours a week does the office manager spend on swaps and time-off today? [to learn]
3. What does a bad or missed shift cost them, in money or in complaints? [to learn]
4. Does the clinic have that office manager's day free, and who else must agree? [to learn]

If they can't name a signer or a felt cost, the next step is a call with the signer, not a trial. If they can answer all four, pick A, B or C based on how much setup help they need.

**To take to your cofounder:** "Yes to a trial, not to a 30-day open-ended free account. Each trial gets a success test, an end date, and a decision meeting." That keeps the goodwill without giving away the office manager's time for nothing.
