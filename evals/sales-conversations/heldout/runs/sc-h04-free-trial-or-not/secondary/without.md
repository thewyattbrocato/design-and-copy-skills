**Don't give an open, free 30-day trial to everyone. Offer a structured trial that the clinic has to commit to, and do the setup yourselves.** Your cofounder is right that you shouldn't hand-hold everyone. But "self-serve" doesn't describe your product yet, because the first day of value depends on an office manager entering schedules.

**Why the open trial is risky:**
- The clinic pays with a day of staff time, which is a real cost even when no money changes hands. Many will not finish setup, and you won't learn whether the product works for them.
- 30 days is awkward. Setup eats the first 1-2 weeks, and shift swapping only shows value across a full scheduling cycle. You'd end the trial before they've seen it work, or you'd extend it by default.
- Free trials attract people who are only looking. Prospects who are already asking for one may be less committed than you'd like.

**What I'd do instead:**
1. **Make setup faster first.** A CSV template or a bulk importer for common rota patterns could cut a day to an hour. Any cut to setup time makes every other option easier, so this is worth prioritizing.
2. **Offer a "guided pilot" to prospects who ask for a trial.** You or a team member runs the schedule import together with their office manager in one or two sessions, and the trial runs for 30 days. Charge a small fee (even a few hundred dollars) or require a card on file, credited against the first invoice if they convert. This turns the trial into a commitment and makes setup your cost, which is the right place for it.
3. **Agree on a success metric before starting.** For example: "by day 21, at least X swap requests were handled in the app" or "the office manager stops using the old spreadsheet for time off." If they hit it, convert. If not, you've learned why.
4. **Keep open self-serve trials for later,** once the import is fast enough that setup is actually self-serve. Then a 14-day trial makes sense.

**To decide between these:** how many prospects are asking, and how much time would you realistically spend per guided pilot? If it's one or two prospects a month, a guided pilot is clearly worth it. If it's dozens, the import tooling becomes the priority before any trial program.
