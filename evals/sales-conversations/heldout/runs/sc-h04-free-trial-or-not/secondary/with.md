**Goal of this conversation with each prospect: qualify, then agree a dated pilot with a named owner.** Don't give anyone an open 30-day trial yet.

**The real cost of the trial isn't the free period. It's the day of office-manager time.** Under a 30-day trial, the first week or two goes to setup, which leaves little time to see the product work. Your cofounder is right that the product is self-serve. But self-serve means the clinic pays for setup in staff hours, and they may not have agreed to that yet.

**Recommendation: a conditional pilot, not a blanket yes.**

Before any trial, confirm three things:
1. **A felt problem with a number.** For example: how many shift-swap or time-off requests a month go through texts or the front desk, and how many hours that takes. `[to learn]`
2. **A signer or a route to one.** Usually the practice owner or a clinic director. The office manager is the person who does the work, not the person who buys.
3. **Named effort from their side.** The office manager's time, plus a second person to check the rota.

If any of these is missing, the next step is to get it, not to start a trial.

**How to structure the pilot if the three things are in place:**
- **Cut setup time first, then offer the trial.** Ask whether they can export their current rota (spreadsheet or scheduling software). Offer to do the import yourselves in a session, so the office manager's day drops to a short review. If you can't do that yet, a 30-day trial is the wrong offer.
- **Set a success test in their numbers.** For example: "by day 14, at least 80% of swap requests go through the app instead of texts," or "time-off approvals done in-app within 24 hours." They pick the number.
- **Use a shorter window, or add checkpoints.** A 14-day pilot with a day-7 check-in fits better than 30 days with nothing until the end. Keep the 30 days if they need a month-end cycle to judge it.
- **Set a stop point.** If the day-7 check shows no use, end it kindly and record why.
- **Make conversion opt-in.** State the price and the decision date up front. Nothing is bought until they say yes in writing. Don't pull access to speed up a signature, and if paperwork runs late, offer a dated extension at a stated price.
- **Put it in writing the same day,** with owners and dates on both sides.

**For the cofounder:** a self-serve free tier or sandbox with sample data, with no commitment, works for people who just want to look around. It doesn't work for a clinic that would have to load its own rota, and it's not the same offer as a pilot. Offering both (a sandbox for anyone, a guided pilot for qualified clinics) would match what each buyer is asking for.

**Two things I'd need to firm this up:** how long the setup takes you to do yourselves, if you ever run it, and whether you have a CSV or spreadsheet import. If setup stays a full office-manager day, the pilot will mostly test their patience, not the product.
