**My recommendation:** offer a trial, but not an open 30-day self-serve one. Offer a scoped, guided trial to prospects who pass a short qualification check. Your cofounder is right that self-serve is the product's promise. Your concern is also right: the expensive part here is a day of an office manager's unpaid time, so an open trial means you're asking for a project before you've shown value.

**Facts first**
- Known: setup takes about a day of an office manager's time; two prospects asked for 30 days; clinics are 10-60 staff.
- Unknown: how many of your past trials finished schedule entry; what share converted; what the current swap and time-off process costs them per week `[to learn]`.
- Assumed: a 30-day trial with no setup help will mostly stall at the import step.

**Qualification gate, before any trial:**
1. **Felt problem:** how many swap or time-off requests they handle a week, and how long each takes now `[to learn]`.
2. **Signer:** who approves tool purchases, and whether that person is in the conversation.
3. **Input:** will the office manager block a session and share current schedules or a spreadsheet? If they won't, that's your answer.

**If the gate passes, offer this instead of a free-for-all trial:**
- **Guided setup included:** you or a teammate load their schedules in a 60-90 minute session with the office manager, from their files. This removes the day of work that makes trials fail.
- **Success test, agreed in writing:** e.g. the clinic approves its first real swap or time-off request in the app within 14 days, or processes N requests. Pick N with them.
- **Dated decision:** a checkpoint at day 14 and an end date at day 30. Nothing converts without a yes in writing; no auto-charge.
- **One trial per clinic**, with a named owner on each side.

**Why not 30 days open:** a trial only proves value after schedules are loaded. If the office manager loads them alone and then gets busy, the trial ends with no swap ever processed, and you learn nothing. Guided setup is what makes the trial a real test.
