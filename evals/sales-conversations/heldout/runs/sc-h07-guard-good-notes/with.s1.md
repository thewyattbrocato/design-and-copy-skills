**Overall:** These are strong notes. They have a felt problem, a named signer, a dated next step on both calendars, and a concrete input from the buyer (Dee's sample). I'd fix the four gaps below before sending, in this order.

**1. Goal and gate: the pilot has no success test until 23 Oct**
- Weak moment: "Plan: ask Marcus on 23 Oct what result in the pilot he would need to see to buy."
- Cost: You'd write and send the proposal on 17 Oct without knowing what it has to prove. If the pilot goes quiet, you'd be asking the question after the work is done.
- Replacement: Ask Marcus for the success measure before the proposal goes out, by a short call or a one-line email. For example: "For the pilot to be worth it, what would the quotes need to look like, and how fast?" Then write his answer into the proposal. On the 23rd you confirm it rather than discover it.
- Also add a line on what the pilot needs from him: time from one showroom's estimators and from Dee.

**2. Discovery: the problem is in his words, but the numbers don't connect**
- Weak moment: "loses roughly 1 in 4 jobs" and "14 quotes/month go cold" sit side by side with $6,800.
- Cost: Your manager will multiply them, and the result will look precise when it isn't. These are two estimates, and the notes don't say whether they describe the same jobs.
- Replacement: Record what you have as his estimates, and add `[to learn]` lines for:
  - total quotes per month;
  - how many of the 14 went to a faster competitor;
  - the quote turnaround that would be fast enough;
  - his margin per job.

  Don't put a dollar total in the notes. "That's my whole margin" is a good quote and should stay.
- Ask Dee for the quote counts in the same sample she's already sending.

**3. The showing: check the pilot against his volume**
- Weak moment: "2-week pilot scoped to one showroom."
- Cost: The notes don't say how many quotes that covers. With 14 cold quotes a month across three showrooms, two weeks at one showroom may be too few to show a difference. A pilot that proves nothing feeds the "wait until after spring" fallback.
- Replacement: Add the expected pilot quote count as `[to confirm from Dee's sample]`. Add the pilot start and end dates, and work back from the 1 March go-live to set the latest decision date. The notes currently have no decision date at all.

**4. Adoption and stakeholders: the failure is recorded but not its cause**
- Weak moment: "tried a template system 2 yrs ago, staff dropped it."
- Cost: This is the likeliest way this deal fails, and the notes don't say why it happened. The estimators who would have to change how they work are also not in the 23 Oct meeting or the pilot plan.
- Replacement: Add `[to learn: why staff dropped it]` and ask Dee on the 23rd. Name one estimator at the pilot showroom as a participant.
- Dee's switchover concern is noted, which is good. Say what the pilot will do about it, for example that she doesn't retype during the pilot, or that the old process keeps running in parallel.
