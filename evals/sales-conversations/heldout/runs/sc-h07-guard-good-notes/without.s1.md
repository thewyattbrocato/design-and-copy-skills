**Critique of your call notes**

**What works:** The notes are specific and quantified, with a clear decision process, a dated next step, and an honest risk with a plan. The Dee concern and the failed template system are the kind of detail a manager wants.

**Fix before sending**

1. **The dates don't match the weekdays.** Tues 14 Oct, Fri 17 Oct and Thurs 23 Oct all match 2025. Today is 28 Oct 2026, when 14 Oct was a Wednesday, 17 Oct a Saturday and 23 Oct a Friday. Either the year is wrong or the weekdays are. If the notes are from this year, the 23 Oct review has already happened and your manager will read the "next step" as stale. Add the year, or update the status.

2. **Lost jobs and cold quotes aren't the same thing.** Marcus says he loses about 1 in 4 jobs to whoever quotes first. He also says about 14 quotes a month "go cold". The notes treat these as one problem. Say which number is the pain and mark both as his estimates, not measured figures.

3. **"That's my whole margin" is a quote about revenue.** 14 × $6,800 is about $95k a month in quoted revenue, not margin. It's still a strong point, but state it as revenue and don't imply margin. Even 1 or 2 recovered jobs a month roughly covers the $9k a year, so add that one-line payback. It's your best argument and it's missing.

**Gaps your manager will ask about**

- **Pilot success criteria.** You plan to ask Marcus on 23 Oct what result he'd need to see. Put a proposed metric in the 17 Oct proposal instead, such as site visit to quote sent, from about 3-4 days down to under 1. Dee's sample of last month's quotes gives you the baseline. Asking him cold on the day lets him define the bar, and he may set it high.
- **A plan for the pilot going quiet.** Your risk is that the pilot goes quiet and he says "wait until after spring". The plan is only a question at the end, which is passive. Add a mid-pilot check-in, such as a call or a short usage summary after week 1. Also note that the 1 March deadline leaves room for a post-pilot decision, which makes "wait" easier for him to choose.
- **Adoption risk.** Staff dropped the last tool, but the notes don't say why or who the estimators are. Find out what killed the template system. Also name which showroom the pilot covers and who its estimators are.
- **Dee's role.** She is a blocker in the making: she does the retyping and she owns the switchover concern. Note whether she has any say in the decision, and whether the pilot reduces her work or only adds to it.
- **Integration.** Which invoicing system does Dee use? The notes don't say, and it determines whether the retyping can actually be eliminated.
- **Pilot terms.** Is the pilot free or paid? Does the $9k a year cover all 3 showrooms? If it's close to $15k with add-ons, the accountant gets pulled in.

- Two-line summary at the top: deal size, stage, next step and date, main risk. Managers skim.
