**Goal of this follow-up:** get a named next meeting on a specific date, or a clear no.

**What likely happened**

Nine days of silence after "send me more information and I'll take a look" most often means one of these:

- **It was a polite close.** "Send me more" is a common way to end a call without saying no. Nothing on the call set a date or an owner, so the deck landed in an inbox with no reason to be opened soon.
- **The deck answered the wrong question.** A deck is a pitch. He may have wanted something about his sites, his current system, or how a badge-reissue or door-schedule change would work for his staff. You don't know yet which one, and that's the gap to close.
- **He isn't the signer.** Hospital access control usually involves IT or security review, possibly procurement, and often an existing vendor or integrator. He may be waiting for someone else, or may not have started the internal conversation.
- **The problem wasn't sized.** Unless the call put a number on it (doors, badges, sites, hours lost, incidents), it's easy to put off.

I can't tell which from here. Nine days in a hospital procurement cycle isn't unusual on its own. What matters is that the call ended without a next step you both agreed.

**What to do now**

Send one short follow-up. Don't resend the deck and don't send a "just checking in." Refer to something specific he said, ask one question that would tell you where he is, and offer two dated options with a clear exit:

> Subject: [Hospital group] access control, next step?
>
> Hi [Name], following up from our call on [date]. You mentioned [specific pain point in his words]. Since you've had a chance to look at the deck, would it help more to do a 20-minute walk-through of how this would work across [his sites / his current system], or to get the right material to whoever else reviews this, such as IT security or procurement?
>
> Would [Tue Nov 10] or [Wed Nov 11] at [time] work for 20 minutes? If the timing isn't right, just say so and I'll close this out.

Fill the brackets with what he actually said. Don't invent a pain point.

**Then:**

- **If he replies with a date,** confirm it in writing the same day with who attends and what you'll cover.
- **If he says he's not the decision-maker,** ask who is and what that person will need. Ask him for a warm introduction if he's willing.
- **If there's no reply,** send one more short note after about five business days that offers the two exits: keep going, or close it out and talk again in a named month. If that gets no answer, stop and log it as stalled.
- **If he says no,** ask one line for the reason ("Is it timing, or not a fit?"), record it in his words, and stop.

**Before you send, check two things:** what problem he described on the call, and whether he said who else is involved. If you don't have either, the follow-up should ask for them rather than assume them.
