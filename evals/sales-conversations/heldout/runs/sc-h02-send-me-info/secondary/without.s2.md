Nine days of silence after "send me more information" usually means the deal didn't stall. It means no next step was ever agreed. "I'll take a look" is a polite close, not a commitment. Some likely explanations:

- **The deck went into a busy inbox** and hasn't been opened, or it got filed and forgotten.
- **He's not the decision-maker.** Facilities can evaluate access control, but a hospital group often needs IT, information security, or procurement to sign off, especially for software that touches a network or staff identity data. He may be waiting to bring it to them, or he may not have decided to.
- **Timing.** Hospital budgets and capital projects run on fixed cycles. If there's no budget this quarter, he may not have a reason to act yet.
- **He may have an incumbent system** he's happy enough with, or one he's contractually locked into.

You can't tell which from here, so the next step is to find out, not to guess.

**What to do now**

1. **Send one short follow-up today or tomorrow.** Don't just "check in." Ask a question that's easy to answer and gives him a reason to reply. For example:

   > Hi [Name], I wanted to follow up on the deck and one-pager I sent last week. Before I put more time into this on my end, can you tell me: who else would need to weigh in on something like this at [Hospital Group] (IT, security, procurement), and is there a budget or review cycle this would need to fit into? Happy to do a 20-minute call with whoever that is if it's useful.

   Asking who else is involved is the most important part. It gives him an easy way to move you forward without having to decide anything himself.

2. **If he doesn't reply in about 5 business days, try once more** with a different angle, such as a one-line note on a specific problem you solve for facilities teams (door-schedule changes, badge deactivation on termination, audit reports). Keep it to a few sentences.

3. **If there's still nothing after that, send a short closing note.** Something like: "I'll assume the timing isn't right. If that changes, I'm glad to reconnect." Breakup emails often get replies because they clarify the situation.

4. **In parallel, look for the other stakeholders yourself.** On LinkedIn or the hospital group's site, look for the IT security or infrastructure lead, the director of facilities, or procurement. A short, referenced note ("[Name] suggested I reach out") to someone in IT is often more productive than a second email to the facilities manager.

**Before the next call, check your materials.** Did the deck end with a specific next step and a date? If not, add one to your follow-up. A one-pager that says "here's what we'd propose for a pilot on one building" gives him something concrete to forward internally.
