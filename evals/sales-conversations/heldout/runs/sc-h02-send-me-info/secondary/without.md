Nine days of silence after "send me more information" usually means the deal never got a real next step, not that he's rejected you. "I'll take a look" is a polite close, and nothing in it committed him to a meeting, a date, or anyone else's involvement.

**Likely causes**

- **It went to a busy inbox.** Facilities managers juggle maintenance, construction, and compliance. Your email competes with everything else, and a deck with no specific ask is easy to defer.
- **He's sharing it internally, and that's slow.** Door access touches IT, security, and sometimes procurement or a system-wide committee. He may be forwarding it, and nobody has picked it up yet.
- **There's no trigger.** You don't know yet if they have a renewal, a failing system, a new building, or a budget cycle. Without that, your software is a "nice to have."
- **The materials were generic.** A deck can't answer "how does this fit *our* sites?" If he skimmed it and saw nothing specific, it's easy to ignore.

**What to do now**

1. **Send one short follow-up today.** Nine days is a reasonable point to nudge. Keep it brief and ask one concrete question:

   > Subject: Following up: door access at [Hospital Group]
   >
   > Hi [Name],
   >
   > Following up on our call on [date]. Did you get a chance to look through the deck and one-pager?
   >
   > One question that would help me point you to the right things: roughly how many sites and doors are you managing today, and is your current system up for renewal or replacement in the next year or so?
   >
   > If it's useful, I can do a 20-minute walkthrough with you and whoever else would weigh in (IT or security, for example).
   >
   > Best,
   > [Your name]

2. **Ask who else is involved.** Door access in a hospital almost always needs an IT or security sign-off, and often procurement. Ask directly: "Who else would need to see this before it moved forward?" Then ask if you can reach out to them, or ask him to introduce you.

3. **If you hear nothing in about 7 more days,** send a short close-the-loop note: "I'll assume the timing isn't right. If that changes, happy to reconnect." This often gets a reply because it removes the pressure, and a real "not now" with a reason is still useful information.

4. **Find out what would have to be true to buy.** On the next conversation, ask about the current system, the pain point, the decision timeline, and the budget owner. If you can't get those answers, the deal is probably not as far along as it feels.
