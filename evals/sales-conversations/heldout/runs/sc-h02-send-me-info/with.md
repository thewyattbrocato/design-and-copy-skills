**What probably happened:** "Send me more information" was most likely a polite way to end the call. It wasn't a buying signal. You also sent a deck and a one-pager, which give him nothing to do and nothing to decide. Facilities managers at hospital groups are busy, and badge and door access usually involves IT, security, compliance and a budget holder above him. A generic deck is easy to put off. I'm inferring this from what you've told me. Silence doesn't prove he's lost interest, and it doesn't prove he's interested.

**What I don't know, which changes the read:**
- Did he describe a specific problem on the call, such as audit findings, lost badges, a legacy system, or a project already budgeted? If you can name one in his words, you're in better shape than if he only said "interesting."
- Did he say who signs or who else would weigh in?
- Did you agree on a date for the next conversation, or did it end at "send me info"?

If the answers are "no problem named, no signer, no date," he never agreed there was a problem to solve. That means you're back at discovery, not chasing a stalled deal. Don't use urgency, "last chance" or "just checking in" wording.

**What to do:** Send one short message, not a third round of material. Reply in the original thread so it's easy to find. Tie it to something he said, ask one narrow question, and end with a dated step and a way out:

> Hi [Name], following up on the deck and one-pager from [day]. On our call you mentioned [specific thing he said, e.g. the problem with X]. Rather than have you read through slides, one question: is that something you're actively looking to change this year, or is it more of a background issue right now?
>
> If it's active, I'd suggest 20 minutes on [specific day/time] to look at [that one thing] against your setup, and to work out who else would need to weigh in. If it's not a priority, just say so and I'll close this out. Happy to reconnect in [month] instead.

Notes on why it's built this way:
- The question asks him to say whether the problem is real, which a deck can't do for him.
- Both exits are named, so a "no" is easy to give and doesn't make him feel pushed.
- It proposes a specific time. "Let me know" asks him to do the planning.
- If you have no concrete thing he said, replace the bracket with the question you most need answered, such as how many doors or sites are involved or what prompted him to take the call. Leave it as `[to learn]` rather than guessing.

**After that:**
- **If he doesn't reply in about a week,** send one last short note. Say you'll assume the timing isn't right, you'll close it out on your side, and he can reach you whenever it becomes a priority. Then stop.
- **If there's a second contact,** such as someone in security or IT or the person he reports to, ask him for an intro on the call. Don't try to go around him.
- **For your own pipeline,** rank this lower until he has named a problem, a signer and a date. Don't forecast it as late-stage.
