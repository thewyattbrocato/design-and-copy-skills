**Goal: book one dated 20-minute call, or get a clear "not now."**

**What probably happened**

I'm inferring this from what you've told me, so treat it as a best guess.

- **The call ended without a dated next step.** "Send me more information" followed by "I'll take a look" has no date and no owner. The deck was the whole next step, and it's easy to leave unread.
- **The problem may not be sized yet.** "Sounds interesting" is not the same as "this costs us something." If you didn't leave the call with a number from him (incidents, audit findings, hours spent on badge admin, a replacement date), he has no reason to prioritize your deck over his actual workload.
- **He may not be the decision-maker.** In a hospital group, door access usually involves security, IT, compliance and finance as well as facilities. He may be waiting to see whether this is worth raising with anyone.
- **A deck and a one-pager answer "what is this?"** They don't answer "what should I do with it?"

It's less likely that he's hiding something or lost interest. Nine days of silence from a busy facilities manager is ordinary.

**What to do now**

1. **Send one short message now.** Don't write "just checking in," and don't mention urgency or the cost of waiting. Ask what the material should help him decide, and give him two easy exits. Draft:

   > Hi [Name], I sent the deck and one-pager on [date]. Rather than leave you with a stack to read, I'd like to make this useful: what's the one thing you'd want to settle first, e.g. [your most relevant example from the call: integration with your current system / cost / compliance]? If it helps, I can do 20 minutes on [day/time] and focus on only that. And if the timing is wrong or it's not a fit, just say so and I'll close this out. No hard feelings.

   Fill in the bracketed example from what he actually said on the call. If you don't remember him raising anything specific, ask the question without an example.

2. **If you get no reply in 3–4 business days, call once** and leave a short voicemail that names the same question and the same two exits. Don't change channel again after that.

3. **If there's still nothing, send a final close-out note.** Say you'll assume the timing isn't right, say you're happy to reconnect in [month] if he wants that, and stop. Rank this deal low in your forecast in the meantime. It's an unqualified lead, not a late-stage one.

**If he replies**

- **If he gives you a concern,** answer that one thing and ask for the 20 minutes.
- **If he says it's interesting but he needs to take it to others,** ask who they are and what each would want to see. Offer to join one short conversation, so you aren't relying on him to forward a deck. Don't offer a demo or pilot until three things are on the table: a problem someone feels and can size, a person who signs or a route to a signature, and what they'll put in.
- **If he says "not now,"** ask once whether it's timing or fit, record his reason in his words, and set a return date only if he's glad to have one.
