**What probably happened:** almost certainly not a "no." "Send me more information" is the lowest-cost way to end a call politely. It often means "mildly interested, but not a priority," and nothing in it commits him to anything. Hospital facilities managers also get pulled into incidents, audits, and Joint Commission prep constantly. And a deck plus a one-pager asks him to do the work of figuring out why it matters to him. Silence at 9 days is normal and says little about his interest.

**What to do:**

1. **Follow up now, not later.** Nine days is a fine time to follow up. Waiting longer only makes it colder.

2. **Don't send "just checking in" or "did you get my materials?"** That asks him for effort and gives him nothing. Reply on your original email thread and add one specific, useful thing tied to what he said on the call. For example:
   - A short example of how a similar hospital or health system handled the problem he mentioned (access audits, contractor or vendor badging, lockdown procedures, offboarding, compliance reporting)
   - One concrete insight, such as a common gap in hospital access control, a regulatory angle, or a number
   - A specific, low-effort next step

3. **Make the ask small and specific.** Something like:

   > Hi [Name], following up on the deck I sent. You mentioned [specific pain point from the call]. [One sentence of relevant proof or insight.] Would a 15-minute walkthrough of just that piece make sense next week? I could do Tuesday or Thursday afternoon.

   Offering two times beats "let me know if you're interested."

4. **If there's no reply in 3–4 business days, try a different channel.** Send a short LinkedIn message or make a brief call. Voicemail plus a one-line email works well.

5. **Run a light cadence after that.** Aim for 3–4 more touches over about 3 weeks, each adding something new (a case study, a relevant news item, a question). Then send a polite breakup note, such as "I'll assume the timing isn't right and close this out, but happy to reconnect whenever it makes sense." Those often get replies.

6. **Widen the net.** If you haven't already, ask whether anyone else should be involved, such as IT, security, or compliance. At a hospital group, a facilities manager rarely decides this alone, and he may be waiting to find out who should look at it. You can offer: "Happy to loop in whoever on your team handles security or IT if that's useful."
