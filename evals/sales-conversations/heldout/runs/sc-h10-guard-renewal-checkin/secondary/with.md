Here's a 20-minute agenda that keeps it light but still gets you useful information before the renewal.

**1. Open (2 min)**
Thank them for three years, and say the purpose is to make sure the app still fits how they work before renewal.

**2. How they use it today (5 min)**
- Which parts of the app does the crew use most? Which does the office use?
- What would they miss most if it were gone?

**3. Wins they can point to (4 min)**
- Ask what got better since they started: fewer missed jobs, faster scheduling, easier invoicing.
- If you have their usage or time-saved numbers, share one. Customers remember specifics better than "you're doing great."

**4. Friction they haven't mentioned (4 min)**
- "What's something you work around that you wish worked differently?"
- "If you could add one thing, what would it be?"

"Everything's great" often hides small annoyances. You're asking, not pitching, so just write down what you hear.

**5. Where the business is going (3 min)**
- Any plans to add crews, service lines, or seasonal staff? Growth is the most natural opening for seats or add-ons, but don't push it in this meeting.
- Any changes in who owns the decision or who uses the app day to day?

**6. Renewal and next step (2 min)**
- Confirm the renewal date and terms.
- Set a specific next touchpoint, such as a call after renewal or a quarterly check-in on the calendar.
- If it comes up naturally, ask whether they'd be open to a referral or a short case study. Don't force it.

**Before the call, decide:** whether a flat renewal is what you actually want. Three years at the same price is a reasonable choice for a loyal customer, but if your costs or product have changed, a small increase or a multi-year option may be worth raising now rather than later. That's a separate conversation from this one, so settle it beforehand.
