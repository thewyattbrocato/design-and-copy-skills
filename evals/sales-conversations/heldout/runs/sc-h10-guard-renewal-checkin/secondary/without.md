**20-minute check-in agenda**

1. **Open (2 min).** Thank them for three years and ask how the season is going. Let them talk first.

2. **How they're using it (6 min).** Ask what a typical day looks like, from scheduling a crew to invoicing. Ask which features they rely on most. You're listening for workflows they've built around the app and any workarounds they've made.

3. **Friction, lightly (4 min).** Ask: "Is there anything that's annoying but not worth a support ticket?" Even happy customers usually have one small thing. Hearing it now is cheaper than hearing about it at renewal.

4. **What's coming for them (4 min).** Ask what changes for the business in the next year: hiring, new service lines, more crews, new equipment. Growth is the most likely source of future expansion, and it's a natural lead-in to anything new you offer.

5. **Renewal and close (3 min).** Confirm the renewal is on track and the terms are what they expect. Say you're glad to keep them on. If you'd like a referral or a case study, ask once, lightly, and let them say no.

**Two notes:**
- Since renewal is next month, don't let the meeting be only social. Make sure you leave with a clear "yes, we're renewing" or a specific concern to address before it's too late.
- Keeping the price flat for a three-year customer is a reasonable call. If you want to keep it that way, mention the renewal as a loyalty gesture so they know it was deliberate. That tends to earn more goodwill than the price just not changing.
