Here's a 20-minute agenda. Keep it conversational, and spend most of the time asking questions.

**1. Open (2 min)**
Thank them for three years and say the purpose is to see how things are going. Make clear it's not a sales call.

**2. How it's going (5 min)**
- "What does your team use the app for most day to day?"
- "Walk me through a typical week or a busy-season day."

Listen for specific examples. Those are what you can reuse later, like in a testimonial or case study, if they're willing.

**3. Small frictions (4 min)**
- "Is there anything you work around, or that's slower than it should be?"
- "Anything you've wished it did?"

Even happy customers have small annoyances. Hearing them now is cheaper than hearing about them at renewal, and this is the most useful part of the call.

**4. What's changing on their side (4 min)**
- "Any growth plans for the next year? New crews, new services, more seasonal hiring?"
- "Anything that would change how you use the app?"

Headcount and service changes affect whether their plan still fits. Note anything they mention.

**5. What's next (2 min)**
Ask what they'd want from the product. Mention one or two upcoming things only if they're real and likely to ship.

**6. Renewal heads-up (2 min)**
- Mention that renewal is coming up next month, and confirm who handles billing and who should be included in the renewal conversation.
- If pricing is staying flat, say so clearly. Long-time customers like hearing that without having to ask. Confirm internally first that it's approved.
- If any terms are changing, tell them now rather than in the renewal email.

**7. Close (1 min)**
Thank them and name one concrete follow-up, such as "I'll send the feature you asked about" or "I'll check on that integration." Then do the follow-up.

**Before the call:** Look at their usage for any recent drop-off, check for open support tickets, and confirm who your main contact is.
