**Verdict:** this is a menu tour, not a demo. Eight sections in 45 minutes gives about 5 minutes each. Nothing in the plan says what the three of them need to see, so they have to guess which screen is their answer.

**1. Goal and fit**

- **"I can go through it all and they'll see how much it does."** The plan has no goal, and breadth isn't a goal. Three people who run dispatch for 40 techs will remember the one or two things that fix their day, not the other six. Pick one goal, for example: *they agree to a dated next step (a trial on their own data, or a meeting with whoever signs).* Decide that before you build anything.
- **No gate check.** I don't know from what you've told me whether you've had discovery. Before a full demo, three things should be true:
  - They feel a problem they can put a number on.
  - You know who signs, or how a signature happens.
  - They'll put something in, such as last month's schedule or a second stakeholder.

  If you haven't had discovery, make the first 10 minutes of tomorrow discovery and demo only what it points to. If you have, write down what they said, because it should drive the cut.
- **Attendees aren't planned for.** For each of the three, write: what they're measured on, why they're missing it today, and what the product must do about it. A head of dispatch likely cares about drive time, idle gaps and same-day capacity. Team leads likely care about reschedules, tech complaints and call-backs. Those are guesses until you've heard them, so `[to learn]`.

**2. Discovery**

- **Item 1, "Intro and company background, 5 min."** This costs 11% of the meeting on you, not them. Replace it with a 2-minute situation line: "From what I understand, you run ~40 techs, [problem], because [reason]. Today I'll show X and Y. Is that right, and what's missing?" That check also lets you correct the plan live.
- **Questions to get answered, each with the number it yields:**
  - How many jobs per tech per day, against what you'd like? (Yields job count and the target.)
  - How long does a same-day emergency insert take today? (Yields minutes of dispatcher time.)
  - How many missed or late arrivals last month, and what did they cost? (Yields a miss rate and a cost.)
  - What do you run on now, and what's the renewal date? (Yields the switching window.)
  - Who else signs off? (Yields the route to purchase.)

**3. The showing**

- **Order is menu order (dashboard first).** A dashboard is the least recognizable thing you can open with. Show the result first: a day's board, finished, that looks like theirs, with the route already optimized. Then show the shortest path to it.
- **Cut line.** Write three lists: **in**, **out**, **reserve**.
  - Likely **in**: calendar and drag-drop booking, auto-routing and map, and the tech app (whichever answers their stated problem).
  - Likely **reserve**: reporting and integrations, unless someone asks. Ask about integrations early, though, because a missing accounting or CRM link can sink a deal.
  - Likely **out**: the dashboard tour, and customer texts, unless they raised missed appointments.

  If time halves, everything below the line goes.
- **Sample data.** If you can't use their data, label the demo data "sample data" on screen. Don't say "imagine this is yours."
- **Questions only arrive at the end (4 minutes).** Pricing and Q&A together in 4 minutes means questions get cut. Triage them live: answer yes/no ones now, park the rest on a visible list. Leave about 12 minutes for questions and next step.
- **Mid-demo failure.** If a step breaks, say so, show a prepared screenshot, and move on. Don't retry in front of them.

**4. Wording**

- **Item 8, "Pricing."** Putting price in the last 4 minutes, after a feature tour, invites sticker shock with no value anchored. Show value in their numbers first, then give one figure with what it covers, then stop talking. If the head of dispatch asks about price earlier, give a range and what moves it.
- **Adjectives.** Cut "powerful" or "seamless" unless you can attach a count, a measured figure or a thing shown.
- **The end is missing a next step.** Item 8 doesn't end on one. Close with who does what by when, said aloud, on both calendars, and confirmed in writing today. For example: "You send me a week of schedules by Friday; I'll build your routes on it and we review Tuesday at 2 with whoever signs."

**Suggested 45 minutes**

| Min | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Situation line, confirm or correct |
| 3–7 | Result first: finished day board, auto-routed, on sample data |
| 7–22 | Shortest path, then the 2–3 abilities tied to their stated problems |

| 25–35 | Reserve items, only if asked; parked questions |
