The plan is a feature tour, and that's the main risk. Three people who run dispatch will remember a few concrete moments, not eight features, and 45 minutes won't leave room for them to react.

**What's strong**
- Covering the core dispatch loop (calendar, routing, tech app, customer texts) is the right territory for this audience.
- Starting with company background shows you did some homework.

**What to change**

1. **Start with their problems, not your company.** Cut the background to about a minute. Spend 5 to 7 minutes asking how dispatch works today: what tool or spreadsheet, how they handle same-day emergencies, where the day usually falls apart, and what a missed or late appointment costs them. Their answers should decide which features you show.

2. **Demo fewer things, using their scenarios.** Eight segments at 4 to 8 minutes each means every feature gets a surface-level look. Pick two or three workflows that match what they told you and go deep on those. For plumbing and HVAC, that probably means a same-day emergency being inserted into a full schedule, rerouting around it, and telling the customer the tech's ETA.

3. **Cut or defer reporting and integrations.** Reporting is rarely what changes a dispatcher's day. Integrations only matter if they name the system they already use. Offer them as a follow-up.

4. **Let them drive, at least briefly.** Hand over the mouse for the drag-and-drop booking. Dispatchers who try it themselves remember it.

5. **Don't end on pricing.** Pricing at minute 41 gets rushed, and it lands better after they've seen value. Ask early what they're paying now or what a dispatch software budget looks like, so you aren't guessing at the close. Also confirm who signs. The head of dispatch may be the champion rather than the decision maker.

**Revised 45-minute structure**
- **0–7 min:** Discovery questions. Confirm pain points and the tools they use now.
- **7–20 min:** Dispatch board. Calendar, drag-and-drop booking, and auto-routing on a day they'd recognize. Let someone click.
- **20–30 min:** Tech app and customer texts, framed around missed appointments and "where's my tech" calls.
- **30–35 min:** Reporting or integrations only if their discovery answers call for it.
- **35–45 min:** Open questions, pricing, and a concrete next step, such as a pilot with one or two crews and a follow-up date.

**Prep before tomorrow**
- Load demo data that looks like their business: trade-specific job types (drain clear, no-cool call, furnace tune-up), realistic durations, and a mix of scheduled and emergency calls. Generic demo data makes it feel generic.
- Have a backup in case the live environment is slow or the Wi-Fi drops. Screenshots or a recorded walkthrough are enough.
- Find out whether they use ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, or something else. Know the two or three differences that matter to them, and don't trash the competitor.
- Prepare one or two sharp ROI questions, such as "How many callbacks did you have last month?" or "What does one missed same-day call cost you?" That gives the pricing conversation something to hang on.
