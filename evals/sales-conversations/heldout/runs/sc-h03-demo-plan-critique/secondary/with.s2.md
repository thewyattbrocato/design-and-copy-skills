## Critique, ranked

**1. No goal, and "they'll see how much it does" is the wrong bet.** Eight features in 45 minutes is a tour. Three people will leave with a blur of screens and no reason to act. The only ability that matters to a dispatch team is the one that removes their current pain, so the plan needs to start from that pain, not from your feature list.

**2. No discovery, and no gate check.** Nothing in the agenda asks how dispatch works today, how many jobs a day, what goes wrong, or who signs. Before you show anything, you need a felt problem, a signer or a route to one, and something they'll put in. The plan has none of these. If the gate isn't met by minute 15, the right move is to shrink the demo and book the signer, not to keep clicking.

**3. Intro at 5 minutes is spent on what you already know.** You know their size and trade. Say so in one sentence and ask them to confirm it.

**4. Pricing at minute 41 is priced before value.** Four minutes for pricing plus Q&A means the price lands cold and the questions get cut short. Price should come after the buyer has seen their problem solved, as one figure with what it covers, and then you stop talking.

**5. Wrong order for this audience.** Auto-routing is the strongest feature for a dispatch head, and it's at minute 16 at the earliest. Mobile app and reporting are the least likely to matter to the head of dispatch in this room. Dashboard and integrations are generic.

**6. No next step.** The plan ends on pricing and Q&A. It needs a dated, owned close: who will do what, by when, on both calendars.
