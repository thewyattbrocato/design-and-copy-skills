**Goal:** Show the two dispatch abilities the head of dispatch names as their pain, then book a dated meeting with whoever signs.

**Facts first**

- **Known:** three attendees (head of dispatch, two team leads); mid-size plumbing and HVAC company with about 40 techs; 45 minutes tomorrow at 2pm; you sell scheduling and routing.
- **Unknown:** what hurts today [to learn]; who signs and whether they are in the room [to learn]; current software and what must connect to it [to learn]; jobs per day and reschedules per week [to learn].
- **Assumed:** the head of dispatch feels the scheduling pain daily. That is likely, but it is not yet confirmed.

**The main problem: it's a tour, not a demo.** Eight segments of 4 to 8 minutes each means no single ability gets enough time to land. "See how much it does" is the product's goal, not the buyer's. Nobody in the room has said which of those abilities matters, so most of the 45 minutes will be spent on things they may never use. Your product knowledge is the reason you can run a full tour, but it doesn't tell you which parts they need.

**Weak moments and what they cost**

1. **"Intro and company background, 5 min."** You will spend five minutes telling them what they already know. Replace it with a 3-minute confirmation: "Here's what I understand about your dispatch; is that right?" Then ask what a bad week looks like.
2. **"Dashboard overview" first.** It leads with features, not the result they would recognise. Open on tomorrow's board with their job types, labelled sample data, already rebuilt after a cancellation.
3. **Eight equal segments.** Equal time means the audience's priorities get no weight. Give the most time to the abilities they named; put everything else in reserve.
4. **Customer texts and mobile app, 10 min combined.** These matter to team leads, but only if arrival complaints or missed techs came up earlier. If not, they are reserve.
5. **Integrations, 5 min, unasked.** If an integration is a hard requirement, you need to know it before the meeting. Ask it as a pre-meeting question; don't demo it blind.
6. **Pricing at minute 41 of 45.** Price shown last, in four minutes, with Q&A squeezed around it, reads as a bolt-on. Show value first, then one figure with what it covers, then stop talking.
7. **No ending.** The plan has no next step with an owner and a date. "Let me know your thoughts" would end the call.
8. **No gate check.** Nothing confirms that someone who can sign is in the room, or that the problem is felt and sized. If the signer isn't there, the goal should be to book them, not to close.

**Revised 45-minute shape (use only what the attendees named)**

| Min | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Confirm the problem in their words; ask for the number that matters, e.g. jobs rebuilt per day [to learn] |
| 3–6 | Show the result first: tomorrow's board, labelled sample data, rebuilt after a cancellation |
| 6–14 | Drag-and-drop rebooking, if they named rebooking as pain |
| 14–24 | Auto-routing and map, measured against their drive-time or job-count number |
| 24–29 | Texts only if arrival or no-show problems came up; otherwise reserve |
| 29–32 | Summary: the abilities shown and the change in their number |
| 32–37 | Parked questions answered |
| 37–42 | Price as one figure with what it covers, then stop talking |
| 42–45 | Next step, said aloud: who, what, date, and the signer invited |

**Gate check:** Before the call, ask the head of dispatch who signs and what would make this worth their time. If no signer is available, the outcome is a dated meeting with the signer, not a pitch.

**Two notes for you to check:** Confirm the numbers and integrations before the call, and make sure the sample data is clearly labelled.
