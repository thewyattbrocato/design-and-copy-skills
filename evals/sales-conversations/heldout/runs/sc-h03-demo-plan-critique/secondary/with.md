**Goal of this call:** Learn whether the dispatch problem is felt and sized, and book a named next meeting. Don't demo the full product. Your plan has no goal line, and as written it fails the qualification gate: you don't yet know the problem, who signs, or what they'll share.

**Facts first**
- Known from you: 45 minutes, tomorrow 2pm; head of dispatch plus two team leads; about 40 techs; plumbing and HVAC; you sell scheduling and routing software.
- Unknown: their current pain and its cost, whether they've already named a problem, who signs and the route to purchase, their current field-service or accounting system, and whether they expect pricing.
- Assumed: this is a first or second meeting. If you've already had discovery, tell me what they said and I'll redo this.

**Critique, ranked**

1. **Goal and fit (biggest issue).** "They'll see how much it does" is the wrong bet. Three busy people in a feature tour remember nothing and conclude it's a lot to learn. Eight segments in 45 minutes means nothing gets finished, and you'll be talking at minute 40 with no time for their reaction.
2. **No gate check.** You don't know whether anyone in the room feels a problem you can size, whether the head of dispatch can sign, or whether they'll share anything. Demoing before that means you're showing without a basis for what matters.
3. **Opening wastes five minutes.** They know their company. Replace the background slides with a one-sentence situation line and a question: "You've got about 40 techs; what's the part of dispatch that's costing you the most time right now?"
4. **Dashboard and reporting are generic.** Five minutes each on overview and reporting is breadth, not proof. Show only what answers a named problem; park the rest.
5. **Pricing in the last four minutes.** Price after value, as one figure with what it covers. Rushed at the end it reads as a closing push, and it's the moment people are least able to weigh it.
6. **Nothing is sized.** No question yields a number. Ask things like: how many jobs per tech per day; how often do reschedules or missed windows happen per week; what does a missed or late appointment cost; how many drive-time hours per day across the fleet. Mark anything you don't have as `[to learn]`.
7. **No next step.** "Let me know" or "I'll follow up" is not an ending. Finish with one dated step, an owner, and a calendar invite sent that day.

**Revised 45-minute structure** (assumes a real dispatch problem is confirmed in the first 10 minutes)

| Min | Segment | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Situation line, confirm what's known | Open with their numbers, not your deck |
| 5–15 | Size the problem and identify who feels it | Head of dispatch, team leads, and who signs |
| 15–35 | Show 2 agreed abilities, result first | Likely: scheduling/calendar booking and auto-routing with map view |
| 35–40 | Summary and parked questions | Name each ability and the open items |
| 40–45 | Next step: who, what, when | Book the named follow-up before anyone leaves |

Cut lines:
- **In:** whatever answers the problem they named (likely calendar booking, routing, and text notifications if missed windows come up).
- **Reserve:** mobile app, reporting, integrations. Show only if a team lead asks how techs will use it or what it connects to.
- **Out:** the company background, the generic dashboard tour, and pricing unless someone asks. If asked, give one figure with what it includes, then stop talking.
