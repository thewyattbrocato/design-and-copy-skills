Your plan is organized around the product, but your audience is listening for their own problems. A dispatch head and two team leads will judge the demo on whether it fixes the daily pain of missed windows, long drive times, double-booked techs, and calls that go to voicemail. A complete tour of features is less likely to win them over.

**Main issues**

- **Feature tour instead of problem-solving.** "They'll see how much it does" is the wrong goal. Eight sections in 45 minutes means each one is a brief preview, and they'll forget most of it. Pick two or three workflows that match their pain and go deep.
- **No time for questions or interruptions.** Your sections add up to exactly 45 minutes, with nothing left for tech hiccups, side questions, or a slow map load. Expect them to interrupt, and plan for it.
- **Too much time on intro.** Five minutes on company background is wasted when they already know their business. Use 1-2 minutes to state the goal, then ask questions.
- **Pricing last and rushed.** Four minutes at the end leaves no room for the objections that pricing usually brings up. Consider sending pricing ahead of time or moving it to the close.
- **Integrations may be a deal-breaker.** If they use a specific field service, accounting, or CRM system, that matters more than reporting. Ask about this before the demo. If the answer is no, cut the section.
- **Mobile app is for techs, not the buyers.** Show it as a short "what happens in the field" moment tied to the dispatch flow, not as a standalone section.

**Things to do before tomorrow**

- Ask the head of dispatch in advance what they want to leave with and which tools they use now. If you can't reach them, open with those questions.
- Use demo data that looks like their work: drain clogs, no-cool calls, water heater swaps, and their actual service zones. Generic data makes the routing look like a toy.
- Show the override. Dispatchers often distrust auto-routing. Show the constraints the engine respects (skills, certifications, truck stock, time windows), then show how a dispatcher can override it.
- Run the demo once against a timer. Note what you can cut.
- Have a backup: screenshots or a recorded clip for the map and texts, in case the network fails.

**Suggested structure (45 min)**

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Their goals, current tools, top 2 pains. Adjust the plan based on answers. |
| 3–18 | Calendar and drag-and-drop booking, then auto-routing and map, using a realistic emergency call that gets slotted into a full day. Let the dispatch head drive. |
| 18–26 | Customer text notifications (ETA, reminders) and a quick tech app walkthrough from a job arrival to invoice. |
| 26–34 | Reporting on the metrics they care about (drive time, jobs per tech, first-time fix), plus integrations only if they use those systems. |
| 34–40 | Pricing and what a pilot would look like. |
| 40–45 | Open Q&A and agree on a specific next step. |

Close with a concrete next step, such as a two-week pilot with one team, and confirm who else needs to approve it. Your strength with the product is an asset here, but use it to answer their questions, not to narrate everything.
