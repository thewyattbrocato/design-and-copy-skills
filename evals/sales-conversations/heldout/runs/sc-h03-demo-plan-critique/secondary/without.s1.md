Your sequence follows the product, but these buyers will judge it by their own day, so I'd restructure around their pain.

**The main problem: a feature tour, not a conversation.** "They'll see how much it does" is the instinct to avoid. Head of dispatch and team leads don't buy breadth. They buy relief from the specific mess they deal with every morning: rebuilding the board after a sick call, tech drive time, emergency calls that blow up the day, customers asking "where's my tech?" Eight modules back to back will blur together, and the one that matters most to them may land at minute 20 after they've stopped listening.

**Other issues:**

- **No discovery.** Your plan has no time to ask questions. Spend 5 to 7 minutes up front asking how they build tomorrow's schedule today, how many reschedules a day they absorb, what breaks, and what they currently use (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, spreadsheets, etc.). Their answers tell you which 2 or 3 features to show in depth.
- **Integrations are a likely deal-breaker, and you've given them 5 minutes at the end of the list.** For an HVAC shop, the question is usually "does it talk to our CRM/accounting?" Find out before the call, and cover only what they name.
- **Pricing at minute 41 is too late.** They'll ask about per-tech cost once they're interested. Have a number ready for 40 techs and say it when it comes up, not as a closing slide.
- **Your timing has no buffer.** The 45 minutes sum exactly, with no room for questions or a detour. Something will run long, and the pricing and close will get squeezed.
- **Techs are the end users, but the audience is dispatch.** Show the mobile app briefly, framed as "the tech taps 'on my way' and the customer gets a text," not as a standalone feature.

**A restructured version:**

1. **0–7 min:** Discovery. Ask about their week, their pain points, and their current tools.
2. **7–17 min:** Calendar and drag-and-drop, but in a real scenario. Use a mess that looks like their Tuesday: one tech calls out sick, two emergency no-cool calls come in, one job runs over. Build the fix live.
3. **17–27 min:** Auto-routing and map. Use the same scenario to show drive time and the assignment the engine suggests. Quantify it if you can ("this saves about X miles a day across 40 techs").
4. **27–33 min:** Customer texts and the tech app as one loop: the tech taps "on my way," the customer gets an ETA text, and dispatch sees it update.
5. **33–38 min:** Reporting and integrations, limited to what they raised in discovery.
6. **38–45 min:** Pricing for their team size, Q&A, and a specific next step (a pilot with one region or their own data, with a date).

**Prep:**

- Load sample data that looks like their service area and trade mix, and use their company name if you can.
- Record a backup video or keep screenshots in case the Wi-Fi fails.
- Test on the room's connection and display setup beforehand.
- Send them a short agenda in advance asking what they most want to see. Their answer also shows you which module to slow down on.
