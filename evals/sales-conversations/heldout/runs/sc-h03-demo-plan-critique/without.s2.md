**The main problem:** this plan is a tour of your product, not a response to their problems. "They'll see how much it does" is the risk. A head of dispatch who sees eight features will remember the two that mattered to them, if any, and you won't know which two. Showing everything also tells them you don't know what they care about.

**Specific issues**

- **No discovery.** You spend 0 minutes learning about their situation and 5 minutes on your company. They'll have already looked you up, so cut the background to 1 minute. Spend the freed time asking questions.
- **The order follows your menu.** The dashboard overview is the weakest slot. Dispatchers don't buy dashboards, they buy fewer 7am scrambles. Start with their worst day, not your home screen.
- **Features are shown, not scenarios.** Drag-and-drop booking and the routing engine are the heart of your pitch to dispatch. Show them solving a real situation: an emergency burst-pipe call comes in at 10am, two techs are running late, and one job needs an HVAC-certified tech. Add skill matching, drive time, and truck inventory if you have them. That is more persuasive than 8 minutes of calendar clicking.
- **Q&A is squeezed.** Four minutes for pricing, questions, and next steps is too little. With three people in the room, you want at least 10 minutes of back-and-forth. Interruptions mid-demo are a good sign, so leave slack for them.
- **No close.** Nothing in the plan covers next steps, such as a trial with their real data, a follow-up with the owner or finance, or migration and onboarding. Migration is often the unspoken objection for a 40-tech shop that already runs on something.
- **Reporting and integrations get 5 minutes, but it depends.** If they run QuickBooks or another system, integrations may be a dealbreaker and deserve real time. If not, give them 1 minute.

**Suggested restructure (45 min)**

1. **Discovery (8 min).** Ask about their current tools, what breaks on a bad day, and how many reschedules and emergencies they handle. Take notes and reuse their words later.
2. **Their top 2–3 workflows, demoed end to end (22 min).** Likely candidates are same-day emergency insertion with routing and the map, the tech's view in the mobile app, and the customer text updates that cut "where's my tech?" calls. Let the team leads drive questions about the tech app. They live with the consequences.
3. **Quick hits (3 min).** Reporting and integrations, only what they asked about.
4. **Pricing, objections, next steps (10 min).** End with a specific ask, like a pilot with a few techs or a data import.
5. **Intro (1–2 min)** at the very start.

**Before tomorrow**

- Load demo data that looks like theirs: about 40 techs, plumbing and HVAC job types, local-sounding addresses.
- Have a fallback, such as a recorded clip or screenshots, for the routing engine in case it lags or fails.
- Rehearse the three scenarios, not the feature list. Being good at the product helps most when you can skip around in response to what they react to.
