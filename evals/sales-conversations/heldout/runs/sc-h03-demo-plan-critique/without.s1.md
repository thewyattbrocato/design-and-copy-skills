Your plan is a feature tour in menu order. Going through everything shows how much the product does, but it won't show them that it solves their problems. A dispatch head with 40 techs wants to know whether Tuesday at 10am, when two techs call out and three emergency calls arrive, gets easier. Eight sections at about 5 minutes each also leaves no room to dig into the one thing they care about.

**Main problems**

1. **No discovery.** You don't know what they use now (spreadsheets, whiteboard, ServiceTitan, Jobber?) or what hurts. Without that, you're guessing which 8 minutes matter. Ask on a short pre-call today, or spend the first 5–8 minutes of the demo on it.
2. **The intro is 5 minutes of you talking about yourself.** Cut it to 1–2 minutes. Spend the time on their situation instead.
3. **The dashboard overview is the weakest slot.** Dashboards are abstract until people have seen the workflow that feeds them. Drop it, or show it last as the result.
4. **The order follows the product, not their day.** Routing is probably your strongest differentiator, and it's fourth.
5. **Pricing and Q&A get 4 minutes at the end.** Questions will come up during the demo, and dispatchers will interrupt. If you hold them until the end, you'll either rush or run over. Budget for that, and leave real time for pricing and next steps.
6. **No close.** The plan ends at pricing, with no agreed next step such as a trial, a pilot with a few techs, or a follow-up with the owner.

**Suggested restructure (45 min)**

- **0–8:** Quick intro, then discovery. Ask how they dispatch today, what happens when an emergency call comes in, how they handle skills and certifications (gas, refrigerant), and what customers complain about.
- **8–30:** Run one story, "a day in your shop," using HVAC and plumbing jobs rather than generic ones.
  - The morning schedule comes in and auto-routing builds the day (map view).
  - An emergency call arrives, you drag-and-drop to reshuffle, and the route updates.
  - The customer gets the text notification.
  - The tech sees the change on the mobile app and closes out the job.
- **30–36:** Show reporting and integrations only where they answered a pain point. Name their accounting and field-service tools specifically.
- **36–45:** Pricing for 40 techs, questions, and a specific next step.

**Prep for tomorrow**

- Load demo data that looks like their business: about 40 techs, plumbing and HVAC job types, realistic local addresses.
- Pre-stage the emergency-call moment so it works the first time.
- Prepare an answer on how data migrates from their current system. It's the most common worry.
- Expect "what happens when it's wrong?" about auto-routing. Dispatchers don't trust algorithms by default, so show how they can override it.
- Have a fallback, such as screenshots or a recording, if the live demo breaks.
