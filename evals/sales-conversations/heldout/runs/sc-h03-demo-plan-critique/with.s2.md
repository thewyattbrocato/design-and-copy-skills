**Critique, ranked**

1. **No stated goal, and no gate check (goal and fit).**
 - Your plan is "go through it all so they see how much it does." That's an inform-only demo. With nothing to choose what to show, you have nothing to cut.
 - Before you build the plan, check three things. Is there a problem someone in the room feels and can size? Is there a signer or a route to one? Will they put something in, such as data, time, or a second stakeholder?
 - You didn't say whether the head of dispatch signs. You also didn't say what they told you before this meeting. Write down `[to learn]` for each unknown. If nobody in the room feels a problem, tomorrow should be discovery, not a demo.

2. **No discovery or situation line (discovery).**
 - Your plan has 5 minutes of intro and company background and no questions. Cut your company background to about 60 seconds. Nobody there needs it.
 - Use that time to confirm what you already know, in one sentence ("I understood X; is that still right?"). Then ask for the numbers that decide what you show. Each question should yield a number:
 - Jobs booked per day, and how many get rescheduled: `[to learn]`
 - Minutes dispatch spends building tomorrow's routes: `[to learn]`
 - No-shows and "where's my tech?" calls per week: `[to learn]`
 - What a missed or late job cost last time: `[to learn]`
 - Who decides, and what else they're comparing: `[to learn]`

3. **The agenda follows your menu, not their problems (the showing).**
 - Seven feature segments in 45 minutes averages about 6 minutes each. That's a tour, and a tour gives them nothing to remember.
 - Plan around each person. For the head of dispatch and each team lead, write the problem they're measured on, why it isn't being met, and what the product must do about it. Then sort every feature into **in**, **out**, and **reserve**.
 - My guess is that auto-routing and the map, drag-and-drop booking, and the tech app are **in**. Customer texts are probably **in** only if no-shows or "where's my tech" calls are a pain point. Dashboard and reporting/integrations are likely **reserve**, shown only if someone asks. Confirm those guesses against what they tell you.
 - Mark a cut line. If time halves, everything below it goes.

4. **The first thing they'd recognize as an answer comes too late (the showing).**
 - Right now the dashboard comes first. It's the least tied to a problem, and the first real payoff (routing) doesn't appear until about minute 18.
 - Open with the result: a finished day with routes already built, on sample data labeled "sample data." Or use a real sample of their day if they've agreed to share one. Then show the shortest path to get there.
 - Add a one-line summary after each segment. Leave the strongest screen up during questions.

5. **Q&A is squeezed in with pricing in 4 minutes (the showing).**
 - Questions will come during the demo, so triage them. Answer now if it's a sentence and tied to something they've asked for. Park the rest on a visible list and answer them at the end.

 - Don't quote price at the end of a rushed block. Price comes after value, as one figure with what it covers. If they ask for it earlier, answer or give a range and say what moves it.
