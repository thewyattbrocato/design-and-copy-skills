**Critique, ranked: goal and fit, discovery, the showing, wording**

**1. No goal, and no check that the gate is met**
- Quote: "I can go through it all and they'll see how much it does." Cost: the goal is to show breadth, so nothing is chosen for these three people. Eight sections of features is a menu tour, and buyers usually remember little of one.
- You haven't said whether anyone in the room has told you a problem. You also haven't said who signs or what they'll put in. Head of dispatch and team leads may not be the signer. If a problem and a signer are still `[to learn]`, tomorrow is a discovery-flavored session and you should say so openly. If you do know them, the demo has to prove those specific things.
- Replacement first line for the plan: "Goal: show that [their stated dispatch problem, in their numbers] goes away, and leave with a dated next step with the person who signs."

**2. No discovery, and no people-based plan**
- Quote: "Intro and company background, 5 min." Cost: your company's story spends a ninth of the time on what they don't need. Swap it for a situation line and confirmation: "From our earlier conversation, [problem] is costing you [number], because [reason]. Is that still right?" Use what you already know. If you know nothing, say why you're asking and ask for the situation.
- Write one line for each attendee: what they're measured on, why it's not being met, and what the product must do about it. A head of dispatch might be measured on [jobs per tech per day, drive time, same-day fill rate: `[to learn]`]. Team leads probably feel it as morning rework and callbacks. Ask each person how their work is judged. Don't assume.

**3. The showing: structure and order**
- Quote: "Dashboard overview" at minute 5. Cost: a dashboard shows nothing about their problem and delays the first screen they'd recognize as their answer. Open on the result. For example, show a day with a real-looking mix of emergency calls, a no-show and a tech running late, rebuilt and routed. Label it sample data. Then show the shortest path to get there.
- Quote: "Calendar view and drag-and-drop booking, 8 min" followed by "Auto-routing and map view, 8 min". Cost: these are two views of the same ability, and they take 16 of your 45 minutes. Ordering by menu is the issue. Order by what they care about most. If the main pain is the day falling apart at 10am, start there.
- Quote: "Reporting and integrations, 5 min." Cost: this is a skim of everything. Integrations only matter if you know their stack (QuickBooks, ServiceTitan, etc., `[to learn]`). Move it to reserve and show it only if someone asks.
- Sort the features before you build anything. Put in what answers a named problem, out what you won't show, and reserve what you'll show only on request. Mark a cut line: if the time halves, everything below it goes. Notifications and the tech app are probably in only if they've named missed appointments, late arrivals, or techs calling in for job details.
- Add a summary after each segment, ending on the strongest screen, which you leave up. You currently have no summaries and no time for them.
- Budget at least 10 minutes for questions during the demo. Park off-track ones on a visible list and answer them at the end. Answer "can it do X?" in a sentence. If it can't, say "no, not today" and ask whether it's a nice-to-have or a real need. Never promise a feature that doesn't exist.

**4. Pricing and Q&A in 4 minutes**
- Cost: price squeezed in at the end with no time to talk about value. It will land cold, and Q&A gets crowded out. Plan: put the price after value, give one figure with what it covers, then stop talking. Keep a flip condition ready for if they push back on the first number. Don't offer a discount in response to a first reaction.

**5. No next step**
- Cost: the plan ends on pricing and has no dated step. Replacement for the last 3 minutes: "Based on what you saw, the next step is [named step: a call with whoever signs, or a ten-minute check of your own job data]. I'll send the recap today. Can we put [day, time] on both calendars?" Name who owns what.

**Suggested shape for 45 minutes** (adjust once you know their problem)
- 0–3: situation line, confirm it, and ask each person what they want to see
- 3–8: the result first, in a form they'd recognize, then the fast path
- 8–28: two or three agreed abilities in the order they care about, with a summary after each
- 28–38: parked questions and Q&A
- 38–45: price (one figure, then stop) and the dated next step
