## Before the call (20 min)

- **Her LinkedIn:** tenure, prior roles (any facilities, ops, or multi-unit food service background), and what she posts about.
- **The chain's site and press:** confirm the six locations, any recent openings or remodels (new kitchens mean new equipment and warranties), and any expansion news.
- **Job postings:** a "maintenance tech" or "facilities" listing suggests in-house coverage, and that changes the pitch.
- **Your own numbers:** have your plan pricing, response-time SLA, and coverage area ready, plus one or two anonymized examples of a walk-in failure you fixed and what it saved the customer.

## Goal for the call

Discovery, not a pitch. Leave with (1) a clear picture of how they handle failures today, (2) who decides and what the budget process looks like, and (3) a specific next step, such as a walk-through of one location or a list of their equipment.

## Questions to ask

**Current setup**
- Who services your refrigeration today? In-house, a contractor, or whoever answers the phone?
- Walk me through the last time a walk-in or prep cooler failed. What happened, how long was it down, what did it cost?
- Do you have service contracts now? What do they cover, and what's left out?
- How old is the equipment, roughly, across the locations?

**Pain and priorities**
- When something breaks at 9pm on a Friday, who gets the call and how long until someone shows up?
- Have you lost product or had a health inspection issue tied to temperatures?
- How do you track maintenance history across six sites today?
- What would make you say a vendor was worth it a year from now?

**Decision process**
- Who else would be involved in a decision like this? Does anyone above you sign off?
- Is there a budget cycle, or does this come out of operating costs?
- Have you bought maintenance services like this before? What did you like or dislike?

## How to frame your pitch

Keep it to two sentences, tied to what she told you. For example: "Sounds like the risk is a cooler going down on a weekend with no one to call. We prepay for guaranteed response and scheduled checkups, so you're not negotiating a rush rate at the worst moment." Save price for after you understand their situation.

## Likely objections

- **"We already have someone."** Ask: "How fast can they get out there on a Sunday night? Is that in writing?"
- **"It's expensive."** Compare it to the cost of one failure: product loss, a closed prep line, an emergency rate.
- **"What if we don't use it?"** Explain what the prepaid plan includes (scheduled visits, usage reporting, unused balance terms if you have them).
- **"We have six locations, that's complicated."** Position it as one account and one response standard across all six, with one invoice.

## Closing

Don't end with "let me know." Propose something specific:
- "Could we do a 30-minute walk-through at your highest-volume location next week?"
- "Could you send me a list of equipment by location, plus your last year of repair invoices? I'll put together a quote from that."
