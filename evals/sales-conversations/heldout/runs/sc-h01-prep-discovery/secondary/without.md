Here's a prep plan built around the call's real job: learn how her locations run and what they've been burned by, and don't pitch much.

## Goal for the 30 minutes
- Leave with a clear picture of her current setup (who fixes equipment now, how that goes, what it costs).
- Identify one concrete pain point you can solve.
- Agree on a next step: usually a walkthrough of one location or a sample plan quote for her footprint.

## Before the call (15 min)
- **Her background:** LinkedIn history, how long she's been in ops, whether she came from a larger chain or a single-unit operator. This tells you how much process she expects.
- **The chain:** Check Google/Yelp reviews for all 6 locations. Look for complaints about warm food, slow service, or "walk-in was off," which signal cold-storage pain. Local health inspection reports (often public online) show repeat temperature or cold-holding violations.
- **Her "sure, 30 min" reply:** Short and open. She agreed to talk, not to buy. Treat her as a peer who's evaluating, not a prospect you're closing.
- **Your own homework:** Know what your plans cover (labor, parts, after-hours, response time SLA, what happens to unused prepaid balance). She will ask, and vague answers cost trust.

## Discovery questions
**Current state**
1. How are cooler and refrigeration issues handled today? Who do you call, and how fast do they show up?
2. Walk me through the last time something failed. What did it cost, in food, sales, or stress?
3. Do you have a maintenance vendor under contract now, or is it call-when-broken?
4. Are the six locations on the same equipment, or does each one have its own mix and age?

**Pain and priorities**
5. What keeps you up at night about refrigeration?
6. Has a health inspection or a food-safety scare ever involved cold holding?
7. Where do you lose the most money: emergency repair premiums, lost product, or downtime?

**Decision process**
8. Who else weighs in on a vendor like this? Owners, a finance person, the location managers?
9. How do you evaluate vendors? What made you take this call?
10. If this worked, what would make it a clear win in six months?

Ask the questions, then listen. Let her talk for most of the 30 minutes.

## Likely objections and how to handle them
- **"We already have a repair guy."** Ask how often he's actually been fast when it mattered, and whether he tracks PM history across all six sites. Don't attack the incumbent.
- **"Prepaid feels like a lock-in."** Be upfront about terms, cancellation, and what happens to unused prepay. Trust matters more here than a clever close.
- **"Our units are old, so the plan won't pay off."** Ask which units are most failure-prone. A prepay plan with a PM schedule may be the right call for them, or the wrong one, and an honest answer helps you more.
- **"Send me something."** Ask what she'd want to see first, then ask for a 15-minute walk of one location.

## What not to do
- Don't open with a deck or a price sheet.
- Don't promise response times or savings you haven't confirmed for her footprint.
- Don't badmouth other vendors.

## Close
Summarize what you heard in two or three sentences, then propose one specific next step with a date: "Can I walk one of your locations next week, and then send a plan for all six?" Follow up within 24 hours with a short email that repeats her pain points in her words and names the next step.
