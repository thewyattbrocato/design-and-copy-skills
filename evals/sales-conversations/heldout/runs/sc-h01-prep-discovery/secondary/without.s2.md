Here's a prep plan for tomorrow. I can't browse, so I haven't looked up the chain. Section 1 lists what to check yourself before the call.

## 1. Homework (20 min)
- **Locations:** Look up all six on Google Maps. Note hours and reviews that mention coolers, walk-ins, or "closed due to equipment."
- **Health inspections:** Many counties publish inspection reports online. Search for cold-holding, refrigeration, or temperature violations at each location. This is the most useful pain signal you can find, and you can reference it carefully ("I noticed a few cold-holding notes; is that something you're tracking?").
- **Her background:** Tenure, prior roles (did she come from a restaurant group with a maintenance program?), and any recent expansion or openings.
- **Your own numbers:** Know your response-time commitment, what the plan covers (parts, labor, after-hours), and the price range. Don't lead with price.

## 2. Goal for the call
Leave with:
1. A picture of how they handle breakdowns today (vendor, cost, response time).
2. The equipment count and age across the six sites.
3. Who else is involved in the decision.
4. A specific next step, such as a site walkthrough or a sample quote for one location.

You're there to learn, not pitch. If you're talking for more than about half the call, you're probably pitching too early.

## 3. Suggested agenda (30 min)
- **0–3 min:** Thank her. Restate what you do in one sentence and ask what she'd most like to get out of the call.
- **3–18 min:** Discovery (questions below).
- **18–25 min:** Reflect back what you heard. Only then describe how your plan addresses it, briefly.
- **25–30 min:** Agree on a next step, an owner, and a date.

## 4. Discovery questions
**Current state**
- "Walk me through the last time a walk-in or prep cooler went down. What happened, start to finish?"
- "Who do you call today, and how do you pay them: per call, retainer, or a mix?"
- "What's the response time you actually get, and what's the worst one you remember?"
- "How much product do you typically lose when a walk-in fails? Has anyone tracked it?"

**Equipment and sites**
- "Roughly how many walk-ins and prep units across the six sites? Do they vary in age or brand?"
- "Are any of them already showing problems you're watching?"
- "Does each location manage its own maintenance, or is it centralized?"

**Pain and priorities**
- "If you could fix one thing about refrigeration maintenance, what would it be?"
- "Has a health inspector ever cited cold holding or refrigeration? What changed afterward?"
- "How do you budget for repairs? Is it predictable or lumpy?"

**Decision**
- "Who else would weigh in on something like this? Finance? Corporate?"
- "Have you looked at maintenance programs before? What did you like or dislike?"
- "What would make this a clear yes for you?"

## 5. Value framing (use only once she gives you numbers)
Do the math with her, not for her. A simple version:

> (Inventory at risk per walk-in failure) + (emergency call premium) + (lost service or 86'd items) = cost of one breakdown.
> Compare that to the annual prepaid plan.

Ramen-specific angles to listen for:
- Stock and broth batches that can't be held through a failure.
- Chashu, eggs, and sauces in prep units during dinner rush.
- Weekend and late-night failures, when emergency rates are highest.

## 6. Likely objections
- **"We already have a repair company."** Ask how they're doing. Don't argue. Position the plan as scheduled checkups plus priority response, which may complement their current vendor.
- **"We don't want to prepay."** Ask about their preferred structure. Be ready to explain what happens to unused balance and whether there's a monthly option.
- **"What if you go under?"** Have a clear answer ready on continuity, insurance, and contract terms.
- **"Our units are old."** Offer a baseline assessment first. That's a reasonable next step either way.
- **"Send me something."** Ask what specifically they'd want to see, then offer a one-location sample rather than a generic deck.

## 7. Close and follow-up
- Propose a concrete next step: "Could we do a 45-minute walkthrough at one location next week?"
- Confirm who's involved and what they need to decide.
- Send a recap within 24 hours: what you heard, the agreed next step, and the date. Keep it to five lines.
