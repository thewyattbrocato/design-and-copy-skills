# Call prep: 30-min intro with an ops director, 6-location ramen chain

**Your situation:** She said "sure, 30 min" to a cold LinkedIn message, so she's curious but hasn't signed up for a pitch. Treat this as a discovery call. Your goal is to learn whether refrigeration downtime hurts them and to earn a second conversation. Don't try to close.

## Why she should care (hypotheses to test)
- **Spoilage and lost service.** Ramen depends on broth, pork, eggs, noodles, and fresh toppings, and a dead walk-in on a Friday afternoon is expensive. Prep kitchen refrigeration matters even more if she runs a commissary model.
- **Six locations means scattered vendors.** She likely has ad hoc repair relationships, with different techs, prices, and response times per site.
- **Reactive maintenance.** Most restaurants only call when something breaks, so scheduled checkups are your wedge.
- **Budget predictability.** Prepaid plans turn surprise repair bills into a known cost.

These are guesses. Her answers should confirm or kill each one.

## Suggested flow (30 min)
1. **Open (2 min).** Thank her, then set the frame: "I'd like to learn how you handle refrigeration today and see if there's a fit. If not, no hard feelings. Does that work?" Ask early how she wants to use the time.
2. **Discovery (15–18 min).** This is where most of the call should go (questions below).
3. **Brief relevant pitch (5 min).** Only describe the parts of your plan that match what she told you.
4. **Next step (3–5 min).** Propose something small, like a site audit of one location, a quote for all six, or a call with whoever handles facilities.

## Discovery questions
- "Walk me through the last time a cooler or reach-in failed. What happened, and what did it cost?"
- "Who do you call when something dies? How fast do they show up, and how does it vary by location?"
- "Is anyone doing preventive maintenance today, or is it mostly fix-when-broken?"
- "How old is the equipment? Any locations that worry you?"
- "Do you have a central prep kitchen, or does each location prep on its own?"
- "Who decides on maintenance vendors, you or the GMs? Is there a budget line for it?"
- "How do you handle repair costs today? Per-incident invoices, a service contract, or something else?"
- "What would a good refrigeration vendor do that your current setup doesn't?"

Follow up on any pain she mentions with "what did that cost you?" Her own numbers will persuade her far better than yours.

## Handling likely objections
- **"We already have a guy."** Reply with "Great, how's he do on after-hours response? Does he cover all six locations?" Don't attack the incumbent. Position yourself as backup or a consolidation option.
- **"Prepaid sounds like a lot upfront."** Be ready with the math: plan cost versus one emergency call-out plus lost inventory. Know your payment options (monthly billing, per-location, etc.) before the call.
- **"Send me info."** Say "Happy to. What would you want it to answer so it's actually useful?" Then book a follow-up before you hang up.

## Before Thursday
- Skim her LinkedIn: tenure, background, anything she's posted. Look for a natural opener, not a flattery line.
- Look up the chain: locations, whether they're in one metro area, any recent openings or expansions (growth means new equipment and stretched ops), and Google reviews mentioning food safety or closures.
- Know your numbers: response-time guarantee, coverage area, what's included in a plan, pricing range, and what you *don't* cover. Avoid hedging when she asks.
- Have one short customer example ready, ideally another multi-location restaurant group.
- Decide your "ask" for the end of the call so you don't improvise it.

## Things to avoid
- Don't open with a feature dump. She doesn't know you yet.
- Don't assume she owns the budget decision. Ask.
- Don't leave without a dated next step.
