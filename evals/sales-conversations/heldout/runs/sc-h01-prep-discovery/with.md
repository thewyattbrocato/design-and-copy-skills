## Questions, in order
Each one is tagged with the number it should give you.

1. **"Walk me through the last time a walk-in or prep cooler went down. What happened, and how fast did anyone get there?"**
   Yields: hours to response, and the date of the last event.
   *If she says "it's been fine":* ask how many repairs or service calls she had across all 6 locations in the past year `[to learn]`. If the answer is near zero, the problem may be small. See "Fit" below.

2. **"What was in it, and what did that failure cost you?"**
   Yields: dollars of product lost, lost service days, and any emergency repair bill.
   *If she can't say:* "Could someone pull the last year's repair invoices?" That becomes the next step. Don't suggest a number for her to agree with.
   *If the cost isn't in dollars* (a health inspection, a closed location), record it in her words and don't price it.

3. **"Who services the equipment now? A vendor, the landlord, each GM calling someone ad hoc?"**
   Yields: number of vendors, who owns the relationship, and what she spends per year on repairs and checkups.

4. **"What happens on a weekend or a Friday night when something dies?"**
   Yields: after-hours response time now, and who gets the call (GM, her, a vendor).

5. **"How old is the equipment, and is anyone doing scheduled checkups?"**
   Yields: units per location, age, and the checkup frequency now (if any).

6. **"What would a good outcome look like, and why that bar?"**
   Yields: her target (for example, response within X hours or no lost product), and her motive. The "why" often shows who else cares, such as an owner or a health-inspection concern.

7. **"Who else weighs in on a spend like this, and how did you buy a service contract last time?"**
   Yields: signer, route to purchase, and timing. Also ask whether this is budgeted today. If she says "no budget," ask whether she means no money or no priority.

## Gate check (before you offer anything)
Don't pitch a plan, send a proposal, or quote prices until all three are on the table:
- She feels a problem and can size it.
- You know who signs, or the route to a signature.
- She'll commit something: invoice history, a second stakeholder, or a site walkthrough.

If one is missing, the next step is to get it. If she asks about price before then, give a range and what moves it (locations, units, response tier) `[fill in your real pricing]`, then stop talking. Don't discount in response to a first reaction.

## Fit
- **Small problem:** if she has had few failures and a reliable vendor, say so. Offer something lighter, such as a single-site trial of scheduled checkups. If it's still a weak fit, step out kindly and ask whether she knows a chain with an aging fleet.
- **Cause outside your plan:** if the failures come from something a plan can't fix (undersized units, a landlord-owned system), say that plainly.

## Closing (last 3 minutes)
Summarize what you heard in her words and numbers. Then ask for the dated step from the top. Confirm it in writing today. If she says "send me info" or "I'll think about it," ask what specifically she'd want to see. Then pin a date for the follow-up conversation, not "I'll circle back."

If she says no, confirm it, note her reason in her words, and stop.
