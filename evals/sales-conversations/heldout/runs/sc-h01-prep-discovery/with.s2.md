## Questions, in order

1. **"Walk me through the last time a walk-in or prep cooler went down. What happened, and who did you call?"**
   - Yields: the most recent event, response time (hours), and who the vendor is.
   - If vague ("it happens"): "About how many times in the last 12 months, across all six?" (count)

2. **"What did that cost: product thrown out, a day of closed prep, the repair bill?"**
   - Yields: dollars, lost service days.
   - If she has no number: `[to learn]`. Don't suggest one. The next step becomes "could someone pull the last year's refrigeration repair invoices?"
   - If it's not about money (a health inspection, say), record her words and note it isn't priced.

3. **"How is repair handled now: an in-house person, a per-call vendor, or a mix? What do you spend on it in a year?"**
   - Yields: annual spend ($), number of vendors, and whether money is already going to this problem.

4. **"How old is the equipment, and does anyone check it on a schedule?"**
   - Yields: unit count, age, and whether checkups exist today.

5. **"If a cooler died on a Saturday night, what would you want to happen? Why is that your bar?"**
   - Yields: target response time (hours) and the motive. A bar like "four hours" may come from a promise to someone, which tells you who else cares.

6. **"Has anything been tried or changed since that failure?"**
   - Yields: whether she's actively fixing this, or whether it's low priority.

7. **"Who else would weigh in on a service contract like this, and how was your last one decided?"**
   - Yields: who signs, who objects, and the route to purchase.
