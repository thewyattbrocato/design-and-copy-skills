# Call prep: ops director, 6-location ramen chain

## Goal for a 30-minute first call
You have no relationship and she only said "sure," so don't pitch. Aim to find out whether refrigeration downtime is a real problem for her, and to earn a second conversation (a site list, a quote, or a pilot at one location). If she talks more than half the time, the call is going well.

## Before Thursday (20 minutes)
- **Her background:** Check her LinkedIn for tenure and previous companies. Someone who came up through multi-unit operations will care about uptime and consistency. Someone from finance or franchising will care more about cost predictability.
- **The chain:** Look up the locations (are they all in one metro or spread out?), whether there's a central prep kitchen, and whether it's growing. Read recent Google and Yelp reviews for anything about food safety, closures, or "out of X today."
- **Your coverage:** Know whether you can service all six addresses. If you can't, that's the first thing she'll ask about.
- **Your proof points:** Have one or two short stories ready, such as "a walk-in died Friday at 4pm, we were there in X hours, and they saved $Y of inventory." Specific numbers beat claims.

## Why this should matter to her (hypotheses to test, not assert)
- **Cooler failure is expensive and badly timed.** One walk-in loss means thousands in product plus lost service. Ramen depends on pork, eggs, noodles, and broth components, so there's real inventory at stake.
- **A central prep kitchen is a single point of failure** feeding all six locations.
- **She may be handling repairs ad hoc.** That means calling whoever is available, with inconsistent response times and surprise invoices, and each GM may be doing it differently.
- **Health inspection and food safety risk** if temps drift between failures.
- **Budget predictability.** A prepaid plan converts surprise repair bills into a fixed line item.

## Suggested call structure (30 min)
1. **Open (2 min):** Thank her, then set the agenda: "I'd like to learn how you handle refrigeration today, share briefly what we do, and see if it's worth a next step. Does that work?" Ask if she has a hard stop.
2. **Discovery (15–18 min):** The questions below.
3. **Short pitch (5 min):** Only the parts that match what she told you.
4. **Next step (3–5 min):** Propose something specific.

## Discovery questions
**Current state**
- How do you handle it today when a cooler or freezer goes down? Who gets called?
- Do you have a preferred vendor, or does each location handle it?
- Is anyone doing scheduled maintenance, or is it all reactive?

**Pain and history**
- Have you had a refrigeration failure in the last year or two? What happened, and what did it cost you in product and service?
- How long did it take to get someone out? Was that acceptable?
- How old is the equipment? Do you have a prep kitchen, and what runs through it?

**Decision-making**
- Who decides on this, you or the owners/finance? Is this something you'd handle at the group level, or do GMs decide?
- How do you budget for repairs and maintenance now?
- If you were to change how this is handled, what would need to be true?

## Your pitch (keep to ~2 minutes)
Tie it to what she said. Cover three things:
1. Fast response when something fails (give your actual guarantee or typical time).
2. Scheduled checkups that catch problems before they become failures.
3. Prepaid means a predictable cost across all six locations, with one vendor and one number to call.

## Likely objections
| Objection | Response direction |
|---|---|
| "We already have a repair company." | "Great. How's their response time been when it's urgent? We'd be a backup or a pilot at one location, not a replacement." |
| "Prepaid sounds like I'm paying for something I might not use." | Walk through the math of one emergency call plus lost inventory against the plan cost. Ask what covers the plan (parts? labor? after-hours?), and be ready to explain it clearly. |
| "I'm not the decision maker." | "Who else would weigh in? Would it help if I put together a one-pager you could share?" |
| "Send me some info." | "Happy to. So I send the right thing, what matters most: response time, cost, or coverage?" That keeps the conversation going. |
| "Bad timing/budget." | Ask when she plans the next budget cycle, and offer a no-cost equipment assessment at one location. |

## Close
Propose one concrete step, such as a free walkthrough of one location and the prep kitchen, or a quote for all six based on equipment counts. Get a date on the calendar before you hang up.

## Things to avoid
- Don't open with a long company intro.
- Don't quote a price before you know her equipment and sites.
- Don't assume her pain. Ask first.
- Don't promise response times you can't consistently hit.
