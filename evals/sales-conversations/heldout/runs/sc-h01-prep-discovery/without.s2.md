# Call prep: ops director at a 6-location ramen chain

## Goal for this call
Discovery, not a pitch. She said "sure, 30 min" to a cold LinkedIn message, so she's curious but not sold. A good outcome is one of these:
- You learn how they handle refrigeration failures today.
- She agrees to a concrete next step, such as a site walkthrough, a quote on one location, or a short follow-up with a colleague.

Aim to talk about 30% of the time.

## Before Thursday (15 minutes)
- **Locations:** Map the 6 sites. Are they in one metro or spread out? Response-time promises only matter if you cover them.
- **Structure:** Check whether there's a central prep kitchen or commissary. If so, that's the highest-stakes asset, since one failure hits all 6 stores.
- **Her background:** Skim her LinkedIn for tenure, previous chains, and recent posts. Open with one genuine, specific point from it.
- **Company news:** Look for new openings, expansion plans, or health-inspection issues. Growth makes this pitch much stronger.

## Suggested flow
1. **Open (2 min):** Thank her, then confirm the time. Say something like: "I'd love to understand how you handle refrigeration today. If it sounds like a fit, we can talk about what we do. If not, no hard feelings."
2. **Discovery (15–18 min):** Questions below.
3. **Brief positioning (5 min):** Tie what you offer directly to what she told you. Skip the generic overview.
4. **Next step (3–5 min):** Propose something specific.

## Discovery questions
**Current state**
- When a walk-in or reach-in goes down, what happens? Who gets the call, and who do they call?
- Do you use one vendor, several, or whoever answers? Is it handled per store or centrally?
- Is any of it covered by landlords or warranties?

**Pain**
- When did you last lose a cooler or have a failure? What did it cost in product, lost service, and scrambling?
- How long did it take to get someone out, especially nights and weekends?
- Does anything ever get skipped, like condenser cleaning, door gaskets, or temperature checks, until it breaks?

**Stakes (ramen-specific)**
- Stocks, tare, pork, fresh noodles, and eggs are expensive and temperature-sensitive. Ask what a lost walk-in's worth of inventory would mean for them.
- Ask how they handle temperature logging and health inspections.

**Buying**
- Does maintenance sit in her budget or at the GM level?
- How do they feel about prepaying versus paying per call? Who would sign off on a plan?
- Is she trying to cut vendor headaches, cost, or both?

## Likely objections
- **"We already have a guy."** Ask how fast he responds at 10pm on a Saturday, and what happens if he's unavailable. You don't need to replace him. You can be the backup or cover the stores he doesn't.
- **"Prepaid sounds like a commitment."** Be ready to explain what the plan covers, what happens if they're unhappy, and how pricing compares to one emergency call plus lost inventory.
- **"Send me info."** Reply with "Happy to. What would you want it to answer?" Then try to book a follow-up.
- **"Not a priority."** Ask what is, and whether refrigeration has caused problems before.

## Close
Propose a small, concrete step: "Could we do a walkthrough or quote for your highest-risk location, probably the prep kitchen, and then compare it to what you're doing now?" Put a date on it before you hang up.

## Things to avoid
- Don't open with a features list or a scripted pitch.
- Don't claim you know their problems. Frame them as hypotheses and ask.
- Don't push for a close on a first call with someone who hasn't met you.

- Any case study or number, like average cost of a failure or average response time, that you can cite.
