**Don't lead with 40% off.** Even at 40% off, she'd pay about $288/month for a product built for brands with 10+ retail accounts, and she has 2. She's telling you the price is hard, but the bigger issue is that she's paying for capacity she won't use. A deep discount also sets up a bad renewal: at full price in 12 months, she'll either churn or feel misled. If the discount closes the deal and she churns later, you've lost the revenue and the reference.

**What I'd do:**

1. **Check whether there's a fit.** Before pricing, find out what she needs ordering to do. For 2 wholesale accounts and about 40 orders a month, she may need an inventory count and an order sheet, not a wholesale platform. If a smaller tier or plan exists, offer that. I don't know your pricing, so check what's available.
2. **If a right-sized plan exists, offer that at full price.** It's a better close than a discount on the wrong product.
3. **If there's no right-sized plan and she's not a fit, say so.** Closing her on a discount you can't sustain is worse for your numbers over time than losing the deal now.
4. **If your manager insists on a discount, make it structured:** an annual prepay discount (lower monthly cost, commitment on your side), or a time-boxed ramp (e.g., 50% off for 3 months, then full price, with a check-in). Get the terms in writing and flag the fit concern to your manager.

**What I'd say to her:**

> "I'm glad it's been a good fit to talk through. Before we talk price, I want to be straight with you: this platform is built for brands with 10+ wholesale accounts, and you have 2. You'd be paying for a lot you wouldn't use. Can you walk me through how you handle orders and inventory right now, and which parts take the most time? That'll tell us whether this is the right tool, or whether there's something smaller that fits. If it is, I'd rather find you the right plan than a discount on one that's too big."

Then listen. If the answer is "I'd use it if it were cheaper," that's a sign the product doesn't match her needs. Ask what she'd use day to day and whether she'd still want it at $100/month. If the honest answer is no, it's better to tell her it's not a fit than to close her.

Also tell your manager directly: "This is a fit problem, not a price problem, and a 40% discount just pushes the renewal issue out." That's a more useful conversation than discussing how much to discount.
