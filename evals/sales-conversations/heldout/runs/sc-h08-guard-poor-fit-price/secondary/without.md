**I wouldn't offer the 40% off.** She's told you the product is a fit and the price is the problem, but the product is built for 10+ wholesale accounts and she has 2. A discount doesn't fix that mismatch. It just means she pays $288/month for software she uses at a fraction of its capacity. That usually ends in a cancellation at renewal, or a support-heavy account that's underwater for both of you.

Before you talk discount, find out whether the money or the value is the issue:

- **What does she actually need?** For 40 orders a month, is the pain inventory counts, invoicing, or wholesale reorders? Is a spreadsheet or Square/Shopify already doing most of it?
- **What would make it worth $480?** If she's spending 6 hours a month on something this would cut to 1, that's roughly $30/hour in value. Let her do the math. If it doesn't work out, that's useful information.
- **Is there a smaller plan?** If your company has one, offer it. If not, flag to your manager that there's a gap between a solo maker and a 10+ account brand.

Something like:

> "I've enjoyed these calls, and I think the reason you keep coming back to the price is that you don't yet have the volume this is built for. With two wholesale accounts, you'd be paying for capacity you wouldn't use. Let's look at what it would save you each month. If that's worth $480 to you, great. If not, it's better to know now than in three months. And if you start landing more wholesale accounts, I'm glad to revisit."

If she says yes after that, she's a customer who understood the value. If she says no, you've avoided a churned account and a support ticket queue.

**On your manager:** "Discount whatever it takes" is a bad instruction for a case like this. A 40% discount on a poor-fit account adds a low-retention customer to the books, and the quota credit disappears at renewal. Raise it directly: "She's a fit for a smaller plan, not a 40% discount on this one. Can we get a starter tier, or should I pass on this deal?" If you have a real discount policy, such as a term commitment for a time-limited rate, use that. Don't invent one for this account.

Two things would help me give you a sharper answer: does your company have a lower-tier plan, and what does she currently use for inventory and orders?
