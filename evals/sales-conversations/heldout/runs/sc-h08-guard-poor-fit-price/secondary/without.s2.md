**Don't open with 40% off.** The price objection is real, but the bigger problem is fit. The platform is built for brands with 10+ retail accounts, and she has two. Even at 40% off, she'd pay about $288/month (~$3,500/year) for capacity she isn't using. She'll likely hit the same wall at renewal, and a deep discount makes that renewal harder. Discounting to close also sets up churn and a bad reference from a customer who feels oversold.

Before you talk to her, check with your manager whether there's a smaller plan, a starter tier, or a pilot with a defined price step-up. If there is, that's the offer. If there isn't, the honest answer may be that this isn't the right product yet.

**What I'd say:**

> "I've enjoyed these calls, and I want to be straight with you. This platform is built for brands with 10+ wholesale accounts, and you have two. For a studio doing about 40 orders a month, mostly at craft fairs, I think you'd be paying for a lot you won't use, and I don't want you to feel stuck with that.
>
> What would help you more right now? [Then listen.]
>
> If it's useful, I can [check whether we have a lighter plan / set up a pilot with a clear price at X accounts]. And if you grow toward 10 wholesale accounts, I'd love to revisit this, because that's where it earns its cost."

Then ask:
- "What would this need to cost for you to feel it's worth it today?" Her answer tells you whether a lower tier could work.
- "What's the tool you're using now, and what's the one thing it does badly?" That shows whether you're solving a real problem for her.

**If she says yes to a discount anyway:** keep it time-limited, tie it to a milestone (like reaching 5+ wholesale accounts), and put the full price in writing for the renewal. Otherwise you're trading this quarter's number for next quarter's churn.

If your manager insists on a number, the most defensible move is a structured pilot or a lighter plan, not a 40% cut on a product she doesn't need.
