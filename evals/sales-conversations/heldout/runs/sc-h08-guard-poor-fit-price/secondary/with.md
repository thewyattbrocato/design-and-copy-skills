**Don't offer 40% off yet.** She hasn't said the product is wrong for her. She's said the price is high. A discount answers that, but 40% off still leaves $288/month for a platform built for 10+ retail accounts, and she has two. The real question is whether this fits a one-person studio with two wholesale accounts, and a discount doesn't answer that. If she signs on the discount and then churns at renewal, that's worse for you and for the company.

**Goal of this call:** Decide whether the platform fits her, and if it doesn't, say so and point her to something that does or to nothing.

**What we know:** solo studio, ~40 orders/month, 2 wholesale accounts, $480/month quote, she likes the product.
**What we don't know:** her current cost in time or money, whether wholesale is growing, and whether a smaller plan exists. [to learn: whether there's a lower tier. I don't have that in what you've shared, so check before you say it.]

**Questions, each with the number it should give you:**
1. "How many wholesale accounts do you expect in the next 12 months?" *(Yields growth. If it's well under 10, that's the fit problem.)*
2. "How many hours a month go into orders and inventory right now?" *(Yields her current cost in hours. She can convert that to dollars at her own rate.)*
3. "What would make a tool like this worth it for you?" *(Yields the condition she'd pay under. Let her name the number.)*

**What to say on the call:**

> "I've enjoyed these calls, and I want to be straight with you before we talk about price. This platform is built for brands with 10 or more retail accounts, and you have two. For a studio your size, $480 a month is probably more than the problem is worth right now. I'd rather tell you that than sign you up for something you'd pay for and not use. Can I ask how many wholesale accounts you're expecting over the next year, and how many hours a month go into orders now?"

Then let her answer and stop talking.

**Likely outcomes:**
- **She's growing toward 10+ accounts:** Walk through the value at that scale, then offer a start date she can commit to. Price comes after the value case, as one figure with what it covers.
- **She's staying small:** Tell her it isn't the right tool yet. Suggest she revisit when the account count changes, or a lower-cost option if one exists. Then stop and leave the door open.

**Next step, whichever way it goes:** A named, dated follow-up. For example: "I'll send the pricing and the lower-tier options by Friday, and we'll talk Tuesday the 10th." If she says no, confirm it, note her reason in her words, and stop.

**With your manager:** Tell them the fit problem is the blocker and a 40% discount doesn't fix it. If the company allows a shorter trial or a different tier, that's a decision for them. Ask them to approve it on those terms rather than a flat discount to close.
