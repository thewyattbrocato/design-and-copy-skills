**Don't lead with 40% off.** At $288/month she'd be paying for a product built for 10+ retail accounts when she has 2. A discount doesn't fix that mismatch. It delays it until renewal, when she'll see she's paying for features she doesn't use and churn. A closed deal that churns in three months won't help your quota or your manager's numbers.

Before you talk price again, find out what the $480 is competing against. Ask:

- "When you think about the cost, what are you comparing it to? Spreadsheets, a bookkeeper, your time?"
- "How many hours a month do orders and inventory take you right now?"
- "Which parts of the platform would you actually use with two wholesale accounts?"

If the hours she names are worth more than $480, you have a real value case. If they aren't, the honest answer may be that this isn't the right product yet.

**What I'd say:**

> "I've really enjoyed these calls, and I want to be straight with you. This platform is built for brands with 10 or more wholesale accounts. With two accounts and about 40 orders a month, you may be paying for capacity you won't use for a while. Before we talk about price, can you walk me through how you handle orders and inventory today, and roughly how many hours that takes each month? Then we can see whether this actually saves you money or whether it's too early for you."

If she says it's too early, that's a fine outcome. Tell her when it would make sense (for example, when wholesale accounts reach 8 to 10) and ask whether you can check in then.

**If she's a fit and needs a number to move:** you can offer a time-limited intro price, but only if it's tied to her usage and has a stated end date. For example, a discount for the first 3 months that steps up to full price, with a clear note of what it will cost after that. Don't offer a deep discount open-ended. Also check whether a smaller plan exists before you discount the full product.

**With your manager:** tell them she's a poor fit at this price, and that a 40% discount would probably produce a churn in six months instead of a closed deal. Ask whether there's an approved lower tier or pilot program you can offer instead. If the only option is discounting a product she doesn't need, that's a decision for them to make with that risk in view, not something to offer her on your own.
