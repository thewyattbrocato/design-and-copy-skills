**Goal of the next call:** qualify fit. Find out whether her problem is big enough to justify $480/month, and say so plainly if it isn't.

**Short answer: don't offer 40% off yet.** She hasn't said the product fails her. She's said the price is high for her right now, and a discount answers that without checking whether she needs the product. The price isn't the main issue. The platform is built for 10+ retail accounts and she has 2. A 40% discount would cut the bill to about $288/month for a tool she may not use. If she renews at full price later, she'll likely churn. If she expects the discount to continue, you'll have a renewal problem.

**Facts**
- Known: one-person studio, ~40 orders/month, 2 wholesale accounts, has said she loves the product, objects to price on all 3 calls.
- Unknown: hours per month on inventory and wholesale orders today, what errors cost her, whether she plans to reach 10+ accounts, and whether she has budget.
- Assumed: most of her 40 orders come from craft fairs, which this platform may not cover. Check this before the call.

**What to say on the next call**

> "Before we talk price, I want to be straight with you about fit. This platform is built for brands running 10-plus wholesale accounts, and you have two. I'd rather work out whether it's worth it for you now than sell you a discount on something you'd stop using. How many hours a month do you spend on inventory and wholesale orders today? And when a count is off, what does that cost you?"

**Then branch on her answer:**
- **Small numbers (a few hours a month, no real errors):** Tell her it's not the right fit yet. Name what she should use instead, and say when the platform would make sense (for example, at 10 accounts). Keep the door open. Don't discount.
- **Real numbers (say, 10+ hours a month plus costly errors):** Build the value case in her numbers: "You said X hours at about $Y an hour. If this saves that, does $480 make sense?" Quote the price once, with what it covers, then stop talking. If she still says it's too much, ask what would have to be true for it to work. Don't offer a discount in response to her first price reaction.

**Next step (say it aloud, put it on both calendars):** "I'll send you a one-page summary of what we covered by [date]. Let's meet for 15 minutes on [date] to decide whether it's worth it now." You own both.

**For your manager:** A 40% discount on a deal where the product doesn't fit is a weak close. It likely turns into churn at renewal. Ask whether a smaller plan exists for two-account businesses. If it does, offer that at list price. If your manager still wants a discount, that's their call, but make the value case first.
