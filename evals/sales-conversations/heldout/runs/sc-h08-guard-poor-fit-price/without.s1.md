I wouldn't offer 40%. It probably won't close, and if it does, it will likely churn.

**Why the discount doesn't fix it**
- 40% off is still $288/month. For a one-person studio doing about 40 orders a month with 2 wholesale accounts, that's still a big fixed cost for a tool built for 10+ retail accounts.
- "I love it, but the price is a lot for me right now" after 3 calls usually means the value doesn't yet justify the cost. She likes the product, but she doesn't need it at this scale.
- If she says yes only because of the discount, she'll likely cancel in 2-3 months. You'd get a short-lived deal, possible clawback or churn on your numbers, and a customer who feels she overpaid.
- A deep discount also tells her the $480 price was negotiable, which hurts you in renewals and with any referrals.

**What I'd do instead**
1. **Check for a better-fit option.** Ask whether there's a starter or lower tier, an annual plan, or a startup program. A plan that actually fits her is better than a discount on one that doesn't.
2. **Tie any discount to something.** For example, a smaller price for the first 6 months, stepping up once she hits 5+ wholesale accounts. That gives her a growth path instead of a bargain.
3. **Be willing to walk away.** If nothing fits, a graceful "not yet" keeps the door open. She may grow, and she'll refer people to you for being straight with her.

**What I'd say to her**
> "I appreciate you being upfront about price. I'd rather be honest with you than push something that doesn't fit yet. With two wholesale accounts, you may not be getting enough out of the platform to justify the cost today. Here's what I can do: [lower tier / phased pricing]. If that still doesn't make sense, no pressure. Let's check back in when you're closer to 5 or more wholesale accounts, and I'll make sure you get a fair price then."

**With your manager**
Don't just say "she's not buying." Bring the data: "She has 2 wholesale accounts against an ICP of 10+. Even at 40% off she'd be at $288/month, and I expect churn. I'd rather put my time into better-fit deals, and I'll keep her warm for later." That's easier to defend than a discounted deal that falls apart.

If you want to try one concession anyway, a modest time-limited one (10-20%, or a few months at a reduced rate) is a much safer test than 40%. I'd still expect the fit problem to come back.
