**Don't offer 40%.** Price isn't the real problem. She has 2 wholesale accounts and the platform is built for 10+, so she'd pay for capacity and features she won't use. At 40% off she'd still pay $288/mo, about $3,450 a year. For a one-person studio selling at craft fairs, that's probably still "a lot right now." If she signs anyway, you get a customer likely to churn in a few months, who feels oversold and tells other makers so. That's a bad deal for you even on quota.

Goal of the next call: reach a clear yes or no on a right-sized offer, or close out kindly.

**What to say to her:**

"You've told me three times you like it and that the price is a lot right now. I believe both. I'd rather be straight with you: this plan is built for brands with 10 or more retail accounts, and you have 2. I don't want you paying for things you won't use.

Before I suggest anything, help me see where it sits for you. What's the part you'd actually use week to week: tracking inventory, taking wholesale orders, or something else? [to learn] And what would feel reasonable to spend monthly on this? [to learn]

Depending on what you say, I see three options:
1. **A smaller plan or tier.** If we have one that fits 2 accounts, I'll price that for you. [Check what exists.]
2. **Wait until you have more wholesale accounts.** If you're planning to grow wholesale, say to [X accounts] by [date], then this becomes the right tool and I'd happily pick it up then.
3. **Not a fit right now.** If neither works, I'll tell you that, and I'll point you to something lighter if I know of one."

Then stop talking and let her answer.

**If she wants it and a fair price closes the gap:** a discount is reasonable at that point, but tie it to something. For example, an annual commitment, or a rate that steps up when she reaches 10 accounts. That's a real trade, not a reflex cut.

**On your manager:** "Whatever it takes" probably doesn't mean closing a deal that churns in 90 days. Ask before you offer anything: "Is there a smaller plan or a startup rate for a 2-account customer, and what's the floor on discounting?" Get the answer in writing, so you aren't improvising 40% on your own authority.

**End the call with a dated next step.** For example: "I'll find out by Friday whether a smaller plan exists and email you the exact price. Can we talk Monday at 2 to decide yes or no?" That puts a who, a what and a when on the calendar.

Two things to fill in before you call: what plans or discount limits you actually have, and whether she plans to grow wholesale. That second answer decides whether to wait for her or let her go.
