I wouldn't offer 40%, and I'd be careful about closing her at all unless the product fits.

**Why the discount is the wrong move**
- "The price is a lot for me right now" after three calls usually means the value doesn't justify the cost at her size, not that she needs a better deal. At 40% off she'd still pay $288/month, about $3,450 a year, for a tool built for 10+ accounts when she has 2.
- With 40 orders a month and 2 wholesale accounts, she'd likely use a small fraction of the platform. A discounted customer who doesn't get value churns in 3 to 6 months, and she may leave a bad impression with other makers.
- A deep discount also sets a reference price. If she grows, or talks to other studios, you're stuck at that number.

**What I'd do instead**
1. Check whether there's a smaller plan, a startup or small-business tier, or a month-to-month option. A modest, structured offer is fine, such as a lower tier or a discount tied to an annual commitment. A blanket 40% isn't.
2. Ask your manager whether "whatever it takes" covers a plan that fits her. Closing a small, right-sized deal is better than a big discount that churns.
3. If nothing fits, let her go on good terms. A pottery studio that grows to 10 wholesale accounts is a real customer later.

**What I'd say to her**
> "You've told me a few times the price is a lot right now, and I want to take that seriously. Honestly, with two wholesale accounts, you'd be paying for a lot of capability you won't use yet. Before I suggest anything, what would you need to see to make this worth it, and is there a point, like more wholesale accounts, where it clearly would be?
>
> If there's a smaller option that fits, I'll walk you through it. If not, I'd rather tell you it's probably not the right fit yet than sell you something that feels like overhead. Either way, I'd love to check in when you're closer to [X accounts]."

This might not close her this month. But it protects your reputation and avoids a deal that churns and counts against you later. If your manager pushes back, you can say: "She has 2 accounts on a platform built for 10+. A 40% discount still leaves her overpaying, and I expect her to churn. I'd rather offer [smaller plan/annual option] or revisit when she grows."
