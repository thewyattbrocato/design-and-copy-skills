**Goal of the next call: reach a yes or no on a right-sized offer, or close out kindly if there isn't one.**

**Don't lead with 40% off.** She has told you three times that the problem is fit, not discount size.
- At 40 orders a month and 2 wholesale accounts, she's using a small part of a tool built for 10+ retail accounts.
- Even at 40% off she'd pay $288/month, about $7 per order. That's probably still "a lot for right now."
- A discount given in reply to a price reaction teaches her that the price was negotiable, and it doesn't change the fit. If she signs, she's likely to churn once the novelty fades.
- If your manager wants a close, a customer who stays is worth more than a discounted one who leaves in 90 days.

**What I'd say to her:**

> "You've told me a few times you like it but the price is a lot right now, and I'd rather be straight with you than just push a discount. This product is built for brands with ten or more wholesale accounts, and you have two. I don't want you paying for things you won't use yet. Two questions: what would have to be true for $480 a month to feel easy, and is wholesale something you're planning to grow? [to learn: her target number and her wholesale plans]"

Then stop talking and let her answer. What you do next depends on what she says:

1. **If she's planning to grow wholesale** (a date or a number of accounts), offer a smaller, reversible start. That could be a lighter plan if you have one, or a lower tier now with a clear step up when she hits a set number of accounts. Tie it to her milestone, not to your quota. Check with your manager that the structure is allowed.
2. **If she has no growth plan,** tell her this isn't the right fit yet and why. Ask if she'd like you to check back when she has about five accounts, and if she knows another wholesale owner who has outgrown spreadsheets. A kind no keeps the door open and may get you a referral.
3. **If she names a real pain you haven't heard yet,** such as lost orders, overselling at fairs, or hours spent on invoicing, get the number from her: hours per week, or what a mistake cost last time. Price only comes up after that.

**Next step to close the call:** say it aloud and send it in writing the same day, with a date. For example: "I'll send the smaller-plan details by Thursday, and we'll talk Monday at 2 to decide yes or no." If she says no, confirm it and note her reason in her words.

**Two notes:**
- Discounting is a decision for you and your manager. If you do discount, tie it to something concrete, like an annual commitment or a growth milestone.
- Tell your manager this is a poor-fit account. It's worth knowing for forecasting, and it protects you from a churned deal later.
