I'd use a table, not a list. 72 numbers is a lot for a chart to label, and owners will want to look up exact figures. A 12-row by 8-column grid fits easily on one page.

**Layout**
- **Across the top:** Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, then one or two summary columns: **Jan→Jun change** (absolute or %) and maybe **6-month average**.
- **Down the side:** the four classes as grouped blocks, with the three gyms nested inside each block. That gives 12 rows.

I'd group by class rather than by gym because the question the owners will ask is "why is spin strong at Riverside but flat at Northgate?" With class as the outer group, the three gyms for a class sit directly above and below each other. If you expect each owner to mostly care about their own gym, flip it and group by gym. I think comparison is the better default for a joint meeting.

**What to add so it's readable rather than just complete**
- **Headline above the table:** one or two sentences with the takeaway, such as "Barre is up everywhere; Old Mill boxing is down 18% since January." People will read that before the numbers.
- **Light heat shading** on the cells, or bold and color only the Change column. The eye should land on the outliers, not scan 72 numbers.
- **A subtotal row per class** (all three gyms combined), if the owners care about the business as a whole. A total row for each gym at the bottom works too, but only if "average weekly attendance" can be meaningfully summed.
- **A small sparkline** in place of or next to the Change column, if your tool supports it.

**Optional companion**
If there's room, add a small-multiples chart: four panels (one per class), each with three lines (one per gym) across Jan–Jun, with the same y-axis in every panel. It shows trends faster than the table, but I'd keep it secondary. The table is the page, and the chart supports it.
