Use one table. You have 72 exact numbers that people will look up and compare, and 12 rows by 6 columns fits on a page easily. A list would bury the comparisons. A chart would lose the exact figures the owners will ask for.

**Layout**
- **Down the side:** the 12 gym and class combinations, grouped in three blocks by gym (Northgate, Riverside, Old Mill). Use the same class order in every block (spin, yoga, boxing, barre) so the eye can run down a class across blocks. Put the gym name once as a group label, not on every row.
- **Across the top:** Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, in time order.
- **Last column:** change from Jan to Jun. Pick one form, either absolute (+6) or percent (+12%), and use it throughout. This column is where the owners will see who is growing and who is sliding, so it's worth the space.
- **Gym total row:** add one per block, bolded and under a slightly heavier rule. It's the sum of the four classes' average weekly attendance, which is a fair "total weekly visits" figure.

**Formatting**
- Put the unit in the title or caption ("Average weekly attendance per class, Jan–Jun") and leave it out of the cells.
- Show whole numbers, right-aligned, in tabular figures. Use a real minus sign for negative changes. If a month is missing, use a dash instead of a blank or a 0.
- Use thin hairlines between rows and nothing else: no cell borders, no stripes.
- Keep the text at 11–12pt or larger so it's readable when printed.
- If you want emphasis, bold or tint only the few cells you plan to talk about, such as the biggest gain and the biggest drop. Give them a "+" or "−" sign so the meaning doesn't rest on color.

**Above the table**
Add one headline sentence that says what you want the owners to notice, for example "Yoga is up at every gym; Old Mill boxing is down 18%." Without it, people have to find the story in 72 numbers themselves.

**Swapping the layout**
If Thursday's discussion is more about classes than gyms (for example "should we cut barre?"), flip the grouping. Make the four classes the blocks and the three gyms the rows within each. Everything else stays the same.

**Optional extra**
If there's room, put a small line chart under the table, one line per gym total. It shows the trend at a glance, and the table keeps the exact numbers. Don't try to chart all 12 series.
