A table isn't the best fit here. Use a **short list**, ideally grouped under a few headings.

**Why not a table?** Tables work when each item has the same attributes (rule, who it applies to, penalty, and so on). Your rules are single sentences of different kinds, so a table would have one lonely column. It also tends to squash badly on phones, and a lot of garden visitors will check the rules from their phone at the gate.

**What I'd do:** group the rules into a few headings, with one rule per bullet. The bullets read better than a paragraph and are easier to scan.

**Watering and plots**
- Water before 9am or after 6pm, never midday.
- Take only what's in your own plot, and ask before picking from the shared herb bed.

**Compost and tools**
- Compost goes in the green bin. Anything diseased goes in the black bin, never the green one.
- Tools go back on the shed hooks, cleaned, before you leave.

**Visitors**
- Children are welcome but must be with an adult.
- Dogs on a lead, and please pick up after them.

**Access**
- The gate code changes on the 1st of every month. Check the noticeboard.

A few small tips:
- If you'd rather not have headings, a plain numbered list also works, and it lets people say "rule 4".
- Bold the key phrase in each rule (**never midday**, **never the green one**) so people skimming still catch the important part.
- If the rules grow past about a dozen, headings become more valuable. At seven, they're optional.
