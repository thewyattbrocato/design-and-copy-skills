I'd skip the table. Your rules are single sentences, so a table would have nothing to put in a second column, and tables tend to render badly on phones.

**Use a short list, grouped under a few headings.** Seven unordered rules are easy to scan, but grouping makes them quicker to find:

**Watering and plots**
- Water before 9am or after 6pm, never midday.
- Take only what's in your own plot. Ask before picking from the shared herb bed.

**Compost**
- Compost goes in the green bin.
- Anything diseased goes in the black bin, never the green one.

**Tools and site**
- Tools go back on the shed hooks, cleaned, before you leave.
- The gate code changes on the 1st of every month. Check the noticeboard.

**Children and dogs**
- Children are welcome but must be with an adult.
- Dogs must be on a lead. Please pick up after them.

A few suggestions:

- **Bold the key detail** in each rule, such as *before 9am or after 6pm*, *black bin*, and *1st of every month*. People skim, and this helps them find the rule that matters to them.
- **Keep the order by relevance.** The gate code is something people check often, so you could move it to the top or give it its own "Access" line.
- **Splitting the compost rule in two** is optional, but it makes the "never the green one" warning stand out.

A table would make sense if you later add details that fit columns, like a rule plus who to contact or a rule plus the consequence. For these seven rules, a grouped list is clearer.
