I'd skip the table and use a short list, grouped under a few headings.

**Why not a table:** Tables work when items share the same attributes, like a rule, a time and a penalty. Yours are single sentences of different shapes, so a table would either have one wide column (a list with extra borders) or force you to invent a second column. Tables also tend to be awkward on phones, and many of your visitors will probably check the rules on-site.

**What I'd do instead:** group the rules under a few scannable headings, with one rule per bullet and the key words in bold.

**Watering and plots**
- Water **before 9am or after 6pm**, never midday.
- Take only what's in **your own plot**. Ask before picking from the shared herb bed.

**Compost**
- Compost goes in the **green bin**.
- Anything **diseased** goes in the **black bin**, never the green one.

**Tools and site**
- Return tools to the **shed hooks**, cleaned, before you leave.
- The **gate code changes on the 1st of every month**. Check the noticeboard.

**Visitors**
- Children are welcome but must be **with an adult**.
- Dogs on a **lead**, and please pick up after them.

**Notes:**
- I split the compost rule in two because the "never the green one" part is the one people will get wrong, and it stands out better on its own line.
- If you later add more detail, such as who to contact or what happens if rules are broken, a table with columns for rule and details could start to make sense. For these seven, the grouped list is clearer.
- If the site supports it, put the gate code and compost rules at the top, since they're the most practical day-to-day.
