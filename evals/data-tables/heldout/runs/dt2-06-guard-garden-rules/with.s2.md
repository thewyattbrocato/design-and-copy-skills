I'd skip the table. A table suits data with the same attributes across rows, like prices or opening hours. Your rules are single sentences with no shared columns, so a table would force an artificial split, and it tends to read badly on phones.

A short list works better. With seven rules, I'd group them under a few plain headings so people can find the one they need:

**Watering and harvesting**
- Water before 9am or after 6pm, never midday.
- Take only what's in your own plot, and ask before picking from the shared herb bed.

**Compost and tools**
- Compost goes in the green bin. Anything diseased goes in the black bin, never the green one.
- Tools go back on the shed hooks, cleaned, before you leave.

**Visitors**
- Children are welcome but must be with an adult.
- Dogs on a lead, and please pick up after them.

**Access**
- The gate code changes on the 1st of every month. Check the noticeboard.

A few tips:
- **Bullets or numbers:** Use bullets unless you want to refer to rules by number ("see rule 3"). Then use a numbered list.
- **Bold the key words:** Bolding "never midday" and "never the green one" makes the easy-to-miss parts stand out.
- **Gate code:** If it matters a lot, give it its own short callout at the top. It's the rule people will look for most often.

You could use a two-column table if you later add something like "who to contact" for each area. For this content, a grouped list is clearer.
