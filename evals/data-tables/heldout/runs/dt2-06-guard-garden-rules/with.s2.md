I'd skip the table. A table suits items that share the same attributes, like plan, price, and limit. Your rules are single, unrelated statements, so a table would leave you with one real column and a lot of empty chrome. It also tends to get awkward on phones.

I'd use a short bulleted list with a bold lead-in on each rule, so people can scan it. Visitors will look for the rule that applies to them, such as dogs or the gate code, and the bold words let them find it fast.

Here's how it could look:

**Garden rules**

- **Watering:** Water before 9am or after 6pm, never midday.
- **Picking:** Take only what's in your own plot. Ask before picking from the shared herb bed.
- **Compost:** Compost goes in the green bin. Anything diseased goes in the black bin, never the green one.
- **Tools:** Clean tools and put them back on the shed hooks before you leave.
- **Children:** Children are welcome but must be with an adult.
- **Dogs:** Keep dogs on a lead, and please pick up after them.
- **Gate code:** The code changes on the 1st of every month. Check the noticeboard.

A few optional tweaks:

- **Use a numbered list** only if you'll refer to rules by number, for example "see rule 3" in emails.
- **Add headings** if the list grows past about 10 rules. "In your plot" (watering, picking, compost), "Shared spaces" (tools, herb bed), and "Visitors" (children, dogs) would work.
- **Pull out the gate code** as a short callout at the top if people look for it often. It's the one item that changes.

I tightened a couple of lines, such as the tools rule, so each starts with what to do. The meaning is the same.
