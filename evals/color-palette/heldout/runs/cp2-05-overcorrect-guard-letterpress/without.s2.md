Your palette is already good, so I wouldn't change the five colors. The cream, deep ink, and rust read as letterpress, and having one accent is what keeps it from looking cheap. A few small changes would add polish.

**1. Sage fails as text (about 3:1 on paper).**
- It's fine for rules and decorative marks.
- If your tags put text in sage, use Ink text on a pale sage tint (about `#dfe5df`), or use a darker sage for the text (about `#4f6455`).

**2. Add a few supporting neutrals.** These aren't new accents. They give you depth without adding hues.
- A second paper tone for cards, banded rows, and the footer: about `#ede6d6`.
- A hairline border: Ink at 12–15% opacity, instead of gray or full-strength lines.
- A darker rust for hover and pressed states: about `#933620`.

**3. Check rust at small sizes.** It's about 4.8:1 on paper, which passes but only just. Keep it for links, buttons, and large text. Avoid it for small captions or fine print.

**4. Use rust less often.** If a page has more than one or two rust elements in view, it stops feeling like an accent. Let Ink carry most of the emphasis, and save rust for the main call to action and for links.

**5. Most of the "premium" feel will come from outside the palette:**
- Generous whitespace and a narrow text column.
- A refined serif for headings, with a quiet sans or the same serif for body text.
- Thin sage rules to separate sections.
- Real paper texture, or photos of your actual printed pieces, in place of flat fills.

**6. Print versus screen.** Paper cream and rust will shift on press. If the price list is printed on cream stock, you may want to drop the paper fill in the print file and let the stock supply it. Proof the rust and sage on the real paper before you commit.
