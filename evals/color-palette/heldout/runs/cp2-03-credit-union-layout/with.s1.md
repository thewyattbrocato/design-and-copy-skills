**Pick: deep forest green, `#1F5A3D`.**

You didn't give a brand color, so I chose this one for three reasons:
- It fits "Fernhollow" and the member-owned, local feel.
- Navy reads as a generic bank.
- Plum is distinctive but says little about money or trust, and it's the riskiest to pair with rates and calculators.

Green is a category cliché for finance, but that's fine here.

**Supporting colors**
- **Page ground:** warm off-white `#F6F4EE`.
- **Ink:** green-black `#1B261F`.
- **Pale green tint:** `#E6EFE8`.
- **Straw accent:** `#F4E3A8`, used once, for the Join button.
- **Footer ground:** `#14211A`.

**Where color goes**

| Section | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Hero** | This is the one large area of green. Use `#1F5A3D` as the ground with off-white text, about 8:1. **Join today** is straw `#F4E3A8` with `#14211A` text, about 13:1. The button is about 6:1 against the green. It's the only warm color on the page, so it's the clear action. |
| **Rates strip** | Put it on the pale tint `#E6EFE8` with ink-colored numbers. Don't color the numbers. Don't use green or red for rates going up or down. If you show a change, use an arrow and a word. |
| **Calculator tiles** | Use white cards on the off-white page, with a green icon and green link text (about 7.5:1 on off-white). All three tiles get the same treatment. Don't give each its own hue. Use a green outline or a slightly darker green fill on hover. |
| **Testimonials** | Keep this row neutral: ink text on off-white, with at most a small green quote mark. This row should feel human and quiet, not branded. |
| **Footer** | Use the dark `#14211A` ground with off-white `#E8EEE9` text for hours (about 14:1). Links can be a light green tint. Don't use the lead green as text here, because it's too dark against the footer. |

**Where color shouldn't show up**
- **Body text and headings:** use the ink, not green.
- **Backgrounds of sections other than the hero:** if every section is green, the hero loses its weight.
- **Gradients, and any second accent hue:** the straw button is enough.
- **Error and warning states in calculators:** these need their own red or amber, plus an icon and a label. Don't reuse the brand green for success or the brand color for errors.
- **Meaning carried by color alone:** for example, a green dot for "open" in the branch hours. Write "Open" or "Closed".

**Notes**
- The contrast ratios are my estimates from the hex values, not tool-measured. Check the final pairs in your build.
- The straw button is a judgment call. If you'd rather keep the page to one hue, make the button off-white with green text. It loses some pop but stays on-brand.
