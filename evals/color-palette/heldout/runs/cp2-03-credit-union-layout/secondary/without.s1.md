**Pick: deep forest green.** Navy is the default for almost every bank, so it would make Fernhollow look like one more bank. Plum is distinctive, but it reads as lifestyle or beauty more than money and trust. Forest green suggests growth and community, sets you apart from the big blue brands, and holds up well with a light background. A starting point is around `#1F4D3A`, which gives roughly 9.6:1 contrast against white.

Because green also means "up" or "good," keep it away from anything that needs a clear status signal. Use it as the brand's voice, not as a data color.

**Hero**
- Use a light background (off-white or a faint warm neutral) with the headline in near-black or deep green.
- Make the Join today button solid forest green with white text. It's the strongest color moment on the page, so it should be the only solid fill in the hero.
- Keep the hero photo or illustration natural. Don't tint it green.

**Rates strip**
- Keep the strip neutral: white or light gray, with rate numbers in dark ink.
- Use green only for a thin rule or small labels.
- Don't color the rates green or red. Green rates read as "good," and red reads as "bad," which misleads members when a rate is simply lower.

**Calculator tiles**
- Make all three tiles identical: white, with a hairline border or a soft shadow.
- Put green on one small element per tile, such as the icon or the button outline.
- Don't give each tile its own tint. That breaks the set into three unrelated products.

**Testimonials**
- Keep the cards neutral. Quote marks or a short green accent line are enough.
- Leave the member photos uncolored.
- Avoid green-tinted cards, which add a lot of color to a section that should feel human.

**Footer**
- Use the dark green as the page's closing bookend. This is the one place the full green appears as a field, and it ties the page together.
- Set branch hours in off-white for contrast. Green text on dark green will be hard to read.
- Use a lighter tint of the green for links and dividers.

**Keep out of the design entirely**
- Body text, headings on light backgrounds in bright green, or anything that depends on green to be read.
- Gradients that move from green into other colors.
- Using green for form success states or errors. Give those their own semantic colors so they don't blur into the brand.
- Any second brand color that competes with the green. If you need one, use a warm neutral such as cream or sand, not another saturated hue.
