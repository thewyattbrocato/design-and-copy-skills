**Pick: deep forest green.**

- **Navy** is the safest choice for finance, but nearly every bank uses it, so Fernhollow would look like one more bank.
- **Plum** is distinctive, but it reads as lifestyle or beauty and weakens the "stable, trustworthy" signal a credit union needs.
- **Forest green** says member-owned, local, and steady without looking cheap. It also separates you from the big-bank blues.

**Where color should show**

- **Hero:** Use the green as the hero background or a large band. This is your one big color moment on the page.
- **Join today button:** Don't use the deep green on the green hero, or the button disappears. Use a brighter or lighter green, or invert it to white with green text. Check that the label passes 4.5:1 contrast.
- **Rates strip:** Keep the background neutral (white or a very pale green-gray). Use green sparingly for the rate figures and their labels. Don't use red and green to show rate direction, since green is your brand color. Use arrows or words too.
- **Calculator tiles:** Use white or light neutral cards with a green icon, a thin top border, or a hover state. Keep the tiles quiet so they don't compete with the hero.
- **Testimonials:** Mostly neutral. A green quotation mark or a small name accent is enough. Colored testimonial cards would compete with the calculators.
- **Footer:** Use a near-black green, one step darker than the hero, so it doesn't blend into a deep green hero if they sit close together. Set the branch hours in high-contrast white or light gray. Use a pale green for links and hover states.

**Where color should not show**

- **Body text and most headings:** Keep these near-black or dark gray. Colored body copy hurts readability and makes the page feel busy.
- **Every section background:** Alternate neutral and green surfaces so the green keeps its meaning.
- **Error, success, and warning states:** Don't use your brand green for "success." Form validation and confirmations need their own signals so they don't read as brand decoration.
- **Dates, hours, and rate numbers:** These are information. Keep them in high-contrast neutral text, not tinted text that is harder to read.
- **Icons across the board:** Use green on one or two key icons, not all of them.

The rule of thumb: green appears in the hero, the primary CTA, and small accents. Everything that carries information stays neutral.
