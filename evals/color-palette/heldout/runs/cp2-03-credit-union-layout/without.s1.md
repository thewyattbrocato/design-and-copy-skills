**Pick the deep forest green.** It matches the name (Fernhollow), says "local and member-owned" instead of "national bank," and avoids the navy that nearly every bank uses. Plum is memorable but reads as lifestyle or wellness, and it works against you when you're asking people to trust you with a mortgage. Choose a green dark enough to carry white text at AA contrast, something around `#1F4D3A`. A bright or minty green would read as a fintech app or a cannabis brand.

Treat green as the color for actions and identity, not as decoration. Use it where you want a click or where you want to say "this is us." Everywhere else, use warm off-white, near-black text, and neutral grays.

**Hero**
- Use green for the Join today button (solid fill, white text), and nowhere else in the hero. It should be the only strongly colored thing above the fold.
- Keep the background light or photographic, with real members or local imagery. A full-bleed green hero makes the button disappear into it.
- If you want a second accent, use a warm one (a muted gold or clay) very sparingly, only for small highlights.

**Rates strip**
- Keep the strip neutral: white or pale gray, with dark text.
- The rate numbers should be large and near-black. Rates are the content, so don't color them all green.
- Use green only for the small things that guide the eye: a thin top rule, the "See all rates" link, or one highlighted "best rate" marker.
- Don't use green and red to signal good and bad rates. Green already means "brand" here, so it would read as an endorsement of every number.

**Calculator tiles**
- Give the tiles a neutral card background with a thin border.
- Use green for each tile's icon and its "Calculate" link or button. Keep the tile titles dark.
- Don't fill the tiles solid green. Three green blocks would outweigh the hero button and confuse what the primary action is.

**Testimonials**
- Use almost no color here. Dark text on off-white, with member photos or initials.
- A single green quote mark or small accent rule is enough.
- Don't put the quotes on a green or tinted band. Testimonials should feel human, not branded.

**Dark footer**
- Make the footer a very dark green-black, a shade or two below the brand green, rather than neutral charcoal. This is the one large area of brand color, and it ties the page together.
- Use off-white text for the branch hours and a lighter tint of green for links and hover states.
- Check the contrast on the small hours text especially.

**Page-wide rules**
- Green means "you can act here," so use it consistently for buttons and links. Don't use it for headings or backgrounds as well.
- Reserve red and amber for real errors and warnings, such as form validation.
- Keep green to roughly 10% of the visible page.
