**Go with the deep forest green.** The name Fernhollow already points to it. Green also reads as local and member-owned, where navy reads as "generic bank" and is the default for most competitors in a mid-size town. Plum is distinctive, but it's harder to make feel trustworthy around money, and it's harder to pair with the red/green/amber semantic colors you'll need for rates and calculator results. Pick a green that's dark and slightly blue-leaning (around `#1F4D3A`), not a yellow-leaning one, so it feels steady rather than "eco" or "cannabis." Pair it with a warm off-white background and one small accent, such as a muted gold or clay, used sparingly.

**Where color should show up**

- **Hero:** This is the biggest use of green. Use either a solid green panel or a photo with a green overlay. The **Join today** button should be the single most saturated, highest-contrast thing on the page. On a green hero, make it the warm accent or off-white with green text. On a light hero, make it solid green.
- **Rate strip:** Keep the background quiet (off-white or a very pale green tint). Use green only for the rate numbers themselves, or as a thin top rule. The numbers are the content, so they should be the only thing carrying color.
- **Calculator tiles:** Use white or off-white tiles with a green icon and a green text link or arrow. Green appears on hover and focus states. This shows "interactive" without three heavy green blocks competing with the hero.
- **Testimonials:** Use almost no color. A small green quote mark or a thin rule is enough. Let the faces and words do the work.
- **Footer:** Use a very dark green-black instead of neutral charcoal, so the footer feels like part of the brand. Branch hours should be off-white text. Use the accent color only for links or the "today's hours" highlight.

**Where color shouldn't show up**

- **Body text and headings:** Use near-black or a very dark green-gray, not brand green. Green text on white at body size is tiring to read and can fail contrast.
- **Section backgrounds:** Don't make every band a different tint. Alternate between off-white and white, with green reserved for the hero and footer so they act as bookends.
- **Rate direction or good/bad signals:** Brand green will be confused with "positive" status. If you show rate changes or calculator results, use a separate semantic palette, and never use brand green to mean "good" or "approved."
- **More than one filled button style:** If the calculators and rates also get solid green buttons, **Join today** loses its pull. Make everything else an outline or text link.
- **The accent color:** Keep it to the primary CTA and one or two small highlights. If it appears in every section, it stops being an accent.

As a rule of thumb, green should cover roughly 15–20% of the page: the hero, the footer, the CTA, and small interactive cues. Everything else should be neutral, so that when green appears it tells people where to look or click.
