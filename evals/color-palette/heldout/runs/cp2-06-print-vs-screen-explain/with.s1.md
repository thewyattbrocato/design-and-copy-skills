It's partly a cop-out. The physics is real, but it doesn't mean the printer can't do better.

**What's true**
- Your screen makes color by emitting red, green and blue light. #ff6a1a is a very saturated orange, and that sits near the edge of what a screen can show.
- Standard CMYK ink on paper can't reach it. Ink works by absorbing light, so it's always duller than a glowing screen.
- Uncoated or cream stock makes it worse. Uncoated paper soaks up the ink and flattens the color, which is where the muddy brown comes from.
- A straight RGB→CMYK conversion gives about a C0 M70 Y95 K0 orange. It will always look somewhat muted next to the website.

**What the printer is skipping**
- A good press can print a lively orange. Mixing a **spot ink (Pantone)** is the standard answer for a brand color.
- Brownish usually means one of three things: a K (black) component crept into the mix, the file was converted with the wrong color profile, or the stock is absorbent.
- A proper proof should be built from a color profile that matches the paper. That's something to ask them about.

**What to do**
1. **Ask for a Pantone spot ink.** Pick a Pantone Solid Coated or Uncoated swatch that matches your orange. Orange 021 C is a common bright one, but choose from a physical swatch book, not a screen. A spot ink is a premixed ink, so it prints bright. It costs a bit more because it's an extra plate.
2. **If it has to be CMYK, tune it by hand.** Ask for a build with no black, roughly C0 M65–72 Y95–100 K0, and have them adjust it on a proof. Don't rely on automatic conversion.
3. **Ask about the paper.** Coated or silk stock will keep the orange much brighter than uncoated. If you want an uncoated, natural look, expect the orange to be softer and pick the Pantone from the uncoated book.
4. **Insist on a physical proof.** Get a press proof or a swatch of the actual ink on the actual stock, and judge it in daylight. Don't judge it from a PDF or a screenshot, because screens shift color.
5. **Accept a close match, not an identical one.** Even with a spot ink, paper won't glow like a monitor. Put it next to your site and aim for "clearly the same orange," not a pixel match.
6. **Save a print version of the color in your brand notes.** For example: "Web #ff6a1a / Print Pantone ___ C / CMYK ___." Then every future job starts from the right values.

You could send the printer something like this: *"Our brand orange is #ff6a1a on screen. Can you quote this menu with a Pantone spot ink on [coated/uncoated] stock, and send a physical proof before we approve?"*
