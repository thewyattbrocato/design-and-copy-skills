The printer is partly right, but "just how print works" skips over what you can do about it.

**Why it happened:** Screens mix red, green, and blue light, which can produce very saturated colors. Print mixes cyan, magenta, yellow, and black inks on paper, which can't reach many of those colors. #ff6a1a is a vivid orange near the edge of what four-color printing can reproduce. When the file is converted to CMYK, the closest printable orange comes out less saturated, and on paper it can look brownish. The paper also matters: uncoated stock absorbs ink and dulls color further, and a glossy or silk coated stock keeps it brighter.

**What to do:**

1. **Ask for a Pantone spot color for the orange.** This is the most reliable fix. A spot ink is mixed to a specific color, so it can be much closer to your web orange than a CMYK build. Ask the printer to find the nearest Pantone match to #ff6a1a and check it against a physical swatch book on your paper stock before approving. Spot orange works best if the orange is an accent, such as logo, headers, or price tags, and the rest of the menu is black or one or two colors. Spot inks usually add cost, so ask for a quote.

2. **If the menu must be full-color CMYK, get the printer's ICC profile and soft-proof with it.** In Illustrator, InDesign, or Photoshop, load the profile, turn on Proof Colors, and choose the printer's profile. Then adjust the orange until the on-screen preview looks as close as possible to the target. Your designer can do this. A naive RGB-to-CMYK conversion of #ff6a1a comes out around C0 M58 Y90 K0, and the printer's profile may shift that further.

3. **Ask for a press-matched or contract proof, not just a digital one.** Digital proofs are often printed on a different device than the press, so they may not match the final run.

4. **Choose the paper with the color in mind.** Show the printer your web orange and a few paper samples. A brighter coated or silk stock will make the orange look more vivid than uncoated stock.

5. **Set expectations.** Even with the best setup, a printed orange won't look identical to a backlit screen. The goal is to get the printed piece as close as possible, not an exact match.
