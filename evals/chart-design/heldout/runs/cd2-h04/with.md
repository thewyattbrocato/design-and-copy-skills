Your colleagues' instinct is right. The data shows steady, modest growth, and the chart presents it as a surge.

**Why it feels off**

1. **The truncated axis is the main problem.** Bars encode value by length, so a bar chart that starts at 4,800 isn't honest. Q4 (4,950) is only 3% higher than Q1 (4,820), but on a 4,800–4,960 axis its bar is about 150 units tall against 20 for Q1. That's roughly 7.5 times taller for a 3% change.
2. **The title makes a claim the data doesn't support.** "Huge momentum!" sits on a gain of about 130 tickets over a year. Growth is steady at about 1% per quarter (+41, +44, +45), so it's consistent but not accelerating.
3. **The 3D effect and bright green add hype.** 3D distorts bar heights and makes values harder to read. Saturated green adds a celebratory tone the data doesn't carry.
4. **There are no value labels.** With no numbers on the bars, readers only see the exaggerated heights. They never see that the real figures are 4,820 and 4,950.
5. **There's no comparison.** Without a target, last year's quarters, or the ticket inflow, "resolved" has no context. Higher resolution counts may just mean more tickets came in.

**What I'd change**

- **Start the axis at zero.** The four bars will look nearly equal, which is the accurate picture. If you want to show the small rise, use a line chart with a labeled, zoomed axis. A line can zoom honestly and a bar can't.
- **Use flat, solid bars in one muted color.** Drop the 3D. Use a single accent only if one bar needs emphasis.
- **Label each bar directly** with its value (4,820 / 4,861 / 4,905 / 4,950) and remove the need to read the axis.
- **Add a comparison.** Good options are the same quarters last year, a target, or tickets opened per quarter. Any of them would show whether this is real progress.
- **Retitle with what the data says.** For example: "Tickets resolved grew about 1% per quarter, reaching 4,950 in Q4." You could also write "Resolutions up 2.7% since Q1, no sign of acceleration," if you want the plain version. A neutral "Tickets resolved per quarter" also works.

The honest story is still a good one: four consecutive quarters of growth, with output steady and reliable. That claim holds up if someone questions it. "Huge momentum" doesn't, and it risks making the rest of the deck look inflated too.
