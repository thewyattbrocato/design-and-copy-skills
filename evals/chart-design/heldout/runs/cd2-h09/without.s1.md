**What I checked**
- **Bar lengths:** All five bars use the same scale of 40px per day from a zero baseline (392 ÷ 9.8 = 240 ÷ 6.0 = 124 ÷ 3.1 = 40). That makes the bar lengths an honest comparison.
- **Title against data:** "9.8 days" and "3 to 6 days at our other workshops" match the values, since the others run from 3.1 to 6.0.
- **Layout:** Nothing clips. The "9.8" label ends around x=520 in a 560-wide viewBox. The bars are sorted, and the one highlighted bar plus direct value labels makes the point without a legend.

**Optional tweaks**
1. **Gray contrast:** `#9ca3af` on white is about 2.5:1, a little under the usual 3:1 for graphical elements. Print will probably mute it further. `#8b919c` or `#6b7280` would hold up better. The orange would still stand out, and the direct labels mean no information is lost either way.
2. **Units on the labels:** The subtitle says "days", but a printed page gets skimmed. Writing "9.8 days" on the top bar only would remove any doubt without adding clutter.
3. **Source detail:** If you have room, add a note to the subtitle on whether "average" is a mean or a median, and roughly how many repairs it covers. If one workshop handles far fewer repairs, readers may ask.
4. **Grayscale check:** If any copies will be printed in black and white, run a test page. The orange is clearly darker than the gray, so it should still read, but it's worth confirming.

I'd call it done unless the gray contrast bothers you.
