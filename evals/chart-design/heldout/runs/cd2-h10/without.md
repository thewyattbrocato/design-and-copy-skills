A pie chart works here. It's a part-to-whole split with only three slices, and one slice (62%) is clearly dominant, which is the case pies handle well.

Some tips to make it read well from the back of a room:
- **Label the slices directly.** Put "Hardware 62%", "Software 25%" and "Other 13%" on or next to each slice, with no legend.
- **Start at 12 o'clock and go clockwise, largest first.** Hardware then fills the right side and the order is obvious.
- **Emphasize hardware.** Give it a strong color and make the other two muted, since that's the story.
- **Put the total in the title or center.** For example, "1,240 repair tickets this year". A donut gives you room for this.
- **Consider adding counts.** They're about 769 hardware, 310 software and 161 other, which sums to 1,240.

If you want something other than a pie, use a single horizontal stacked bar or three sorted horizontal bars, labeled the same way. Bars make it easier to compare 25% against 13%. But if the point is "most of our work is hardware," the pie makes that point fastest.
