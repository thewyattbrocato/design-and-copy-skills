No, she's not right here. The zero-baseline rule applies to bar charts, not line charts.

**Why bars need zero:** A bar encodes its value in its length, so truncating the axis distorts the ratios between bars.

**Why your line chart doesn't:** A line encodes position and change over time, so the reader is looking at the shape of the trend. Zero is also arbitrary for Celsius. It's just the freezing point of water, not a true "no temperature" baseline, so 24°C isn't "twice as warm" as 12°C. Starting at zero would squash a 3.5°C range into a nearly flat line and hide the day-to-day variation you want to show.

**Your axis is reasonable.** 20–26 is a tight, sensible fit for data between 21.4 and 24.9. It's not exaggerating anything.

**A few things that keep it honest, and may satisfy your coworker:**
- Label the y-axis clearly with units (°C) and show the tick values.
- Don't stretch the chart so tall that a 1–2°C change looks dramatic. A moderate aspect ratio works well.
- If the audience is non-technical, a short note in the subtitle or caption, such as "Axis starts at 20°C", makes the scale explicit.
- If you want to give context for scale, you could add a light reference line, such as a seasonal average or a threshold that matters (a swimming or ecological limit).

Her instinct would be right if this were a bar chart, or if the axis were cut in a way that made a small change look huge for a persuasive purpose. Neither applies here.
