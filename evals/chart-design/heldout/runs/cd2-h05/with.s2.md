No, she's mostly wrong. For a line chart of temperature, an axis from 20 to 26 °C is fine.

**Why zero isn't required here**
- **Bars need zero, lines don't.** A bar's length is the value, so a cut-off baseline distorts it. A line shows position and change, so it can zoom in on the range where the data lives.
- **Zero isn't meaningful for this data.** 0 °C is the freezing point of water, not an absence of temperature. A 0–26 axis would squash a 3.5-degree range into the top 15% of the chart and hide the day-to-day pattern.
- **Your range is sensible.** The data runs from 21.4 to 24.9, and 20–26 gives modest padding without inventing drama.

**Where she has a point**
A zoomed axis can make a small change look big, so check that the chart doesn't do that:
- **Label the axis clearly.** Include the unit (°C) and tick labels. Her concern is really about readers not noticing the zoom, and clear labels address it.
- **Keep the aspect ratio sensible.** Make the chart wider than tall, so a swing of about 3.5 °C doesn't look like a cliff.
- **Make the title match the data.** If you're showing a gentle warming trend, don't title it as if it were a dramatic spike.
- **Consider a reference line.** A July average or a threshold, such as a swimming comfort or ecological limit, helps readers judge whether 3.5 degrees matters.

**One caveat**
If you ever switch this to bars, for example weekly averages, then the baseline should start at zero or you should switch to dots. For the daily line chart, you can leave it as is.

If you tell her it's a line chart of a quantity where zero isn't meaningful, and that the axis is labeled, that should settle it.
