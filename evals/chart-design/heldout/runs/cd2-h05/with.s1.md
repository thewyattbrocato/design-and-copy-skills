No, she's mostly wrong. Your chart is fine as it is, provided the axis is clearly labeled.

**Why zero isn't required here:** The zero-baseline rule applies to bars and areas, where the length or filled area *is* the value. A line chart encodes values by position, so the reader compares points and slopes, not lengths. Water temperature in °C is also a case where zero is arbitrary. A lake at 0 °C is frozen, and nothing about July at Lake Verity sits anywhere near that. If you started at zero, your data (21.4–24.9) would be squashed into the top 15% of the plot and the day-to-day variation would be hard to see.

**Your range is reasonable:** 20–26 frames the data (about 3.5 °C of spread) without extra empty space, and it doesn't make a small change look dramatic.

**What would make her concern valid:**
- The axis isn't labeled clearly. Put the unit on it (°C) and show tick labels such as 20, 22, 24, 26, so the reader can see it doesn't start at zero.
- The chart is a bar chart. If you'd drawn daily temperatures as bars, a 20 °C baseline would make a 1-degree difference look huge. Switch to a line or dots in that case.
- The title overstates the change. A title like "Lake Verity heats up sharply" over a 3-degree swing would mislead, whatever the axis does. Keep it descriptive, such as "Daily water temperature, Lake Verity, July."

**If you want to settle it with your coworker:** Make sure the axis is labeled and the title is neutral. You could also add a note like "Axis starts at 20 °C." Then there's nothing misleading, and no reason to redo it from zero.
