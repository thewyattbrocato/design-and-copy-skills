Make Dunmore the only colored line and turn the chart into a sentence you can read at a glance.

1. **Highlight Dunmore, grey out the rest.** Draw Dunmore in one strong color (a dark blue or red works well) and the other five in the same light grey, around #BBBBBB. The tool's six default colors make all six lines compete. With one accent color, the eye goes straight to the line that climbed.

2. **Optionally, shade the other clinics' range.** Instead of five grey lines, draw one light grey band from the lowest to the highest of the other five clinics each quarter. It shows "the normal range is 35–50" directly, and Dunmore visibly leaves it. If your tool can't do bands, five thin grey lines work fine.

3. **Remove the legend and label the lines directly.** Put "Dunmore" at the end of its line and "Other clinics" next to the grey band or lines. Delete the legend. Readers shouldn't have to match colors to a box.

4. **Annotate the two Dunmore values.** Add labels for "42 min" at Q1 2023 and "71 min" at Q4 2025 on its line. These are the numbers you care about, so they should appear on the chart, not only in your head.

5. **Remove the gridlines.** Delete them, or keep at most a few very light horizontal lines. Keep a y-axis with a clear label, "Average ER wait (minutes)", and a readable tick scale such as 30, 40, 50, 60, 70. The x-axis can label just the years, or Q1 and Q3 if the tool allows.

6. **Make the title state the finding.** Replace "Wait Time by Clinic" with something like: *"Dunmore's ER wait rose from 42 to 71 minutes; other clinics stayed between 35 and 50."* It's longer, but a reader gets the point without studying the chart.

7. **Try small multiples if the tool can't do steps 1–4.** Make six small panels, one per clinic, with the same y-axis scale on each, and give Dunmore's panel a colored border or title. Each line is then easy to read, and the comparison still holds because the scales match.

Most charting tools can do steps 1, 3, 5 and 6 through their formatting menus. If yours can't do direct labels or bands, export the data to a spreadsheet or a plotting library and make the chart there, which takes about ten minutes.
