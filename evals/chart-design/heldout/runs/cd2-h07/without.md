I'd use a 4×3 grid of small multiples: one small line chart per store, all on the same slide.

**Why not one chart**
- Twelve lines on one axis is unreadable.
- A shared axis running 0 to 9,000 flattens the 400-visitor store into a line along the bottom, so you can't see its seasonality.

**Layout**
- **Panels:** Give each store its own panel with weeks 1–52 on a shared x-axis. Only label the x-axis on the bottom row, with ticks at roughly weeks 1, 13, 26, 39 and 52, or month names.
- **Y-axes:** Give each panel its own y-scale, starting at zero. Because the scales differ, put each store's name and typical volume in its panel title (for example, "Downtown · ~9,000/wk"). That keeps the size differences visible without forcing a common axis.
- **Order:** Sort panels by size, largest to smallest, reading left to right and then down. The pattern outliers will then stand out against a consistent order.
- **Color:** Draw the ten "normal" stores in one muted gray or blue. Give Airport and Harbor Mall a single accent color each, and use the same accent for their panel titles.
- **Annotations:** Only annotate what supports your story.
  - On the normal stores, add a small dot or label at the pre-Christmas peak.
  - On Airport, label the July peak ("Peaks wk ~28").
  - On Harbor Mall, add a vertical marker at week 30 with a short note ("Renovation begins").
- **Title:** Make the slide title the takeaway, not a description. For example: "10 of 12 stores peak before Christmas. Airport peaks in July, and Harbor Mall dropped after its week-30 renovation."

**Two optional tweaks**
- If you want to compare seasonal shape directly, index each store to its own average (100 = typical week) and put every panel on the same y-axis. Then Airport's odd shape is obvious at a glance. The cost is that you lose absolute size, so keep the volumes in the panel titles.
- For Airport and Harbor Mall, you could draw a faint gray "typical store" curve behind the line to show how far they deviate. That only works if you use the indexed version above.
