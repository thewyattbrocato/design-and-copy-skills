Use a 3×4 grid of small multiples, one panel per branch, all on the same axes. I haven't built anything yet; this is the structure.

**Why not one chart.** Twelve lines on one chart can't be told apart. The color guidance I'm working from caps categorical colors at 8, and a printed page has no hover to help. Small multiples avoid both problems and let the board compare branches by shape.

**Page structure (A4 portrait, about 15 mm margins)**

1. **Header (top ~30 mm)**
   - A headline that states the finding, for example: "Visits peaked in summer at 11 of 12 branches; Dunmere fell sharply after its July roof closure." Check the count against your data before you print it.
   - A one-line subtitle: "Monthly visits by branch, January–December 2025."

2. **Chart grid (middle ~190 mm)**
   - Three columns by four rows, in alphabetical order, which is the order you gave. Board members will look for their own branch, so alphabetical is easier to scan than ranking by size.
   - Each panel has the branch name top-left in bold and one line of monthly visits beneath it.
   - **Shared y-axis across all panels.** Label it only on the left column and put light gridlines in every panel. Label the x-axis only on the bottom row, using single letters J F M A M J J A S O N D.
   - A pale band behind June–August in every panel shows the summer reading period. It makes the seasonal pattern obvious without any legend.
   - **Color:** draw the 11 normal branches in one neutral dark color (a single dark blue or gray). Draw Dunmere alone in a contrasting accent, with a heavier line. Color follows the branch, so don't recolor anything.
   - **Dunmere panel:** add a small vertical tick at July with the label "Roof closed". This annotation matters more than the color, because it still works in grayscale.
   - **Direct labels:** mark each panel's peak month with its value, and nothing else. Print has no tooltips, so this replaces them. Labeling every point would clutter the panels.

3. **Footer (bottom ~25 mm)**
   - Source and definition of "visits" (for example, door counts or the system that records them).
   - A note that all panels share one scale.
   - A note on Dunmere: the closure date and whether the figures after it are partial or estimated.
   - Optionally, a compact table of annual totals per branch, which gives readers exact numbers to go with the shapes.

**Print details**
- Keep text at 7 pt or larger (panel titles 9–10 pt). Lines should be at least 0.75 pt, with Dunmere's about 1.5 pt.
- Check that it reads in black and white, since boards often photocopy handouts. The bold line and the annotation carry Dunmere, not the color.
- Use a white background, light gray gridlines, and no chart borders. Give each panel about 5 mm of gutter.

**One decision for you.** A shared y-axis is honest, and it shows which branches are large. If your biggest branch has more than about 5× the visits of the smallest, the small ones will look flat and their summer peak will disappear. In that case, either keep the shared axis and accept that, or index each branch to its own January–June average (=100). Indexing makes the seasonal shape and Dunmere's drop easy to see, but you lose absolute volumes, and you'd move those into the totals table.
