## Page structure (A4 portrait, top to bottom)

**1. Header (about 15% of the page)**
- **Title (states the finding):** "Visits peaked in summer at most branches; Dunmere fell sharply after its July roof closure." Check the count first. If it's 11 of 12, say so.
- **Subtitle (small, gray):** "Monthly visits per branch, January–December 2025. All charts share the same vertical scale."

**2. Chart grid (about 75%)**
- **Layout:** 3 columns × 4 rows. That fits portrait and gives each panel a wide, short shape that suits a time series.
- **Order:** Sort by annual total, largest first, so the grid reads as a ranking. Alphabetical is better if the board will mostly look up their own branch. Pick one and say which in the subtitle.
- **Panel header:** Branch name on the left, annual total on the right, e.g. "Carrow 48,200".
- **Line:** One line per panel, with a dot and value label on the peak month.
- **Summer band:** A faint gray band behind Jun–Aug in every panel, so the summer peak is easy to see.
- **X-axis:** Single-letter months (J F M A M J J A S O N D), with no rotated labels.
- **Y-axis:** The same range in every panel, starting at 0. Label it only on the left column, with 2–3 faint gridlines.

**3. Footer (about 10%)**
- **Source and date:** Source, "data as of [date]", and the definition of a "visit" (door count, or something else).
- **Notes:** Any caveats, such as Dunmere's closure dates.
- **Takeaway:** One or two sentences of plain text for anyone who skips the charts.

## Where the emphasis goes
- **Eleven panels in one muted color:** Use mid-gray or dark slate. This is the context.
- **Dunmere in one accent color:** Use a darker, heavier line than the others, plus a vertical marker at July labeled "Roof closed". It should be the only colored panel.
- **Dunmere in grayscale:** The accent has to survive a black-and-white printer, so also make the line heavier and add a light tint behind the panel.
- **Dunmere stays in its normal position:** Don't pull it out of the grid. If 2024 data exists, add a thin dashed 2024 line in the Dunmere panel to show what a normal July–December looks like. Otherwise leave it off, because I don't want to guess at a baseline.

## Decisions that depend on your data
- **Shared vs. per-panel scale:** A shared scale is the honest way to compare branches. If your smallest branch has less than about a quarter of the largest's visits, its line will look flat. In that case, use a shared scale and say so, or give each panel its own scale and print the peak value in every panel. I'd avoid mixing the two.
- **July at Dunmere:** If July was only partly affected by the closure, draw that point hollow or dashed and note it.
- **Print legibility:** Keep text at 7pt or larger, lines at 1pt or heavier, and margins of at least 12 mm. Do a test print before the meeting.
