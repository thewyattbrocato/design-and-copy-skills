# Layout for the one-page handout

**Use small multiples: 12 identical mini-charts in one grid, with one shared scale.** A single chart with 12 lines can't work. The palette limit is about 8 distinguishable hues, and 12 lines would tangle into spaghetti. Panels also let Dunmere stand out without a legend.

## Page structure (A4 portrait, about 190 × 277 mm usable)

**1. Header (top ~30 mm)**
- **Headline that states the finding:** "Visits peaked in summer at most branches; Dunmere fell sharply after its July roof closure". Avoid a label like "Monthly visits 2025".
- **Subtitle (one line):** "Monthly visits by branch, January–December 2025".

**2. Chart grid (middle ~200 mm)**
- **3 columns × 4 rows**, which suits portrait. Each panel is about 60 × 48 mm.
- **Order:** keep alphabetical (Ashby → Linden) so board members can find their branch. Don't rank by size.
- **Panel contents:**
  - Branch name at top-left, in bold ink.
  - One thin line (2 px or about 1 pt) for the 12 months.
  - Light horizontal gridlines only.
  - X-axis labels J F M A M J J A S O N D, shown on the bottom row only.
  - Y-axis labels on the left column only.
- **Shared y-axis across all panels.** The same scale lets people compare branches honestly. If your smallest branches end up as flat lines, add a second version indexed to each branch's own average. Don't give each panel a free scale without saying so.
- **Summer band:** a faint grey shaded strip behind June–August in every panel. Label it once ("Summer reading") in the first panel. This shows the shared pattern without any extra ink.
- **Peak label:** a small dot and value on each branch's peak month only. Print has no hover, so label the key values and leave the rest.

**3. Dunmere emphasis**
- Draw the 11 other branches in a neutral dark grey. Draw Dunmere in one accent colour, such as a strong orange or blue, with a slightly heavier line.
- Add a thin vertical marker at July in the Dunmere panel, with a short annotation: "Roof closure, July".
- Optionally give Dunmere's panel a subtle tinted background or border. Keep that as the only departure from the pattern.
- **Don't colour the 12 branches differently.** Colour here means "the exception", and that is the only message it should carry.

**4. Footer (bottom ~25 mm)**
- Source and definition of "visits" (for example door counts vs. loans), and the data period.
- A one-line note on Dunmere: closure date and whether it has reopened.
- A line on the scale: "All panels share the same vertical scale (0–X visits)".
- Optionally, a two- or three-line takeaway: the summer peak range, and Dunmere's drop as a percentage against its June level.

## Print-specific points
- **Check it in greyscale.** The accent line must still read as different through weight and the annotation, not colour alone.
- **Minimum text size:** 7–8 pt for axis labels, 9–10 pt for branch names, 14–18 pt for the headline.
- **Keep ink light.** Use thin lines, pale grid, and no chart borders, boxes, or 3D effects.
- **Leave margins** of at least 10–12 mm for office printers.
