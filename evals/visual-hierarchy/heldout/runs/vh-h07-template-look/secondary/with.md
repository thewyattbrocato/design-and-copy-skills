The page looks generic for two reasons. Effects are doing work that content should do, and the layout has no structure that belongs to a dog groomer. Your copy and screenshots are probably the fastest way to fix it.

**What's making it generic, most costly first**

1. **Every section has the same decoration.** The pill, gradient headline, glowing button and blobs all carry no information. They say "startup template," and repeating them everywhere means nothing stands out. The glow and gradient headline also compete with the main button for attention.
2. **The three-card grid is a default.** Identical cards with an icon tile, bold title and two grey lines show three features without ranking them. Nothing says which one matters most to a groomer.
3. **Pricing copies the feature grid.** Plans are peers, so they should share one treatment, but the recommended plan should stand out through position, size or border weight, not a colored badge and glow.
4. **The "AI-powered" pill is the generic claim.** Every competitor says it. It also points the reader at your technology instead of their problem.

**What would make it yours**

- **Speak groomer.** Use their words: appointments, bath-and-brush vs. full groom, deposits, tips, no-shows, rebooking, end-of-day cash, mobile vans. Describe the specific chores you remove, such as chasing unpaid deposits or reconciling card tips against the day's bookings.
- **Show the real product in the hero.** A screenshot of an actual invoice or end-of-day close, with real-looking client and pet names, does more than a gradient blob. Make it the one visual with weight on the page.
- **Rank the features as a sequence.** Rewrite them as three tasks in order: what happens at checkout, what happens at end of day, what happens at month end. Use a list or alternating rows, not a card grid, and keep an icon only if it helps the reader.
- **Use one flat primary button.** Drop the glow. Keep one solid color for the action and make the secondary action a text link.
- **Remove the blobs and the gradient headline.** If you want a brand moment, pick one device, such as a single warm accent color used for the recommended plan or one illustrated detail, and keep it quieter than the headline.
- **Make the pricing section its own layout.** A simple comparison table or a two-column list with one plan marked by a heavier border works well. Don't reuse the feature cards.
- **Don't invent proof.** If you lack customer counts or testimonials, use a visible placeholder such as "[number of groomers]" or leave the slot out.

**Quick test:** squint at the page. Right now the pill, headline, button glow and blobs probably all read at about the same level. After the changes, the screenshot and the primary button should be the first things you see.

I haven't seen your code or the page itself, so these are judgment calls from your description. If you share the file or a screenshot, I can point to the exact elements to change and make the edits. I'd keep the copy structure and any content that already works, and change only the parts that break the order.
