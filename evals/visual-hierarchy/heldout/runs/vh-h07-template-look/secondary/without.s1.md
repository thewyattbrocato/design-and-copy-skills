The page looks generic because its visual choices come from the category, not from your customers. Pill badge, gradient headline, glowing button, icon-tile cards, blobs, and three-up pricing are the default kit for AI SaaS templates. Each one is a signal that says "startup," and none of them says anything about dog groomers. The content has the same problem: "AI-powered," three equal features, and interchangeable benefit copy could sit on a payroll tool, a scheduling app, or a CRM.

A quick test: cover the logo. If another product could use the same page unchanged, it's generic.

**What would make it yours**

Start from the groomer's day. They're often a sole operator with wet hands, a dog in the tub, and a phone in a towel. Their money comes in as card payments, cash tips, and deposits. Their expenses are shampoo, blades, van fuel, and insurance. Most do their books at 10pm or at the end of the quarter. Every section should reflect that.

- **Headline in their words.** Something like "Your books, closed out before the last dog is dry." Make a specific claim you can back up, not "streamline your finances."
- **Hero visual of the work itself.** Use a real screenshot of a day's appointments and tips, or a photo of an actual salon's receipts. Drop the blob. If you have no real imagery yet, a simple, honest illustration of a grooming station with a ledger beats an abstract shape.
- **Replace the feature cards with a walkthrough.** Show one real scenario: a deposit came in, a tip was split, and a supply order was logged, then the result. Or use one large annotated screenshot with three short callouts. Varied layouts keep the page from reading as a template.
- **Use the trade's vocabulary.** Tip pools, deposits, no-shows, booking fees, and mobile-van mileage are more distinctive than "transactions" and "insights." Accuracy matters here too: make sure the tax and category language matches what the product actually does.
- **Social proof from named groomers.** Include salon names, city, and a specific outcome, like "I stopped doing my books on Sunday nights." A generic testimonial doesn't help.
- **Pricing that answers a real question.** Try a single plan with a plain list of what's included, or compare against their current cost, like a spreadsheet plus a bookkeeper's monthly fee. Avoid three identical cards.

**Visual cleanup**

- Remove the gradient text, the glow, and the blobs. Use one flat brand color and one accent, ideally drawn from something in the trade: kraft paper, towel white, a shampoo-bottle teal, or whatever your brand is.
- Make the primary button solid with no shadow and a plain verb: "Start free" or "Try it on your next appointment."
- Pick a typeface with some character for headings and keep body text plain. Tighten the heading spacing.
- Drop the "New: AI-powered" pill unless AI is doing something specific and useful. If it is, say what it does in the copy instead of in a badge.
