# Long-form article hierarchy

**The page's job:** get the reader into the piece and keep them reading comfortably to the end. The title says what it is, the standfirst makes the case for reading, and the body carries the reader through. Everything else supports that.

## Levels

| Level | Content | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Primary** | Title | Entry point and the single boss of the page |
| **2. Secondary** | Standfirst, body text | The standfirst is the bridge in. The body is the main content, and it gets the most space. |
| **3. Tertiary** | Subheads, pull quotes | Navigation and rhythm inside the body |
| **4. Meta** | Byline, date, related links | Context and exits, kept quiet |

## Treatment

**Title (level 1)**
- It is the largest and heaviest element on the page, using two levers: size and weight. A serif display face or a bold sans both work.
- Put it at the top of the reading edge with generous space above and below. Space does more work here than any effect.
- Keep it to a tight measure and short line lengths, and use tighter leading than the body.
- No color accent, underline or decoration. Strongest value contrast on the page.

**Standfirst (level 2, lead-in)**
- Size it about 1.25–1.5× the body, in regular or light weight. It should sit clearly between the title and the body, and not look like a second headline.
- Use the same text color as the body, or one modest step softer. Keep it well above the contrast floor.
- Limit it to two or three lines. Put a visible gap below it, so the title and standfirst read as one group and the body starts a new one.

**Byline and date (level 4, meta)**
- Place them directly under the standfirst, or between the title and standfirst if the standfirst is long. Pick one placement and use it on every article.
- Set them small. The author's name gets a weight step (medium or semibold), and the date is regular and a little lighter. Separate them with space or a single divider, and don't add icons.
- Use a quiet gray that still passes contrast. Don't make the byline a button or a badge.

**Body text (level 2, the bulk)**
- This is the reading baseline. Use a comfortable size with generous leading, and a measure of roughly 60–75 characters.
- Use one flush-left edge, with ragged right. Don't center or justify.
- Separate paragraphs with consistent spacing and no indents. The strongest text contrast goes here after the title, because this is what people read for an extended time.
- Links inside it get one treatment, such as an underline or a color plus underline, used the same way everywhere. Don't use bold or italic for emphasis except where it is truly needed.

**Subheads (level 3)**
- Use one or two heading levels at most. The first level steps up from the body by at least 1.25×, in semibold, in the same text color.
- Put more space above than below, so each subhead attaches to the section it introduces.
- Keep them flush with the body edge, with no rules, numbers or color unless the article has a structure that needs them.
- The second level, if you need one, differs by weight or size, not by adding color.

**Pull quotes (level 3, rhythm)**
- Use them sparingly, about one per 800–1,000 words. They repeat a line already in the text, so they must not outrank the title.
- Set them larger than the body and lighter than the title, in the display face or italic, and not bold.
- Give them a distinct position: indented, or hung into the margin. Use one marker only, either a thin rule or a large quotation mark, never both.
- Keep the color neutral or a single accent. Reserve the accent for this role so it keeps its meaning.
- On narrow screens, drop them into the column with space around them and no float.

**Related links (level 4, exits)**
- Put them after the article ends, set apart by space and a small label ("Related" or "Keep reading"). Don't interrupt the body with them.
- Each link is a headline in the link style, with an optional small meta line (section, date) in meta treatment.
- Use one consistent list or row layout. Skip boxes, shadows and thumbnails unless images carry real information.
- Don't use a loud button. If there is a single next-step action, such as "Next article", it gets the one prominent link treatment here.

## Rules that hold it together

- **One reading edge.** Title, standfirst, byline, body and subheads share one left edge. Pull quotes may break out of it, and that is the deliberate surprise.
- **Visible steps.** Each neighboring level differs by about 1.25–1.5× in size, or by two weight steps, or by a clear value difference.
- **Emphasis budget.** Only a small share of the page, about a tenth, should be bold, colored or large. In practice that is the title, subheads and pull quotes.
- **Same role, same style.** Every subhead, link and pull quote looks the same throughout the article and across articles.
- **Marks must earn their place.** Include a rule or a box only if it separates or identifies something. Space does most of the grouping here.

## Checks

- Squint at the page. The title should be the dominant shape, with subheads as light punctuation down the body.
- Convert to grayscale. The order should survive.
- Remove all styling. Position and grouping should still show title, then standfirst, then byline, then body.
