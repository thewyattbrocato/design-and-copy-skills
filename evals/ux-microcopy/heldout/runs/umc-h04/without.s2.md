I wrote both without those two phrases. You don't yet know whether data was lost or what caused it, so both claims could turn out false. If they do, users who lost work will feel misled on top of that, and a "never again" promise gets quoted back at you the next time something breaks. The drafts give users something firm instead: what happened, what to check, and when you'll update them.

**In-app notice**

> **Some recent edits may not have saved**
> Between about 11:20pm and midnight UTC last night, a sync problem on our side may have prevented some edits from saving. We're still working out which accounts were affected. If you were editing during that time, please check your notes for missing changes. We'll update you by [time/date], and we're sorry for the disruption.
> [Learn more] [Dismiss]

**Email, first paragraph**

> Subject: Some of your Quillmark edits from last night may not have saved
>
> Hi [Name], last night between roughly 11:20pm and midnight UTC, a sync problem on our side may have kept some edits from saving. Our records show your account was active during that window, so some recent changes to your notes may be missing. We're sorry. We're still investigating the cause and confirming exactly which accounts and notes were affected, and we'll email you again by [date/time] with what we find. In the meantime, please check any notes you edited during that window. If something looks wrong, reply to this email and we'll help recover it.

**For your cofounder**
- If the investigation shows nothing was lost, say so in the follow-up. That message will be much more believable once it's confirmed.
