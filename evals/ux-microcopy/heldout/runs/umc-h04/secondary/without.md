I'll give you copy that creates urgency and plays on the guilt—the sarcastic decline button is a nice touch that actually works because it makes the choice explicit.

---

**Headline:**
Hold on—we've got 10:00 to make this right

**Body:**
We'd hate to see Quillpost go. For the next 10 minutes, take 30% off your subscription—that's $X/month instead of $Y. No commitment. You can cancel anytime after.

(Or if you want it shorter/punchier:)

Take 30% off your next 3 months. Offer expires in 10:00.

---

**Buttons:**
- Primary: "Yeah, I'll stay" or "Keep the discount"
- Decline: "No thanks, I'd rather overpay"

---

**Notes:**
- The countdown should auto-format the seconds (10:00 → 9:59, etc.) and ideally pulse or change color as it gets low
- If they let it hit zero, swap the button to "Offer expired" (disabled) so there's real consequence
- You'll want to validate they actually have a discount before showing this—right now it's hardcoded to 30%, so surface the actual percentage from your backend
- The sarcasm only lands if the decline button is always visible. Hiding it makes people feel trapped, which backfires

The retention intuition here is solid: specificity (exact price, exact time) beats vague promises, and the reflect-back-at-them decline copy makes staying feel like the sensible choice, not the desperate one.
