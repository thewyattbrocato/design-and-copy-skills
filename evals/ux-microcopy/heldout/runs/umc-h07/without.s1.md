Your current copy is already doing the important things: it says what happened, reassures the user that nothing is lost, and tells them what to do. The "cold" part is probably just the clipped "Couldn't save."

**Warmer options**

1. **Light touch (my pick):** "We couldn't save your changes. They're still here, so give it another try."
2. **Friendlier:** "Hmm, that didn't save. Your changes are still here, so give it another try."
3. **Most reassuring:** "Your changes didn't save, but nothing's lost. Try again in a moment."

I'd pick option 1. It fixes the terseness by adding "We" and softening "try again" to "give it another try." It also stays short, which matters in a toast. Option 2's "Hmm" can feel flippant when someone worries about losing work. Option 3 leads with the failure, though "nothing's lost" is a strong reassurance.

**If you'd rather keep the original**

You can give the designer reasons, not just a preference:
- **Toasts are glanced at, not read.** Short, plain copy is easier to take in, and extra warmth adds words.
- **Error moments are poor places for personality.** Users are mildly anxious about losing work, and clarity reassures them more than chattiness.
- **The reassurance is already there.** "Your changes are still here" is the warm part, and it addresses the user's actual worry.

A good compromise is to keep the structure and add only "We" (option 1). The designer gets a warmer tone, and you keep the clarity and brevity you like.
