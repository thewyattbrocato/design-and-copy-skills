Your current message actually already does the hard work of being reassuring—"Your changes are still here" is genuinely warm; it tells users their work is safe. That's the emotional core that matters in a save failure.

Where you could soften it: "Couldn't" is declarative and technical. Here are a few paths, each slightly friendlier while keeping the same clarity:

**Minimal change (one word):**
- "We couldn't save. Your changes are still here, so try again."
  - Shifts responsibility to the system ("we") instead of stating it coldly; feels more like the product is working *with* the user.

**More conversational:**
- "We ran into trouble saving. Your changes are safe—try again."
  - "Ran into trouble" is friendlier than "couldn't"; breaks up the sentence so the reassurance lands sooner.

**If you want to keep it tighter:**
- "Trouble saving. Your changes are safe—try again."
  - Quickest version, still warm because "safe" is the reassurance.

**Why the designer has a point:** Error messages don't need to be cold. Even though you need clarity and a next step (which you have), tone still matters when someone is already frustrated. "Couldn't save" reads like the system announcing a problem; "We ran into trouble" sounds like it's telling you *together*.

**But they should know:** The reassurance ("still here" / "safe") is what makes this feel okay, so don't cut that part. Any version that drops it would actually feel colder, even with friendlier words around it.

Pick whichever matches your app's voice—the minimal change is the safest if you're not sure the team's brand is conversational.
