Here's a reason to consider the change: error messages are inherently frustrating moments, and a warmer tone makes users feel supported rather than lectured. The current version is clear and reassuring, but it reads procedurally—like the app is explaining the situation *at* the user. A friendlier version acknowledges the moment feels annoying for them.

That said, it's a real tradeoff: overly casual can seem dismissive of a real problem. Here are a few options that stay professional while warming it up:

1. **"Oops! Your changes are safe—let's try saving again."** — Uses "oops" to acknowledge the hiccup, "let's" feels collaborative.

2. **"Hmm, that didn't save. Don't worry, your work is here. Ready to try again?"** — "Don't worry" is reassuring, the question invites action without demanding it.

3. **"That didn't work, but your changes are safe here. Ready to give it another shot?"** — "Give it another shot" is slightly more casual/encouraging.

All three keep the reassurance that their work isn't lost—the thing that actually matters—but feel less clinical. I'd probably lean toward option 2 if the app's voice is conversational, or option 1 if it's a bit more playful.
