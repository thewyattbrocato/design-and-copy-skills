Your instinct to defend it has merit—the current message actually does its job well. It leads with what happened, reassures the user their work isn't lost (the key fear in this moment), and tells them what to do. That directness is appropriate for an error, especially one that's already stressful.

That said, there's room to warm it *slightly* without losing clarity:

**Option 1** (one small shift):
"We couldn't save that. Your changes are still here—try again."

This just swaps the subject from the action ("Couldn't save") to the system ("We couldn't save"), which feels slightly less harsh and puts a gentle acknowledgment in there without an apology.

**Option 2** (softer and gentler):
"We ran into a hiccup. Your changes are safe—want to try saving again?"

This trades some directness for warmth ("ran into a hiccup" feels less like a failure, "want to try" is softer than "try again"), but it's still clear and quick.

**Option 3** (minimal warm-up):
"Saving didn't work that time. Your changes are safe—try again."

This one just makes it slightly more conversational ("didn't work that time" versus "couldn't save").

**My take**: The designer isn't wrong that Option 1 or 2 would feel a bit warmer, but your version isn't cold—it's *reassuring*, which is what matters most when something failed. If your team's voice is generally direct and clear, defend the original. If it's a bit more conversational elsewhere, Option 1 is the smallest shift that nods to the feedback without sacrificing clarity.
