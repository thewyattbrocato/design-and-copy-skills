Your current message is actually doing a lot right—it's reassuring ("Your changes are still here"), action-oriented, and concise. Those are valuable in an error state when people are already frustrated.

The "coldness" probably comes from two things: "Couldn't save" opens negatively without any acknowledgment, and the imperative "try again" feels a bit curt. A warmer version could soften both while keeping the clarity:

**Option 1** (empathetic)
"We couldn't save your changes just now, but they're safe here. Try again whenever you're ready."

**Option 2** (encouraging)
"Having trouble saving. No worries—your work is safe. Give it another shot when you're ready."

**Option 3** (subtle warmth)
"Looks like saving didn't work that time. Your changes are safe though—feel free to try again."

The tradeoff: the original is tighter and feels more "no-nonsense," which some users actually prefer in error messages. The warmer versions add a bit more personality and acknowledge the friction, but they're also longer.

Before you push back to the designer, you could ask: *What specifically feels cold to you?* Are they reacting to the overall tone, or specific words? That might point you toward whether you need a full rewrite or just tweaking one or two phrases. That conversation might also surface whether they've heard from users that the current message feels dismissive—that would be a reason to change it.
