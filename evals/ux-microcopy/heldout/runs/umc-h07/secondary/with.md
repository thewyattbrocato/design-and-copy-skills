Your message is already solid—it has a reassuring structure (names the problem, confirms safety, offers the action). The terseness is the coldness. Here are three options that warm it with minimal changes:

**Minimal warmth:**
"We couldn't save that. Your changes are here and safe—try again?"

**Medium warmth:**
"We had trouble saving. Your changes are right here, whenever you're ready to try again."

**Warmest (while staying brief):**
"We couldn't save just now. Your changes are safe and waiting for you. Ready to try again?"

The shift comes from:
- Using "we" (shows the product took responsibility, not the user)
- Dropping the imperative tone in "try again" and making it more conversational
- A small flourish like "waiting for you" or "whenever you're ready" that acknowledges the user's feelings without being saccharine

The original is perfectly *functional*—if your designer's team is split, that's a defensible reason to keep it. Friendlier doesn't always mean better. But if you want to lean in, the "minimal warmth" option is a safe middle ground that costs almost nothing in brevity.
