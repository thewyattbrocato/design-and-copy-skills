# Layout for a heat pump water heater explainer

**Core idea:** make the diagram the spine of the page. Don't put it in a box beside 600 words of text. Walk readers through it in the order the energy moves.

## 1. Top of page (above the fold)
- **Headline in homeowner terms:** "How a heat pump water heater heats your water using 70% less electricity" (use a figure you can back up, or drop the number).
- **One-sentence analogy:** "It works like a refrigerator in reverse: instead of pushing heat out of a box, it pulls heat out of the air and puts it into your water."
- A small version of the diagram, or a short summary line, so skimmers get the gist right away.

## 2. The diagram, as a numbered walkthrough
Number the parts in the order the heat travels, and match each number to a short text block:

1. **Fan pulls in room air.** It's warm enough to carry usable heat, even in a cool garage.
2. **Evaporator coil absorbs the heat.** Refrigerant inside boils at a low temperature and takes the heat from the air. Cool, dry air leaves the unit.
3. **Compressor squeezes the refrigerant.** This raises its temperature a lot.
4. **Condenser coil, wrapped around the tank, gives the heat to the water.** The refrigerant cools and turns back to liquid, then the loop repeats.
5. **Water flows through.** Cold water enters at the bottom and hot water leaves at the top.

Ways to present it:
- **Best:** a sticky diagram on desktop, with the matching step highlighting as the reader scrolls. This is the one worth building if your site setup allows it.
- **Simpler:** the full diagram at the top, then each step with a small cropped or zoomed view of that part.
- **Mobile:** stack each step as a diagram crop followed by 2–3 sentences. Make sure the diagram labels are readable at phone width, or you'll need a simplified mobile version.

## 3. Diagram design tips
- **Use color for temperature:** blue for cold water and cool air, orange or red for heat and hot water. Make the heat-flow arrows visually distinct from the water-flow arrows. Mixing the two is the most common source of confusion.
- **Label in plain words**, with the technical term in parentheses: "Heat-absorbing coil (evaporator)."
- **Add a short "refrigerant loop" callout.** The key point is that refrigerant is a closed loop and never mixes with your water. People often wonder about that.

## 4. After the walkthrough
- **"Why it saves money"** (short): it moves heat instead of making it, so it uses far less electricity than a standard electric resistance heater.
- **"What to know before you buy"** (3–5 bullets): needs space and airflow, the room gets slightly cooler and drier, makes some fan noise, and works best in a space that stays above roughly 40°F. Many units also have a backup electric element.
- **FAQ** with 3–4 real customer questions, such as noise, cold garages, installation time, and rebates.
- **Call to action:** "Wondering if it fits your home? Call Tidewater Plumbing for a free estimate," with a phone number and button. Put it at the end and once mid-page, not only in the header.

## 5. Handling the 600 words
- Aim for about 40–60 words per step. Anything beyond that probably belongs in the FAQ or "before you buy" sections.
- Keep sentences short and lead with the verb ("The fan pulls in air…").
- Define each term the first time you use it, then use it consistently. Don't switch between "evaporator," "coil," and "heat exchanger."
- Cut the thermodynamics. Homeowners need to know *that* the refrigerant carries heat, not why it boils at a low temperature.

## 6. Accessibility and SEO
- Give the diagram real alt text that summarizes the flow, and put the steps in real HTML text, not in the image.
- Use descriptive subheadings ("How the evaporator coil captures heat"), since people search for those phrases.
