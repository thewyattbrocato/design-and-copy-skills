# Recommended layout

**Lead with the idea, then the diagram, then the details.** Most homeowners get stuck on one point: the unit *moves* heat instead of *making* it. Say that before anything else.

## 1. Top of page (above the fold)
- **Headline:** "How a Heat Pump Water Heater Works"
- **One-sentence summary:** "It doesn't burn fuel or just heat a coil. It pulls heat out of the surrounding air and moves it into your water, like a refrigerator running in reverse."
- Optionally, one line on the payoff (typically much less electricity than a standard electric tank). Only include figures you can source.

## 2. The diagram, large and early
- Put it right after the summary, full width on desktop. On mobile, make sure it's legible without pinching to zoom.
- **Number the steps directly on the diagram** (①–④) and match them to numbered text below. This ties the picture to the words.
- **Color-code temperature.** Use blue for cool air and cold water, and orange/red for warm refrigerant and hot water. Your arrows will then carry meaning without being read.
- **Label parts in plain language:** "Fan pulls in room air," "Evaporator coil (absorbs heat)," "Compressor (concentrates the heat)," "Condenser coil (releases heat into the water)."
- **Consider adding an expansion valve and a dashed refrigerant loop.** Your diagram doesn't list the valve, and without it the loop doesn't close. If you want to keep it simple, a faint loop line and a single sentence of text is enough.
- Write real alt text describing the process, for accessibility and SEO.

## 3. Step-by-step walkthrough (the core of your 600 words)
Use 4 short numbered steps next to or under the diagram, 2–3 sentences each:
1. **Air in:** The fan draws in room air across the evaporator coil. The refrigerant is colder than the air, so it absorbs heat. This is also why cooler, drier air comes out.
2. **Compress:** The compressor squeezes the refrigerant gas, which makes it much hotter.
3. **Transfer:** The hot refrigerant runs through the condenser coil around the tank and gives its heat to the water.
4. **Reset:** The refrigerant cools and loses pressure, then returns to the evaporator to repeat.

Add one line on the water side: cold water enters at the bottom, hot water is drawn from the top.

## 4. One analogy, used once
"Like a refrigerator, but the cold goes into the room and the heat goes into your water." Don't stack several analogies.

## 5. "What this means for your home" (the rest of your copy)
Homeowners care about this more than the physics. Use short headed sections or a small icon grid:
- **Efficiency and running cost**
- **Space and install needs:** it needs air volume around it, usually a garage, basement, or utility room
- **Noise:** roughly like a window AC or dehumidifier
- **Cool, dry air:** a bonus in a basement, a drawback in a small closet
- **Cold climates:** what happens in cold spaces, and the backup electric elements or hybrid mode

## 6. Short FAQ and call to action
- 3–5 questions, collapsed accordion style: "Does it work in winter?", "What if I lose power?", "How long does it last?"
- A clear CTA near the end, and a smaller one near the top: "Thinking about one? Get a quote from Tidewater Plumbing." Include a phone number or button.

## Writing and formatting tips
- Keep paragraphs to 2–3 lines, with a subhead every ~100 words.
- Define jargon on first use: evaporator, condenser, and refrigerant each get a plain-English parenthetical.
- Avoid "COP" and "UEF" in the main flow. If you want them, put them in a small "For the curious" box.
- Consider a short looping animation of the diagram (arrows pulsing, coil color shifting) if your site can handle it. A static diagram with numbered steps works fine, though.
