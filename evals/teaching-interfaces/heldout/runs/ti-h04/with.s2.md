# Page layout

**Takeaway the page serves:** after reading, a homeowner can say, "A heat pump water heater moves heat from the air around it into the water, instead of making heat. That's why it uses less electricity."

Every element should serve that sentence.

## Top to bottom

1. **Heading that states the takeaway.** For example: "A heat pump water heater moves heat into your water instead of making it." Follow it with one plain sentence of context, such as "It works like a refrigerator running in reverse: a fridge pulls heat out of its inside, and this pulls heat out of the air and puts it in your tank." Skip any mascot or hero photo.

2. **The diagram, in three stills (not one busy picture).** Your diagram has about eight parts and three flows, so name the parts first and then show the process. Use the same drawing three times, with the active parts at full strength and the rest greyed:
   - **Step 1, "Pick up heat from the air."** Highlight the fan, the evaporator coil, "Warm room air in" and "Cooler air out."
   - **Step 2, "Concentrate it."** Highlight the compressor. Say in one sentence that it squeezes the heat into a hotter form.
   - **Step 3, "Hand it to the water."** Highlight the condenser coil around the tank and "Heat moves into water."
   - Show the water path (cold in at the bottom, hot out at the top) from the start, in a different line style from the heat path. It isn't part of the heat steps, but it orients the reader.

   Put each step's sentence directly above or beside its still, with a visible "Next" control. Don't put it in a caption far below. Keep each step to roughly 40–70 words. That puts the core explanation at about 200 words, and the remaining ~400 go to the section below.

3. **"Questions homeowners ask."** Move the rest of your 600 words here as short collapsible answers, so people who want more can open them. Likely ones are "Where does the cool air go?", "Does it work in a cold garage or basement?", and "What happens if I use a lot of hot water at once?" Answer these only with facts from your own copy or the manufacturer. If the units you install have electric backup elements, say so here. I haven't assumed anything about your models.

4. **One call to action.** Use a single "Get a quote for a heat pump water heater" button with your phone number. Put it after the explanation, not before.

## Diagram fixes
- **Labels on the parts.** Put "Fan," "Evaporator coil," "Compressor," "Condenser coil," "Cold water in" and "Hot water out" on or touching each part. Don't use a lettered legend.
- **Arrow labels.** Write them out: "Warm room air in," "Cooler air out," "Heat moves into water."
- **Second channel for color.** Use a different line style for heat versus water, such as dashed versus solid. Don't rely on red versus blue alone. The diagram should still read in grayscale.
- **Mobile.** Stack the stills top to bottom and keep label text at about 12px or larger. If the labels get too small, split the image rather than shrinking the type.
- **Text alternative.** Add a short one that lists the parts in reading order. For example: "Air enters at the top through a fan and evaporator coil, a compressor raises the heat, and a condenser coil around the tank passes it to water that enters cold at the bottom and leaves hot at the top."
- **Stand-alone test.** Hide your paragraphs. If someone can't state the takeaway from the heading and labels alone, a label or arrow is missing.

## What to leave out
- No separate "how it works in 10 steps" list.
- No jargon section on refrigerant chemistry.
- No savings numbers unless you can cite where they come from. Use a placeholder like `[savings figure + source]` until you have one.

## Two notes
- I assumed your readers are homeowners with no technical background and that the page is mostly read on phones. If most visitors are on desktop, the three stills can sit side by side.
- Once the page is live, judge it by whether the questions you get on quote calls change. For example, you'd expect fewer "so does it make its own heat?" questions. Don't judge it by time on page.
