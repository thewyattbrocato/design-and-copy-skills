# Layout for the heat pump water heater explainer

**What readers should be able to do afterwards:** say in one sentence why this water heater uses less electricity than a regular electric one. It *moves* heat from the surrounding air into the water instead of *making* heat. Every element on the page should support that sentence.

## Page order

**1. Heading and takeaway (top)**
- Plain heading, such as "How a heat pump water heater works".
- Directly under it, one sentence: "It doesn't burn fuel or glow a heating element to make heat. It pulls heat out of the air around it and moves it into your water."
- Optionally add "Think of a refrigerator running in reverse," since most homeowners already know a fridge moves heat.

**2. The diagram, built up in 4 steps (the core of the page)**

Don't show all of it at once. Your diagram has about eight parts plus three arrow types, which is too much for a newcomer to take in. Use the same drawing four times, adding one thing each time. The new part is highlighted and the earlier parts are dimmed but still visible.

| Step | Adds | The one sentence beside it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tank, cold inlet (bottom), hot outlet (top) | The part you already know: cold water in, hot water out. |
| 2 | Fan and evaporator coil, with air-in and cool-air-out arrows | The fan pulls room air across a cold coil. The coil soaks up the air's heat, so the air leaves cooler. |
| 3 | Compressor | The compressor squeezes that captured heat into a much hotter, more concentrated form. |
| 4 | Condenser coil around the tank, with the heat-into-water arrow | The hot coil wraps the tank and hands its heat to the water. |

Rules for this section:
- **Put each label on its part.** Use a name touching the part or a short leader line. Don't use a lettered key or legend below the figure.
- **Put each step's sentence directly above or beside its picture.** On phones, stack the steps vertically with the sentence over its image, so no caption ends up far from what it describes.
- **Use stills, not a looping animation, as the default.** Readers can go at their own pace, and the steps print well. If you want motion for the refrigerant loop, make it a short, pausable add-on that respects reduced-motion settings.
- **Name the parts as they appear.** Introduce the names in the order of the steps, so readers meet "evaporator" at the moment it matters.
- **Show the finished diagram once at the end of step 4.** Don't repeat it in full anywhere else.

**3. Your 600 words, split up**
- Cut the text into the four step sentences plus a short expansion under each, about 60–100 words per step. Don't put one 600-word block beside or below the diagram.
- Anything that doesn't explain *how it works* goes in a separate section below, such as installation space, noise, or the cool air it puts out in its room. Short questions and answers work well there. Check that claims there match what you actually tell customers, since they're about your installs.

**4. "What this means for your home" (short)**
- Two or three bullets tying the mechanism to what a homeowner notices. Only include facts you're prepared to stand behind. Don't add efficiency or savings numbers unless you can source them.

**5. Call to action (last)**
- A plain "Ask Tidewater about a heat pump water heater" with your contact method. Put it after they understand, not at the top.

## Things to leave out
- No hero photo or stock image bigger than the diagram.
- No auto-playing video or background music.
- No jargon before it's defined, such as "refrigerant cycle" or "COP".
- If you make a video version, give it captions and a transcript, and don't put the full script on screen as well.

## How to check it worked
Show the page to three or four people who don't work in the trade. Ask them a few minutes later to explain in their own words where the heat comes from. If they say "the element heats it," step 2 or 3 needs work.

**Assumptions:**
- I assumed a standard single-tank, air-source unit as described. I haven't seen your diagram or text, so I didn't invent any labels or figures.
- If you send the 600 words, I can split them across the four steps and mark what to move to the lower section.
