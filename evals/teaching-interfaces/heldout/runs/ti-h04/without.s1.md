# Layout for the heat pump water heater page

**Core idea:** The diagram is the page. Build the text around it so each paragraph points at a part of the picture.

## Top to bottom

1. **Headline and one-line hook (about 40 words).**
   - Something like "It doesn't make heat. It moves it."
   - Add a familiar analogy: "It works like a refrigerator in reverse, pulling heat out of the air around it and putting it into your water."
   - Add one concrete payoff, such as "uses about a third of the electricity of a standard electric tank." Check that number against the manufacturer specs for the models you install.

2. **The diagram, large and right under the hook.**
   - Number the parts 1–4 directly on the image. Don't make people match a legend to the picture.
   - Use plain-language labels first, with the technical term in smaller type. For example: "Heat collector (evaporator coil)" and "Heat pump (compressor)."
   - Use color consistently: warm orange or red for heat and warm air, blue for cold water, and a cooler gray or teal for the cooled air leaving the unit.
   - Make the "heat moving into the water" arrow the most visually prominent one. It's the main point.

3. **"How it works" in 4 numbered steps (about 300 words).**
   Each step is a short heading, 2–3 sentences, and a number matching the diagram. On desktop, put the steps beside the diagram. On mobile, stack the diagram first and the steps below it.
   - **Step 1:** The fan pulls in room air, and the evaporator coil absorbs its heat.
   - **Step 2:** The compressor concentrates that heat and raises its temperature.
   - **Step 3:** The condenser coil wrapped around the tank passes the heat into the water.
   - **Step 4:** Cooled air leaves the unit, and hot water is drawn from the top while cold water enters at the bottom.

   Don't explain refrigerant physics beyond one sentence. Homeowners need "heat gets moved," not the thermodynamic cycle.

4. **"What this means in your home" (about 150 words).** This is where the explanation turns practical, and it's where a plumbing company earns trust. Cover:
   - It cools and dehumidifies the room it sits in, which is good in a hot garage and not so good in a small, cold closet.
   - It needs adequate airspace and clearance, and a condensate drain.
   - It makes some fan noise.
   - It has a backup electric mode for high-demand periods.

5. **Short FAQ or "Is this right for your home?" (about 100 words)**, followed by a single call to action such as "Get a free estimate from Tidewater Plumbing."

## Other tips

- **Keep paragraphs short and put the diagram first.** Many visitors will only look at the picture and the hook, so those two have to work on their own.
- **Write alt text that describes the flow**, not just "heat pump diagram." For example: "Air enters at the top, passes over a coil that absorbs heat, and exits cooler. A compressor sends that heat to a coil around the tank."
- **Consider a simple reveal** if your site supports it. The diagram could highlight one part at a time as each step is selected. A static numbered version is fine and more reliable on mobile.
- **Cut the 600 words rather than expand them.** If the draft runs long, move the extra detail into a collapsible "Want more detail?" section so the main flow stays clean.
