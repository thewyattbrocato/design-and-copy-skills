I'd lead with the diagram and a one-sentence explanation, then walk through the process step by step in the same order as the diagram. Homeowners mostly want to know three things: what it does, why it's efficient, and what it means for them.

**Suggested layout**

1. **Headline and one-line summary** (about 20 words). Something like: "A heat pump water heater pulls heat out of the air and moves it into your water." If you want a hook, add: "It works like a refrigerator running in reverse."

2. **The diagram, full width, right below the summary.** Number the components 1 to 6 in the order the heat travels, and label them with the same words your text uses. Add a short caption that says what the arrows mean. Don't rely on the arrows alone.

3. **"How it works," as five or six numbered steps** (about 250 words). Each step gets a bold lead-in and one or two sentences, and each matches a number on the diagram. Something like:
   - **Air in:** The fan pulls in warm room air over the evaporator coil.
   - **Heat absorbed:** Refrigerant in the evaporator coil picks up that heat and turns to gas.
   - **Compressed:** The compressor squeezes the gas, which raises its temperature.
   - **Heat released:** The hot gas flows through the condenser coil wrapped around the tank and transfers its heat to the water.
   - **Hot water rises:** Heated water collects at the top, where it leaves through the hot-water outlet. Cold water enters at the bottom to replace it.
   - **Cool air out:** The air leaving the unit is cooler than when it came in, because its heat went into the water.

   Keep "follow the water" and "follow the air" as two short parallel lists if you want to show both paths. People often find that clearer than one combined list.

4. **"Why it saves energy"** (about 80 words). One idea: it moves heat instead of making it, so it uses roughly a third to a half the electricity of a standard electric tank. Give a rough figure and say it depends on conditions. Keep this short so it doesn't overshadow the explanation.

5. **"What homeowners ask"** (about 150 words, as Q&A). This is where a plumbing company can add value. Likely questions:
   - Does it make noise?
   - Will it cool down my basement or utility room?
   - Does it work in a cold climate?
   - How much space does it need?
   - Does it need a special outlet or venting?

6. **Call to action** for Tidewater: a short line plus a phone number or booking button, placed after the answers.

**Other suggestions**

- Break the 600 words into short paragraphs with subheadings. Most readers scan before they read, so the headings should make sense on their own.
- On mobile, let the diagram stack above the steps and keep the numbers visible. Make the labels large enough to read on a phone.
- Write alt text that describes the process ("Warm room air is drawn over a refrigerant coil that absorbs heat, which is then moved into the tank water"), not just "heat pump water heater diagram."
- If the diagram's labels are small, consider a simplified version for the page with fewer labels, and link to the detailed one.
