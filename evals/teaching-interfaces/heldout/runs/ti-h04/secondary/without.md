I'd organize the page around the order heat moves, not the order of the parts. Homeowners tend to picture a heater as "something that makes heat," so the page should first show that this one moves heat from the air into the water.

**1. Hero: one plain sentence and the diagram (above the fold)**
Something like: "A heat pump water heater pulls heat from the air around it and moves that heat into your water, using about a third of the electricity of a standard electric tank." Put the diagram right below it.

**2. Diagram with numbered markers**
Add numbers 1 to 4 to the diagram, matching the steps below. Use the same component names in the diagram and the text (for example, "evaporator coil," not "cooling coil" in one place and "evaporator" in another). Write alt text that describes the loop in words, since screen readers and people on slow connections won't see the arrows.

**3. "How it works": four numbered steps (about 250 words)**
1. **Air in:** The fan pulls in room air and blows it across the evaporator coil.
2. **Heat absorbed:** A refrigerant in the coil is cold enough to pick up heat from that air. The air leaves cooler.
3. **Compressed:** The compressor squeezes the refrigerant, which raises its temperature.
4. **Heat delivered:** The hot refrigerant flows through the condenser coil wrapped around the tank and gives its heat to the water. The refrigerant cools, expands, and cycles back to step 1.

Keep each step to two or three sentences. Put the cold inlet and hot outlet in a short sentence after step 4 ("Cold water enters at the bottom, is heated, and leaves from the top"), since the tank layout is useful but not the core idea.

**4. "What you'll notice" (about 150 words)**
Answer the questions homeowners actually have before they call you:
- Cool, dehumidified air comes out of the unit, which can help in a basement or utility room in summer.
- It makes some noise from the fan and compressor, so location matters.
- It needs a minimum room size and airflow, so check the manufacturer's spec. Many models need a room of a certain volume to work well.
- It needs a condensate drain.
- Efficiency drops in very cold spaces, and many units switch to electric resistance backup when that happens.

**5. Efficiency callout (about 50 words)**
A short box with one comparison, such as "about 3 times the efficiency of a standard electric water heater." Use the manufacturer's rated number if you can cite one, and keep the source near the claim.

**6. FAQ (about 100 words)**
Three or four questions: Where can it go? Does it work in winter? Is it noisy? What does maintenance involve? These answer the objections that would otherwise send people to a competitor's page.

**7. Call to action**
Close with a short line and a button for Tidewater: "Not sure if a heat pump water heater fits your home? Call us for a free site check."

**Mobile:** Stack the diagram above the steps, and make sure the numbered markers still line up with the step numbers when the image shrinks.

**Two things to check before publishing:**
- Confirm the claims in the efficiency callout and the room-size requirement against the manufacturer's spec sheets for the models you install.
- Make sure the diagram's arrows match the step order. If the arrows show the refrigerant flow in a different direction than the text describes, readers will get confused.
