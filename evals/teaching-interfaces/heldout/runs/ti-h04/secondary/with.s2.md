**Recommended layout: one drawing, four steps, one sentence beside each part**

Your diagram has about eight parts plus three arrows. That is too many for a first read, so don't show it all at once. Use the same drawing four times. Each time, the parts for that step are full strength and the rest are dimmed, not removed, so readers keep the whole picture while they follow one piece.

**Page outline**

1. **Heading and one-sentence takeaway.** Something like: "A heat pump water heater heats your water by moving heat from the air into the tank." Nothing else competes with it above the fold.

2. **Start with what they already know.** Show only the tank, the cold-water inlet at the bottom and the hot-water outlet at the top. One sentence: "It's a normal tank. What's different is how it gets the water hot."

3. **Step 1: Air in, heat taken out.** Highlight the fan and the evaporator coil. Put the sentence right beside them: "A fan pulls in room air and passes it over a coil. The coil picks up heat from that air." Show the air-in arrow here.

4. **Step 2: Compressor.** Highlight the compressor. "A compressor squeezes the refrigerant, which makes it much hotter."

5. **Step 3: Heat into water.** Highlight the condenser coil wrapped around the tank. "The hot refrigerant gives its heat to the water through the tank wall." Show the heat-into-water arrow here.

6. **Step 4: Cool air out, loop repeats.** Highlight the cool-air-out arrow and the loop. "The refrigerant cools, goes back to the coil, and the cycle starts again."

7. **Short "what this means for you" section.** Two or three plain points, filled in from your own facts, for example where the unit sits in the home ([where your unit goes]), what it needs for air space, and what it means for electricity use. Don't add numbers you can't back up.

8. **One next step.** A single call to action, such as "Call Tidewater to check whether your home has room for one." Keep it out of the explanation itself.

**Why this works**

- Each part is labeled on or next to its drawing, not in a key below the figure. A legend makes readers hunt and memorize codes.
- Each step's sentence sits right above or beside its picture. On a phone, put the sentence above the picture, not far below it.
- Use still images with a visible highlight, not an animation. They work with reduced motion, screen readers and printing. If you want motion, add an optional short loop that has captions and a transcript, and keep the stills as the main path.
- Your 600 words split naturally into four segments of about 100 to 130 words. Keep the spoken or written sentence per step short, since the picture carries the rest.

**Two things to check before publishing**

- **The diagram omits the expansion valve.** Most heat pump loops include one between the condenser and the evaporator. Either add it, or state on the page that the drawing is simplified. Otherwise a technically minded reader may catch the gap and doubt the page.
- **The arrows need matching words.** "Air in," "cool air out" and "heat into water" should use the same terms as the sentences beside them, so readers connect them without translating.
