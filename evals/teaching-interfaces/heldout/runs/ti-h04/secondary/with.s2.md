## Layout for the Tidewater explainer

**Takeaway heading:** "A heat pump water heater moves heat from the air into your water."

Put the diagram directly under the heading, above the fold on desktop. Readers should be able to state the idea from the heading and labels alone.

### Page order

1. **Heading and one-sentence summary** (about 40 words)
   Say what it does and what it saves them, and leave out the mechanism. Example: "It heats your water with the warmth already in your home's air, using about [a third] of the electricity of a standard electric tank." Fill the bracket from manufacturer data only.

2. **Diagram with labels on the parts** (the hero)
   Labels go on the parts, not in a legend. Keep each under about 25 characters:
   - Fan: "Pulls in room air"
   - Evaporator coil: "Absorbs heat from air"
   - Compressor: "Raises refrigerant temperature"
   - Condenser coil: "Gives heat to water"
   - Tank: "Stores hot water"
   - Cold inlet: "Cold water in"
   - Hot outlet: "Hot water out"
   - Air arrows: "Air in" and "Cooler air out"

3. **The gap in the current diagram:** the refrigerant loop is missing. The arrows show air and heat, but nothing shows what carries heat from the evaporator, through the compressor, to the condenser. Add a line between those three parts, in a different style (for example dashed), labeled "Refrigerant loop." Without it, readers will not understand how the heat travels, which is the point of the page.

4. **One cycle, in four short steps** (about 200 words)
   Put each sentence directly beside or above the part it describes. On mobile, put each step's text above a cropped still of the diagram showing only that part lit up. Don't use a numbered legend on the picture.
   - Fan pulls room air across the evaporator.
   - Refrigerant in the evaporator picks up that heat and turns to gas.
   - Compressor squeezes the gas, which makes it much hotter.
   - Condenser coil wrapped around the tank releases the heat into the cold water.

   The cooled refrigerant then loops back, and the air leaves cooler.

5. **Where the water goes** (about 80 words)
   Cold water enters at the bottom and hot water leaves at the top. This is a separate question from how the heat moves, so give it its own short section and, if you want a picture, a small simple tank sketch. Don't add it to the main diagram.

6. **What it needs from the room** (about 100 words, placeholders where needed)
   This is the question homeowners will ask first. Cover airflow, the room temperature range, noise, and clearance. Use `[manufacturer clearance]` and similar placeholders for numbers you have not verified. Do not state them as facts.

7. **Close with one next step** (about 40 words)
   One call to action, such as "Ask us whether a heat pump fits your home," linking to a contact or estimate form.

### Writing notes

- The sentence to test: after reading, can a homeowner explain to a salesperson where the heat comes from and what the fan does? Cut anything that does not help with that.
- Trim the 600 words to about 450 by moving the water-path and room-needs content into short, scannable sections. Each section should answer one question.
- Alt text for the diagram should name the parts in order and the refrigerant loop, in two sentences at most. Put a fuller version as a list beside the figure.
- Check the labels at phone width. If they drop below about 12px, restack them or shorten them rather than shrinking the type.

Two assumptions to confirm: the page is a standard web page with no video or audio, and the diagram can be redrawn to add the refrigerant loop.
