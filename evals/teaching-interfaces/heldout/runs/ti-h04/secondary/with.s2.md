I'd lead with the diagram and walk through it in the order heat travels: **air → refrigerant → water**. Homeowners tend to get lost in the loop, so keep one main path.

**Suggested order**

1. **Headline and one-sentence summary** (about 40 words). Something like: "A heat pump water heater pulls heat out of the air and moves it into your water, using a fraction of the electricity a standard electric tank needs."
2. **The diagram, large, right below the summary.** Number the six parts (fan, evaporator, compressor, condenser, tank, inlet/outlet) and use the same numbers in the text. Use SVG or real HTML labels rather than a flat image with small text, so it stays readable on phones and screen readers. Add alt text that describes the flow.
3. **"How it works" as five numbered steps** (about 250 words, roughly 50 each):
   1. Fan pulls room air across the evaporator coil.
   2. Refrigerant in the evaporator absorbs that heat and turns to gas.
   3. The compressor squeezes the gas, which raises its temperature.
   4. The condenser coil wrapped around the tank releases that heat into the water.
   5. The refrigerant cools, becomes liquid again, and the cycle repeats. Cold water enters at the bottom and hot water leaves at the top.
4. **"Questions people actually ask"** (about 150 words). Use short headed answers, not a paragraph. Good candidates:
   - Does it cool the room? Yes, somewhat. Putting it in a utility room or basement makes this a feature.
   - Does it work in a cold garage? It works best above about 40°F. Below that, it may use the electric backup more.
   - Is it noisy? It's about as loud as a dehumidifier. Mention where it can be placed.
   - What if I run out of hot water? Many models have a backup heating element and a vacation or efficiency mode.
5. **Closing call to action** (about 50 words). Put "Book a free assessment" or similar here, after the teaching is done.

**Other tips**

- If you want an interactive touch, highlight the matching diagram part when a reader hovers or taps a step. That helps people connect words to parts.
- Keep the diagram's arrows consistent with the text. If the diagram shows "cool air out," the text should explain that this is a side effect, not the goal.
- Put any sizing or electrical details (kWh savings, tank sizes, climate limits) in a separate section or FAQ, so the core explanation stays short.
