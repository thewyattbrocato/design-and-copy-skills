**The one thing:** after reading, a homeowner can say, "It doesn't make heat. It moves heat from the air around it into my water, so it uses less electricity."

Everything on the page should serve that sentence.

## Layout, top to bottom

**1. Heading and one-line answer (above the fold)**
- Heading: "How a heat pump water heater works."
- Under it: "It pulls heat out of the air in the room and moves it into your water. It doesn't burn fuel or heat the water directly with a coil." Add the electricity-savings idea in plain words here too. Don't give a savings number unless you can source it.

**2. The diagram, built up in 3 stills, not shown all at once**
Your diagram has about eight parts. Newcomers can't absorb that in one view. Use the same drawing three times, adding one idea each time and marking what changed:

| Step | Shows | Label on the part |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Warm room air in | Fan and evaporator coil on top, with arrows for air in and cool air out | "Fan pulls in room air" / "Coil absorbs the air's heat" / "Cooler air leaves" |
| 2. Heat gets concentrated | Add the compressor | "Compressor boosts the heat to a higher temperature" |
| 3. Heat goes into the water | Add the condenser coil on the tank, the cold inlet and the hot outlet | "Coil wrapped around the tank releases heat into the water" / "Cold in" / "Hot out" |

Rules for the stills:
- **Put labels on the parts, not in a legend.** Don't use lettered or numbered keys. Use short leader lines if you need them.
- **Put each step's one or two sentences directly above or beside its still.** On mobile, stack them as caption, then picture, then the next caption. Don't put all the text below the whole figure.
- **Start with the tank, inlet and outlet alone.** Readers know those already. Then add the unfamiliar parts.
- **Keep the heat arrows one color throughout.** Heat moving should look like the same thing in all three stills.
- **Add real alt text to every still.** Describe the change, for example "Compressor added between the coil and the tank."

Then show the **full diagram once** as a recap, with all labels on it.

**3. A short analogy, one or two sentences**
Use something like "It's a refrigerator working in reverse. A fridge moves heat out of the box into your kitchen. This moves heat out of the room into your water." It helps because people already have a model for the fridge. Place it right after step 1 or in the intro, not in a separate section.

**4. Your 600 words, split to fit the picture**
Don't put them in one block. Sort them like this:
- **Process (~150–200 words)** becomes the captions on the three stills.
- **What it means for the homeowner (~150 words)** goes below the diagram, under plain headings. These are things like: it cools and dehumidifies the room slightly, it needs some open air space around it, and it's quieter or louder than people expect. Include only what your text already says.
- **Cut or move everything else.** If a paragraph doesn't help someone say the one sentence above, drop it or put it in an "If you want more detail" expandable section. Keep the compressor and refrigerant detail there, not in the main flow.

**5. Call to action at the end**
Something like "Wondering if one fits your home? Call Tidewater Plumbing for [a free estimate / your actual offer]." Keep it to one action with one phone number or button. Don't invent offers.

## What to avoid
- **Showing the full eight-part diagram first** with a legend. It's accurate, but people tend to skim it and learn little.
- **Stock photos or decorative heroes** that take more room than the diagram. The diagram should be the biggest element.
- **Animation as the only explanation.** If you add a short flow animation, make it pausable and let it respect reduced-motion settings. The stills should work without it.
- **Unexplained jargon.** If you say "evaporator" or "condenser," label it in plain words in the same spot. Use "coil that absorbs heat" or "coil that releases heat" next to the term.

## How to check it works
Ask 3–5 homeowners to read the page, then explain it back to you without looking. If they say "it moves heat from the air into the water," it's working. Check again a few days later to see whether they still remember it. Don't quote a retention figure until you've measured one.
