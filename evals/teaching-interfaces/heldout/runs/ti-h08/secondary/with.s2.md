You're right. A 40-step tour on day one will mostly be dismissed or forgotten before these users need the shortcuts. Experienced people skip it, and new users can't hold that many keys in memory.

**What to do instead**

1. **Show the shortcut where the action already is.** When someone uses the mouse to split the view or jump to a matter, show the key next to that control in the menu or tooltip. This teaches at the moment of use with no tour.

2. **Make a command palette the main way to discover shortcuts.** Lawyers already search for things. A searchable palette (for example, Cmd+K) that lists each action with its shortcut teaches the keys as a side effect of looking for something. It also works as the reference.

3. **Nudge from behavior, once.** If someone opens matters from the sidebar five times, show a single dismissible hint: "Cmd+[ jumps between matters." Cap it at one hint per shortcut and never repeat a dismissed one. No streaks, reminders, or badges.

4. **Teach a few on day one, tied to real work.** Pick two or three shortcuts for the tasks a new user does first, such as opening a matter or creating a note. Teach them inside that task, not as a separate lesson.

5. **Group the rest by job, and introduce them later.** Matter navigation, citation, and layout are separate clusters. Surface a cluster when a user first reaches that kind of work.

6. **Keep a one-page reference that is optional.** Put it in help and make it printable. Experts can pull it up when they want it.

**Check first:** Shortcuts in legal software often collide with Word, the document management system, or the OS. If your users have such conflicts, remapping and a conflict check matter more than any tutorial. I haven't seen your product, so this is an assumption.

**Measure the result, not the tour.** Track whether people use the keyboard for your top 10 actions in weeks 2 to 4, without a prompt. Tour completion rates don't show whether anyone learned anything. This gives the PM a concrete goal to agree to, instead of an argument about onboarding.
