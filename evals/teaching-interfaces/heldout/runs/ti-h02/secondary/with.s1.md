**Verdict:** The tour is an inventory of the product, shown before anyone has a reason to care. Step 1 asks for time and gives nothing back, so the 80% skip rate at that step is the expected result.

**Why people skip**

- **It asks for attention before offering value.** Step 1 is a promise of a tour, not a task. Nothing about it says what the person will be able to do afterwards.
- **Steps 2–4 describe controls and numbers they can already see.** Sidebar and top-bar labels are on screen, so reading them aloud adds nothing. Step 4 describes dashboard figures that a new account probably shows as empty or zero, so it teaches nothing either.
- **Everything is stacked over the dashboard.** Six clicks of "Next" with the real work blocked is a gate, and people learn that Skip is the way out.
- **The useful content comes last.** Creating an invoice (step 5) is the one thing that matters, and it's buried in a list of options.
- **Step 7 says "You're all set!" after nothing has happened.** There's nothing to be set up for yet.

**What I'd change**

1. **Remove the sequential tour.** Don't show all seven steps at once over the dashboard.
2. **Make the empty dashboard explain itself.** One line on what the area is for, one action, no dead controls. For example: "Revenue, overdue invoices and cash flow fill in after you send your first invoice." Button: "Create invoice" (with "N" shown as a hint).
3. **Turn step 5 into the first task.** Open the invoice form with the fields that matter to a first invoice: client, line items, due date. Show taxes, discounts and payment link when the person reaches for them, or as "Add tax" and similar optional rows. The sentence to hold to: "After this, they can send an invoice."
4. **Offer sample data as a starting point, if you have it.** Label it "Sample" and make it deletable in one action. Never show plausible numbers that look like the person's own.
5. **Move bank connection out of first run.** Ask for it at the moment it pays off: when an invoice has been sent and is waiting for payment, show "Match payments automatically: connect your bank" with a one-line reason.
6. **Replace step 7 with feedback on what happened.** After the first invoice is sent: "Sent to [client]. When they pay, it will show as paid here." That states what now works and points to the next step.
7. **Keep help reachable, not forced.** Put the help center behind a "?" icon, and let people reopen any tip from there.

If you keep any tour at all, limit it to three steps tied to the first successful invoice, with Skip visible and a way back from Help.

**How to measure it:** Don't track tour completion. Track whether people send an invoice within their first session, and whether they're still sending invoices a week later without help. Also check whether the skippers do that: if they do, the tour is just unused; if they don't, the first-task flow is the fix.

*Assumptions:* I assumed a new account's dashboard is empty and that you can change the first-run flow. *Trade-off:* a guided first task takes more build work than a tour, but it's the only version that gives people something to do before they decide whether to keep going.
