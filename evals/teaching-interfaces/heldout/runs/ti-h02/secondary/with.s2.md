**Why they skip:** The tour asks people to sit through seven screens of description before they have done anything. Steps 2 through 4 list the sidebar, top bar, and dashboard, which is an inventory of the product, not a reason to use it. Step 1 is a promise of a tour with no payoff yet, so "Skip" is the rational click. Skipping step 1 also skips everything after it, so the exit is the only thing the person can see.

**What I'd change:**

1. **Replace the tour with one guided task.** Its sentence: "After this, you can send an invoice to a client." Start with "Create your first invoice" as the primary action.
2. **Make step 5 the task, not a description of it.** Open the invoice form with a sample client and one sample line item, labeled as sample and easy to replace. The person edits the item, sets a due date, and sends or saves it. Taxes, discounts, and payment link can be labeled fields in the form, not a list in a tour. Show the `+` and `N` shortcut where the person is about to use it, not on a slide.
3. **Cut steps 1–4 and 6 from the first run.**
   - Sidebar and top bar: let people find these when they need them. Nine nav items on one screen is too many to remember.
   - Dashboard: when there is no data, don't show a cash-flow chart full of zeros. Show a short empty state that says what the area is for and offers the one action that starts it.
   - Bank connection: move it out of onboarding. After the first invoice is sent or paid, offer it in context: "Connect your bank to match payments automatically." It becomes useful once there are payments to match.
4. **End with what happened, not "You're all set!"** For example: "Invoice sent to [client]. When they pay through the link, it will show here as paid." Then one next step.
5. **Keep Skip, and make it reversible.** Add a "Show me around" or "Getting started" entry in the help center, so skipping doesn't mean losing the content forever.

**How to measure it:** Track the share of new users who create and send an invoice in their first session, and the share who create a second one unaided within a week. Tour completion is the wrong target.

**Notes:**
- I haven't seen your product, so the sample-data and empty-state details are assumptions. I also don't know whether Skip ends the tour permanently. Check that first, because it changes how much the Skip rate tells you.
- Before redesigning, check whether the 80% skip happens before anyone reads step 1 or after. If it's immediate, the problem is that the screen offers nothing, not that the copy is too long.
