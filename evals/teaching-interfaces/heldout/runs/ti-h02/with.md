**Verdict:** The tour is a control inventory shown before the person has done anything. Skipping it is a reasonable response.

**Why 80% skip on step 1**
- **Step 1 offers nothing.** "Let's take a quick tour" promises work with no payoff. Skip is the cheapest way out, so people take it.
- **The tour covers the whole product.** Steps 2–4 list about 20 labels (nine sidebar items, five top-bar items, five dashboard widgets). Nobody can hold that, and the labels are already visible on screen.
- **It blocks the dashboard.** People came to do something, and an overlay on top of the dashboard stands between them and it.
- **The one useful step comes too late.** Creating an invoice is step 5, after three steps of orientation. Step 6 (bank connect) is a setup task that doesn't belong in a tour, and step 7 is just ceremony.
- **People can't tell what they'd get.** Seven steps with no stated outcome gives no reason to continue.
- **Skipping at step 1 may be a cheap signal.** It doesn't prove the later steps are bad, only that step 1 doesn't earn attention. Checking where the remaining 20% drop off would help.

**What I'd change**
1. **Name the one outcome:** "Afterwards they can send their first invoice." Everything else is cut or deferred.
2. **Replace the tour with a guided first task.** Cut steps 1–4 and 7. Open on the dashboard with a single prompt beside the + button: "Create your first invoice." Then go into the real invoice form.
3. **Give them a starting point.** Offer a clearly labeled sample client or a partly filled invoice, replaceable in one action, so they aren't facing a blank form. Don't show sample numbers that could pass for their real revenue.
4. **Teach only a few things in the form:** client, line items, due date and send. Taxes, discounts and the payment link stay as normal fields that need no teaching. If people struggle with any of them, fix the field label or default rather than adding a tooltip.
5. **Confirm in their terms after sending.** For example: "Invoice #1 sent to [client]. You'll see it under Outstanding on your dashboard." Then point to the next step.
6. **Move bank connect to when it matters.** Show it as a prompt on the first paid or outstanding invoice: "Connect your bank to match payments automatically." The path is Settings > Banking, as you wrote it.
7. **Keep the N shortcut as a small hint in the + button's tooltip.** Don't make it a tour step.
8. **Keep skip and a way back.** Skip stays visible and puts a "Take the guided invoice" item in the help center. Experienced freelancers moving from another invoicing tool shouldn't be forced through it.
9. **Leave out the sidebar and top bar.** If one label is confusing, rename it. Empty sections like Clients or Expenses should explain themselves with a purpose line and one action when first opened.

**How to judge it:** Don't use tour completion. Compare the share of new users who send an invoice within their first session, and the share who create a second invoice unaided a week later, against today's cohort.

*Assumptions: the + button opens an invoice form and the product can supply sample data. I haven't seen your UI or the funnel past step 1.*
