What your starter does after you feed it

A starter is a jar of flour and water full of wild yeast and bacteria. When you feed it, it eats, bubbles and grows, then runs out of food and settles. Learn to spot the top of that rise, and you’ll know when to mix your dough.

Why timing matters

At the peak, the yeast is at its most active and plentiful, so it lifts your dough reliably. Before it, the starter is still weak. After it, the yeast is hungry and the acid has built up, so dough tends to rise slowly and taste sharper.

How to tell where you are

  • Mark it. Put a rubber band on the jar at the starter’s level right after feeding.
  • Rising: level is above the band, top still flat or slightly curved.
  • Peak: domed top, lots of bubbles, level about as high as it will go.
  • Collapse: dome flattens or dips and the level falls back toward the band.

Warmer rooms speed all of this up and cool rooms slow it down, so watch the jar more than the clock. The first time, check every hour or so and note when yours peaks. That time is your personal schedule for mixing.
