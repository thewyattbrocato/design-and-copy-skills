Rye & Ember · Sourdough basics

A starter's day, from feeding to collapse

A starter is flour and water full of living yeast and bacteria. After you feed it, it goes through one rise-and-fall cycle. After this page you can tell where your starter is in that cycle, and when to mix your dough.

Why the timing matters when you mix

  • Too early (rising): not enough gas-making power yet, so the dough rises slowly.
  • At the peak: the most active starter gives the most reliable rise and a milder flavor.
  • Too late (collapse): it's weaker and more sour, so the dough can be slow and slack.

Work backwards. Watch your own jar for a couple of cycles and note how many hours after feeding it peaks, then feed that long before you want to mix. Example only: if yours peaks about 6 hours after feeding and you want to mix at 2 pm, feed at 8 am.

Missed the peak? Feed it again and mix at the next one.

Hours are a rough guide for a warm kitchen. A cooler room, or more flour and water relative to the starter, slows everything down. Trust your jar over the clock.
