Rye & Ember

Your starter rises and falls once a day. Mix your dough at the top.

After you feed a sourdough starter, it goes through four stages. Once you know them, you can pick the best moment to start your bread.

1. Feed

Time zero

You stir in fresh flour and water. The yeast and bacteria now have plenty to eat. It looks flat and a bit thick.

2. Rising

The first few hours

They eat and give off gas, so bubbles form and the starter swells. It smells mild and yeasty.

3. Peak

Often about 4–8 hours in

The highest point. The top is domed and bubbly all the way through. It has its strongest lift and mildest smell.

4. Collapse

After the peak

Food runs low and gas escapes faster than it's made. The top flattens or dips and leaves a residue on the glass. The smell turns sharper. It's still alive, only hungry.

Mix your dough at the peak, or a little before it

The starter is at its strongest, so the dough rises reliably. If you wait too long, you're mixing with a hungry starter, which tends to give a slower, more sour dough.

Find your own timing this week

Put a rubber band on the jar at the starter's level (the dashed line above). Feed it and note the time. Check once an hour. When the dome is highest, note how many hours it took. Next time, feed that many hours before you want to mix.

Hours are rough and vary from kitchen to kitchen. A warm room (about 75°F / 24°C) speeds the cycle up, and a cool room slows it down. How much you feed it matters too: more fresh flour and water per bit of starter means a slower rise. Missed the peak? Feed again and the cycle restarts.
