A starter’s day: rise, peak, collapse

A sourdough starter is a jar of flour, water, and wild yeast. After you feed it, the yeast eat the fresh flour and release gas, so the jar swells, tops out, then sinks. Mix your dough at the right moment on that curve and the bread rises well. Mix at the wrong moment and it doesn’t.

  1. 1 · Fed

    Hour 0–1

    You stir in fresh flour and water. It’s smooth and flat, with few bubbles. Put a rubber band at the starter’s level.

  2. 2 · Rising

    Hours 1–5

    Bubbles form and the jar fills. The yeast are busy and the smell is sweet and yeasty.

  3. 3 · Peak

    Around hour 5–7

    About double or more, with a domed top and bubbles throughout. This is the strongest it gets.

  4. 4 · Collapse

    Hour 7 onward

    The food runs low, so the dome sinks and leaves streaks on the glass. It smells sharp and sour. It’s hungry, not dead.

Why the timing matters

Mix near the peak, a little before or right at it. The yeast are at full strength, so your dough rises reliably and tastes balanced.

Too early (still flat) and the yeast are weak, so the loaf is dense and slow. Too late (collapsed) and there’s less lift and a sourer, sometimes gummy crumb. A collapsed starter isn’t ruined. Feed it again and wait for the next peak.

Timings assume a warm kitchen (about 75°F / 24°C) and a feed of 1 part starter, 2 parts flour, 2 parts water by weight. A cooler room slows everything down, and a warmer one speeds it up, so go by the look of the jar and not the clock.
