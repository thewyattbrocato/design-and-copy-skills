What your starter does after you feed it

A starter is flour and water full of wild yeast. After a feeding it rises, peaks, then falls. After reading this you can tell when it's ready to mix into dough.

  • Feed

    Start of the clock Mix in fresh flour and water. The yeast has new food, but it's spread thin, so little happens yet.

  • Rising

    Hours 1 to the peak Yeast eats the flour and makes gas. Bubbles fill the starter and push it up the jar.

  • Peak

    Highest point Full of bubbles with a domed top. The yeast is at its strongest, so this is when to mix.

  • Collapse

    After the peak Food runs low and the gas escapes. The top flattens, then dips. It still lives, but it's weaker and more sour.

Why timing matters: a starter mixed in near its peak has the most lift and a mild flavor, so your dough rises well. Mix it too early and it's too weak to lift the loaf. Mix it after the collapse and you get a sour, sluggish dough. If it has already collapsed, feed it again and wait for the next peak.

To watch it: put a rubber band around the jar at the starter's level right after feeding. When it has climbed well above the band and the top is domed, it's close to ready. Don't go by the clock alone. Warm kitchens speed this cycle up and cool ones slow it down, and so does a bigger feed. Many home starters peak somewhere around 4 to 8 hours after feeding, so learn your own by checking your jar.
