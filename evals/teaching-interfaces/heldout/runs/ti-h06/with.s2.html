Rye & Ember · Sourdough basics

Your starter rises, peaks, then falls. Mix when it's near the top.

A starter is a jar of flour and water full of wild yeast. After you feed it, it goes through one cycle. Afterwards you'll be able to look at your jar and tell whether it's ready for dough.

1 Fed

You stir in fresh flour and water. Yeast and bacteria now have food.
Looks like: flat, dense, no bubbles.

2 Rising

They eat and make gas, which pushes the starter up.
Looks like: bubbles through the jar, growing height.

3 Peak

Most active and strongest, with gas still trapped.
Looks like: highest point, domed top.

4 Collapse

Food is used up, gas escapes, the structure sags.
Looks like: dip or flat top, sour smell.

Too early (still at stage 1–2)

Not enough active yeast yet, so dough rises slowly and can turn out dense.

Too late (stage 4)

The starter is spent and acidic, so dough can be slack, sticky and sour, and rises poorly.

Try it tonight: after feeding, put a rubber band around the jar at the starter's level. When it has grown well past the band and the top is domed, it's near peak. That's when to mix.
