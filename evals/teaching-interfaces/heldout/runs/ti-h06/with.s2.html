A starter's day, from feeding to collapse

A starter is a jar of flour and water full of wild yeast and bacteria. After you feed it, it goes through one predictable wave. Once you can read that wave, you'll know when it's ready to mix into dough.

  • 1. Feed

    Stir in fresh flour and water. This is the starter's meal and the start of the clock.

  • 2. Rising

    Bubbles form and the level climbs. Slow at first, then faster.

  • 3. Peak

    As high as it will go, with a domed, bubbly top. Strongest and sweetest-smelling.

  • 4. Collapse

    The dome sinks and the smell turns sharper. It's hungry again.

Why timing matters for your dough: your starter is what makes the loaf rise. At the peak it holds the most gas and the most active yeast, so the dough puffs up well. After it collapses, it's out of food and weaker, so the dough tends to rise slowly and taste sharper.

Try this: right after feeding, put a rubber band around the jar at the starter's level. When it has grown well past the band and the top is domed and bubbly, mix your dough. If it's already sinking, give it a fresh feed and wait for the next peak.

The shape of the wave is the same every time. How long it takes depends on your kitchen's temperature, your flour, and how much starter you feed. Watch the jar, not the clock.
