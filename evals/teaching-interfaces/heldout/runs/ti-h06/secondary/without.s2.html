Rye & Ember

How a sourdough starter moves through its day

After you feed a starter, it goes through a predictable cycle: it rises, peaks, and then falls. Mixing your dough at the right point in that cycle gives you the most lift and the best flavor.

Starter cycle diagram

Times are rough. A warmer kitchen speeds the cycle up, and a cooler one slows it down. Watch the starter rather than the clock.

  1. 1. Feed

    Add fresh flour and water to the starter. This gives the yeast and bacteria new food and resets the cycle.

  2. 2. Rise

    The microbes multiply and produce gas. Bubbles form, and the starter grows and domes.

  3. 3. Peak

    The starter is at its airiest, usually close to double its size, with a domed top. This is the best time to mix dough.

  4. 4. Collapse

    The top flattens or sinks, and acid keeps building. Dough mixed now rises less and tastes sourer.

Why the timing matters

Mix your dough when the starter is at or just before its peak. If you mix too early, the starter hasn't built enough lift yet. If you mix after it collapses, the gluten has weakened and the flavor is more acidic. The peak is when you get the most rise with the cleanest flavor.
