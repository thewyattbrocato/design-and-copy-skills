The starter cycle after a feed

After reading this, you'll be able to tell when your starter is ready to mix into dough: at its peak, or just before it starts to fall.

  1. 1Feed

    Fresh flour and water go in. The starter is at its lowest point, and the yeast and bacteria start working on the new food.

  2. 2Rise

    Bubbles form and the starter grows steadily. It gets airier and more domed on top.

  3. 3Peak

    The starter is at its highest, often domed and full of bubbles. This is the best time to mix it into dough.

  4. 4Collapse

    The top flattens and may dimple or wrinkle as the structure weakens. The starter is past its best and more acidic.

Why the timing matters

Dough leavened with a starter at peak has the most lift and the cleanest sour flavor. Mix it too early and the yeast hasn't built enough strength, so the loaf is dense. Mix it after collapse and the gluten is weakened and the flavor turns sharp.

Plan your bake backwards from the peak. If the starter peaks at 6 hours, that's when you mix.

Times vary. A warm kitchen speeds the cycle up and a cool one slows it down, and a stronger or wetter starter behaves differently too. Judge by what you see (domed top, bubbles throughout, then flattening), not by the clock.
