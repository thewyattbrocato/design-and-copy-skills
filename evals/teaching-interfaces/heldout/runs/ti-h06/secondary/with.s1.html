The starter cycle after feeding

Feed your starter and it climbs, tops out, then sinks. Mix your dough while it is near the top.

  1. 1

    Feed

    Add fresh flour and water to the starter. The yeast and bacteria get new food.

  2. 2

    Rise

    Bubbles form and the volume grows as yeast releases gas. Acid builds slowly.

  3. 3

    Peak

    The starter is at its highest, often roughly doubled, with a domed, bubbly top.

  4. 4

    Collapse

    The top sinks and may show dark liquid. Food runs low, and acid weakens the gluten.

Why timing matters for your dough

  • Mix at or just before the peak: there is plenty of active yeast for a lively rise.
  • Mix too early: the starter is still building, so the rise is slow.
  • Mix after the collapse: leavening weakens, the loaf comes out flatter, and the flavor turns more sour.

Tip: put a rubber band around the jar at the level right after feeding. Watch the starter climb past it instead of watching the clock.
