Starter Basics

The Sourdough Starter Cycle

Feed your starter and it goes through four stages: rise, peak, and collapse. Knowing where it is in that cycle tells you when to mix your dough.

  1. Feed 0 h

    Discard most of the starter, then add fresh flour and water. Equal weights of starter, flour, and water is a common start.

  2. Rise About 1 to 5 h

    Yeast and bacteria multiply and make gas. Bubbles form and the surface starts to dome.

  3. Peak About 5 to 7 h

    Gas production is at its highest. The starter is domed, bubbly, and roughly 2 to 2.5 times its fed volume.

  4. Collapse After peak

    Gas escapes faster than it forms, the gluten weakens, and acid builds up. The top sinks and may show a grayish liquid.

Why the timing matters for mixing

Mix your dough when the starter is at peak, or just before it. That is when it has the most lift. If you wait until it has collapsed, the dough rises more slowly, comes out more sour, and can turn slack.

Use the signs, not just the clock. In a warm kitchen, expect a peak in about 4 to 6 hours. In a cool one it can take 8 to 12 hours or more. Look for a domed top, bubbles throughout, and roughly doubled volume.
