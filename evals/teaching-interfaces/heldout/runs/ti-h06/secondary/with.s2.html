How a starter rises and falls after you feed it

Feed your starter and it goes through four stages. Knowing where it is in that cycle tells you when to mix your dough.

Mix at peak, or just before

The starter has the most lift, so the dough rises well and keeps a mild, balanced flavour.

Mix after it collapses

Less lift and more acid. The dough can feel slack, rise slowly, and taste sharper than you planned.

Watch the starter, not only the clock. Warm kitchens move faster and cool ones slower. Look for a domed top, bubbles across the surface, and a jiggly jar. Note the hour it peaks the first few times and use that as your mixing window.

Times are rough guides for a typical room temperature and will vary with your kitchen and your starter.
