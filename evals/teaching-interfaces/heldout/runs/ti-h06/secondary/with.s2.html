How your starter rises, peaks, and falls

Feed your starter and it goes through a predictable cycle. Knowing where it is in that cycle tells you when to mix your dough.

  1. 1. Feed

    Hour 0

    You add fresh flour and water. The starter is at its baseline level and the yeast and bacteria have plenty of food.

  2. 2. Rise

    About hours 1 to 3

    Microbes multiply and produce gas. Bubbles form and the starter swells steadily.

  3. 3. Peak

    About hours 4 to 5

    The starter is at its highest, with a domed top and a web of bubbles. Its leavening power is at its strongest.

  4. 4. Collapse

    Hour 6 onward

    The top sinks and may look slack or watery. Food runs low and acid builds up, which weakens the gluten.

Why timing matters for mixing dough

Mix your dough when the starter is at or just before its peak. At that point it lifts the dough most reliably. Mixing too early means a sluggish rise. Mixing after collapse means weaker gluten, a slack dough, and often a sour, flat loaf.

Rough guide only. Watch the starter, not the clock: look for a domed top, bubbles throughout, and a jar that has roughly doubled or tripled.
