The starter cycle, from feeding to collapse

After you feed a sourdough starter, it swells, peaks, and falls again. Knowing where it is in that cycle tells you when to mix your dough.

Illustrative curve. Real timing depends on your starter, flour, and room temperature.

1. Feed

Time 0

Fresh flour and water go in. The starter is dense, smooth, and barely bubbly. This resets the cycle.

2. Rise

About 0 to 4 hours

Yeast and bacteria multiply and make gas. Bubbles form throughout, and the starter grows in volume.

3. Peak

About 4 to 6 hours

The starter has roughly doubled, with a domed top and large bubbles. This is when most recipes call for mixing.

4. Collapse

After peak

The gas escapes faster than it forms, so the top sinks and flattens. The starter turns more acidic, and its leavening weakens.

Why the timing matters

Mix at or just before peak for the most lift and a predictable bulk rise.

Mix too early and the dough has less lift, so bulk fermentation runs long.

Mix after collapse and the loaf rises less and tastes more sour.

Tip: put a rubber band around the jar at the starter's level right after feeding. Mix when it has about doubled and domed. In a cool kitchen this can take 10 hours or more.
