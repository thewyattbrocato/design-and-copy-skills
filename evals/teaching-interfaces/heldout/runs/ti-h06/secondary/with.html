How your starter cycles, and when to mix

After you feed a starter, it goes through a predictable rise and fall. Mix your dough near the top of that curve, not after it has fallen.

The cycle after one feeding

1. Feed Fresh flour and water go in. The starter is dense and mostly flat.
2. Rise Yeast wakes up and makes gas. Bubbles appear and the level climbs.
3. Peak Highest point. The top is domed and full of bubbles. This is the window to mix.
4. Collapse Gas escapes, the top sinks, and the starter turns more sour with less lift.

Mix when the starter is at or just before its peak.

At peak, the yeast is most active and the starter has the most lift to give your dough. After collapse, the yeast has used much of its food, acidity has built up, and the dough will rise more slowly and may taste sharper.

Timing depends on kitchen warmth: a warm room moves through the cycle faster, a cool one slower. Watch the jar, not the clock.
