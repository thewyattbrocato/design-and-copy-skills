What your starter does after a feed

Feed your starter and it goes through a predictable arc: it wakes up, rises, peaks, then slowly falls. Knowing where it is on that arc tells you when to mix your dough.

  1. 1. Feed

    Hour 0

    Fresh flour and water go in. Yeast and bacteria start eating again and gas begins to form.

  2. 2. Rise

    About hours 1–5

    Bubbles multiply and the starter swells. The surface gets domed and airy.

  3. 3. Peak

    About hours 5–7

    Roughly doubled or more, domed, full of bubbles. Gluten is strong and yeast is most active.

  4. 4. Collapse

    After about hour 7

    The top flattens and sinks, and liquid may pool on top. Acid has built up and the yeast is running low on food.

Why the timing matters for mixing

Mix at or just before the peak. That is when the starter has the most lift and the gluten in your dough is strongest, so the loaf rises well and holds its shape.

Mixing too early means weak leavening: a slow, dense bulk and a tight crumb. Mixing after collapse means the dough is acidic and slack: it turns sticky, spreads, and bakes flat with a sharper sour taste.

Warmer kitchens run faster and cooler ones slower, and a bigger feed stretches the timeline. Watch the starter, not the clock: look for a domed top and bubbles all the way through, and mix when it is there.
