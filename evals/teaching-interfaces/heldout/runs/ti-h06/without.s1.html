A Sourdough Starter’s Day, Hour by Hour

A starter is a jar of flour, water and wild yeast. After you feed it, it rises, tops out, and falls, like a slow tide. Knowing where it is in that cycle tells you the best moment to mix your dough.

1 · Fed

Hour 0

You stir in fresh flour and water. The yeast and bacteria suddenly have lots to eat. Mark the jar’s level with a rubber band.

2 · Rising

Hours ~1–5

Bubbles build and push the starter up. It smells mild and sweet, a bit like yogurt or fruit.

3 · Peak

Hours ~5–7

Highest and most active: doubled or tripled, domed, full of bubbles. It’s gentle, tangy and strong.

4 · Collapse

Hours ~7+

The food runs low and the bubbles pop. The dome flattens, the smell turns sharp, and the yeast is tired.

Why the timing matters

The starter is what makes your dough rise, so you want it at full strength.

  • Too early (still rising): not enough yeast yet. The dough rises slowly and can bake up dense.
  • At or just before the peak: yeast is at its most active and the flavor is balanced. Dough rises on schedule.
  • Too late (collapsed): the yeast is spent and the acid is high. Dough comes out sour, slack and sluggish. It can still work, but feed it again and wait for the next peak if you can.

Warm kitchens speed everything up, and cool ones slow it down. Don’t trust the clock alone. Watch the rubber band: when the level has doubled or more, the top is domed and it’s full of bubbles, it’s ready.
