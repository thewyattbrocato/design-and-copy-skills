**Fix these four: #2, #4, #9 and #12.**

1. **#2, error shown after the door opens.** Two of six people left without their groceries until staff stepped in. It's a complete failure, and the cause is a misleading screen, not user error. Wrong feedback at the moment of success is also likely to repeat at every store.
2. **#4, separate chilled locker.** Three of six missed it, and two went home without their frozen items. That means lost or spoiled food and likely refunds or complaints, not just inconvenience.
3. **#9, order code vs. PIN.** Four of six hit it, and two did it twice. It's the most common real error in the log, and it happens at the start, so it affects everyone. Clearer labels, or rejecting the wrong type of code with a specific message, should help.
4. **#12, top row unreachable for the wheelchair user.** This is only one of six, but it's a total barrier for a whole group of shoppers, not a random hiccup. They couldn't complete the task without staff, and an unstaffed pickup point is meant to work without staff. Rolling this out to 30 stores also carries accessibility and legal risk. A fix could be as simple as assigning lower lockers to anyone who asks.

**Why not the others**
- **#1, finding the scanner (5 of 6).** This is the closest call and the first runner-up. Everyone found it, so the cost is about 40 seconds, not a failure. It's likely a cheap fix, such as an arrow or label, so do it if there's room. It also sits on the same step as #9, so one redesign of step 1 could cover both.
- **#5, hidden language icon.** Only one person hit it, but they gave up. It's a strong runner-up if language coverage matters in your markets.
- **#8, timeout (2 of 6).** It's recoverable, but it's at the same step as #4. Extending the timeout while you fix #4 would be cheap.
- **#6 and #3.** People hesitated or complained, but nobody failed.
- **#7, #10, #14.** These are preferences or feature requests, not usability problems.
- **#11 and #13.** Log these as follow-ups. Glare is a hardware or placement issue. A four-minute help response is an operations problem, and it could make #2 worse.

**Caveats**
- With six sessions, the counts are rough. I ranked mainly on severity (did anyone fail or leave without their items?), then on frequency.
- I don't know the engineering cost of each fix. If #12 turns out to be a hardware change, not a software one, swap in #1 and treat #12 as a separate accessibility item.
