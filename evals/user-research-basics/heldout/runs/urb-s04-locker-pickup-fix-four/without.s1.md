**Fix these four: #2, #4, #9 and #1.**

1. **#2, the error shown after the door opened.** Both people who hit it left without their groceries until staff stepped in. The locker worked, but the screen told them it hadn't. At 30 stores, this would mean staff intervention and abandoned orders every time it happens. The fix is likely to be small: don't show an error after a successful open, or make the message say "your door is open".

2. **#4, the separate chilled locker.** Two of the 6 went home without their frozen items. That's lost product, a refund or redelivery, and a food-safety problem. Show a clear prompt at the step where they collect their bags, and don't let them finish while chilled items are uncollected.

3. **#9, confusing the order code with the PIN.** It hit 4 of 6, and two people got it wrong twice. It's a real error rate on the main path, and it will cost time and staff help at scale. Better labels, separate screens, or a clear example of each code should fix it.

4. **#1, finding where to scan the order code.** It hit 5 of 6 on the first step, and each person lost about 40 seconds. Everyone got through it, so it's less severe than the three above. But nearly every shopper sees it, and it sets the tone for the whole visit. #1 and #9 are both on step 1, so one redesign of that screen can cover both.

**What I left out**
- **#12, the wheelchair user who couldn't reach the top row.** It only hit 1 of 6, but it's a serious accessibility problem, and the user needed staff. If the fix is cheap, I'd do it anyway. For example, assign lower-row lockers to anyone who needs them. It may also be a physical problem rather than software, so it's worth checking with ops.
- **#5, the hidden language choice.** One person gave up and went to the counter. It's worth doing next, especially if the stores serve multilingual communities.
- **#8, the timeout, and #6, the double-charge worry.** Both are annoyances. Nobody failed because of either. A text fix on the finish button ("You won't be charged again") is cheap.
- **#3, #7, #10, #11, #13 and #14.** These are low severity, preferences, or feature requests. The exception is #13, the help button that took four minutes. That's an operations problem, and it makes #2 worse, since staff are the fallback.

With only 6 sessions, the counts are rough. I ranked mainly by what happened to people, meaning who failed or left without their items, and used frequency to break ties.
