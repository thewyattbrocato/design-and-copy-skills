**Fix these four: #2, #4, #9 and #1.**

**1. #2, error shown after the door opened (2 of 6, step 3).**
- Both people left without their bags.
- This is a failed task, and it's cheap to fix: show success once the door opens.
- Customers who leave their groceries behind will complain and ask for refunds.

**2. #4, separate chilled locker missed (3 of 6, step 4).**
- Two people went home without their frozen items.
- That is lost food and money, and it happens at the end of the flow.
- A clear on-screen prompt, or a "1 more locker to open" checklist, should fix it.

**3. #9, order code confused with PIN (4 of 6, step 1).**
- It's frequent, and two people made the error twice.
- It sits at the start of the flow, so it hurts the first impression.
- Better labels and an inline hint when the wrong format is typed should fix it.

**4. #1, can't find where to scan (5 of 6, step 1).**
- It's the most common problem, and it hits nearly everyone at the first step.
- Everyone recovered, so it's less severe than the others. I include it because it's probably a cheap fix (signage and a visual cue on the screen) that affects almost every shopper in all 30 stores.

**Close calls**
- **#12 (wheelchair user couldn't reach the top row)** was the hardest to leave out. It's a task failure and an access issue, even at 1 of 6. It may not be an engineering fix, though. Assigning lower lockers first, or letting people request an accessible locker, could be handled by ops. I'd still treat it as a must-resolve before rollout.
- **#5 (hidden language icon)** led to someone giving up, but only 1 of 6 hit it. It's a good next fix.
- **#8 (timeout) and #6 (fear of double charge)** are real frictions, but people recovered. A reassuring line like "You won't be charged again" is a trivial copy change you can slip in.
- **#3, #7, #10, #11, #14** are preferences or lower-impact issues. **#13 (four-minute help wait)** is an ops problem rather than an engineering one.

One caution: with only 6 sessions, "1 of 6" doesn't mean rare. Across 30 stores, the single-person failures (#5, #12) will recur, so don't drop them.
