**My pick: #2, #4, #9, #1** (plus #12 if you can stretch).

**Why these**

- **#2 – Error shown after the door opens (2/6, step 3).** Both affected shoppers abandoned their bags. That's a total task failure, and it makes the service look broken. It's probably a status/messaging fix, so it's cheap relative to the harm.
- **#4 – Separate chilled locker not noticed (3/6, step 4).** Half the sample, and two went home without their frozen items. That means spoiled food, refunds and complaints, so the cost goes beyond usability. Fix it with a clear prompt at the relevant step, such as "You also have items in locker X".
- **#9 – Order code confused with PIN (4/6, step 1).** It's the second most common problem, and two people made the error twice. Nobody failed outright, but it's repeated friction at the entry point. Labeling the two fields distinctly should fix it.
- **#1 – Can't find the scanner (5/6, step 1).** It's the most common problem, and it cost about 40 seconds for everyone. All of them found it, so the harm is lower than the three above. It still hits nearly everyone at the very first step, and a sign or highlight is a quick fix. It also fits with #9, since both are step 1 and could be fixed together.

**What I left out, and why**

- **#12 (wheelchair user couldn't reach the top row):** This is a severe failure, because it's an access barrier that needed staff. It's 1 of 6, but accessibility problems shouldn't be judged by frequency. The fix may be hardware or a layout change, though, so it may not fit in the engineering window. I'd flag it as a separate track. A software workaround is to assign lower lockers to people who indicate they need them. If engineering has any spare capacity, I'd swap it in for #1.
- **#5 (hidden language option):** The one person affected gave up. The fix is cheap, but it's only 1 of 6. It's the next candidate if you have room.
- **#8 (timeout while unpacking):** 2 of 6, with a rescan as the cost. It's annoying but recoverable.
- **#6 (worry about a double charge):** 3 of 6, but all finished. A short reassurance line would help, and it's cheap copy.
- **#3, #7, #10, #11, #14:** Nobody failed, or these are preferences or feature requests. They can wait.
- **#13 (four-minute help wait):** Operations rather than engineering. It matters because it's the safety net for #2, #4 and #12, so raise it with store ops.

**Caveat:** With 6 sessions, the counts are rough. I ranked mainly by severity (did people fail or leave without their items), then by frequency. The two groups affected by #2 and #4 might overlap, so check that against your logs.
