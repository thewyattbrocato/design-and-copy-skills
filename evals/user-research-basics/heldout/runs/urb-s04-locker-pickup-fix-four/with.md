**The four to fix: #2, #4, #9 and #1.**

1. **#2: error shown after the locker opened (2 of 6, step 3).** Both people walked away without their bags until staff helped. The locker worked and the screen told them it hadn't, so the screen caused the failure. It will recur at every store, and the fix is probably a message change.

2. **#4: separate chilled locker missed (3 of 6, step 4).** Two went home without their frozen items. That is lost food and a refund or complaint, and it was the most common failure in the sessions. Make the chilled locker impossible to miss at the moment the ambient locker opens, and don't let the person finish with items outstanding.

3. **#9: order code confused with PIN (4 of 6, step 1).** Two people made the mistake twice. Everyone got through, but it was the second most common problem, and it's a labeling fix.

4. **#1: couldn't find where to scan (5 of 6, step 1).** Almost everyone hit it and it cost about 40 seconds before they found it. Nobody failed, so it ranks below the three above. It's the first thing every shopper sees, and it's probably a cheap fix. It sits on the same step as #9, so one redesign of the first screen could cover both.

**Why not the others**
- **#5 (language) and #12 (wheelchair reach):** Each blocked the one person who hit it, so they are serious, but each is a single session. I'd treat them as leads, not rank them above problems that stopped several people.
  - #12 is a physical and accessibility issue, not just a screen issue. It may carry legal obligations, so check it separately. A software workaround could be assigning lower lockers to people who ask for them.
  - If engineering has room for a fifth fix, #5 is likely cheap.
- **#8 (timeout, 2 of 6) and #6 (fear of double charge, 3 of 6):** These slowed or worried people, but everyone finished. Both are good next candidates. #8 may be a one-line timeout change.
- **#3, #7, #10, #11, #13 and #14:** These are preferences, feature requests or small annoyances, and nobody failed because of them.
  - #13, the four-minute help wait, is an operations problem. Pass it to store staff.
  - #11, the glare, is a store-environment problem. Pass it to the store's facilities team.

**Caveats**
- Six sessions at one store is enough to show what breaks, but not to say how often it will break across 30 stores. I've given counts, not percentages, for that reason.
- #2 and #4 each have only a couple of failures behind them. After the fixes, retest with a few new people. Check that #2 and #4 no longer end with someone leaving without their order.
