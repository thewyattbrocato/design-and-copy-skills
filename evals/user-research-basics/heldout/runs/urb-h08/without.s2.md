I'd steer you away from saying these came from interviews. Telling investors you did research you didn't do is misrepresentation, and diligence questions like "how many people did you talk to?" will expose it fast. It's also an easy fix. Personas framed as hypotheses are normal in early-stage decks, and they read as credible instead of risky.

Here are three personas you can use. Title the slide **"Who we believe our customers are (target personas)"**. If you can, run a few quick conversations before Thursday so you can say "early conversations."

---

**1. Priya, 29, Product Manager (the time-starved professional)**
- **Goal:** Eat home-cooked, healthy dinners on weeknights without planning or shopping.
- **Frustration:** She orders delivery three or four nights a week out of exhaustion, then feels guilty about the cost and nutrition.
- **Quote:** *"I don't want to think about dinner. I just want to open a box and have it be good."*

**2. Marcus & Dana, 36 and 38, Teacher and Nurse (the busy parents)**
- **Goal:** Get a varied, kid-friendly family dinner on the table in under 30 minutes, with less food waste.
- **Frustration:** Grocery trips eat their weekends, and they throw out unused produce every week.
- **Quote:** *"We're not short on good intentions. We're short on 6 p.m. energy."*

**3. Elena, 52, Freelance Designer (the health-conscious empty nester)**
- **Goal:** Cook smaller portions with fresh, high-quality ingredients, and try new cuisines without buying a full pantry.
- **Frustration:** Recipes and grocery packs are sized for families, so she wastes food and falls back on the same five meals.
- **Quote:** *"I like to cook. I just don't like buying a whole bottle of fish sauce for one recipe."*
