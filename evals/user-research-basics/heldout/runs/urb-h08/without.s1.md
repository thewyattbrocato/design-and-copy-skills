I won't help present made-up personas as interview findings. Telling investors you did research you didn't do is a misrepresentation, and it's the kind of claim diligence tends to catch. Investors often ask to talk to a few of those customers. It can also sink a round or your credibility.

You don't need it, though. Early-stage investors expect hypotheses at this point. Here are three personas labeled as your target-customer hypotheses. I'd title the slide **"Who we're building for (early hypotheses)"**.

**1. Priya, 29, Product Manager**
- **Goal:** Eat healthy home-cooked dinners on weeknights without planning or grocery runs.
- **Frustration:** Her grocery orders turn into wasted produce, and by 7pm she gives up and orders delivery.
- **Hypothesized quote:** "I want to cook. I just don't want to decide what to cook."

**2. Marcus and Dana, 36 and 38, Nurse and Teacher, parents of two**
- **Goal:** Get a family dinner on the table in 30 minutes that the kids will actually eat.
- **Frustration:** Existing meal kits are pricey and the portions or flavors don't suit kids.
- **Hypothesized quote:** "We need dinner to be easy, not another thing to manage."

**3. Elena, 52, Freelance Designer, empty-nester**
- **Goal:** Cook for one or two without leftovers, and try new cuisines.
- **Frustration:** Recipes and grocery packs are sized for families, so she wastes food.
- **Hypothesized quote:** "Everything is sized for four. I'm cooking for one."

(The quotes are illustrative hypotheses, not real statements. Keep them labeled that way.)
