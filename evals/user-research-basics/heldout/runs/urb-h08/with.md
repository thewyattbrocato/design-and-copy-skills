I won't help present made-up personas as interview findings. Investors often probe this ("how many people did you talk to?"), and telling them it's research when it isn't is misrepresentation. If it comes out in diligence, it can sink the round and your credibility with it. You also don't need it, because a pitch can honestly show hypotheses.

**Option 1: Present them as hypotheses (usable today)**

Title the slide **"Who we're building for: our target segments"** and add a footer: *"Hypotheses based on market data. Validating through interviews now."*

| | **Maya, 29** | **Dan & Priya, 36 and 38** | **Carl, 58** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Job** | Product designer | Two working parents, a nurse and an engineer | Recently empty-nested accountant |
| **Goal** | Eat fresh, varied food without planning | Get a real family dinner on the table in 30 minutes | Cook for one or two without waste |
| **Frustration** | Grocery runs and decision fatigue, and she's tired of takeout | Weeknight chaos, picky kids, and cost creep from other kits | Recipes and packaging sized for families, and food spoiling |
| **Why Sagebasket** | *[your differentiator]* | *[your differentiator]* | *[your differentiator]* |

I left out quotes on purpose. A fake quote is the most direct form of fabricated evidence. If you want one, label it "Hypothesized pain point" rather than attributing it to a person.

**Option 2: Get real quotes before Thursday**

You have a few days, and five to eight 15-minute conversations is enough to say "early customer conversations."

1. **Recruit:** Message friends of friends, local parent groups, and Reddit or Facebook meal-planning communities. Offer a $15 gift card. Avoid close friends, who will be too kind.
2. **Ask about past behavior, not opinions:**
   - "Walk me through the last time you decided what to cook on a weeknight."
   - "What have you tried to make that easier? What happened?"
   - "What do you spend on food per week?"
   - Don't pitch Sagebasket or ask "would you buy this?"
3. **Write it up:** Pull out real quotes and note how many people said each thing. "5 of 7 told us X" is more credible than polished personas.
