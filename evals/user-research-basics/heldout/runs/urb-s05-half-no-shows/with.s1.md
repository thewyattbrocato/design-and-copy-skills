With 15 bookings I can't tell you which cause matters most. These are likely causes, ranked by how much I'd bet on each. You can check the first three against your own booking data.

## What's probably going wrong

1. **The lead time is too long.** Booking two to three weeks out is the biggest suspect. Plans change, and a form filled in on impulse from a newsletter is forgotten by then. The only contact in between is one confirmation email.
2. **The slots don't match when members are free.** Climbers tend to climb after work and on weekends. Weekday 9 or 10am slots probably mean taking time off work, so people book hopefully and then can't make it.
3. **Nothing reminds people or makes rescheduling easy.** After the confirmation there are no reminders, and no easy way to move or cancel. Someone who can't make it has no simple option, and not showing up is the easiest thing to do.
4. **The commitment is low and the reward comes late.** A newsletter click plus a form takes 30 seconds. The $20 arrives only after the session, and it may not be worth a weekday morning.
5. **Newsletter recruiting draws casual clickers.** People who click through are not the same as people who have decided to talk to you.

## What to change

- **Shrink the gap.** Show only slots within the next 2 to 7 days. Rolling weekly availability works better than a calendar weeks out.
- **Add evenings and weekends.** Add slots at the times members are actually at or near the gym, and offer a video call so nobody has to travel.
- **Send reminders.** Send them 24 hours and 1 to 2 hours before. SMS usually works better than email. Put a one-click reschedule or cancel link in each reminder.
- **Make it feel personal.** Send the confirmation from a named person, saying what the session is and that you're counting on them. Ask them to reply "yes" to confirm. If there's no reply, release the slot and offer it to someone else.
- **Recruit in more places.** Add an in-app prompt, and ask people at the gym front desk. People who agree face to face rarely flake.
- **Rethink the incentive.** If you can, pay for the time at a rate that fits the slot. Consider splitting it, with part promised up front and the rest after the session. Never charge people for no-shows.
- **Over-book.** Until the rate drops, book about 30 to 40% more sessions than you need. Keep a short backup list for the slots people drop.

## Check first (about an hour)

For the 7 no-shows, compare each person's booking lead time, slot time and source with the 8 who showed up. If the no-shows are mostly the longest lead times, that points to cause 1. If they cluster in the 9am slots, it points to cause 2. You could also send the 7 a short, non-judgmental note asking what got in the way. Reply patterns will beat my guesses.

After you make the changes, track the no-show rate for a few weeks. With so few sessions per batch, judge it by "n of N", not percentages.
