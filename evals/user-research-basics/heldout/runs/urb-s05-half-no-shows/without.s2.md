Most of this is probably a mismatch between the slots you offer and your members' schedules, made worse by a long, unreminded gap before the session. With 15 bookings I can't prove which factor matters most, but the pattern is typical.

## What's likely going wrong

1. **The 2-3 week lead time is probably the biggest factor.** Someone who clicks a newsletter link is acting on a passing impulse. Three weeks later they've forgotten, or their plans changed.
2. **Weekday 9 and 10am slots don't fit climbers.** Gym members mostly climb evenings and weekends, and many have day jobs. Those slots may only attract people who booked optimistically and then couldn't make it.
3. **You send one confirmation email and no reminders.** Nothing refreshes the commitment between booking and the session.
4. **It's easy to ghost.** There's no calendar invite, no obvious reschedule or cancel link, and no person they'd feel rude letting down.
5. **The incentive may not be visible at booking time.** You pay after the session, so if the form doesn't make the $20 prominent, it isn't motivating the booking.

## What I'd change, in priority order

1. **Shorten the lead time.** Only offer slots 2-5 days out. If the newsletter goes out monthly, open slots for a week or so after each send, or use a rolling scheduler.
2. **Add evening and weekend slots.** For example, 6-8pm weekdays and Saturday mornings. Better still, let people choose among many times.
3. **Add reminders.** Send a calendar invite (.ics) at booking, a reminder 24 hours before, and one 1-2 hours before. SMS is far more effective than email if you can collect phone numbers.
4. **Make rescheduling one click.** Put a reschedule/cancel link in every email. A rescheduled session is much better than a no-show.
5. **Make it feel personal.** Send the confirmation from a real person, with a short note on what the session is and how long it takes. Keep sessions short (20-30 minutes) and offer video if they aren't already remote.
6. **Put the $20 in the newsletter blurb and the confirmation email.** Say "$20 gift card for a 30-minute chat" up front.
7. **Overbook.** Even after these fixes, expect 15-25% no-shows. Booking 20-25% more people than you need is cheaper than losing sessions.

## How to check

Look at your 7 no-shows against the 8 who came. Compare lead time, slot time, and whether they were recent app users. If the no-shows cluster at 3 weeks out or at 9am, that confirms the diagnosis. Even with a small sample, a stark split is informative.

If you can only change two things, shorten the lead time and add reminders. Those are the cheapest fixes, and they usually cut no-shows the most.
