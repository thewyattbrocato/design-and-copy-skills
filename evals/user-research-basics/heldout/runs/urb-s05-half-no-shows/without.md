Your no-show rate is 47%, and I'd guess most of it comes from three things. I'm inferring this from your setup, not from your data.

## Likely causes

1. **The lead time is too long.** People sign up on impulse from a newsletter, then the session is 2-3 weeks away. By then the motivation has faded, the calendar has changed, and the confirmation email is buried. This is probably the biggest factor.
2. **The slots don't fit your audience.** Climbers tend to climb evenings and weekends. Weekday 9-10am slots likely conflict with their jobs, so you may be selecting for people who can't actually make it.
3. **Nothing reminds or anchors them.** After one automatic email there are no reminders, no calendar invite, and no easy way to reschedule. Silently skipping costs them nothing.
4. **The incentive comes too late.** The $20 arrives after the session, and it's small compared with taking time off work.

## What to change

- **Shorten the window.** Only offer slots 2-5 days out, and open new ones weekly. If your newsletter is monthly, a rolling "next available" list works better than fixed dates.
- **Add evening (6-8pm) and weekend slots.** If the sessions are remote, this is easy. If they're in person at the gym, consider holding them right before or after peak climbing hours.
- **Make the confirmation stick.** Include a calendar invite (.ics) and a one-click reschedule or cancel link. Send reminders at 24 hours and 2 hours before, by SMS if you collect phone numbers.
- **Ask for a reply.** A short personal note from a real person ("reply with 'yes' to hold your spot") filters out low-intent signups. Release the slot if there's no reply.
- **Rework the incentive.** Consider a bigger amount, or gym credit such as a day pass or a month of membership. Gym credit costs you less and is worth more to this audience. Say clearly that the incentive is for completed sessions.
- **Overbook.** Even after these fixes, 10-20% no-shows is normal. Schedule about 15-25% more people than you need, or keep a short waitlist you can message the same day.

## Check first

Fifteen bookings is a small sample. If you can, compare the no-shows with the attendees by lead time, time slot, and signup date. If the no-shows cluster in the 3-week-out bookings or the 9am slots, that confirms the diagnosis. Then change lead time and slots first, since those cost nothing, and add reminders and incentive changes next.
