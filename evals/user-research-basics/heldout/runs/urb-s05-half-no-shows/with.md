**What's going wrong:** I can't tell for sure from 7 of 15 no-shows. These are the likely causes, roughly in order of how much I'd bet on each.

1. **The gap is too long.** Booking two to three weeks out is the biggest risk. People forget, and their schedules change. Nothing re-engages them in between except one confirmation email.
2. **The slots may not match when climbers are free.** Climbing gym members often climb before or after work. Weekday 9 and 10am slots are work hours for many of them. Anyone who picked a slot because it was the only one offered will bail when the day arrives.
3. **Booking costs almost nothing.** A newsletter link and a form is a low-commitment signup. Nothing makes it feel like a scheduled appointment with a person.
4. **There are no reminders and no easy way out.** One automatic email is easy to miss or land in spam, and it isn't a calendar entry. If someone can't make it, they have no simple way to reschedule, so they just don't come.
5. **The incentive comes after the session.** The $20 only matters if people see it and believe it. If the form doesn't say it up front, it does nothing for show-up rates.

**What to change** (all cheap):
- **Offer slots within 2 to 7 days,** not 2 to 3 weeks.
- **Add evening and weekend slots,** or a "tell us a time that works" option.
- **Send a calendar invite** (.ics) with the confirmation, plus reminders 24 hours and 1 hour before. Put a one-click reschedule or cancel link in each.
- **Put the $20 on the booking form and in the confirmation,** worded as "$20 gift card for your time."
- **Make the confirmation come from a named person** with a reply-to address, so it reads like a person expecting them.
- **Keep sessions short** (20 to 30 minutes) and offer video or a quick chat at the gym.
- **Overbook.** If you need 8 sessions, book about 12 for now. Keep a short waitlist to fill cancellations.

**Cheapest next check:** Email the 7 no-shows with one question: "What got in the way?" Also check whether the confirmation email reached them (spam folder, or a bounce). This tells you whether the problem is timing, forgetting or something else, and you'll hear it in their words instead of mine.

**How to tell if it worked:** Track show-ups as "n of N" over the next 15 or so bookings. With groups that small, don't read much into percentages. If you change several things at once, you won't know which one helped. That's fine for now, since the goal is to get people to show up.

**Two notes:**
- I'm assuming no other data exists, such as which slots the no-shows picked or how far out they booked. If you can pull that, check whether the 7 no-shows skew toward the furthest-out or the earliest slots.
- A human needs to send the follow-up email, change the form and slots, and set up the reminders.
