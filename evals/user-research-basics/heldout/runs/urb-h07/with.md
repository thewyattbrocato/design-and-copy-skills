**Not as a win, no.** The data supports "B is ahead so far," not "B won." I'd hold off on telling the board it won.

**Why the result is weak (my calculation from your numbers):**
- **The gap is within noise.** That's about 68 purchases for A and 78 for B, a difference of 10 purchases. The p-value is about 0.4, which means a gap this size would show up fairly often by chance alone. The 95% interval for B's true lift is roughly **−0.7 to +1.7 percentage points**, so B could be slightly worse or noticeably better.
- **The test is far too small for this effect.** To reliably detect a 3.6% to 4.1% difference you'd need roughly 24,000 visitors per variant. You have 1,900. At about 630 visitors per day per variant, that's more than a month.
- **Three days isn't a full cycle.** Ticketing traffic usually swings by weekday versus weekend and by on-sale or event announcements. Run the test over whole weeks. Monday to Wednesday misses the weekend.
- **Checking early inflates false wins.** If you keep looking and stop when B is ahead, you'll "find" winners that aren't real.

**What you can do by Friday:**
1. **Be honest with the board.** Something like: "B is trending ahead (4.1% vs 3.6%), but it's early and not conclusive. We'll have a reliable read on [date]."
2. **Decide the stopping rule now.** Pick one metric (purchase rate), a minimum lift worth acting on, and a duration of whole weeks. Don't stop early because B looks good.
3. **If the boss insists on shipping B,** frame it as a low-risk judgment call, not a proven win. The label change is cheap to reverse, and the data shows no sign that B hurts. Say it that way, and keep the test running or monitor purchase rate after the switch.

Two things only you can check: whether traffic or events were unusual this week, and whether purchase rate is the metric you set up front. If the real traffic is much higher than 630 per day per variant, the timeline shrinks, but the conclusion is the same for now.
