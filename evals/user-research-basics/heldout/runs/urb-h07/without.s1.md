Not honestly, at least not as "B won." The data so far doesn't separate B from A.

**The numbers**
- A: 3.6% and B: 4.1% on about 1,900 visitors each, so the observed lift is 0.5 percentage points (about 14% relative).
- A two-proportion test gives z ≈ 0.8 and p ≈ 0.42. A gap this size would show up by chance roughly 4 times in 10 even if the labels did nothing.
- The 95% confidence interval for B minus A is about **−0.7 to +1.7 points**. B could be worse, the same, or noticeably better.

**Why it's too early**
- **Sample size:** To reliably detect a 0.5-point lift from a 3.6% baseline, you'd need roughly 24,000 visitors per variant. You have about 1,900. At the current traffic rate, that's about a month, not a few days.
- **Duration:** You've run about two and a half days, so the test hasn't covered a full weekly cycle. Ticketing traffic swings with on-sale dates, event announcements, and weekday versus weekend buyers. Run at least one or two full weeks.
- **Peeking:** If you check daily and ship when B is ahead, you inflate the false-positive rate well beyond the nominal 5%.

**What you can tell your boss and the board**
- Don't say "B won" or "B lifts conversion 14%." If the board hears that, it will become a number people expect to see in revenue.
- You can say: "Early results are directionally positive but not statistically conclusive. We're continuing the test through [date] to get a reliable read."

**If your boss wants to ship Friday anyway**
This is a label change, so it's cheap and reversible, and shipping B is a defensible business call. Be upfront that it's a judgment call and not a measured win. A reasonable version:
1. Keep the test running (or keep a holdout on A) so you still get a real answer.
2. Report it to the board as "shipped on a hunch, measurement in progress," not as a result.
3. Agree on a stopping rule now (sample size or end date, and the metric) so the decision isn't made by whichever way the numbers look that day.
