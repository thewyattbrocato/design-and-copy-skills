**No, not as "B won."** The data so far can't tell B apart from noise.

**The numbers**
- A has about 68 purchases from 1,900 visitors (3.6%). B has about 78 (4.1%).
- The gap is 0.5 percentage points, about 10 extra purchases. A two-proportion test gives p ≈ 0.42.
- The 95% interval for B minus A runs from roughly **−0.7 to +1.7 points**. B could be modestly better, but A could also be slightly better.
- If you tested two identical labels, a gap this size would show up about 4 times in 10.

**Other problems**
- The test has run about 2.5 days. That doesn't cover a full week, and ticketing traffic swings by weekday and with on-sales. Two days that include a Monday and Tuesday may not look like your usual mix.
- Checking results mid-test and shipping when B is ahead inflates false positives. Wednesday's lead can easily vanish or flip.
- I'm assuming the success metric was purchase rate and was chosen before launch, not picked after seeing the results.

**What it would take**
To reliably detect a lift from 3.6% to 4.1% (80% power, 5% significance), you need about 23,000 visitors per variant. At your current pace of roughly 900 per variant per day, that's about 3.5 weeks. If the true lift is smaller than 0.5 points, it would take longer.

**What you can tell your boss**
- **Option 1, my recommendation:** Keep the test running to a pre-set end date, such as 3–4 full weeks, and decide then. Tell the board "early signal is positive, result due [date]." That is accurate.
- **Option 2:** If Friday is fixed and the label change is cheap and easy to reverse, you can ship B anyway. Call it a judgment call with no evidence of harm, not a win. Keep a holdback or keep measuring so you can revert. Don't tell the board it was proven.
- **Option 3:** If you have a lot of traffic during a big on-sale, you could reach the required sample sooner. That needs real numbers from you, not an extrapolation.

The one thing to avoid is telling the board "B lifted purchases 14%." The interval includes zero and negative values, so that claim could be reversed within weeks.

A human needs to confirm that purchase rate was the pre-chosen metric and that traffic was split randomly, for example by checking that both arms got a similar mix of devices and sources.
