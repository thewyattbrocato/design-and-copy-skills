Mostly fine for a gut check. The task wording is good because it doesn't use the label "pause", so you aren't handing them the answer. A few tweaks will make it more reliable.

**Biggest risk: support people aren't typical users.**
- They know the app's structure and vocabulary far better than customers do, so they'll find the switch faster than a real user would.
- Treat "support finds it easily" as a weak pass. Treat "support struggles" as a strong fail.
- Skip anyone who has seen or worked on the feature. If you can grab one or two people from outside support (finance, engineering), swap them in.

**Setup**
- **Starting point:** Decide where they begin (home screen, logged in) and keep it identical for all three.
- **Test account:** Use one with an upcoming box scheduled, so pausing actually matters and there's a visible result.
- **Phone:** Use a device with the right build already loaded so you aren't fumbling during sessions.
- **Dates:** If the switch needs dates or a duration, "two weeks" is a good prompt. Make sure the flow can express it.

**During the session**
- **Silence rule:** Agree on a give-up point (say 3 minutes stuck), then end the task. Don't rescue them before that.
- **Think aloud:** Ask them to say what they're looking for as they go. Watching alone tells you where they went, but not what they expected to find there.
- **Success criteria:** Write down what counts as success before you start. Finding the switch isn't enough. Did they use it correctly, and did they notice the confirmation? Ask "how would you know it worked?" afterward.
- **What to log:** Note each path (screens and taps, including wrong turns), not just the hesitations. Wrong turns show you where people expect the feature to live.

**Process**
- Treat the first session as a pilot. If the task wording or setup is off, fix it before sessions two and three.
- Ask for permission if you want to screen-record.
- Three people is enough to catch the obvious problems, but not to size them. Don't read anything into "2 of 3".

If all three go straight to it, you're probably fine. If even one hunts around for a while, look closely at where they went, because that's where the switch probably belongs.
