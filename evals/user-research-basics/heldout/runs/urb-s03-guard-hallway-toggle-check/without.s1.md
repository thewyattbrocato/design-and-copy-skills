Your plan is fine for a gut check. A few tweaks will make it more useful.

**Things to fix**
- **Support staff know the app.** They've probably seen the settings screens many times and may already know the switch exists. That makes it look easier to find than it will be for customers. If you can get anyone from outside support and product, even one person, swap them in. Otherwise, just treat "found it fast" with some suspicion.
- **Ask them to think aloud.** Silent watching shows you where they hesitate, but not why. Say something like "say whatever you're thinking as you go, and I won't answer questions." Then you'll hear things like "I'd expect this under Subscription" or "is this skip or cancel?"
- **Set up the phone beforehand.** Use a test account with an active subscription and upcoming boxes, and reset it between people. Make sure notifications and other apps won't interrupt.
- **Decide when you'll stop them.** If someone is lost for about 2 minutes, move on. Note that they failed, and don't hint.

**Your task script**
It's good because it doesn't say "pause." Two small additions:
- Hand it over written, or read it identically each time, so you don't drift into hints.
- Add a date, like "you leave next Friday and are back two weeks later." Then you can check whether they set the right range, not just whether they found the switch.

**What to write down**
You're already noting hesitations. Also capture:
- Where they went first (the wrong tabs and menus count as findings).
- Whether they reached the switch at all.
- Whether they set the dates correctly.
- Whether they believed it worked. Ask "How sure are you that no box will arrive?" afterward. A switch that's easy to find but gives no clear confirmation is still a problem.
- Any words they used for it ("skip," "hold," "cancel"), which may suggest better labels.

**Logistics**
- Sit beside them, not behind, so it feels less like a test.
- Tell them you're testing the app, not them.
- Screen-record if they're okay with it. Your notes will miss things.
- Treat the first session as a dry run for your script. If it goes badly because of the setup, fix that before the next one.

Three people won't give you numbers, but they will show you any big problem. If two of three get stuck in the same place, you've got your answer.
