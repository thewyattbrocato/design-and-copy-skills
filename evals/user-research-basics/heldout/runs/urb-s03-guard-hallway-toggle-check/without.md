Your plan is mostly fine for a gut check. The task wording is good: it describes a goal and doesn't name the feature. A few tweaks would help:

- **Support staff are biased participants.** They know the product and its vocabulary better than real customers do, so they'll likely find the switch faster than a customer would. If anyone has heard about the pause feature, note it or swap them out. If you can grab someone from finance, ops, or another team, do that for at least one session.
- **Ask them to think aloud.** Silence tells you where they hesitate, but not why. Something like "say whatever you're thinking as you go" costs nothing, and you still keep your mouth shut. Don't ask questions mid-task.
- **Start from the app's home screen**, not from settings, so the path to the switch is part of what you're testing.
- **Decide in advance what counts as success.** For example: the pause is on, the dates cover the two weeks, and they can tell it worked. Finding the switch but setting the wrong dates, or not noticing a confirmation, is a separate problem. Also note if they skip individual deliveries instead of pausing. That's a valid route, and it suggests the switch isn't discoverable.
- **Set a give-up point.** If someone is stuck for about 2 minutes, move on and record it as a fail. Don't hint.
- **Use a test account, not their real one.** Reset it between sessions, and have a fresh state each time so the second person isn't affected by the first.
- **Record the screen if you can.** Your notes will miss things, and a recording also lets you check them later.

Three people won't give you numbers, but they're enough to catch the obvious problems. If two of three hesitate in the same place, that's a real signal.
