Your plan is mostly fine. It's a good quick check. Your task wording is the best part: it doesn't say "pause", "settings" or "deliveries", so it won't hand anyone the answer.

**Changes I'd make**

1. **Be careful with the sample.** Support staff know the product and may already know the switch exists. If they struggle, that's a strong signal. If they find it, it's weak evidence. Swap in at least one or two people from outside support and product, such as finance or ops, who haven't seen the feature. Also ask each person up front whether they've seen it, and note the answer.
2. **Don't be the only observer if you can avoid it.** You built it, so your reactions will leak, like a nod, a sigh or leaning in. Sit beside or slightly behind them. Don't rescue anyone. Agree on a stop rule beforehand, for example "I'm done" or about 4 minutes stuck, and then move on.
3. **Set the start state.** Use a test account with an active subscription. Start from the app's home screen, not from settings. Reset the account between people so nobody inherits a pause someone else set.
4. **Define success before you start.** I'd count it only if they find the switch and finish it correctly, with the right dates and the confirmation step. For each person, note the path they took, the wrong turns, and where they paused. Also note if they go somewhere else entirely, such as "skip a week", cancel, or contact support. Those wrong turns tell you what to rename or move.
5. **Ask one question at the end.** Something like "How would you know it worked?" Finding the switch doesn't help much if people don't trust that it took effect.
6. **Ask for consent and let them think aloud.** Say you're testing the app, not them. Ask them to say what they're looking for as they go, and record the screen if they agree. That's easier than writing notes while watching.

**How to read the result**

Report it as "2 of 3 found it", not as percentages. If two or more hit the same snag, fix it by renaming or moving the switch, then retest with new people. If all three sail through, that's encouraging but not proof. Colleagues are an easier audience than real customers, so a few real customers would be the follow-up.

You'll need to recruit the people and run the sessions yourself.
