The plan is mostly sound. The scenario-based task, watching without helping, and not naming the switch are all right. A few things would make it more useful.

**The biggest risk: support staff aren't a clean sample.**
They know the product, the vocabulary, and probably this feature. If they find it fast, that tells you little. If they struggle, it's a strong signal. If you can, swap at least one or two for people outside the team or company who use similar apps but haven't seen yours. Friends, a neighbor, or someone from another department all work. If support is all you can get this afternoon, go ahead, but treat a quick success as weak evidence.

**Tweaks to the session**
- **Ask them to think aloud.** Silent watching shows you where they hesitate but not why. Say "say what you're looking for and what you expect to happen." If they go quiet, prompt with "what are you thinking?" rather than offering hints.
- **Start from the app's home screen.** Don't open on Settings, and don't say "in settings."
- **Watch your wording.** "Going on holiday, make sure no boxes arrive" is good. Avoid "pause," "deliveries," and "away," because they match the switch label and give it away.
- **Agree on a stop rule up front.** For example, if they're stuck for about 2 minutes, you move on. Note it as a fail.
- **Include confirmation in the task.** After they flip the switch, ask "how do you know it worked?" Findability is only half of it. They also need to see that it's paused and when it resumes.
- **Use a test account, not your own.** Your own account could have real orders, notifications, or saved state that distract them or get changed by accident.
- **Tell them you're testing the app, not them,** and that they can stop anytime. If you want to record the screen, ask first.

**What to jot down per person**
- The path they took, including wrong turns.
- Where they hesitated, and what they said or expected there.
- Whether they succeeded unaided.
- Whether they'd have given up and contacted support. That's a signal in itself.

**What to conclude**
Three people will surface the obvious problems, and if two of them stumble in the same place, that's worth fixing. It can't tell you what share of users will find the switch, so don't quote it as a percentage. Also, don't change the UI between sessions unless something is badly broken, or you won't be comparing like with like.
