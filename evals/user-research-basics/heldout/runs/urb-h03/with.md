Most of this guide needs rewriting. Questions 2 to 6 ask people to predict, speculate or react to your pitch, and people are poor at all three. Question 1 is fine apart from missing consent, and question 7 is fine as it is.

| # | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Leading: "stressful" puts the answer in their mouth. | Ask about the last time income was uneven. |
| 3 | Hypothetical "would you use". People say yes to be polite. | Ask what they do now to set money aside for taxes. |
| 4 | Asks for a price prediction, anchored on $5/$10/$15. | Ask what they pay for tools today. Interviews can't tell you willingness to pay. |
| 5 | Asks for wants. People describe features, not needs. | Ask about workarounds and what they've tried. |
| 6 | Pitches the product, then asks how they feel. They'll be polite. | Ask about their last invoice, and don't mention Ledgerleaf. |
| Missing | No consent or recording ask, and no warm-up. | Add both at the start. |

**Revised guide (45 min)**

1. **Intro (3 min).** Thank them and say it will take about 45 minutes. Say you're learning how freelancers handle money and there are no right answers. Ask consent to record, and ask whether any observers may sit in. Don't name or describe Ledgerleaf.
2. **Context (5 min).** "Tell me about your freelance work. What do you do, and how long have you been doing it?"
3. **Last uneven month (10 min).** "Think back to the last month when your income was higher or lower than usual. Walk me through what happened." Follow up with "What did you do next?" and "How did you know?"
4. **Taxes (10 min).** "Tell me about the last time you paid taxes on your freelance income. How did you know how much to pay? When did you start working it out? What did you set aside, and how?" Follow up with "What went wrong or surprised you?"
5. **Tools and workarounds (7 min).** "Show me, or describe, where you keep track of money right now. What have you tried before, and why did you stop? What do you pay for, if anything?"
6. **Invoicing (7 min).** "Tell me about the last invoice you sent, from deciding to bill to getting paid. Where did it get stuck or take longer than you expected?"
7. **Close (3 min).** "Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?" Thank them and explain the incentive.

**Notes for tomorrow**
- Whenever someone answers in generalities ("I usually..."), steer them back with "Can you think of the last time?" Then stay quiet a few seconds before you follow up.
- Pricing needs a different method. What they pay for now (item 5) is the closest an interview gets. Don't present it as price validation.
- The guide assumes you're exploring the problem, not testing a built design. If you have a prototype, tell me and I'll add a task-based section.
- Recruit people who filed taxes on freelance income this year. Don't interview friends as the whole sample. You or a teammate has to handle recruiting, consent and recording.
- Eight or so interviews is plenty for a first pass. Report findings as "3 of 8", not percentages.
