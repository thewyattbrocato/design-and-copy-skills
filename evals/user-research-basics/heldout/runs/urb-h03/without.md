Your guide has a few problems that will hurt what you learn. Here they are, most important first.

**Main problems**

1. **Q2 is leading.** "Don't you find it stressful…" tells people the answer you want. Ask instead: "Walk me through the last time your income was uneven. What happened?" You'll get a real story, and you can see whether stress comes up unprompted.

2. **Q3 asks about hypothetical behavior.** People say yes to "would you use X?" and then don't. Ask about what they do now: "How do you handle taxes today? When do you set money aside? What's gone wrong?" If they've never solved the problem, it probably isn't painful enough to pay for.

3. **Q4 anchors on price and gives only three options.** It also assumes they want the product. Their answers will cluster around your numbers. Better options:
   - Ask what they spend now on tools, accountants, or spreadsheets.
   - Use Van Westendorp-style questions: "At what price would this be so cheap you'd doubt it? So expensive you wouldn't consider it?"
   - Treat stated willingness to pay as weak evidence anyway.

4. **Q5 invites a wish list.** "Perfect app" gets you feature requests, which are usually solutions people imagine rather than needs they have. Ask about current tools instead: "What do you use now? What's annoying about it? What have you tried and dropped?"

5. **Q6 is a pitch, and it's leading.** You're telling them invoicing is easy and asking for their feelings, so you'll get politeness. Either drop it or ask about their current process: "Tell me about the last time you invoiced a client. What was hard? How long did it take to get paid?"

**Structural gaps**

- **No screener or background section.** Open with the person's work, how long they've freelanced, how many clients they have, and how their income varies. This helps you segment answers, and it makes the early conversation easier.
- **No consent or recording note.** Add this to Q1: whether you're recording, how you'll use the notes, and that there are no wrong answers.
- **45 minutes is a lot for 7 questions, but there's nothing to dig deeper with.** Plan follow-ups like "Can you give me an example?", "What happened next?", and "Why did that matter?" Past behavior is the most useful thing you can collect.
- **No concrete-moment questions.** Ask about the last time they got an unexpected tax bill, a slow month, or a late-paying client.
- **Q7 is fine,** but a better closer is "Who else should I talk to?" It gives you referrals.

**Suggested flow**

1. Intro, consent, and recording (3 min)
2. Background: work, income pattern, tools (5 min)
3. Money management today: the last slow month, how they plan, what they track (10 min)
4. Taxes: how they set money aside, what's gone wrong (10 min)
5. Invoicing and getting paid: the last time, pain points (8 min)
6. Tools tried and dropped, and what they spend now (5 min)
7. Wrap-up: anything I missed? Who else should I talk to? (4 min)

Show any concept or pricing idea at the end, only after you've heard their unprompted stories, and ask for reactions rather than commitments.
