## Revised guide for Ledgerleaf

**1. Opening and consent (about 3 min).** "Thanks for joining. This will take about 45 minutes. I'm researching how freelancers manage their money. There are no right answers, and I didn't design anything you'll be judging. Is it okay if I record for my notes only? You can skip any question or stop at any time."

**2. Background (about 5 min).** "Tell me about the work you do and how you get paid."
- Probe: how many clients, how often money arrives, how it arrived over the last few months.

**3. Last time you handled money (about 15 min).** "Walk me through the last time you sat down to figure out your money. What prompted it? What did you open or use? What happened next?"
- Probes: "Tell me more about that." "What did you do then?" "What was hard or slow about it?"
- Then: "Tell me about the last month where income was lower or later than you expected. What did you do?"

**4. Taxes (about 10 min).** "Tell me about the last time you dealt with taxes, whether paying, estimating, or setting money aside. What did you do, and when?"
- Probes: how they decided the amount, where the money sat, what surprised them, who or what helped.

**5. Invoicing (about 7 min).** "Tell me about the last invoice you sent, from deciding to bill to getting paid."
- Probes: what tool, what was tedious, what happened when a payment was late.

**6. Current tools and spending (about 3 min).** "What do you use today for any of this, including spreadsheets, notes or an accountant? Have you paid for or stopped using anything? What happened?"

**7. Close (about 2 min).** "Is there anything about how you handle money that I didn't ask about but should have?" Thank them and explain the incentive.

### What I changed and why

- **Q2 ("Don't you find it stressful…")** was leading. It tells people the answer you expect. Q3 now asks for a story about a real recent month, so you hear whether it's stressful in their own words.
- **Q3 ("Would you use…")** asked for a prediction, and people are poor at predicting their own behavior. I replaced it with the last time they handled taxes, which shows whether they already have a workaround.
- **Q4 (price)** gave anchors of $5, $10 and $15, and stated prices are unreliable. Section 6 asks what they've actually paid for or abandoned, which tells you much more. If you need willingness to pay, test it later with a real offer.
- **Q5 ("perfect app")** asked for a wish list, which gives you features nobody may use. Section 3 should produce the problems behind any wishes.
- **Q6 ("Our app makes invoicing really easy…")** pitched the product and fished for agreement. Section 5 asks about the last invoice instead.
- **Q1 and Q7** were fine. I added consent and recording to Q1, and I kept Q7 nearly as written.

### Before tomorrow

- Don't mention Ledgerleaf until the end, if at all. If participants know the sponsor, they tend to be polite.
- The 45 minutes is a plan, not a minimum. Cut section 6 first if you run short.
- If you're also the designer, expect to want to defend the product. Stay quiet, let silences run, and don't rescue people when they struggle.
- After about five interviews, check whether new ones still change your conclusions. If they do, add a few more.

You'll need to handle recruiting, consent, and recording yourself. I assumed these are one-on-one interviews with freelancers who have been paid in the last few months.
