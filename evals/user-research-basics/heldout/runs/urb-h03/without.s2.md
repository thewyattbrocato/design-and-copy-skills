Your guide is a good start, but most of the questions would give you answers that look like validation without telling you much. Here are the main problems, followed by a revised version.

## What to fix

- **Q2 is leading.** "Don't you find it stressful…" tells people what to say. Ask how they handle irregular income and let them bring up the stress themselves.
- **Q3 and Q4 ask about hypotheticals.** People say yes to "would you use X" and overstate what they'd pay. Ask what they do today instead: how they set money aside for taxes, what it costs them, and what they've already paid for.
- **Q4 anchors on $5, $10, or $15.** It also assumes they want the product. Ask about current spending on tools, accountants, and spreadsheets, and save price sensitivity for the end.
- **Q5 asks for a wish list.** Feature lists from "the perfect app" are unreliable. Ask about the last time something went wrong, and what they tried.
- **Q6 is a pitch, not a question.** "Easy invoicing" is your claim, and it pushes them to agree. Ask how they invoice now and what's annoying about it. You also haven't established that invoicing is part of the problem.
- **Missing pieces:**
  - Consent to record.
  - A warm-up.
  - Follow-up probes.
  - A closing question that does more than "anything else?"
- **Seven questions is thin for 45 minutes.** That's fine if you plan to probe. But a guide of single questions tends to turn into a survey.

## Revised guide

**Intro (3 min)**
- Thank them. Say it takes about 45 minutes, and that you're learning, not selling. There are no right answers, and critical feedback helps.
- Ask permission to record.

**Background (5 min)**
1. Tell me about your work. What do you do, and how long have you been freelancing?
2. Walk me through how money comes in during a typical month.

**Current behavior (20 min)**
3. Tell me about the last time your income was higher or lower than you expected. What happened?
4. How do you decide how much to spend or save in a given month?
5. How do you handle taxes? Walk me through the last time you paid them.
   - *Probe:* When do you set money aside? How do you know how much? What happens if you get it wrong?
6. What tools, apps, spreadsheets, or people do you use for any of this? What do you pay for them?
   - *Probe:* Have you tried and abandoned anything? Why?
7. How do you create and send invoices, and track who's paid? Where does that go well or badly?

**Pain and priority (7 min)**
8. Of everything we've covered, what's the most frustrating or costly part?
9. Have you ever tried to fix it? What did you do?

**Concept reaction (5 min, optional)**
10. Show a rough concept or screen of the auto tax set-aside. Ask: "What's your first reaction? What would you expect it to do? What would worry you?"
11. If they're interested: "What would this replace? What would it need to cost for you to switch, and what would be too expensive?" Ask it open-ended, with no price anchors.

**Close (3 min)**
12. Is there something I should have asked but didn't?
13. Who else should I talk to?

## Tips for tomorrow

- Ask "Can you tell me about the last time…?" whenever you can. Real examples beat opinions.
- Follow up on anything vague with "Tell me more" or "Why?" Stay silent a beat longer than feels comfortable.
- Don't defend or explain the product if they criticize it.
- If you're recruiting, check that participants actually freelance with irregular income. Otherwise Q3 and Q4 will go nowhere.
