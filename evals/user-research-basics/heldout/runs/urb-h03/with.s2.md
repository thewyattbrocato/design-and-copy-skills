# Revised interview guide

**Goal (for the team, not read aloud):** Learn how freelancers currently handle irregular income, taxes, and invoicing, and where that goes wrong. This feeds the decision about which problem Ledgerleaf should solve first.

**1. Introduction and consent (3 min)**
- Thanks for joining. This will take about 45 minutes. We're learning how freelancers manage money and there are no right answers.
- Explain what's recorded, who will see it, and how long it's kept. Say whether anyone else is listening.
- They can skip any question or stop at any time.
- Get explicit permission to record before you start recording.

**2. Warm-up (5 min)**
- Tell me about the work you do and how long you've been freelancing.
- Walk me through the last month of work: who you worked for and how you got paid.

**3. Irregular income (10 min)**
- Tell me about the last time money came in later or lower than you expected. What happened next?
- Walk me through how you decided what to spend or hold back last month.
- What do you use to keep track of this today? Show me if you can.

**4. Taxes (10 min)**
- Tell me about the last time you paid taxes, or worked out what you owed. What did you do beforehand?
- How did you decide how much to set aside? Where did that money go?
- Tell me about a time that didn't go as planned.

**5. Invoicing (7 min)**
- Tell me about the last invoice you sent, from start to finish.
- What happened after you sent it?
- Tell me about the last time a client paid late. What did you do?

**6. Close (5 min)**
- Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?
- May I follow up if I have a question?

**Probes:** "Say more about that." "What happened next?" "Why was that?" Then wait. Use the person's own words when you follow up, and don't supply labels like "stressful" or "tax set-aside" yourself.

## What I changed

| # | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | "Don't you find it stressful…" is leading. It supplies the answer, and people tend to agree. | Ask for the last time income was off, and let them say how it felt. |
| 3 | "Would you use…" asks for a prediction. People overstate what they'll do. | Ask how they handled taxes last time. Their current workaround is the evidence. |
| 4 | "How much would you pay… $5, $10, $15" anchors them on your numbers, and stated willingness to pay is unreliable. | Cut it. Ask what they spend on tools today. Pricing needs a different method. |
| 5 | "Perfect budgeting app" gets feature wish lists, not problems. | Cut it. Sections 3–5 surface the problems. |
| 6 | "Our app makes invoicing really easy. How do you feel…" names the product, pitches it, and leads. | Ask about their last real invoice. |
| 7 | This one is fine. | Kept as the close, plus follow-up permission. |
| 1 | It's missing consent and recording permission. | Added in the introduction. |

Notes:
- **Sample:** I assumed you're recruiting freelancers who invoiced someone in the past month or so. Don't mention Ledgerleaf until you actually show materials. If your participants are mostly friends or colleagues, treat what you hear as softer evidence.
- **Before tomorrow:** Run through the guide once aloud with a colleague, since 45 minutes is tight for four topics. If you're short on time, cut or shorten section 5. Have a second person take notes. Label participants P1, P2 and so on, and count findings as "3 of 7" rather than percentages.
