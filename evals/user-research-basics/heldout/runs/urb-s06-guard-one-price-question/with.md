Here's a neutral version:

**"Tell me about the last time you needed a warranty or proof of purchase for something in your home. What did you do?"**

The original question has three problems:
- **It assumes a need.** "An app that tracks the warranties on all your household appliances" presents the solution as useful before the person has said they have the problem.
- **It assumes they'd pay.** "How much would you pay" skips the question of whether they'd pay anything.
- **It asks about a hypothetical.** People are poor at predicting what they'd spend on something they've never used. Past behavior is more reliable.

If you still need a price signal, ask about what they do today instead of what they'd do in theory:

**"What, if anything, do you currently do, or spend, to keep track of warranties and receipts?"**

That gives you their current workaround and its cost, and "if anything" leaves room for the answer to be "nothing."

Your guide may have follow-ups that depend on the old wording. If so, check that they still make sense after the swap.
