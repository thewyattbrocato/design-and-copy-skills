**Reworded question:**

> "Tell me about the last time something in your home broke or stopped working, like an appliance. What did you do from there?"

**Probes, only if they don't come up on their own:**
- "How did you find out whether it was still covered?"
- "Where was the paperwork or receipt, and how did you get to it?"
- "Did you spend any money on it, or on anything to keep track of or protect it? What was that?"

The original asks people to name a price for a product they've just heard described, and "tracks the warranties on all your household appliances" frames the idea as obviously useful. People tend to give a number to be agreeable, and that number doesn't predict what they'd actually pay. The rewrite asks about the last real event and what they spent then. That tells you whether the problem exists and what they already pay to deal with it.

Two notes:
- This replaces one question with a short story prompt plus probes. If your guide has a fixed slot for one line, use the main question and keep the probes as moderator notes.
- If you still need a price signal, look for it in what they already spend (extended plans, replacements, repairs) rather than asking them to predict it.
