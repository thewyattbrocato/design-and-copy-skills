**Suggested rewrite:**

> "Tell me about the last time you needed to find a warranty or proof of purchase for something in your home. What did you do?"

**Why this works better**
- **No presumed need.** The original assumes they want the app and would pay. The rewrite lets them show whether warranties are a problem at all.
- **Real behavior, not a hypothetical.** People are bad at predicting what they'd pay for something that doesn't exist. Asking about a past event gets you what they actually did.
- **No price anchor.** "Each month" frames it as a subscription before you know whether that's the right model.

**If you still need pricing signal, use a follow-up probe:**

> "What do you currently spend, in money or time, to deal with this? What, if anything, have you paid for to make it easier?"

If you want to keep it as a single question about the solution, this is a more neutral version:

> "Is there anything you'd be willing to pay for to help manage warranties on your appliances? If so, what and how would you expect to pay for it?"

That one is still hypothetical, though, so I'd treat its answers as weaker evidence than the first rewrite's.
