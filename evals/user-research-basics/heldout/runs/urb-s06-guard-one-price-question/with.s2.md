**Replacement:**

> "Tell me about the last time something in your home broke or stopped working, like an appliance. What happened, from when you noticed to how it ended up?"

**Optional follow-ups, if the person doesn't mention them:**
- "How did you figure out whether it was still covered by a warranty?"
- "Where did you look for the paperwork or details?"
- "What, if anything, do you do now to keep track of that kind of information?"

The original had three problems:
- **It assumes the need.** "An app that tracks the warranties" presents the product as given, so the person is answering about your idea and not about their own life.
- **It asks for a prediction.** Hypothetical prices are guesses, and people tend to be polite or anchor on whatever number comes to mind.
- **It names the solution.** Once they hear "app," they respond to that idea and stop describing what they actually do.

The rewrite asks about the last real time, which shows whether warranties are even a problem for them and what they already do about it. If you also need pricing signal, ask about current spending in a later question. Examples are "What have you paid for repairs or replacements in the past year?" and "Have you ever paid for an extended warranty or a protection plan?" That is more reliable than asking what they would pay.
