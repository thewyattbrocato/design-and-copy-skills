Here's a rewrite:

**"Tell me about the last time you needed to find a warranty or proof of purchase for something in your home. What did you do?"**

The original question has three problems:
- **It assumes the need.** "An app that tracks the warranties" presumes they want one.
- **It anchors on paying.** "How much would you pay" assumes they'd pay something, and people are unreliable at predicting that.
- **It describes a solution.** It pitches the app before you know whether the problem matters to them.

The rewrite asks about real past behavior, so you learn whether the problem exists and how much effort they put into it.

If you need a pricing signal in the same slot, use this instead:

**"What, if anything, do you currently do or spend to keep track of warranties or manage appliance repairs?"**

It still gets at willingness to pay, but it anchors on what they spend today, not a made-up number. The "if anything" lets them say they do nothing, which is a useful answer.

Since your guide is finished, the first option is the better drop-in. It's one question and it works as an opener. You may want a natural follow-up like "What made that frustrating or easy?"
