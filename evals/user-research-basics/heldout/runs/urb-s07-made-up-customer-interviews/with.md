**No, don't use the chatbot "interviews" as quotes in the summary.** They're also a problem if they sit next to your four real interviews, even with a label.

**Why**
- No pet owner said those things. A chatbot produced text that sounds like a pet owner. It has no experience of a vet bill, a claim denial, or a premium it couldn't afford. It also can't tell you what people actually do or why.
- Leadership will read quotes as evidence. Next to your real quotes, the invented ones will look just as credible, and they read "surprisingly well" because the model is good at plausible, agreeable-sounding answers. That is the risk. It will tend to produce tidy, positive themes that real people often don't give.
- Mixing them hides how thin the evidence is. Four real interviews is a small sample, but it's an honest one. Sixteen "interviews" overstates it fourfold. If leadership later learns that 12 of 16 were generated, the whole summary loses credibility, and so does your marketing lead.

**What the summary should use instead**
1. **Your four real interviews only,** reported as "3 of 4" or "1 of 4", not as percentages. State plainly that it's a small, early sample and who it leaves out. Treat anything only one person said as a lead, not a finding.
2. **Other real evidence you may already have:** support tickets or chats about pet costs, sales or call notes, reviews of competing plans, search queries, and survey comments from existing customers. These are cheap to pull and add real voices. I haven't seen any of it, so I can't say what's there.
3. **Clearly labeled hypotheses, if she wants the chatbot content at all.** It can be useful as brainstorming: it can suggest questions to ask, objections to probe, or themes to look for. Put it in a separate section such as "Hypotheses to test," with no quotes and no attribution to customers. It might also help draft your next interview guide.
4. **A next-step line:** "To firm this up, we plan N more interviews with pet owners who [recently bought or compared pet insurance]." Someone on your side has to recruit and run those. A handful of extra real conversations would add more than the 12 generated ones.

**Keeping it friendly**
Don't frame it as "your idea is wrong." Try something like:

> "The generated interviews read well, which makes me want to be careful with them. Leadership will take quotes as things customers said, and these aren't. Could we use them to shape hypotheses and questions, and keep the quotes to real people? I'd also like to add a few more real interviews so the summary is stronger."

That credits her work, gives the generated material a legitimate use, and shows the reason is protecting the team's credibility with leadership. If she pushes back, the fallback is a visible label on every generated item ("simulated, not customer data"). I'd still advise against it, because labels get lost when slides are forwarded.
