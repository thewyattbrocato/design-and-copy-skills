No, I wouldn't put the chatbot interviews in as quotes next to the real ones. You can raise it without a fight, because the problem is factual and not about anyone's intent.

**Why it's a problem**
- The chatbot's "customers" aren't customers. Their quotes aren't evidence of what pet owners think. They show what a language model predicts a pet owner might say, which is mostly an average of its training data and whatever your prompt implied.
- They read well because the model is fluent. Fluency isn't validity. The quotes will probably sound plausible and agreeable, and they'll likely lean toward confirming the plan's appeal, since the prompt was about the plan.
- Leadership will assume every quote comes from a real person. Even with a footnote, quotes placed side by side get remembered as one body of evidence. If someone later finds out 12 of the 16 voices were synthetic, it damages the credibility of the whole summary, including your four real interviews.
- Twelve synthetic interviews would outweigh your four real ones. That could skew how much weight the findings get.

**What the summary should use instead**
1. **Your four real interviews** as the only source of direct quotes. State the sample size plainly: "4 interviews, directional, not statistically representative."
2. **Other real data, if you can get it:** survey responses, support tickets or call notes, reviews of competitor pet insurance, sales or agent feedback, or existing customer feedback on related products.
3. **A clearly labeled hypotheses section**, if the marketing lead's work is useful. It could read: "Themes generated by an AI simulation, not customer data, to be validated." That keeps her work and makes it honest. Use themes and questions from it, not quotes.
4. **A recommendation for the next step:** more real interviews, even 6 to 8 more, or a short survey, to fill the gap.

**How to raise it with her**
You could say something like: "The simulated interviews are a great source of hypotheses, and I'd like to use them that way. But since leadership will read quotes as real customer voice, I'd rather keep quotes to the real interviews and put the AI themes in a labeled 'to validate' section. That way nobody can challenge the findings later." This presents it as protecting her work and the team's credibility.

If she insists on including them, the fallback is clear labeling in a separate section, with no mixing and no attributed quotes that look like interview excerpts. I'd still advise against even that if the summary is meant to inform a decision.
