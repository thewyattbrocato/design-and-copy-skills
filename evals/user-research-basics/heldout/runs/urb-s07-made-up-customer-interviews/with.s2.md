**No. Don't use the chatbot interviews as customer quotes.** Your instinct to check was right.

**Why not**
- Nobody said those words. A chatbot produced plausible text about pet insurance. It has no pets, no vet bills, and no experience of deciding whether to buy a plan. The quotes read well because the model is good at sounding like a pet owner.
- Leadership will read quotes as evidence of what customers think. If the quotes sit next to your four real ones, readers can't tell which is which. Leadership could make pricing or positioning decisions on opinions nobody holds.
- The 12 outnumber your 4 three to one, so they would dominate the picture. Because the chatbot writes fluent, agreeable answers, they would probably also be more favorable and more tidy than real people.
- If it came out later that some "customers" were generated, it would undermine the whole summary, including your four real interviews.

**What the summary can use instead**
1. **Your four real interviews, as the evidence.** Report them honestly as "4 of 4" or "3 of 4", not as percentages, and note who you spoke to and how they were recruited. Four is a small sample, so present the findings as early themes. Single-source points are leads, not conclusions.
2. **Other real evidence that already exists.** Support tickets, sales or call notes, reviews, search queries, and survey comments from current customers are all real. They're usually quicker to pull than new interviews.
3. **The chatbot output, clearly labeled, if she wants to keep it.** It can serve as a source of hypotheses, such as questions to ask or objections to probe. Put it in a separate section or appendix, for example "AI-generated hypotheses, not customer data". It shouldn't contain attributed quotes or counts. Anything it raises that matters should go on a list of things to check with real people.
4. **A "what we still don't know" section, with the cheapest next step.** For example, 4 to 6 more real conversations with pet owners about the last time they paid a vet bill or looked at insurance. A human has to recruit and run them, and that could be you or a recruited panel. That fills the gap the chatbot was filling, and it gives leadership something real to approve.

**Keeping it from becoming a fight**
- Start from her goal. She wants more voices in the summary, and that's reasonable. Say something like: "I'd love to use these, but leadership will treat quotes as real customer voice. Can we label them as hypotheses and use them to sharpen what we ask in the next round of interviews?"
- Point out that this protects her. If the summary is challenged on Monday, "every quote is a real customer" is a position she'll want to be in.
- Offer to do the work. You could draft the summary with the four interviews and the hypotheses section, plus a one-line proposal for the next round of interviews.
