No, I wouldn't use them as quotes next to the real interviews. A chatbot's "pet owners" aren't customers, so the quotes aren't evidence of what any customer thinks. They reflect what the model predicts a pet owner would plausibly say. That's why they read so well: they're fluent and on-theme, and they tend to agree with whatever the prompt implied. Leadership could make pricing or positioning decisions based on "12 customers said X" when nobody said it. Putting them next to the four real interviews would also make the real ones look like part of a larger, consistent sample. If it came out later, it would damage trust in the whole summary, and in your research and the marketing lead's.

**What the summary can use instead**

- **Your four real interviews, presented as what they are.** Say plainly that it's a small qualitative sample (n=4), report themes and direct quotes, and note where it's directional rather than conclusive. Leadership can handle that. They can't handle finding out later that the sample was padded.
- **Other real customer signal you may already have:**
  - Support tickets and chat logs
  - Sales or call notes
  - Reviews of competitor pet insurance plans
  - Survey free-text responses
  - Social or forum posts from pet owners
- **A fast top-up of real interviews or a short survey**, if the summary needs more weight. Even 5–6 more short calls with recruited pet owners would help a lot. You could also propose this as a next step.

**If she wants to keep the chatbot work**

It's fine as a brainstorming tool. It can help draft interview questions, surface objections to test, or suggest hypotheses. If it appears in the summary at all, put it in a separate, clearly labeled section such as "Hypotheses generated with AI, not yet validated with customers." Don't quote it as if people said it.

**Raising it without a fight**

You could say something like: "These read really well, but since they're not from actual customers, I'm worried leadership will read them as evidence. Can we use my four real interviews for quotes and put the AI material in as hypotheses to test? I'd also like to line up a few more real interviews if we have time." That treats her work as useful and puts the concern on the risk to the summary, not on her.
