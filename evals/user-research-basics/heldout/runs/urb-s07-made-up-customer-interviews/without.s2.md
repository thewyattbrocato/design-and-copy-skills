No, I wouldn't put the chatbot-generated quotes in the summary as customer evidence. Leadership will read quotes as things real customers said, and these aren't.

**Why it's a problem**
- **They're not evidence.** The chatbot has no experience with your plan. It produces what pet owners plausibly would say, which tends to be fluent, coherent, and agreeable. That's why they read so well. Real interviews are messier and more contradictory, and they sometimes surprise you.
- **They can mislead a decision.** If leadership makes pricing, positioning, or investment calls partly on invented sentiment, nobody can trace those calls back to a real customer.
- **They would swamp your data.** Twelve synthetic interviews next to four real ones means most of the quotes, and probably the most quotable ones, would be fabricated.
- **It's a credibility risk.** If someone later finds out, it undermines the whole summary, including your four real interviews.

**What the summary can use instead**
1. **Your four real interviews**, quoted and attributed as real. Say plainly that n=4, so it's directional rather than conclusive.
2. **Existing real data**, if you have it. Examples are survey responses, support tickets, sales call notes, reviews, and competitor-plan feedback. These can fill out the picture cheaply.
3. **More real interviews.** If Monday is too soon, flag it as a next step. Even 4–6 more would make a real difference.
4. **The synthetic interviews, if used at all, kept separate.** They can legitimately help generate hypotheses, draft interview questions, or spot topics you hadn't thought to ask about. If she wants them included, put them in a clearly labeled appendix or a "hypotheses to test" section, with no quotes presented as customer voices.

**How to raise it without a fight**
Frame it as protecting her work and the team, not as rejecting it. For example:

> "The AI interviews are a great idea-generator, and I'd like to use them to shape what we test next. But since leadership will read quotes as real customer voice, I'd rather keep the summary to the four real interviews and list the AI themes as hypotheses to validate. That way nobody can question the findings later."

That gives her a way to keep her contribution in the document, in an honest form. If she still wants them mixed in, it's worth asking your manager or whoever owns research standards, rather than settling it between the two of you.
