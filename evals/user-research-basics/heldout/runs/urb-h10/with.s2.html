Paddlepoint: kayak renter interview guide

40 min · one person at a time · Participant code: ______   Date: ________
Goal (for you, not read aloud): Learn how people find, book, and show up for a kayak rental today, and where it goes wrong. Gather stories about the last real time. Don't pitch, demo, or ask what features they want.
Before you start: Recording permission asked Notes template open Phone silenced Not driving (if remote)

1. Intro & consent 0–4 min

"Thanks for making time. I'm learning how people rent kayaks from local outfitters. There are no right answers, and I'm not selling anything. I'm here to listen. Is it OK if I record so I can focus on you instead of typing? Only our small team will hear it, I'll keep your name out of notes, and you can skip any question or stop whenever you like."
Wait for a clear yes before recording.

2. Warm-up 4–8 min

  1. Tell me a bit about yourself and how paddling fits into your life.
  2. When did you last rent a kayak? Where was it, and who was with you?

3. The last rental, start to finish 8–28 min

Core of the session. Let them tell it in order; follow their thread.

  1. Take me back to when you first decided to go. What happened? What made you pick that day?
  2. How did you find that outfitter? What else did you look at? What made you choose them?
  3. Walk me through how you booked, step by step. What did you use? What did you do next? What were you unsure about?
  4. What did you need to know before you'd commit? Did you find it? Where? What did you do if not?
  5. Tell me about the day itself, from arriving to heading out. What was smooth? What was slow or confusing?
  6. Did anything change from your plan: weather, group size, timing, gear? What did you do? Who did you contact, and how?
  7. Tell me about a time a rental didn't go the way you wanted. What happened next?

4. Wider picture 28–35 min

  1. Think of the last time you chose between two places to rent. How did you decide?
  2. How do you handle payment and deposits, and what happened the last time you had to cancel or reschedule?
  3. Who else was involved in planning? How did you coordinate?

5. Close 35–40 min

  1. Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?
  2. May I follow up if I have a quick question later?

Thank them. Stop recording. Name the file: paddlepoint_P#_date.

Probes (use constantly)

  • "Say more about that."  ·  "What happened next?"
  • "Why was that?"  ·  "Can you show me / describe what you saw?"
  • Pause 5 seconds. Silence is your best follow-up.
  • Reuse their words. Don't supply your own labels.

Avoid

  • Don't name or describe Paddlepoint, or ask "would you use / like / pay for…"
  • Don't ask about the future or "usually". Ask about the last time.
  • Don't ask yes/no questions. Ask "what happened?"
  • Don't rescue them from silence or agree and share your own stories.
  • Stop if they're driving or distressed.

Note-taking (mark in the margin)

Do: write what they did and said, with exact quotes in "quotes".   Flag: ! pain/workaround   ? follow up   $ money/deposit   T a tool they used (phone, site, text, social).   Keep your interpretations separate, in brackets.
After each session: save the recording and notes, code the person as P1, P2…, and write 3 lines on what surprised you. Report counts as "3 of 7", not percentages. If sessions stop surprising you, you've heard enough; if they still do, book a few more.
