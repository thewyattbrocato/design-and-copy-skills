Kayak renter interview guide

40 min · one person at a time · Participant: ______   Date: ______
Goal: learn how people actually find, book, pay for and change a kayak rental with a small outfitter, and where it goes wrong. Decision this feeds: [write yours here, e.g. which booking problem to solve first]. Ask about the last real time, not opinions or predictions.

1. Open & consent 0:00 – 0:05

"Thanks for making time. I'm researching how people rent kayaks. There are no right answers, and I didn't build anything you'll be asked to judge. May I record audio for my notes only? You can skip any question or stop anytime."
  • Wait for a clear yes before recording. Start recording.
  • Do not mention Paddlepoint or any product.

2. Warm-up 0:05 – 0:10

  1. Tell me about yourself and how kayaking fits into your life.
  2. When did you last rent a kayak? Where, and with whom?

3. The last rental, start to finish 0:10 – 0:28  (core)

  1. Take me back to the moment you decided to rent. What was going on?
  2. How did you find that outfitter? What else did you look at?
  3. Walk me through how you reserved it, step by step. What did you do first? Then?
  4. How did you know the kayak and time you wanted were available?
  5. How did payment or a deposit work?
  6. What happened on the day: arrival, check-in, waiver, gear, return?
  7. Was there a point where something was unclear, slow, or didn't go as planned? What happened?
Probes: "Tell me more about that." · "What happened next?" · "Why did you do it that way?" · "Can you show me the message/page/text?" · then silence for 5 seconds.

4. Changes & the rest 0:28 – 0:36

  1. Tell me about a time you had to change or cancel a rental (weather, group size, time). What happened?
  2. Tell me about a time a rental didn't work out, or you gave up on one.
  3. How did you rent before that time? What's different about renting from a small shop vs. a larger one?

5. Wrap up 0:36 – 0:40

  1. Is there anything about renting a kayak I didn't ask about that I should know?
  2. Who else do you know who rents kayaks that I might talk to?
Thank them. Confirm any incentive. Stop recording. Spend 5 minutes right after writing down what surprised you.

Ask it neutrally

AvoidSay instead
"Would you use an app to book?""How did you book last time?"
"Was booking easy?""Walk me through booking."
"What features do you want?""What did you do when it didn't work?"
"Do you usually book online?""Tell me about the last time you booked."

Rules for a first-timer

  • You talk ~20%, they talk ~80%.
  • One question at a time. Then wait.
  • Don't help, defend, or explain. Don't pitch.
  • If they say "usually" or "I'd probably", ask: "Think of the last time. What happened?"
  • Skip questions if a story runs long. Section 3 matters most.
  • Stop if they're driving, distressed, or uncomfortable.

Before you start

  • ☐ Participant rented a kayak in the last ~12 months from a small outfitter [adjust window]
  • ☐ Recording app tested
  • ☐ Quiet spot, phone silenced
  • ☐ Clock visible; this sheet printed
  • ☐ Notes page open, one per person

Listen for (jot below)

  • Steps they take, in order
  • Tools: phone, email, site, social, paper
  • Workarounds and waiting
  • Moments of doubt or surprise
  • Their exact words
Notes / quotes / time stamps:
Count what you hear as "3 of 7 people", not percentages. Keep the sample's bias in mind: these are people you could reach. Stop adding interviews when new ones stop changing your conclusions.
