Interview guide: people who rent kayaks from small outfitters

40 minutes, one person at a time. Session code: P___   Date: ________   Start time: ______   Recording consent: ☐ yes ☐ no

Goal (for you, not to read aloud): Learn how renters actually find, book, change and show up for a kayak rental today, and where it goes wrong. Output: a list of real steps and trouble spots, each tied to who said it. Don't pitch or mention the booking tool. Don't ask what they'd want or use.

1 · Intro and consent (3 min)

“Thanks for making time. I'm talking with people about how they rent outdoor gear like kayaks. I'm trying to learn from what you've actually done, so there are no right or wrong answers, and you can't say anything wrong about me. I'll take notes, and with your OK I'd like to record so I don't miss anything. Only I [and my note-taker] will hear it. It's kept until ___ and your name won't appear in anything I share. You can skip any question or stop whenever you like. Is it OK to record?”

Start recording only after a clear yes. If they're driving or distracted, offer to reschedule.

2 · Warm-up (5 min)

  • What do you do outdoors, and how does paddling fit in?
  • Roughly how many times in the past year have you rented a kayak? Where?
  • Who do you usually go with?

3 · Last rental, start to finish (20 min)

A“Tell me about the last time you rented a kayak. Start from when the idea came up.”
Then let them talk. Follow with: what happened next · say more about that · why was that
B“How did you end up choosing that outfitter?”
What else did you look at? What did you need to know, and where did you find it? Anything you couldn't find?
C“Walk me through how you reserved it.”
What did you do first? Phone, site, message, walk-up? What did you have to confirm (times, group size, gear, price, weather)? How did you know it was set?
D“Who else was involved, and how did you sort it out with them?”
Who decided? Who paid? How did the group count or timing get settled?
E“Tell me about the day itself, from arriving to returning the boats.”
What did you do on arrival? Paperwork, payment, waiting? What didn't go as you expected?

4 · Changes and near-misses (7 min)

F“Tell me about a time a rental plan changed.”
Weather, group size, running late, cancelling. What did you do? Who did you contact, how? What happened to the money or the slot?
G“Tell me about a time you wanted to rent a kayak and it didn't happen.”
What got in the way? What did you do instead?
H“What do you do now when you need to check availability or ask a question?”
What do you use instead of a website, if anything?

5 · Close (5 min)

  • “Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?”
  • “Would it be OK to follow up if I have a small question?”
  • Thank them. Stop recording. Give the incentive: ______

Running it

  • Stories over opinions. Pull them back to “what did you do?”
  • Wait. After they answer, count to five before the next question.
  • Use their words. If they say “slot”, you say “slot”.
  • Don't help, nod along, or share your own rental stories.
  • The guide is a checklist. Let them wander, then return to anything uncovered in the last 10 min.
  • If they ask about your project, say “I'll tell you at the end.” Then do.
  • Running long? Cut D and H first. Never cut A or F.
Note tags (write quotes word for word): F finding   B booking   C change/cancel   $ payment   D day-of   W workaround   ! frustration. Keep what happened separate from what you think it means.
Right after each session: save the recording and notes under the session code (P1, P2…) with no names · write 3 lines on what surprised you · jot what you'd change in the guide. After ~5 sessions: report counts as “3 of 5”, not percentages, and say who is missing from your sample (e.g. people who never rent). Stop when new sessions stop changing what you've learned.
