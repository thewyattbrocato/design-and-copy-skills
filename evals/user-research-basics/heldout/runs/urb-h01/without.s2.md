**Reframe "validate" first.** In two weeks with no budget, you can't prove people will pay. You can find out whether there's a real problem, whether the add-on is plausible, and whether a cheap real-behavior test is worth running. Tell your VP that now, so you don't end up presenting weak evidence as validation.

## Week 1: use what you already have (about 3 days of your time)

1. **Mine your data.** Pull the following:
   - How often do vet visits, injuries, or damage disputes happen? Check support tickets, sitter reports, and any existing guarantee claims.
   - What do those incidents cost?
   - What's the average booking value? A $15 add-on on a $40 booking is a very different ask than on a $400 one.
   - Which segments carry the risk (older dogs, multi-night stays, puppies)?
2. **Talk to support and a few sitters.** Ask them what happens when something goes wrong and who pays. This is the fastest read on whether the pain exists.
3. **Interview 8–10 customers.** Email people who booked in the last 30–60 days. A small booking credit as thanks is probably your only cost. Run 20-minute calls on these questions:
   - Tell me about the last time something went wrong, or you worried it might.
   - What did you do about it?
   - Do you already have pet insurance? Did it come up when booking?
   
   Don't pitch the product or ask "would you buy this?" People say yes to that and then don't buy.
4. **Include a few people who bounced or cancelled.** They tell you more about checkout friction than happy customers do.

## Week 2: one cheap behavioral test (use the designer's ~6 hours)

Build a **fake-door at checkout**: an optional add-on line with a price and one-line coverage description. Clicking it leads to an honest "Coming soon, get notified" screen.
- Show it to a small slice of traffic, not everyone, because you're adding friction to checkout.
- Track the click rate, and check whether conversion drops for the group that sees it.
- Be transparent on the landing screen. Don't take payment or imply coverage exists.
- If your volume is low, the result will be directional only. Say so.

The designer's other hours go to the add-on's checkout UI and the "coming soon" screen. Keep it to one variant.

## Questions customers can't answer

These may sink the idea regardless of demand, so get a rough read on each before the meeting:
- **Regulation and partner.** Selling insurance usually requires a licensed insurer or MGA partner. Ask legal or finance, and look at what comparable marketplaces such as Rover and Wag do. This could decide feasibility.
- **Economics.** What commission or margin would you get, and does it justify the effort?
- **Overlap.** If you already offer a booking guarantee, how is this different?
- **Trust.** Would an add-on at checkout feel like an upsell and hurt conversion?

## What to bring to the meeting

A one-pager with:
- the incident data
- themes from the interviews
- fake-door click and conversion numbers, with sample sizes
- the regulatory and partner status
- a recommendation: proceed to a real pilot, run a bigger test, or drop it

If the evidence is thin, say so. That's more useful to the planning meeting than a false "validated."
