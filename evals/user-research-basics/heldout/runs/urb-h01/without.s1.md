You can't fully validate this in two weeks with no budget. You can get enough evidence to say whether it's worth a real test, which is probably what the planning meeting needs.

## Step 1: Pin down the decision (day 1)
Ask the VP: "What would you need to see to say yes or no to this at planning?" Also pin down what the product is. Pet insurance can mean vet-bill coverage, coverage for incidents during a stay (sitter liability, injury, trip cancellation), or a partner-underwritten product. Each has different customer value, and insurance is regulated, so you'll almost certainly need an underwriting or embedded-insurance partner rather than building it yourself.

## Step 2: Mine what you already have (days 1–4, free)
- **Incident data:** how often do bookings involve a vet visit, injury, escape, or damage claim? How big are the costs, and who pays today? This tells you if there's a real problem.
- **Support tickets and reviews:** search for "vet," "injured," "sick," "insurance," "who pays."
- **Checkout funnel and add-on behavior:** if you have tips, extras, or cancellation protection, look at the attach rate and price sensitivity. That's the closest proxy for willingness to pay.
- **Booking value:** a $12 add-on on a $40 stay is a different conversation from one on a $400 stay.

## Step 3: Talk to 8–10 people (days 3–10, free)
- Recruit through a short in-app or email ask to recent bookers, with a small credit or a thank-you if you can swing one.
- Aim for a mix: first-time and repeat owners, owners of older or higher-risk dogs, and 2–3 sitters, since sitters are the ones who'd deal with an incident.
- Keep each call to 20 minutes and focus on past behavior:
  - "Tell me about the last time something went wrong, or you worried, while your dog was with a sitter."
  - "Do you have pet insurance now? How did you choose it?"
  - "What did you do to feel comfortable at booking?"
- Don't pitch the add-on until the end. Then ask for reactions, not "would you buy this?" People overstate intent.

## Step 4: Use your designer's ~6 hours on a fake-door test
- Spend about 2–3 hours on a realistic checkout mock of the add-on, with price and coverage summary.
- Run it as either a small live "coverage coming soon, get notified" test on a slice of checkouts, or as a prototype shown in the interviews.
- Measure opt-in clicks and drop-off at checkout.
- Be honest in the copy: don't take payment or imply coverage exists. Check this with whoever owns legal or compliance, since insurance messaging has its own rules.

## Step 5: Check feasibility early (in parallel, 1–2 emails)
Ask one or two embedded pet-insurance providers about revenue share, minimums, licensing, and integration time. A great customer signal means little if the partner economics or timeline don't work.

## What to bring to the meeting
One page covering:
1. What the data says about the problem's frequency and size.
2. What interviews said, as patterns with a few quotes.
3. Fake-door results, with the caveat that it's directional.
4. Partner feasibility and rough unit economics.
5. What you still don't know, and your recommendation. That will probably be "run a small live pilot with a partner" or "not now," not "validated."

If the VP expected a clean yes, tell them early that two weeks and no budget buys you a de-risking read, not proof.
