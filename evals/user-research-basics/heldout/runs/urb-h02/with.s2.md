A usability test watches a handful of real people try to complete tasks with your design, so you see where they get confused and, through their comments and behavior, learn why. An A/B test ships two versions to many users at once and measures which one performs better on a specific number, like sign-ups or clicks. Usability tests are small, qualitative, and great for finding problems early, even on rough prototypes. A/B tests are large, quantitative, and tell you which option wins, but not why. Most teams use usability testing to shape ideas, then A/B testing to validate them.
