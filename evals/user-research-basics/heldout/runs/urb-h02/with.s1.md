A usability test watches a handful of real people try to complete tasks with your design, so you learn *why* they get confused, hesitate, or succeed. It's qualitative and works even on rough prototypes. An A/B test ships two versions to many live users at once and measures which one performs better on a specific metric, like sign-ups or clicks. It tells you *which* version wins, but not why. Think of usability testing as sitting beside someone to observe their experience, and A/B testing as letting thousands of people vote with their behavior. Good product teams use both together.
