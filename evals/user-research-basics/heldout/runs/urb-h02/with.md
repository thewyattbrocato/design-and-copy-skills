A usability test watches a handful of real people try to use your design, so you learn *why* something confuses them or works well. It's qualitative and works even on a rough prototype. An A/B test shows two live versions to lots of users at once and measures which one gets more of a specific behavior, like clicks or signups. It tells you *which* version wins, but not why. Designers often use usability tests to find problems and shape ideas, then A/B tests to confirm that a change actually moves the numbers.
