**Lead with decisions, not a list of findings.** Product teams act when they see a ranked list with a specific ask on each item. They stall on eight parallel observations.

## Structure

**1. One-slide summary (top of the deck)**
- Two or three sentences: "People can complete the core purchase, but seat selection is failing in ways that cost money and exclude users. Here are the three things we recommend fixing first."
- A small table of the top 3 to 4 issues, each with severity and the ask.

**2. Fix now: blockers (one slide each)**

- **Seat selection**
  - Merge the seat picker and screen reader findings into one story. They're the same screen.
  - Evidence: 4/5 couldn't tell which seats were available, 2/5 bought the wrong deck, and the screen reader user couldn't complete selection at all.
  - Add a 20 to 30 second clip or a screenshot with annotations.
  - Ask: redesign availability states, and make the picker accessible. Name an owner.
  - The accessibility part is a hard blocker, not a nice-to-have. Flag any legal or compliance exposure if it applies to you.
- **Card entry error**
  - 1/5 hit an error and abandoned.
  - It's one person, but it's a lost sale at the last step. Before Monday, check the logs or session recording to see whether it's reproducible.
  - Ask: engineering investigates, with a date to report back.

**3. Fix soon: discoverability (one combined slide)**
- Promo code: 3/5 didn't notice it, and 1 said they'd have used one.
- Terminal map link: 2/5 missed it, both first-time visitors. That's the audience that most needs it.
- Ask: make both more prominent. These are cheap to fix.

**4. Watch list (one slide, brief)**
- Date picker: 1/5 chose the wrong month, noticed, and fixed it. The design recovered from the error, so no action is needed unless it shows up again.
- Home screen colors: 2/5 said "a bit dull." It's subjective and didn't affect any task. Park it for the next visual refresh.

**5. What's working**
- Confirmation page: 5/5 found their ticket. Say so explicitly. It builds goodwill and tells the team what not to touch.

## Tips so it lands
- **Rank by consequence, not by count.** Wrong purchases and abandoned payments outrank a 2/5 opinion about colors.
- **Report raw counts ("4 of 5"), not percentages.** With 5 people the data is qualitative. Say on one line that this is directional evidence, and that the severity comes from what happened (money lost, task blocked), not from the numbers.
- **Every finding gets a fix and an owner.** Use the format *What happened → Why it matters → Recommendation → Decision needed Monday.*
- **Use a short clip or quote for the top issue.** Watching someone buy the wrong deck persuades people more than any chart.
- **Put the full details in an appendix**, with per-participant notes, so the main deck stays to about 5 slides.
