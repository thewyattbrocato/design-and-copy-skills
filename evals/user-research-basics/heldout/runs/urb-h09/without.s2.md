Organize the findings by what the team should do about them, not by screen or task order. Five-person studies are good at surfacing problems and bad at sizing them, so the layout should lead with the decisions you need.

**1. Opening slide: the three asks**
Put the asks first, each with an owner or a request for one. For example:
- Redesign seat availability states and fix screen reader support in the seat picker.
- Investigate the card-entry error (check logs to see if it's a bug).
- Make the promo field and terminal map link more visible.

**2. Prioritized findings table**
Use one row per issue, with columns for evidence, impact, recommendation, and severity.

| Priority | Issue | Evidence | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fix now | Seat picker: availability unclear | 4/5 confused, 2 bought the wrong deck | Wrong purchases mean refunds, support load, and lost trust |
| Fix now | Seat picker: not screen-reader accessible | 1/1 screen reader user couldn't finish | Total blocker, and possibly a compliance risk |
| Fix now / verify | Card entry error | 1/5 abandoned | Lost revenue. Check logs to tell whether it's a bug or a one-off. |
| Next | Promo field is hard to notice | 3/5 missed it, 1 would have used a code | Possible conversion and revenue effect |
| Next | Terminal map link is easy to miss | 2/5 missed it, both first-time visitors | Hits your newest users |
| Watch | Date picker month error | 1/5, self-corrected | Low harm, but worth monitoring |
| Backlog | Home screen colors "a bit dull" | 2/5, subjective | Taste, not a task failure |

Treat the seat picker as one problem with two parts, since the same redesign likely fixes both. The accessibility finding is one participant, but a total blocker doesn't need a bigger sample to matter. Say that explicitly so nobody dismisses it as n=1.

**3. One slide per "fix now" issue**
Each slide should have a short video clip or screenshot of the moment things went wrong, one participant quote, and a proposed fix or next step. Clips persuade people more than counts do.

**4. What worked**
Give this one slide. The confirmation page was 5/5 and the date picker self-corrected. It shows the team what to keep, and it makes the rest of your findings read as balanced.

**5. Close with decisions needed**
List who owns what and by when, and what you need from the room on Monday.

**Framing tips**
- Report counts ("4 of 5"), not percentages, and say once that this is qualitative research with 5 participants. The point is to find problems, not to measure how often they occur.
- Don't weight severity by headcount alone. The payment abandonment and the screen reader failure are 1 person each but cost the most.
- Put the home screen colors and the date picker in an appendix or backlog row. Giving them equal space dilutes the urgent items.
- Send the table as a pre-read so Monday's meeting goes to discussion and assigning owners.
