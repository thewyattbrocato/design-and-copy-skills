## The pattern
- **Pitching:** you described features in the middle of an interview.
- **Leading words:** "chaotic," "never double-book," and "wouldn't it be easier."
- **Reacting to answers:** the "great, that's what we hoped" line rewards agreement.
- **Hypotheticals:** "would you use" asks for predictions, not facts.
- **Missed leads:** his driving-origin question and his past app trial were the most informative moments, and you moved past both.

## What the data supports
From this stretch you have three things: he books by text and a paper diary, he has tried a scheduling app before, and he found $40/month too much. The "handy," "I guess so," and "huh, okay" responses are not evidence of interest.

## A better skeleton
1. "Walk me through the last time someone booked a tuning with you."
2. "What happened between that text and you arriving?"
3. "Tell me about the last time something went wrong with timing or a double-booking."
4. "How did you decide where to drive from that day?"
5. "You mentioned trying an app. What led you to try it, and what made you stop?"
6. Use silence and "tell me more." Show the product only at the end, if at all.
