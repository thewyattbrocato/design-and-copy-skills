Your lead is right, and it starts early. Here are the problems in order, with a rewrite for each.

**1. "right, and that gets pretty chaotic I imagine?"**
This is a leading question. You supplied the pain point ("chaotic") and he just agreed with it ("sometimes, yeah"). His answer was lukewarm, so the problem may not be that big for him.
- Better: "Tell me about the last time a booking got tricky." or "What's the hardest part of that process?"

**2. "we've got a feature where clients pick a slot themselves... would you use that?"**
You pitched your solution and asked a hypothetical. People say yes to "would you use X?" to be polite, and it tells you little about what they actually do. You also moved from learning about him to selling.
- Better: Stay on his current behavior. "Walk me through the last time someone wanted to rebook. What happened?" or "Have you ever double-booked or had to reshuffle a day? What happened?" Concept testing can come later, once you understand the problem.

**3. "it uses your home address and adds travel time automatically, so you never double-book."**
His question ("how does it know where I'm driving from?") was a real clue. It suggests he thinks about travel between jobs, and a mobile tuner may not start from home each time. You answered with a sales claim ("you never double-book") instead of exploring it.
- Better: "Where do you usually drive from on a given day?" or "How do you work out travel time between jobs now?"

**4. "great, that's exactly what we were hoping to hear!"**
This tells him which answers you want. He said "that sounds handy," which is weak, and you treated it as validation. After this he's likely to keep pleasing you.
- Better: Stay neutral. "Okay. What would make it handy, or not?" Better still, ask for a specific past example.

**5. "actually most of them are around 15 now, it's gotten a lot cheaper."**
You argued with his experience. He raised something useful: he tried a similar app, and it cost $40 a month. You corrected him and shut it down, and "huh, okay" was him backing off.
- Better: "Tell me about that app. What made you try it? What happened with it?" His past attempt is your best evidence about what he'll pay for and why he did or didn't stay.

**6. "and wouldn't it be easier if reminders went out automatically too?"**
This is a leading question with a built-in answer, plus a feature pitch. "I guess so, sure" is a polite shrug, not interest.
- Better: "How do you handle reminders and no-shows today?" If it's not a problem, he won't have much to say.

**The pattern**
- You told him the problems (chaotic, double-booking, easier) instead of letting him name them.
- You asked about hypothetical use ("would you use...") instead of past behavior ("tell me about the last time...").
- You pitched and defended the product, which turned a research conversation into a sales call.
- You reacted to answers you liked and corrected ones you didn't.

**Positive:** Your opener ("how do you usually book your tuning visits?") is good. It's open and about his real behavior, and it got you the paper diary and the texting. You could have followed up on those: "What happens after someone texts you?" and "What do you do when the diary and a text conflict?"

**A habit to practice:** After each answer, ask a follow-up like "Can you tell me more about that?" or "What happened next?" before moving on. That replaces most of the leading you did here.
