# Moderator script: pothole report form usability test

Lines in quotes are what you say. Lines in *[italics]* are actions for you. Read it almost word for word. Moderators do that all the time, and participants won't mind.

## Before the session

**Do these before next week:**
- **Get recording consent in writing, ahead of time.** Nobody has agreed yet, so add a line to the invitation email: *"With your permission, we'd like to record the video call so we can review it later. The recording is only seen by our project team. You can say no and still take part and still get the voucher."* Then ask again out loud on the call (see below). Check with whoever handles research or privacy policy at the city about retention and who can see the recordings.
- **Tell participants the PM will be there.** Put it in the invitation too. The script introduces them, so nobody finds out mid-session.
- **Test the link yourself** in a fresh browser. Keep the link in a note so you can re-paste it fast.
- **Decide what you'll do if someone declines recording.** I'd suggest you take notes, and the PM takes notes too.
- **Check that Task 3 is possible in the prototype.** I don't know how your prototype handles "checking later." If there's no status screen, you'll need a scenario that gets them to one. I've scripted it as a scenario below. Adjust it to match what's actually clickable.

## 0:00–0:05 Welcome and consent

*[Before they join, have the recording off, the link ready, and the PM's camera and mic off.]*

> "Hi, thanks so much for joining. Can you hear and see me okay?"

> "My name is [name], and I work on the [city] website team. Today we're testing a new version of the form for reporting potholes. I'll explain how this works, and you can ask me anything along the way.
>
> The most important thing to know is that **we're testing the form, not you.** You can't get anything wrong. If something is confusing, that's useful for us, because it means the form needs fixing.
>
> This should take about 30 minutes. Afterward, we'll email you a $25 grocery voucher today. That's yours whether or not you finish everything, and even if you decide to stop early.
>
> A couple of things before we start. First, my colleague [PM's name] is on the call. They have their camera and microphone off. They're just here to listen and take notes. They won't interrupt.
>
> Second, **we'd like to record this call**, so I can focus on talking with you instead of typing notes. The recording is only used by our project team to review how the form worked, and [state retention and who sees it, per city policy]. Recording is completely optional. If you'd rather not, just say so, and you'll still get the voucher and take part like normal. Are you okay with me recording?"

*[If yes: start recording, then say:]*

> "Okay, I've started recording. Just to confirm for the record: you're okay with us recording this session, yes?"

*[If no: "No problem at all, thank you for telling me. I'll take notes instead." Don't record.]*

> "One last thing: you can stop at any time, for any reason, and you don't need to explain. Any questions before we start?"

## 0:05–0:08 Warm-up

> "Let's start with a few quick questions."

- "Have you ever reported a problem to the city, like a pothole, a streetlight, or something else? How did you do it?"
- "How comfortable do you generally feel using websites to get things done, like paying a bill or filling out a form?"

*[Just listen. Don't evaluate their answers.]*

## 0:08–0:10 Setting up the prototype

> "I'm going to paste a link into the chat. It opens an early version of the form. Some parts might not work yet, because it's a prototype. If something looks odd, it's not you.
>
> Please **share your screen** so I can see what you're doing. [Help if needed.] 
>
> One more request: as you go, **please think out loud.** Tell me what you're looking at, what you expect to happen, and what you're thinking, even small stuff like 'hm, I'm not sure what this is.' There are no wrong or silly comments.
>
> I may not answer questions while you're doing the tasks, and that's on purpose. I want to see what you'd do if I weren't here. If you get really stuck, I'll help, and nothing will go wrong."

*[Paste the link in the chat. Wait until their screen is shared and the prototype is open.]*

## 0:10–0:17 Task 1: report a pothole

> "Think of a pothole you've seen on your street or nearby. If you can't think of one, pick any street you know. If you'd prefer not to use your own address, a nearby cross street or any other address is fine.
>
> Using this form, **report that pothole to the city.** Go ahead, and remember to think out loud."

**Do not mention the map.** Don't say "pin," "locate," or "click." The prototype breaks if they tap the map before typing an address, and your wording shouldn't lead them there.

**Neutral prompts while they work:**
- "What are you thinking right now?"
- "What would you expect to happen next?"
- "What are you looking for?"

**If they tap the map and it breaks:**

> "Looks like the prototype hiccuped. That's the prototype's fault, not yours. Let me paste the link again so we can restart."

*[Paste the link. Then:]*

> "Let's pick up from the start of the task. Same thing: report the pothole."

Don't pretend nothing happened. Don't hint at what to do differently. If they ask "did I do something wrong?", say **"No, you didn't. This is an early version and it has rough spots."** Note the moment for yourself ("tapped map first, tried to select location"). It's still real data about what people expect the map to do, even though the prototype can't handle it. If they break it twice, move on and tell them to type the address, and note that you intervened.

**When they finish:**

> "Thanks. How did that feel? Was anything confusing or surprising?"

## 0:17–0:23 Task 2: add a photo

> "Imagine you took a photo of that pothole on your phone. Using the form, **add a photo to your report.** You don't need a real photo. Just show me what you'd do."

*[Adjust this if the prototype uses a simulated upload. Confirm how it behaves beforehand.]*

> "How did that go? Anything you expected to see that wasn't there?"

## 0:23–0:27 Task 3: check whether it's fixed

> "Now imagine it's about two weeks later and you're wondering whether the city has fixed that pothole. **Show me how you'd find out.** Start wherever you'd normally start."

*[Let them wander. Where they start tells you a lot. If they're truly stuck for 60+ seconds, say: "What would you try next?" Then, if needed, "Let's say you've gotten as far as you'd get. I'll show you where we expected people to go."]*

> "What did you expect to see when you got there?"

## 0:27–0:30 Wrap-up

> "That's all the tasks. A few last questions."

- "Overall, how did the form feel to use?"
- "What was the most confusing or frustrating part?"
- "Was there anything you liked?"
- "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"
- "Anything we didn't ask about that you'd like to tell us?"

> "Thank you, this has been really helpful. I'll send your $25 voucher to the email we have for you [today / within the hour]. If you don't see it by tomorrow, reply to my email and I'll sort it out.
>
> [PM's name], do you have any questions before we wrap up?"

*[PM may ask one or two questions, or just say thanks.]*

> "Okay, I'm going to stop the recording now. Thanks again."

*[Stop the recording. Send the voucher right after the call, while you remember.]*

## Your cheat sheet for "is this right?" and "what does this button do?"

This is the part you're worried about. You don't have to answer these questions, and you shouldn't. Turn the question back to the participant with a calm, neutral line. Memorize two or three of these.

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Is this right?" | "What would you expect to see if it were right?" or "There's no right or wrong. What do you think?" |
| "What does this button do?" | "What do you think it does?" Then: "What makes you think that?" |
| "Should I click here?" | "What would you do if I weren't here?" |
| "Did I do that correctly?" | "You're doing great. Tell me what you were hoping would happen." |
| "I'm not sure what to do." | "What are you looking at? What would you try?" |
| "Am I done?" | "Is that how you'd expect it to look when you're done?" |
| "Is it supposed to do that?" | "What did you expect it to do?" |
| *(a long silence)* | Wait 5–10 seconds. Then: "What's going through your mind?" |
| "This is dumb / I'm bad at this." | "You're not. If it's hard, that tells us something about the form." |

**Rules of thumb:**
- **Silence is fine.** Count to five in your head before you say anything. They'll usually keep going.
- **Don't defend the design,** even if you have the answer. "Oh, that's supposed to…" will bias everything after it.
- **Ask about what they did, not what they'd like.** "What did you expect?" is better than "Would you want X?"
- **If you truly freeze,** say: "That's a great question. Let me note it down and I'll come back to it at the end." Then write it down. You've lost nothing.
- **At the end,** answer any questions they had. They'll be curious, and it's fair to tell them.

## If things go wrong

- **The prototype won't load:** "Let me paste the link again. Can you try a different browser?" If it still fails after about 3 minutes, switch to talking through how they'd report a pothole today, and still send the voucher.
- **They run out of time:** Skip to the wrap-up questions. Task 3 is the one to drop, if you have to.
- **They go quiet or seem uncomfortable:** "We can take a break or stop whenever you like." Stopping doesn't affect the voucher.
- **The PM unmutes or speaks without being asked:** Don't worry about it. Acknowledge them briefly ("Sure, go ahead") and move on. Agree beforehand that the PM sends questions to you by private chat instead.

Since this is your first session, I'd suggest a 10-minute dry run with a colleague playing the participant. Have them ask "is this right?" three times. That's the best fix for the freezing worry.
