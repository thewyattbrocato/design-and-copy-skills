# Moderator script: pothole report form usability session

Lines in **bold quotes** are what you say out loud. Lines in *italics* are notes to you, so don't read them aloud. Times are rough targets for 30 minutes.

---

## Before the sessions (do this first)

Nobody has agreed to recording yet, so fix that before the call and not during it.

1. **Put it in the invitation email.** Something like: *"We'd like to record the video call so our team can review it. The recording is only used for improving this form and won't be shared outside the project team. You can say no and still take part and receive your voucher."*
2. **Check your city's rules** on recording residents (retention, where it's stored, who can view it). I don't know your local requirements, so ask your privacy or legal contact. Then fill in the bracketed parts of the consent line below.
3. **Brief your PM:** camera and mic off the whole time, no typing in the chat, and no questions to the participant. Agree that any questions go to you privately, or wait until the end.
4. **Decide your bug plan.** Open the prototype and note the exact steps that trigger the map bug. Have the link ready to re-paste.
5. **Have the link and the voucher email drafted.** Paste the link into the chat only after recording starts.

---

## Part 1: Welcome and consent (about 4 min)
*Don't start recording yet.*

**"Hi [Name], thanks so much for joining. Can you hear and see me okay?"**

**"My name is [You], and I work on the [City] website team. Today we're testing a new version of the form for reporting potholes. The session will take about 30 minutes."**

**"Before we start, I want to explain a few things."**

**"One: we're testing the website, not you. You can't do anything wrong here. If something is confusing, that tells us the design needs work, so that's useful for us."**

**"Two: there's one other person on the call. [PM name] is on our team and will be watching and taking notes. Their camera and microphone are off, and they won't speak. Are you comfortable with them being here?"**

*If no: ask the PM to leave the call. Don't negotiate.*

**"Three: I'd like to record this call, including your screen and your voice, so that I can review it instead of relying on my notes. The recording will be used only by our project team to improve this form. It will be kept [how long / where, per your policy]. You don't have to agree. If you'd rather I didn't record, that's completely fine, and you'll still get your voucher. Are you okay with me recording?"**

*If yes: say "Great, I'm starting the recording now," start it, then say: **"Just for the recording: you've agreed to be recorded, correct?"** and wait for a clear yes.*
*If no: don't record. Take notes, and your PM can too.*

**"Four: you'll receive the $25 grocery voucher by email today, however the session goes. You can stop at any time, for any reason, and you'll still get it."**

**"Last thing: please think out loud as you go. Say what you're looking at, what you expect, and what's confusing. I may not answer every question while you work, because I want to see what you'd do on your own. That's not me being rude. I'll answer anything I can at the end."**

**"Any questions before we start?"**

---

## Part 2: Warm-up (about 2 min)

**"First, a few quick questions."**

- **"Have you ever reported a problem to the city, like a pothole, a streetlight, or graffiti? How did you do it?"**
- **"How comfortable do you feel using the city's website, on a scale from 'not at all' to 'very'?"**

*Just listen and take notes. Follow up with "Tell me more about that" if something is interesting.*

---

## Part 3: Set up the prototype (about 2 min)

**"Now I'm going to paste a link in the chat. Please open it and share your screen."**

*Paste the link. Wait for the screen share.*

**"Can you see the page? Great. One heads-up: this is an early prototype, not the real website. Some parts may not work or may behave oddly. If something breaks, that's on us and not on you, so just tell me and we'll sort it out."**

---

## Part 4: Tasks (about 17 min total)

*Read each task exactly as written. Don't describe how to do it, and don't use the words on the buttons.*

### Task 1: Report a pothole (about 6 min)

**"Think of a pothole you've seen on your street, or near where you live. Imagine you want to tell the city about it. Using this prototype, please report it. Remember to talk out loud as you go."**

*Watch closely. Don't point or hint.*

*The map bug is the main risk here. The prototype breaks if they tap the map before typing an address. Two options:*

- *Prevention: this is the least biased fix. If you can, ask your PM or designer whether the first screen can open with the address field focused or the map disabled.*
- *If they tap the map and it breaks, say: **"That's a problem with the prototype, not with you. Let me send the link again. Please reload it."** Paste the link, then say: **"Let's go back to the start of the task. Where would you begin?"** Note the event as "participant expected to tap the map first", because that is real design feedback. Don't count it as a participant error.*

### Task 2: Add a photo (about 5 min)

*If they already added a photo in Task 1, skip ahead to the follow-up question. Otherwise:*

**"Now imagine you took a photo of the pothole on your phone. Please add a photo to your report."**

*If the prototype uses a placeholder photo, say: **"You don't need a real photo. Just show me what you'd do."***

### Task 3: Check whether it's been fixed (about 5 min)

**"Now imagine it's two weeks later. You'd like to know whether the city has fixed that pothole. Show me how you'd find out."**

*Don't say "status", "track", or "my reports" unless they do first.*

---

## Part 5: Wrap-up (about 4 min)

**"Thank you, that was really helpful. A few last questions."**

- **"What was the easiest part of what you did?"**
- **"What was the most confusing or frustrating?"**
- **"If you could change one thing about this form, what would it be?"**
- **"Is there anything you expected to see that wasn't there?"**

**"Do you have any questions for me? Now's the time, and I'm happy to answer whatever I can."**

*Now you can answer their earlier questions, such as "was that right?"*

**"Thanks again. I'll stop the recording now. The voucher will be in your inbox today, from [sender address]. If it doesn't arrive, email me at [address]. Take care!"**

*Stop the recording. Send the voucher right after the call, ideally before you set up the next session.*

---

## Handling "Is this right?" and "What does this button do?"

Your goal is to hand the question back without sounding cold. Memorize these three moves:

1. **Reflect it back:** **"What do you think it does?"** / **"What would you expect to happen?"**
2. **Redirect to their thinking:** **"What are you looking for?"** / **"What makes you unsure?"**
3. **Reassure:** **"There's no right or wrong, I just want to see what you'd do."**

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Is this right?" | "There's no right or wrong here. What made you choose that?" |
| "What does this button do?" | "What do you think it will do? Go ahead and try it if you like." |
| "Should I click here?" | "What would you do if I weren't here?" |
| "Did I do it correctly?" | "Tell me what you were hoping would happen." |
| "I'm stuck." | "What are you looking for right now? What have you tried?" |
| Long silence | Count to 10 silently, then: "What are you thinking?" |
| "Sorry, I'm bad at this." | "You're doing great, and this is exactly what we need to see. The design should work for everyone." |
| "Does this work on my phone?" | "Good question. I'll note it. Let's stay with this screen for now." |

**If they're truly stuck** (about 2 minutes with no progress, or visible frustration), escalate gradually:

1. **"Where would you expect to find it?"**
2. **"Take a look around the page. Is there anything that seems relevant?"**
3. **"No problem, let's move on to the next task."** Then note it as a task failure, which is useful information.

*Never say "yes", "no", "correct", or "good job" about their actions. "You're doing great" is fine, because it comments on effort and not on correctness.*

**If you freeze:** it's okay to say, **"Great question. Let me write that down and we'll come back to it."** Then look at your notes. A pause that feels long to you will feel short to them.
