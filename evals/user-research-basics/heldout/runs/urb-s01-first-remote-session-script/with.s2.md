# Moderator script: pothole form usability session (30 min)

Read the quoted lines as written. *Italics* are actions for you. [Brackets] are blanks to fill in before the session.

**Timing:** intro and consent 6 min, warm-up 2, three tasks about 16, follow-up questions 4, close 2.

---

## Before each call (not scripted)

- Check the voucher is ready to send, and fill in the blanks below.
- Ask your PM to stay muted with camera off for the whole call, and not to type in the chat. They should send you notes afterward instead.
- Have the prototype link ready to paste.
- Work out how Task 3 will work. A 30-minute session can't wait two weeks, so the prototype needs a way to show a "later" status. Decide what the person will see and how they get there.
- Decide what happens at the photo step. They may not have a pothole photo on hand, so have a sample photo ready for them to use.

---

## 1. Welcome and consent (about 6 min)

*Don't record yet.*

"Hi [name], thanks for joining. I'm [your name] from [city/department]. Can you hear and see me okay?"

"Today we're looking at an early version of a new online form for reporting potholes. It isn't finished, and some parts may not work yet. **We're testing the form, not you.** Nothing you do can be wrong, and if something is confusing, that's useful for us to know."

"I'll ask you to try a few things and to say out loud what you're thinking as you go. I'll mostly stay quiet and watch. If I don't answer a question while you're working, that's on purpose. I don't want to steer you. I'm happy to answer anything at the end."

"Before we start, two things I need your okay on."

**Observer:** "[PM name], who works on this project, is also on the call. Their camera and microphone are off, and they're just here to watch and take notes. Are you comfortable with them observing?"

**Recording:** "I'd also like to record this call, meaning your screen and your voice, so I can review it instead of relying on my notes. Only the [city project team] would see it. It would be kept for [how long] and then deleted. [Confirm these details first.] Are you okay with me recording?"

- **If yes:** *Start recording.* "Okay, it's recording now. Just so it's on the recording, are you okay with that?"
- **If no or unsure:** "No problem at all. I'll take notes instead. It doesn't change anything else." *Don't push or ask why.*

"Two more things. You can skip anything or stop at any point, for any reason. And your $25 grocery voucher is yours either way. I'll email it to you today. Which email address should I use?"

"Any questions before we begin?"

---

## 2. Warm-up (about 2 min)

"First, a practice round of thinking out loud. Look at something near you, like a mug or a desk lamp, and tell me what you notice as you look at it."

*Wait. Then:* "That's exactly what I'd like. Say whatever comes to mind, even if it seems small."

---

## 3. Set up the prototype (about 1 min)

"I'm going to paste a link in the chat. Please open it and share your screen so I can follow along."

*Paste the link. Wait.*

"I can see it, thanks. Don't click anything yet."

---

## 4. Tasks (about 16 min)

For each task, read the scenario aloud. Then ask: "Can you tell me in your own words what you're being asked to do?" If they get it wrong, the scenario needs fixing, not them. Re-read it plainly. Then say "Go ahead", and go quiet.

### Task 1: Report a pothole

"Think of a pothole you've seen on a street near you, or one you remember. You'd like the city to know about it and get it fixed. Go ahead and do that, the way you normally would."

*If they can't think of one:* "Pick any street you know that you'd pretend has one."

*Do not mention the map, the address field, or any button names.*

### Task 2: Add a photo

"You took a photo of the pothole on your phone and you'd like the city to see it too. [Tell them how the photo works in the prototype, e.g. 'Use the sample photo I'll send you.'] Go ahead."

### Task 3: Check later

"Imagine it's now [two weeks] later. You want to know whether the pothole you reported has been fixed. [One line placing them at the right screen, if needed.] Show me how you'd find out."

*If the prototype or the task doesn't fit this wording, change it now, not mid-session.*

---

## 5. Lines for when they ask you something

Say these in a friendly, even tone, and then stay quiet. Don't explain, hint, or confirm. Jot down the question and what screen they were on, because each question shows where the design didn't answer for them.

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Is this right?" / "Am I doing this correctly?" | "We're testing the form, not you, so there's no right or wrong. What would you try?" |
| "What does this button do?" | "What do you think it does?" *(If they don't know:)* "What would you expect to happen if you clicked it?" |
| "Should I click here?" | "What would you do if I weren't here?" |
| "Can you help me?" / "I'm stuck." | "That's helpful to know. Take a moment, and tell me what you're looking at and what you'd try." *(If they're really stuck:)* "What would you do next if you were at home?" |
| "Is it supposed to do that?" | "What did you expect to happen?" *(Ask this after they've finished the task, not during.)* |
| "Does this form do X?" / "Will it ever do X?" | "Good question. Hold on to it, and I'll answer anything I can at the end." |
| "Do you like it?" / "What do you think I should say?" | "Whatever you honestly think. Nothing you say will upset anyone." |
| Long silence | "Remember to say what you're thinking, even small things." *(Only after a long pause. Don't use it over and over.)* |
| They give up | "That's fine, thanks for trying. What were you expecting to see or happen?" *(Then move to the next task.)* |

Let them finish, struggle, or give up. Resist the urge to rescue them. The silence will feel long, and that's normal.

### If the prototype breaks

You know it breaks if they tap the map before typing an address. Don't warn them in advance, and don't hint at the address field.

If it happens:

"Looks like the prototype stopped working there. That's a problem with the early version, not something you did. Let me send the link again."

*Paste the link, have them reopen it, and restart from the beginning of that task.* Note what they did, but don't count it as a usability finding, since you already know about it. After that, say "Go ahead" and stay quiet.

---

## 6. Follow-up questions (about 4 min)

*Only now do you ask for opinions. Stop the screen share if they want.*

"Thanks, that was really helpful. A few quick questions:"

1. "What was that like overall? Tell me more about [a moment you noticed]."
2. "Was there anything you expected to see that you didn't?"
3. "How do you report something like this today?" *(Skip if time is short.)*
4. "If you did want to use this, what would you do next? What would stop you?"
5. "Is there anything else I should understand that I haven't asked?" *(Wait a few seconds longer than feels comfortable.)*

*Then answer any questions they saved up, as best you can.*

---

## 7. Close (about 2 min)

"That's everything. Thank you. You've helped us make this better."

"I'll email the $25 voucher to [email] today. If it hasn't arrived by [time/day], reply to my email and I'll sort it out."

*Stop the recording if it's running.* "I've stopped the recording."

---

## After each call

- Within the same day, write your raw notes first: what they did and said, which screen, and every question they asked. Add a separate line for what you think it means.
- Label each person as P1, P2, P3, not by name.
- Send the voucher.

**Notes**
