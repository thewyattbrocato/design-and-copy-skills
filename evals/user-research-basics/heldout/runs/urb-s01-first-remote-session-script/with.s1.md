# Moderator script: pothole report prototype (30 min)

Lines in quotes are what you say. Lines in [brackets] are actions for you. Read the quotes word for word if that helps.

**Timing:** welcome and consent 5 min, warm-up 2, tasks 15, questions afterward 5, close 3.

---

## 1. Welcome and consent (5 min)

"Hi [name], thanks for joining. I'm [your name], and I'll be running today's session. Can you hear and see me okay?"

[Wait for their answer.]

"Today we're testing an early version of a new online form for reporting potholes to the city. The session takes about 30 minutes. One thing matters more than the rest: **we're testing the form, not you.** You can't get anything wrong. If something is confusing, that tells us the form needs work, and that's what we want to learn.

It's an early prototype, so some things may not work properly. If something breaks, that's not your fault.

A few things before we start:

**Watching.** My colleague [PM's name], who is a product manager at the city, is also on the call. They have their camera and microphone off. They're here to watch and take notes, and they won't speak. If you'd rather they weren't here, tell me and they'll drop off. That's fine.

**Recording.** I'd like to record this call, including your screen and your voice, so I can review it afterward instead of relying on my notes. Only the project team will see the recording. [Fill in: who has access, how long you keep it, and what happens to it. Confirm this with your team before the call.] Recording is your choice. If you say no, I'll just take notes and nothing changes, including your voucher. Are you okay with me recording?"

[Wait for a clear yes. If no, don't record. If yes:]

"Thank you. I'm starting the recording now."

[Start recording.]

"For the recording: can you confirm you're happy to be recorded today?"

[Wait for a yes.]

"Thanks. **Payment.** You'll get a $25 grocery voucher by email today. You get it whether we finish everything or not, and you can stop or skip anything at any time without giving a reason.

Do you have any questions before we begin?"

## 2. Warm-up: thinking aloud (2 min)

"While you use the form, it helps me a lot if you talk out loud: what you're looking at, what you're thinking, what you expect to happen, and anything that's confusing. Let's practice on something unrelated. Look around the room you're in and pick any object near you, like a mug or a lamp. Tell me what you notice about it."

[Let them talk for about 30 seconds.]

"Perfect, that's exactly it."

## 3. Getting set up (1 min)

"I'm going to paste a link in the chat. Please open it and share your screen so I can see what you see."

[Paste the link and wait for the screen share. If their screen isn't shared yet, help with that only.]

"Great. I won't be able to help with the form while you're using it. That's on purpose, because I want to see how it works without help. I'll answer any questions you have at the end."

## 4. Tasks (15 min)

[Give one task at a time. Paste each card in the chat. Don't use words like "report," "submit," "status," or any other label from the interface in anything you say.]

**Before each task:** "Please read this out loud, then tell me in your own words what you're going to try to do."

[If they restate it wrongly, the card is the problem. Reword it and note it.]

**Task 1 card:**
> "You've noticed a large pothole on a street near your home. It's been there a while. You want the city to know about it. Use the form to do that. Pick a real street you know."

**Task 2 card:**
> "You have a photo of the pothole. You want the city to see what it looks like. Add it."

[They need a photo ready, or use your sample photo file. Confirm this in the pre-call email.]

**Task 3 card:**
> "Imagine two weeks have passed. You wonder whether anything has been done about the pothole you told the city about. Find out."

[Between tasks, say only: "Thanks, here's the next one."]

### While they work

- Stay quiet. Don't nod, say "good," or react to what they do.
- If they go silent for more than about 10–15 seconds, say once, lightly: "Remember to say what you're thinking."
- Let them finish, fail, or give up. Note where they got stuck and how long it took. Don't ask what they expected until the task is over.
- If they say they're done, say: "Okay, thanks."

### If they ask you something (stay on these lines)

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Is this right?" / "Am I doing this right?" | "I can't say, because we're testing the form, not you. What would you try?" |
| "What does this button do?" | "What do you think it does?" [Then, after they try it:] "We'll talk about that at the end." |
| "Should I click here?" | "What would you do if I weren't here?" |
| "Is it supposed to do that?" | "What did you expect to happen?" [Wait. Note the answer.] |
| "I don't know what to do." | "That's useful to know. What are you looking for?" |
| "Can you help me?" | "I can't during the tasks, but I'll answer everything at the end. What would you try next?" |
| "Do you want me to keep going?" | "Keep going as long as you like. If you're stuck and want to move on, just tell me." |
| They apologize or say "I'm so bad at this." | "You're not. If something's hard, that's the form's problem. This is really useful." |
| They ask you to just tell them. | "I will at the end. For now, what's your best guess?" |

[Write down every question they ask and where they asked it. Each one marks a place the form didn't answer for them.]

### If the prototype breaks (map before address)

Treat this one differently from a normal stuck moment. It's a known flaw, so don't count it as their error or ask what they expected.

"That's a problem with the prototype, not something you did. Let me fix it. I'm pasting the link again. Can you reopen it and start that step over?"

[Paste the link, wait for them to reload, and pick up where they left off. Note that it happened and when. Don't explain why.]

If it breaks a second time or they get frustrated: "Sorry about that, the prototype is early. Let's go on to the next one."

## 5. Questions afterward (5 min)

[Only now do you ask about opinions.]

"Thanks, that was really helpful. A few questions about what you just did.

1. How did that go, in your own words?
2. Was there any point where you weren't sure what to do next? [Wait. If yes:] Tell me more about that.
3. Is there anything you expected to see that wasn't there?
4. Think about the last time you noticed a pothole or a similar problem. What did you do about it?
5. If you wanted to report one for real after today, what would you do next?
6. Is there anything else I should understand about how you'd use something like this?"

[Pause longer than feels comfortable after each answer. Don't fill the silence.]

"Now, my colleague may have a few questions too. Is that okay?"

[If the PM has sent you questions privately in chat, ask them in your own words. If not, skip this.]

"Now you can ask me anything about the form or the project. What would you like to know?"

[Answer honestly. If you don't know, say so.]

## 6. Close (3 min)

"Thank you, [name]. This was very helpful. I'll send your $25 grocery voucher to [email] today. Is that still the right address?"

[Confirm the email.]

"Thanks again. I'm stopping the recording now."

[Stop recording, then say goodbye.]

---

## If you freeze

You never have to answer a question about the form during a task. Say one of these and wait:

- "What would you try?"
- "What do you think it does?"
- "Tell me more about that."

Silence is fine, and it's a normal part of the session. Say each line slowly, then stop talking.

## Before the call (you or your team)

1. **Get recording consent in writing, not just on the call.** None of the three has agreed yet. Send an email a few days ahead with the same wording as in the script. Check with your city's privacy or legal contact about retention and who may see the recording. If someone declines, run the session without recording.
2. **Brief the PM.** Camera and mic off, no chat messages to the participant, and questions go to you privately. Their name will show in the participant list, so you name them in the intro.
3. **Email each person ahead** with the time, the video link, and a request to have a photo of a pothole ready. If they don't have one, you'll give them a sample. Don't describe the tasks.
4. **Prepare a sample photo** in case they don't have one.
5. **Check what Task 3 shows.** I assumed the prototype has a screen where someone can look up the status of a report. If it doesn't, tell me and I'll reword the card.
6. **Run it once yourself.** Do all three tasks to find out where the map bug bites, and have the link ready to paste again.
7. **Send the vouchers** the same day to each person.

Two assumptions: you're not the designer of this form, and the PM may ask questions only through you. If you did help design it, add this to the intro: "I helped work on this form, so don't worry about hurting my feelings. I want honest reactions."

With three people, this round will catch problems that hit many users. It won't tell you about problems that hit a few. Fix what you find, then test with new people.
