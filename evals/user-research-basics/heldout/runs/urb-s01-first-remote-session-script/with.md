# Moderator script: pothole report prototype, 30 min

Read the **bold** parts to yourself and the quoted parts aloud. Times are targets.

## Before the call (you must do these)

1. **Get recording consent first.** Nobody has agreed yet. Email each person ahead of time: the call will be recorded, who will see it, how long you'll keep it, and that they can say no and still take part and get the voucher. Ask them to reply yes or no. Ask again aloud on the call, before you press record. If anyone says no, don't record. Just take notes.
2. **Tell them about your product manager in that same email.** Say "a colleague will listen in with camera and mic off." Nobody should find out mid-call.
3. **Have a photo ready.** Task 2 needs a pothole photo the participant can add, such as a file you share or one on your desktop. Test it.
4. **Make sure task 3 works.** The prototype needs some kind of "status" view to find, or the task will fail for reasons that have nothing to do with the design.
5. **Brief your PM.** They stay silent and don't type in the chat. Their questions go to you privately after the call.
6. **Do a dry run with a friend.** Say the script aloud once and time it.

## 1. Welcome and consent (5 min)

> "Hi [name], thanks so much for joining. I'm [your name] from [city]. Can you hear and see me okay?
>
> Here's what we'll do. We're working on a new online form for reporting potholes, and I'd like to see how it works for real people. It's an early version, so it's unfinished and might glitch. **You can't do anything wrong here.** We're testing the form, not you. If something is confusing, that tells us the form needs fixing.
>
> Some things to know. The session is about 30 minutes. You can skip anything or stop at any point, and you'll still get your $25 grocery voucher. I'll email it today after we finish.
>
> There's also one colleague on the call, [PM name], from our product team. They have their camera and mic off and will just be listening and taking notes. They won't talk to you.
>
> I'd like to record this call, which is my screen and our voices, so I don't have to rely on my notes. Only our project team will see it, and we'll delete it after [**fill in: how long**]. Are you okay with me recording?"

- **Wait for a clear yes.** If it's a no, say "No problem at all" and don't record.
- **If yes:** press record, then say, "Okay, it's recording now."

> "One more thing: I didn't design this, and I can't be offended, so please say exactly what you think. Any questions before we start?"

## 2. Warm-up (3 min)

> "First, a quick warm-up question. Have you ever contacted the city about something, like a pothole, a streetlight or missed trash pickup? Tell me about the last time."

Listen. Say "Tell me more" and "What happened next?" Don't comment on their answers.

> "Thanks. When I give you tasks, I'd like you to **think out loud**: say what you're looking at, what you're thinking, and what you expect to happen. Let's practise. Look at something near you, like a mug or a desk drawer, and tell me what you notice."

Let them do it for about 20 seconds. Then say, "Perfect, that's exactly it."

## 3. Setting up the prototype (1 min)

> "I'm going to paste a link in the chat. Please open it and share your screen. Let me know when you can see a page."

Paste the link. Don't describe the page. Wait for them to say they see it.

> "I'll read each task from a card. I can't help during the tasks, because I want to see what you'd do on your own. That's not me being unhelpful, it's what makes the test useful. I'll answer any questions at the end."

## 4. Tasks (about 15 min)

For each task, paste the text in the chat and ask them to **read it aloud and say it back in their own words**. If their restatement is wrong, the task wording is the problem, so reword it.

**Task 1**
> "Think of a pothole you've seen on your street, or a street you know well. You want the city to know about it so they can fix it. Go ahead and do that."

**Task 2**
> "You also have a photo of that pothole. I've put it [on the desktop / in the chat]. Make sure the city can see what it looks like."

**Task 3**
> "Imagine two weeks have gone by. You want to find out whether the city has dealt with the pothole you told them about. See what you can find out."

**What to say during tasks:**
- Be silent. Count to five in your head before you say anything.
- If they go quiet for a long time: "What are you thinking right now?"
- When they say they're done: "Is that where you'd stop?" Then move on.
- If they give up, say "That's helpful to know. Let's move on to the next one."
- **Don't** say "good", "right" or "exactly", and don't nod as if they got it right.
- **Don't** point at anything, hint, or explain the screen.

**If the prototype breaks** (it will if they tap the map before typing an address):
> "That looks like the prototype stopped working. That's not something you did. Let me send the link again so you can reload it."

Paste the link and let them continue. Afterward, ask "What were you expecting to happen there?" and note it. Don't tell them what caused it.

## 5. Questions people ask you during tasks

Have these ready so you don't have to improvise.

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Is this right?" / "Am I doing this correctly?" | "I can't say, because we're testing the form, not you. What would you try?" |
| "What does this button do?" | "What do you think it does?" (If they don't know: "What would you expect to happen if you pressed it?") |
| "Should I click here?" | "It's up to you. What would you do if I weren't here?" |
| "Is there a photo option?" | "What would you look for?" |
| "Did I break it?" | "No, it's an early version. Tell me what you were expecting to happen." |
| "Can I go back?" | "Do whatever you'd normally do." |
| "Is it supposed to look like that?" | "What were you expecting to see?" |
| "I'm stuck." | "What are you thinking of trying?" If they're clearly at a dead end: "Take a moment, then tell me when you'd stop if this were real life." |

If you freeze, say: **"What would you try?"** It works for almost every question.

**Write down every question they ask and the screen they were on.** Each question marks a place where the design didn't answer them.

## 6. Questions afterward (5 min)

Don't ask these during tasks. Stop the screen share if they want to.

> "Thanks, that's the hard part done. A few questions now."

1. "Tell me about what happened when [pick a moment you noticed, e.g. 'you stopped on the second screen']. What were you thinking?"
2. "Was there anything you expected to find and didn't?"
3. "If you'd actually done this, what would you do next? Who else, if anyone, would you tell?"
4. "What did you use before to report problems like this, or what would you use instead?"
5. "Is there anything else I should have asked?"

Answer any questions they held back during the tasks now. For example: "Earlier you asked what the button did. It does X."

## 7. Close (2 min)

> "Thank you, that was really helpful. I'll email your $25 voucher today. Is [email] the best address? Are you okay if I follow up with a quick question if I need one?"

- Stop recording. Say "I'm stopping the recording now."
- Don't ask the PM questions in front of the participant.
- Send the voucher **today, whatever happened** in the session.

## Right after each call

- **Write your notes the same day.** Put what they did and said first. Then add a separate line for what you think it means. Label participants A, B and C, not by name.
- **Save the recording** with the study name, participant code and date.
- **List the questions each person asked**, with the task they were on.
- **Log the map/address failure** if it happened. Don't count it as a finding about the design, because you already know about it.

## A few notes for your nerves

- If you need a moment, say "Let me jot that down." It buys you time.
- Silence feels longer to you than it does to them.
- Participants forgive moderator stumbles easily. They won't forgive an unclear task, so the task wording is where to spend your practice time.
- Three people can't prove anything on their own. Report findings as "2 of 3 people did X" and say that it's a small test. Don't use percentages.

**Assumptions:** you didn't design the prototype, and your team already has a data retention period in mind. If you did design it, tell participants at the start, and stick even more closely to the script.
