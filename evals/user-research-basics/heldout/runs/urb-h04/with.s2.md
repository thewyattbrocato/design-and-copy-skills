# Screener: front desk staff, 30-minute remote interviews

**Intro (shown before questions):** "We're talking with people who work in healthcare offices about how their day-to-day work gets done. This 3-minute questionnaire checks whether a 30-minute video chat is a fit. The chat pays [incentive: `to decide`]. Please don't include any patient names or details in your answers."

**Q1. Which of these did you do at work in the past 2 weeks? (Select all that apply)**
- Answered phones or scheduled appointments *(qualifies)*
- Checked patients in at a front desk or waiting area *(qualifies)*
- Handled billing or insurance questions
- Ordered supplies
- Gave clinical care or took vitals
- Managed staff schedules or payroll
- None of these → **end**

*Continue only if both "scheduling" and "check-in" are selected.*

**Q2. Which best describes where you work?**
- Primary care or family practice clinic
- Specialty clinic
- Hospital department
- Dental or vision office
- Other: ____

*Target: clinics. Specialty clinics are acceptable if they take walk-ins. Hospital departments and dental/vision offices are a lower priority.*

**Q3. Roughly how far is your workplace from the nearest city of 50,000+ people?**
- Under 15 miles → **end** (or waitlist)
- 15–40 miles
- 40+ miles

*Quota target: at least 4 of 6 from the 40+ option. Confirm "rural" against your own definition. `[to decide]`*

**Q4. In the past month, how did patients typically reach you at your workplace? (Select all that apply)**
- Scheduled appointments
- Walk-ins without an appointment *(qualifies)*
- Phone calls asking for same-day visits
- Referrals from other offices

*Require walk-ins.*

**Q5. In a typical week, how many patients are scheduled but don't show up?**
- Almost none
- A few
- Many, and it affects the day
- I'm not sure

*This is a quota question, not a pass/fail one. Aim for a mix, with at least 3 of 6 in the "many" or "a few" bands. Don't screen out "not sure."*

**Q6. What is your role?**
- I work the front desk most days *(qualifies)*
- I supervise the front desk but also work it
- I only supervise or manage *(end)*
- Other: ____

**Q7. Do you, or does anyone in your household, work for a company that makes healthcare software, or in market research or design? (Yes/No)**
Yes → **end**. Also end if they've done a paid research study in the past 3 months.

**Q8 (open, required).** "Think about the last time your waiting room was crowded or a patient didn't show up. In a few sentences, what happened?"

*Screen out blank, vague, or copy-pasted answers. Keep people who describe a specific event.*

**Q9. Logistics:** Name and email. Can you do a video call on a computer or phone with a stable connection? Which days and times in [window] work for you? Is it okay for us to record the call for notes? (Recording consent will be asked again at the start of the call.)

**Handling non-fits:** Anyone who doesn't qualify but completed the survey still gets the incentive. Set their data aside.

## Recruiting the six

I can't find or contact people from here. I have no recruiting channel, and contacting strangers on your behalf is something you should authorize and own. Someone on your team has to do this. Channels that tend to reach rural front-desk staff:

1. **Your own contacts:** any clinics already in your pipeline or that you've demoed to. Ask for staff, not just managers. Note that people who know the product are tainted for design testing, but fine for these discovery interviews.
2. **State rural health associations and Primary Care Associations:** many have newsletters or member lists, and they can forward a recruit link.
3. **Regional medical group administrators:** ask them to introduce front-desk staff, not to choose them for you.
4. **Professional communities:** medical office administration groups on LinkedIn or Facebook, and AAPC or similar local chapters. Check each group's rules before posting.
5. **A paid panel (e.g., Respondent, User Interviews):** quickest, but rural front desk is a thin pool, so filter on Q3.

Aim to contact about 20–25 to get 6, since no-shows and screen-outs are common. Interviews will likely need to be scheduled around clinic hours, probably early morning or lunch.
