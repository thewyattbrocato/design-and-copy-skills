## What's going wrong

**1. The hero never names the customer or the product.**
"Seamless field operations" and "all-in-one, next-generation platform" are phrases every competitor uses. The words "plumbing," "HVAC," "dispatch," and "field service software" appear nowhere in the hero. That hurts conversion, and it also hurts search. Someone who searched "HVAC scheduling software" should see those words on arrival.

**2. There are no outcomes or numbers.**
"Streamline workflows, boost productivity, take operations to the next level" doesn't say what changes for the owner. A 5–30 tech shop cares about things like these:
- more jobs per tech per day
- fewer missed calls and no-shows
- invoices paid in days, not weeks
- less time spent in the office at night

**3. "Why Kettlebridge?" is all claims and no proof.**
"Best-in-class," "industry-leading," and "trusted by teams everywhere" read as filler, and skeptical owners ignore them. This section is where a customer quote, a real number, or a logo strip should go.

**4. The features are one-liners that could describe anything.**
- "Optimizes your routes" doesn't say how much drive time it saves.
- "Work from anywhere" doesn't say what a tech can do from the truck.
- "Get paid faster" doesn't say how.

Name the specific behavior, for example "Techs send an invoice and take card payment before leaving the driveway."

**5. The CTAs are vague and inconsistent.**
"Get started" doesn't say what happens next: a trial, a demo, or a credit card form. The footer says "Book a demo." Pick one primary action and word it the same way throughout. Add a lower-commitment option too, such as a short product tour or a pricing page, because many owners aren't ready to book a call.

**6. "Ready to transform your business?" is empty.**
It's a generic closer. End with a specific promise instead, such as "See how Kettlebridge would run your 12-truck schedule. 20-minute demo."
