The copy is generic enough that a plumbing or HVAC owner can't tell what Kettlebridge does, who it's for, or why it's better than the software they already know. Here are the main problems, most important first.

**1. The headline and subhead say nothing specific.** "Seamless field operations," "all-in-one," "next-generation," "take operations to the next level" could describe any field service tool. Owners who searched for "HVAC dispatch software" see no sign they're in the right place. Search visitors need to see their trade and their problem within a few seconds.

**2. The subhead is pure filler.** "Streamline workflows, boost productivity" are claims nobody can check. Nothing is concrete: no outcome, no number, no named problem like missed calls, double-booked techs, or slow invoicing.

**3. "Why Kettlebridge?" is empty.** "Best-in-class," "industry-leading," and "powerful" are self-assessments that buyers ignore. "Trusted by teams everywhere" is vague, and with no logos, count, or testimonial it reads as unsupported. This section is where you'd normally prove the claims, and right now it has no proof.

**4. The features are too thin to evaluate.** "Our intelligent engine optimizes your routes" doesn't say what it does for the owner (fewer drive hours? more jobs per tech per day?). "Work from anywhere" is a generic phrase. "Get paid faster" is the most concrete of the three, but it still lacks a mechanism or number.

**5. The CTA is inconsistent.** The hero says "Get started," the footer says "Book a demo," and nothing explains what happens next. For a 5-30 tech company, a demo is likely the real conversion goal, so the hero button should probably match it, or at least say what the visitor gets.

**6. Nothing speaks to the 5-30 tech buyer.** The copy never mentions plumbing, HVAC, dispatchers, techs, or the owner's daily pain. Those words are what both search matching and self-selection depend on.
