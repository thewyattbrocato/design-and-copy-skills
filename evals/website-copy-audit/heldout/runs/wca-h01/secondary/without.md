The copy is generic enough that a plumbing or HVAC owner can't tell what Kettlebridge does, who it's for, or why it's better than the field-service software they've already seen. Since people arrive from search, they need to see their own trade and problem within a few seconds.

**Biggest problems**

1. **The headline is slogan-shaped, not benefit-shaped.** "Unlock the power of seamless field operations" could describe any of twenty products. Owners searching for something like "HVAC dispatch software" won't see their words on the page.
2. **The subhead is filler.** "All-in-one, next-generation," "empowers," "streamline workflows," "boost productivity," "take operations to the next level" are all words that carry no information. Cut them and nothing is lost.
3. **Nothing says who it's for.** You know your buyer is a plumbing or HVAC company with 5 to 30 techs, but the page never says so. Naming the trade and team size is the fastest way to make the right visitors feel the page is for them.
4. **The "Why Kettlebridge?" line is unsupported claims.** "Best-in-class," "industry-leading," and "trusted by teams everywhere" are assertions a skeptical owner will discount. Specific numbers or named customers would do more work.
5. **The features are labels, not reasons.** "Work from anywhere" and "Get paid faster" are things every competitor says. Each feature should name the owner's problem and the concrete result.
6. **The CTAs conflict.** The hero says "Get started" and the footer says "Book a demo." Pick one primary action and use it consistently. For a 5–30 tech shop that's probably a demo, since they're buying for a team.
