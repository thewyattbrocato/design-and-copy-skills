The copy never says what Kettlebridge is, who it's for, or why it beats what they use now. A plumbing or HVAC owner who lands from search can't tell in five seconds whether this is for them.

## What's going wrong

**1. The headline and subhead are generic.**
"Unlock the power of seamless field operations" could be about any software for any industry. "All-in-one, next-generation platform," "streamline workflows," "boost productivity" and "next level" are filler. Someone who searched for something like "dispatch software for HVAC" should see that exact world reflected back, and here they don't. Nothing in the hero says plumbing, HVAC, techs, jobs, or customers.

**2. The "Why Kettlebridge?" section is unproven claims.**
"Best-in-class," "industry-leading," "powerful," and "trusted by teams everywhere" are assertions with nothing behind them. Readers skip past them, and they can lower trust. This section needs real numbers, customer names, or quotes, such as "Used by 400 plumbing and HVAC shops" or a quote from an owner.

**3. The features describe categories, not outcomes.**
- "Optimizes your routes" doesn't say what the owner gets. Fewer miles? One more job per tech per day?
- "Work from anywhere" is vague. What can a tech do on their phone: see the job, take photos, collect payment on site?
- "Get paid faster" is the strongest of the three, but it has no specifics. Faster than what, and how?

**4. The calls to action are weak or mismatched.**
"Get started" doesn't say what happens next: a trial, a signup, or a sales call? The footer says "Book a demo," so the page sends two different signals. "Ready to transform your business?" is also generic. Pick one primary action and make the button say what it does, like "Start free 14-day trial" or "See a 15-minute demo."

**5. There's no social proof, pricing signal, or objection handling.**
Owners of 5–30 tech shops are probably comparing you against ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, or a spreadsheet and a whiteboard. Nothing here says why you're a better fit. That could be simpler, cheaper, or built for mid-size crews, and the page should say which.

## What I'd do

**Hero.** Name the audience and the outcome:
> *Dispatching, invoicing, and job tracking for plumbing and HVAC companies with 5–30 techs.*
> Subhead: *Fill your schedule, get techs to jobs with less drive time, and send invoices before they leave the driveway.*

Adjust this to whatever is actually true. I'm guessing at the specifics.

**Proof section.** Replace the adjectives with one or two real numbers and a short owner quote.

**Features.** Rewrite each as "what it does, then what you get":
- *Smart Dispatch: See every tech and job on one board. Drag to reassign, and the route updates. [Result, e.g. "customers average X fewer miles per day"].*
- *Mobile App: Techs see the job history, take photos, and collect payment on site.*
- *Invoicing: Invoice from the truck. [Stat on how much faster customers pay].*

**CTA.** Use one action everywhere, with a button label that describes it.

**Also check:**
- Does the page title and H1 contain the terms people search? If they search "HVAC scheduling software" and the page never says HVAC, that hurts both conversion and ranking.
- Is there a screenshot or short product video? Right now there's no visual evidence the product exists.
- Is pricing, or at least a "from $X per tech" indication, visible? Owners of small shops often leave when they can't tell the cost.

## The caveat

Copy is only one possible cause. If you have analytics, check where visitors drop off. Also check whether the traffic matches your buyers. If search visitors are mostly people researching or hiring, conversion will be low whatever the copy says. But this copy would underperform even with the right traffic.
