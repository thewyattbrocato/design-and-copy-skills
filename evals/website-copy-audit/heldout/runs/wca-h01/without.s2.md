The copy could describe almost any software product. A plumbing or HVAC owner who lands on it can't tell within a few seconds that it's for them, what it does, or why it's better than what they use now. That's probably the main reason it isn't converting.

## What's going wrong

**1. It never names your customer.**
The words plumbing, HVAC, technician, job, and dispatcher appear nowhere. Most owners find you through search, so this hurts twice. Visitors don't see themselves in the page, and the page doesn't contain the terms they searched for, like "HVAC scheduling software" or "plumbing dispatch software."

**2. The hero is filler.**
"Unlock the power of seamless field operations" and "all-in-one, next-generation platform that empowers... take their operations to the next level" say nothing a competitor couldn't also say. Nothing in it is concrete or testable.

**3. The "Why Kettlebridge?" section makes claims with no evidence.**
"Best-in-class," "industry-leading," and "trusted by teams everywhere" are assertions. Without a number, a customer name, or a quote, readers skip past them. They may also trust the rest of the page less.

**4. The features are labels, not outcomes.**
- "Optimizes your routes" doesn't say what that means for an owner, such as fewer miles, more jobs per day, or less fuel.
- "Work from anywhere" doesn't say what a tech does in the app.
- "Get paid faster" doesn't say how much faster or by what mechanism.

**5. The calls to action are inconsistent and vague.**
The hero says "Get started," which doesn't say what happens next: a trial, a form, or a sales call. The footer says "Book a demo." Two different asks make the next step unclear. "Ready to transform your business?" is also generic.

## What to do

1. **Rewrite the hero around who it's for and what changes.** For example:
   - *Headline:* "Dispatch, schedule, and invoice your HVAC or plumbing crew from one place"
   - *Subhead:* "Kettlebridge helps shops with 5–30 techs fit more jobs into each day and get paid sooner. [Add one concrete result.]"
2. **Replace the "Why" section with proof.** Use real numbers, 2–3 named customers or short quotes from owners in your segment, and logos if you have them. If you don't have any of these yet, collecting them is probably your highest-leverage task.
3. **Rewrite each feature as problem → result.** For example: "Smart Dispatch: See who's closest and free, and assign the job in one click. [Customer] cut drive time by X%." Only use numbers you can back up.
4. **Pick one primary CTA and use it everywhere.** Make the button say what happens, like "Book a 20-minute demo" or "Start your free trial." Add a line underneath that removes friction, such as "No credit card" or "Set up in a week."
5. **Use your customers' words for SEO and clarity.** Put "HVAC," "plumbing," "technicians," "service calls," and "dispatch" in the headline, subhead, and section headings. Check how owners actually describe their problems in your sales call notes, support tickets, and reviews, and borrow that language.
