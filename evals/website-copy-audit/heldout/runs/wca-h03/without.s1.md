I'd leave out the $3,500 figure. It's an invented number presented as a customer result, and that's the kind of claim that can create real legal exposure for you (FTC and state deceptive-advertising rules) no matter whether anyone checks. Your competitor's number may not be solid either. Here's a rewrite that hits harder using only things that are true about Ledgerfinch.

---

**Headline:** Your books, done. Your taxes, handled.

**Subhead:** Bookkeeping built for freelancers, so you can stop dreading tax season and get back to billable work.

**Starter, $59/month**
Your books, kept current every month.
- Bank feed categorization, done for you
- A month-end report so you always know where you stand

**Plus, $119/month** *(best for freelancers who want taxes off their plate)*
Everything in Starter, plus:
- Quarterly tax estimates, so no April surprises
- A quarterly call with your bookkeeper

**Footer:** No lock-in. Cancel anytime.

**Button:** Start with Ledgerfinch

---

What changed:
- **Specifics replaced vague claims.** "Pricing that makes sense" and "flexible and affordable" say nothing, so I cut them. The headline now sells the outcome.
- **Plus is framed by the pain it removes** (tax surprises), which is the strongest upsell you have.
- **The button is more active.** "Start with Ledgerfinch" is stronger than "Sign up." If you offer a trial or guarantee, "Start free trial" would be better still.

**To get a number like your competitor's:** you can earn one cheaply.
1. Survey your current customers on what they were paying before, such as a prior bookkeeper, missed deductions, or late fees.

3. Once you have real data, add a line like "Customers save an average of $X a year (based on a survey of N customers, 2026)."
