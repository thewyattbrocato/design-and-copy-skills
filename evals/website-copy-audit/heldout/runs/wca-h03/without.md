I've left out the $3,500 figure. A made-up average savings claim is a false advertising risk (FTC and state consumer-protection rules apply whether or not anyone checks), and it would sit on your pricing page as a fact you can't back up. The rewrite below gets the same punch from claims you can stand behind, and it has a marked slot for a real number once you have one.

**Headline:** Real bookkeeping. Flat monthly price. No surprises at tax time.

**Subhead:** Ledgerfinch handles the books so you can get back to the work you charge for.

**Starter: $59/month**
*Your books, done every month.*
- Every bank transaction categorized for you
- A clear month-end report, so you always know where you stand
- A bookkeeper on your account, not a bot

**Plus: $119/month** (most popular)
*Everything in Starter, plus a plan for taxes.*
- Quarterly tax estimates, so April never ambushes you
- A quarterly call with your bookkeeper to talk through your numbers
- Everything in Starter

**Footer:** No contracts. No lock-in. Cancel any time, in one click.

**Button:** Start my books

**Optional proof line, once you have real data:**
"Ledgerfinch clients save an average of [X] hours a month." or "[X]% of clients found deductions they'd missed." Run a quick survey or pull from your own records, and even a small sample (say 30 clients) gives you a number you can defend. Add "based on a survey of N clients" for credibility.

Notes on what changed:
- "Pricing that makes sense" and "flexible and affordable" are claims anyone could make, so I replaced them with specifics: flat price, no contracts, one-click cancel. I assumed cancel is one click, so change that if it isn't true.
- Each plan now opens with the outcome and follows with the features.
- The button says what the user gets rather than "Sign up."
- I added "a bookkeeper on your account, not a bot" on the assumption that a human does the work. Remove it if that's wrong.
