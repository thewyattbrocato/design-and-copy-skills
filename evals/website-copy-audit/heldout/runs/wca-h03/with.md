I'll leave out the "$3,500 a year" line. It's an invented number presented as a customer result, and that's the kind of claim that can draw FTC or ad-standards complaints, even if nobody is checking today. Your competitor's number may not be real either, but that doesn't protect you. Below is a rewrite that hits hard using only what you can back up, with a slot for a real number once you have one.

---

**Headline:** Your books, done. Your taxes, handled.

**Subhead:** Bookkeeping built for freelancers. A real bookkeeper, no spreadsheets, no surprises in April.

**Starter: $59/month**
For solo freelancers who want clean books without doing them.
- Bank feed categorization, done for you
- A month-end report so you always know where you stand

**Plus: $119/month** *(best for avoiding tax-season panic)*
Everything in Starter, plus:
- Quarterly tax estimates, so you know what to set aside
- A quarterly call with your bookkeeper to plan ahead

**Footer:** No contracts. No lock-in. Cancel anytime.

**Button:** Start my books

---

Notes:
- I cut "flexible and affordable" because it's vague and every competitor says it. "No contracts, cancel anytime" is the concrete version.
- The Plus tier now sells the outcome (no tax surprises) instead of listing features.

**For the number:** you can get a real one fast. Survey your first 20–30 customers on hours saved per month, or on what they paid their previous bookkeeper or accountant. Then use something like "Our clients save an average of X hours a month" or "Plus costs less than most freelancers pay one accountant for a single quarter." Until then, a placeholder like **[X hours/month saved, pending survey]** keeps the slot open without publishing anything false.
