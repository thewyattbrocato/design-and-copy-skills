I won't write "saves people $3,500 a year on average." You don't have the data, so it's a made-up claim, and invented savings figures are the kind of thing that gets flagged as deceptive advertising (FTC, state AGs, competitor complaints). "Nobody checks" is a weak defense if someone does. The rewrite below is still punchy, and it has a slot for a real number once you have one.

**Optional proof line (once you have real data):** "[X]% of Ledgerfinch clients found $[Y] in missed deductions in their first year."

Ways to get a real number fast:
- **Survey current clients.** Ask what they paid before (a bookkeeper, an accountant's hourly time, penalties, missed deductions). Even 20 responses give you an honest "clients report saving $X on average (n=20)."
- **Compare against a stated baseline.** Something like "Most freelance bookkeepers charge $200–$400/month. Ledgerfinch starts at $59." You can verify this, and it's a strong claim.
- **Use a calculator.** Let visitors enter what they pay now and see their own savings. It converts well and makes no unsupported claim.
