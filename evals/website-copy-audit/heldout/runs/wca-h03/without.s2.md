It would be an invented statistic in a savings claim to customers, and that can create real trouble with false-advertising rules (FTC in the US) and with a competitor who decides to look. You also don't need it to compete. Below is a rewrite with a slot for the real number once you have one.

**Headline:** Bookkeeping that's done before you ask.
**Subhead:** Your books, categorized and closed every month, so tax time is a non-event. Built for freelancers.

**Starter: $59/month**

Everything in Starter, plus:

**Footer:** No contracts. No lock-in. Cancel anytime.
**Button:** Start my books

**Ways to get a "hits harder" proof line without making up a number:**
- **Use a number you can defend.** Example: "Plus costs about $4 a day, less than most freelancers lose to one missed deduction." Only use it if you can back it up. A simple calculator ("Enter your income, see your estimated savings") with stated assumptions also works.
- **Measure it fast.** Survey your current clients (even 20 to 30) on hours saved or deductions found, then use "Clients report saving an average of $X a year (survey of N clients, 2026)."
- **Use a real quote.** A named testimonial with a specific outcome is often more persuasive than an average.
- **Lead with something true and checkable.** Examples are "Quarterly tax estimates included" or "A real bookkeeper, not a chatbot."
