I wrote the copy, but I left out the invented customer quotes and the "Sale ends tonight" banner. Made-up reviews and a fake deadline count as deceptive advertising. In the US the FTC rules on fake reviews and false urgency apply, and payment processors and ad platforms can act on them. It would also hurt you if anyone noticed.

**Revised copy**

**Title:** The Nightmoor Weighted Blanket

**Intro:** A weighted blanket that feels like a steady, calming hug. Choose your weight, pull it over you, and settle in.

**Details:**
- Weights: 12 lb, 15 lb, or 20 lb
- Size: Queen (more sizes coming)
- Fill: Glass beads, stitched in sections so they stay evenly spread
- Cover: Cotton, removable and machine washable

**Price:** ~~$129~~ **$109.65** (15% off)

**Button:** Add to cart

**Small print:** Not sure? Return it within 30 days.

Notes:
- I dropped "ultimate" and "premium", since they're vague and nobody believes them.
- "Stitched in sections" is a guess on my part. Only keep it if it's true.
- If you want a weight guide, the usual advice is about 10% of body weight. Add it only if it fits your product.
- If the 15% off runs all year, the $129 price is effectively the fake one. Regulators can treat a "was" price that nobody really paid as misleading. The safer move is to show $109.65 as the price, or say "15% off" without a strikethrough.

**Banner options that are true and still create momentum:**
- "Launch offer: 15% off Nightmoor, all year"
- "15% off every Nightmoor blanket, and free returns for 30 days"
- If you want real urgency, make it real. For example, "Launch pricing ends Dec 31" works if you'll actually end it then.

**Reviews:** Before Friday, you could send blankets to 5–10 friends, testers, or early customers and ask for honest feedback. If you give them a free unit, they have to disclose that in the review. After launch, an automatic email about 2 weeks after delivery will collect real reviews. Until then, the 30-day return policy is your best trust signal, so give it more space than small print.
