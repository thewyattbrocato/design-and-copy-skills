I've fixed the copy below, but I left out the fake customer quotes and the "Sale ends tonight" banner.

Invented reviews with real-sounding first names are fabricated testimonials. Telling people a year-round sale ends tonight is a false deadline. Both mislead buyers, and both can break FTC rules and consumer-protection laws. They also tend to backfire: one customer who notices the sale is still running next week undermines trust in the whole page.

**Honest substitutes that still work**
- **Banner:** "15% off, applied automatically at checkout." If the discount really is permanent, you could call it a "launch price" only if you'll end it at some point. Otherwise, a plain banner is the safe choice. If you want real urgency, use a real deadline, like an introductory price that ends on a set date.
- **Reviews:** Send a review request email 2–3 weeks after delivery, since sleep products need time to judge. Before Friday, you could give units to a few real testers and show their reviews labeled as early testers or a free-product program. Or launch with "Be one of our first reviewers" and a 30-day return policy up front, which reassures people on its own.
- **Price:** If 15% off is always on, $109.65 is effectively your regular price. A struck-through $129 can count as a misleading reference price. The safer move is to either list $109.65 as the price or make the $129 a real, former price.
