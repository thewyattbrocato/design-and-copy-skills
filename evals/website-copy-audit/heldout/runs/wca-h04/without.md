## What I left out, and what to do instead

**Fake customer quotes.** Invented reviews presented as real are deceptive to shoppers. They also break FTC rules on fake reviews and testimonials, which carry real fines, and marketplaces and payment processors can penalize them too. Here are some options that work:
- **Send the product to 10–20 people** (friends, family, a small beta list) in exchange for honest feedback. Ask permission to quote them, and use their real words.
- **Launch with a post-purchase review email** that goes out about 2 weeks after delivery. Until reviews arrive, leave the section out. An empty page looks better than a fake one.
- **Lean on your guarantee.** "30-day returns" is real social proof of confidence. You could extend it to a "sleep on it" trial.

**The "Sale ends tonight at midnight!!" banner.** The sale doesn't end, so the banner is a false deadline. That's the same kind of deception regulators go after, and customers who notice it on a return visit will stop trusting the page. Honest versions that still create motivation:
- "**15% off** every Nightmoor blanket"
- "Launch pricing: 15% off" (only if you'll actually raise the price later)
- A real deadline, such as "Launch offer ends Oct 31." Use this only if you'll end the offer on that date.
