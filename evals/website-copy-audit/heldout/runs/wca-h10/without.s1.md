# Homepage copy: Ember & Rye vs Quillbean and Northpour

## Summary

We're not losing to Northpour's copy. We're losing to Quillbean's, and our headline is as generic as Northpour's. Our strongest material (the Tuesday roast, the Wednesday dispatch, the hand-packing in Leeds) sits in a section below the fold. Quillbean puts its specifics in the first two lines.

## Where we're losing

**1. The headline could belong to anyone.** "Great coffee, delivered" doesn't say who it's for or why us. It doesn't even say "office". Every roaster claims great coffee, so the claim does no work. Quillbean's "Office coffee without the faff" names the audience and takes a position.

**2. Our best proof is buried.** "Roast Tuesday, ship Wednesday" is concrete and checkable. It's the closest thing we have to evidence, and we have no reviews or customer numbers. It should be the headline, not a mid-page section.

**3. The subheading describes a form, not a benefit.** "Choose your beans, choose your frequency, and we take care of the rest" is true of any subscription. Quillbean's sub is a mini-spec: bag size, delivery day, where it's roasted, when it's posted, and an offer (free grinder loan over 3kg a month).

**4. The button asks for more commitment than visitors have given.** "Start a subscription" is a big ask from someone who hasn't seen a price. Quillbean's "See plans and prices" promises information. Northpour's "Learn more" promises nothing, but ours isn't much better for a first-time visitor.

**5. We have no offer.** Quillbean gives a reason to pick a larger plan. We give no reason to act now or pick a size.

**6. Be honest about freshness.** Quillbean says it roasts to order and posts the next morning. That beats our weekly batch on paper. If we make freshness our whole pitch, we invite that comparison. Our edge is a clear weekly rhythm and a small team that packs each order by hand. Lead with the rhythm and the people, and don't claim to be the freshest.

**Northpour** is the weakest of the three. "Elevate", "premium", "discerning" and "expertly" are all unprovable, and it says nothing about delivery, price or location. Don't imitate it, but note that our current sub is closer to its vagueness than to Quillbean's specificity.

## What to change

### Headline and sub

**Current:** Great coffee, delivered

**Proposed:** Office coffee, roasted Tuesday and shipped Wednesday

**Sub, proposed:** Fresh beans from our small team in Leeds. Choose your beans and how often you want them. We roast them that week and pack your order ourselves.

This names the audience, gives a checkable promise and says where we are. Everything in it is already true.

### Button

**Proposed:** Choose your beans

Use "See plans and prices" instead if a pricing page exists. That wording is the better pattern, because it tells visitors what happens next. Keep "Start a subscription" as the final button after the detail section, where visitors are ready for it.

### The roast section (moved up, rewritten)

> **Roasted this week, packed by hand**
> We roast every Tuesday and ship every Wednesday, so your coffee was roasted that week. We're a small team in Leeds, and every order is packed by one of us.

Keep the wording close to the original. It's already good, and it only needs a better position and a stronger lead-in.

### Footer

"Questions? Email hello@emberandrye.example" is fine as a footer. Also put the address near the button, for example "Not sure which beans? Email us". For an office buyer, a reachable human is a selling point.

## Substitutes for missing reviews

We have no social proof, so the copy has to carry credibility through specifics. Three options, each only if true:

- Put a real team photo or first names next to the "packed by one of us" line.
- Print the roast date on the bag and say so on the page.
- Add a simple first-order promise, such as "Not right? Tell us and we'll sort it". Only add this if you're willing to honour it.

Don't invent numbers or testimonials. Visitors will read a weak but honest page more kindly than a padded one.

## Questions to settle before Monday

1. Is there a pricing page we can link to? That decides which button wording we use.
2. Do we want an offer, like Quillbean's grinder loan, or a first-order promise?
3. Can we show the roast date on the bag? If so, it's the best proof we have.
4. Do we deliver UK-wide? If so, the page should say it, because Quillbean and Northpour don't make it clear either.
