# Homepage copy: Ember & Rye vs Quillbean and Northpour

## Summary

We have the strongest raw material of the three. We roast weekly, ship on a fixed day, and a named small team packs every order. But our headline and subhead are the vaguest on the page after Northpour's, and our best proof is in a section below them. Quillbean is winning on specificity and on making the next step feel low-risk.

## Where we're losing

**1. The headline could belong to anyone.** "Great coffee, delivered" makes a claim every roaster makes and says nothing about offices, Leeds, or freshness. Quillbean's "Office coffee without the faff" names the audience and the pain in five words. Northpour's is worse ("Elevate your workplace coffee experience" is pure filler), but beating Northpour isn't the bar.

**2. Our strongest proof is buried.** "Roasted Tuesday, shipped Wednesday" and "packed by one of us" are concrete and believable, and they sit in a section below the fold. Quillbean puts its mechanics (roast to order, posted next morning) in the subhead. We should do the same.

**3. The subhead describes a form, not a benefit.** "Choose your beans, choose your frequency, and we take care of the rest" is a list of options, and "the rest" is vague. Quillbean's subhead does similar work but adds a concrete offer (a free grinder loan above 3kg a month), which gives a buyer a reason to pick them.

**4. No price signal.** Quillbean's button promises "plans and prices". Ours asks for a commitment ("Start a subscription") without telling the visitor what they're committing to. For an office manager comparing three suppliers, that's friction.

**5. Nothing for a buyer who isn't ready.** We have a single CTA and an email in the footer. A cautious buyer has no lower-commitment step.

**6. We can't fix the trust gap with social proof yet.** With no reviews or customer numbers, we shouldn't imply any. Phrases like "loved by offices" would hurt us. The substitute is specificity: real people, a real place, a real schedule.

## Where we're fine

- "Start a subscription" is clear. It only needs support from visible pricing.
- The Leeds and small-team story is a real differentiator against Northpour's generic "premium" language. It just needs to move up.
- We shouldn't try to out-fresh Quillbean. Roasting to order and posting next morning is a stronger claim than ours. Our honest claim is "roasted that week", and we should say exactly that.

## Recommended changes, in priority order

### 1. Headline and subhead (highest impact)

**Headline:**
> Office coffee, roasted in Leeds every Tuesday

**Subhead:**
> Shipped Wednesday, so what reaches your office was roasted that week. Every order is packed by one of our small team. Pick your beans and how often, and change either whenever you like.

*Check before using:* only keep "change either whenever you like" if it's true. If it isn't, cut the sentence.

### 2. Button and a secondary step

**Primary button:**
> See plans and prices

This only works if prices are on the page it links to. If they aren't, keep "Start a subscription" and fix the pricing gap first.

**Secondary link beneath it:**
> Not sure what your office needs? Email us and we'll suggest a plan.

This gives cautious buyers a low-commitment route, and it plays to being small and responsive.

### 3. Rewrite the "We roast on Tuesdays" section

Move it up, directly under the hero, and make it more personal:

> **Roasted Tuesday. Shipped Wednesday.**
> We're a small team in Leeds. We roast once a week, and every order is packed by hand by one of us. If something's wrong with your delivery, you're emailing the person who packed it.

The last sentence turns a small team into a service promise. Keep it only if it's accurate.

### 4. Footer

> Questions about your office order? Email hello@emberandrye.example

## Things to decide before Monday

- **Do we have a real offer?** Quillbean's grinder loan is a concrete hook. If we can offer something similar (a first-bag discount, a free tasting pack, a grinder loan), it belongs in the subhead. I haven't written copy around one because I don't know what we can afford.
- **Can we show a face or a roast day?** A photo of the team packing orders would do more for trust than any claim, given we have no reviews.
- **Delivery time.** We know when we ship, but not when it arrives. If it's reliably 1–2 days, saying so would close the gap with Quillbean's "next morning".
- **Reviews.** Once we have two or three real customer quotes, they should go right under the hero. Until then, no placeholder or implied proof.

## Suggested test

If we can only change one thing, change the headline and subhead together (item 1). It's a small edit, it uses facts we already have, and it's the part every visitor reads.
