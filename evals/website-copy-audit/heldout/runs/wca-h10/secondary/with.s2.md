**Ember & Rye homepage vs. Quillbean and Northpour**

Reviewed: the homepage copy and two competitor homepages as pasted, undated. Not reviewed: layout, pricing or plans pages, the sign-up flow, and emails.

**Findings, ranked**

1. **Major · Button, "Start a subscription."** No price, renewal or cancellation terms appear in the supplied copy. The reader is asked to commit before knowing the cost. Quillbean's button, "See plans and prices," tells the reader what happens next. Fix: put the terms beside the button. Rewrite: "From [price] per [bag size], renews every [frequency] until you cancel. [Cancellation route.]" Use real numbers only if they exist. If you have a plans page, the button can say "See plans and prices."

2. **Major · Headline, "Great coffee, delivered."** Any roaster could say this, and Northpour's "Elevate your workplace coffee experience" is the same kind of line. A stranger can't tell from the headline that this is an office service. "Great coffee" is also a judgement with nothing on the page behind it. Rewrite: "Office coffee, roasted the week it ships."

3. **Major · Sub, "Choose your beans, choose your frequency, and we take care of the rest."** This describes the process in terms every subscription uses. The reader's real question is why this one is different, and the answer is buried. "We take care of the rest" says nothing specific. Rewrite: "We roast on Tuesdays and ship on Wednesdays, so every bag was roasted that week. Choose your beans and how often they come."

4. **Minor · Section, "We're a small team in Leeds…"** The strongest line on the page sits below the fold, after the sub has already set up a generic pitch. Moving the roast-week fact up (finding 3) fixes most of this. The section can stay as it is.

5. **Minor · Proof.** There are no reviews, customer numbers or testimonials. Do not add any. The packing line and the Leeds team are real proof, and they are the only human detail on any of the three pages. If you have a customer who will agree to it, a quote with a name and their permission would add to them.

**What works (keep)**

- "We roast on Tuesdays and ship on Wednesdays, so what you get was roasted that week."
- "every order is packed by one of us."
- "Fresh-roasted beans for your office" (keep the phrase; the rest of the sub changes).
- The footer email line is plain and easy to find.

**Competitor comparison**

- **Who it's for:** All three name an office. Quillbean adds the buyer's problem ("without the faff"). Northpour names a type of buyer ("the discerning office") without naming any problem.
- **Main promise:** Quillbean is about scheduling and speed (next-morning dispatch). Northpour is about quality, with no specifics. Ours is about freshness, and of the three it is the easiest to check.
- **Proof:** Ours is the only page with a human detail. Neither rival offers one in the supplied copy.
- **Price visibility:** Quillbean is ahead here. Its button promises plans and prices, and it gives a concrete offer (a grinder loan over 3kg a month). Ours and Northpour show no price.
- **Terms:** None of the three shows renewal or cancellation in the supplied copy.
- **Tone:** Quillbean is playful. Northpour is stock luxury language, and "ethically sourced" is a claim with no source given. Ours is plain and specific.

The gap worth closing is price visibility. The distinction worth keeping is the weekly roast on a fixed day, packed by one of the team in Leeds. Do not copy Quillbean's grinder loan unless you have an equivalent offer; if you don't, that is a decision for the team rather than a copy fix.

**Rewrites**

Only the flagged lines are rewritten, and each sits beside its finding above. The headline, sub and price line are the three that change. Placeholders in brackets need real numbers from you.

**Fix order**

1. Put price, renewal and cancellation beside the button. *Small* if the numbers exist; otherwise it needs a pricing decision first.
2. Replace the headline with the office and freshness line. *Small.*
3. Rewrite the sub to lead with the roast-week fact. *Small.*
4. Add a real customer quote with permission, or leave proof as it is. *Medium* (depends on customers).
5. Decide whether you have an offer equivalent to Quillbean's. If not, don't invent one. *Medium.*
