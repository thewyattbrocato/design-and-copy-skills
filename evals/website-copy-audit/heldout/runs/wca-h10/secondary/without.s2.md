# Homepage copy: where we're losing and what to change

## Summary

Our copy is the most specific about how the coffee reaches the customer, but the parts a buyer reads first are the weakest. "Great coffee, delivered" could be any coffee company's headline. Northpour is the vaguest of the three, so we already beat it on clarity. Quillbean is the real competitor: it makes concrete promises and tells visitors what it costs.

## Where we're losing

**1. Our headline doesn't speak to anyone.** "Great coffee, delivered" makes no claim a buyer can check. Quillbean's "Office coffee without the faff" names the buyer and the problem.

**2. Our strongest fact is buried.** The Tuesday roast and Wednesday dispatch is the best freshness claim in the set, but it sits in a secondary section below the fold. Quillbean puts its timing in the subhead ("post it the next morning").

**3. We don't say what it costs or how it's bought.** Quillbean's button is "See plans and prices," and it advertises a grinder perk. Our "Start a subscription" asks for commitment before a visitor knows the price or any minimum. Office buyers often need to get approval, so that gap costs us visitors.

**4. Northpour's tone is the wrong model.** "Elevate your workplace coffee experience" and "for the discerning office" are phrases buyers skip. We should beat it on specifics, not borrow its vagueness.

**5. Don't copy Quillbean's "roast to order."** We roast weekly, so that claim would mislead a buyer who asks how fresh the coffee is. Our accurate version, "roasted this week," is more precise and still stronger than anything in the set.

## What to change, in priority order

1. Rewrite the headline and subhead to name the office buyer and the weekly roast.
2. Move the Tuesday/Wednesday timing into the hero.
3. Put pricing, or a link to it, beside the main button.
4. Use "packed by one of us" as our trust signal, since we have no reviews yet.
5. Make the primary button "See plans and prices" and keep "Start a subscription" as a secondary action.

## New copy

**Headline:** Coffee roasted this week. Shipped every Wednesday.

**Subhead:** We're a small roastery in Leeds. Roast on Tuesday, dispatch on Wednesday, and choose your beans and how often you want them. [Add plan prices or minimum here.]

**Trust section:** Every order is packed by one of us. We're a small team, so if you have a question about your delivery, you'll reach the people who actually handle your coffee: hello@emberandrye.example

**Primary button:** See plans and prices
**Secondary link:** Start a subscription

## Things to confirm before this goes live

- **Pricing:** I left a placeholder because I don't have plan prices or minimums. Buyers will look for these first, so this is the most important gap.
- **Grinder loan:** If we offer something similar to Quillbean's grinder perk, it's worth adding. I haven't assumed we have one.
- **"Roasted this week" wording:** Confirm that every order really is roasted on the Tuesday before dispatch, so the claim holds up if a customer checks.
- **Delivery timing:** I haven't promised when the coffee arrives, only when it ships. Add a delivery window if we can state one.

## Bottom line

We win on honesty and specificity, and we're losing because the first thing a visitor reads doesn't show it. Put the weekly roast and the Leeds team in the headline, put the price next to the button, and we'll be clearer than both competitors.
