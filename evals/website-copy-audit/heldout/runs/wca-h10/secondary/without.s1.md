# Homepage Competitive Review: Ember & Rye vs. Quillbean and Northpour

## Summary

Our homepage has the strongest proof of freshness in this set, but it is buried. Quillbean leads with the office buyer's problem and gives a concrete offer. Northpour is vague and could be any roaster. We win on specifics (weekly roasting, a fixed Tuesday/Wednesday cycle, a team in Leeds that packs every order), but none of that appears above the fold. Our headline could belong to any coffee brand.

## Where we're losing

**1. The headline is generic.** "Great coffee, delivered" makes no claim a buyer can check, and every roaster says it. Quillbean's "Office coffee without the faff" names the buyer's frustration and the office context in five words. Ours never says who the product is for beyond the word "office" in the subhead.

**2. Our best differentiator is three sections down.** The fact that we roast weekly and ship on Wednesdays is a strong, specific claim. It sits in a supporting section, so a visitor who scans the headline and subhead never sees it. Quillbean's "We roast to order... post it the next morning" makes a similar freshness claim in the subhead.

**3. Cost is hidden.** Quillbean has a "See plans and prices" button, so a buyer knows they can check the cost right away. Our button, "Start a subscription," asks for commitment before any price is shown. Office buyers usually need a budget figure before they'll even look at a signup flow.

**4. We don't give buyers a reason to trust us, and we haven't replaced the usual proof.** We have no reviews or customer numbers. Quillbean's free grinder loan is a concrete perk that's easy to weigh. We have no equivalent, and we shouldn't invent one. We do have trust signals we aren't using: a small team, a named city, and every order packed by a person.

## Where we're winning

Northpour's copy ("Elevate your workplace coffee experience," "ethically sourced," "for the discerning office") is vague and unverifiable. It's easy to ignore. Our copy describes a real process with real days, and our Leeds team is a point of difference neither competitor can match. We should lean into that.

## What to change

**Headline (replace "Great coffee, delivered"):**
> Office coffee roasted this week, shipped Wednesday.

This names the buyer (office), the freshness claim, and the schedule in one line, and the claim is checkable.

**Subhead:**
> Choose your beans and your delivery frequency. We roast on Tuesdays in Leeds, ship on Wednesdays, and every order is packed by one of our small team.

This moves the Tuesday/Wednesday schedule up and puts the human detail in the first screen.

**Section (keep, shorten):**
> Freshness you can plan around. Roasted on Tuesday, packed by hand, on your doorstep when you expect it.

Careful here: "on your doorstep when you expect it" implies delivery timing we haven't confirmed. Cut it unless the courier timing is reliable.

**Button:**
> See plans and prices

If we're willing to show pricing, this matches Quillbean's clarity and lowers the commitment ask. If we aren't, we should be ready to explain why a buyer shouldn't expect to see prices before signing up.

## Open questions for the team

1. **Can we show prices on the homepage or in a visible plans section?** This is the biggest gap against Quillbean and the change most likely to matter to buyers.
2. **Is there a perk we can offer that's real and affordable?** Even a small, specific offer is better than none.
3. **What's our real delivery window?** We ship Wednesdays. We need to confirm when the box arrives before we write anything that implies a delivery date.
4. **Can we add one real proof point?** Even a named office we supply, with permission, beats generic claims. Until then, the "packed by one of us" line is our best substitute and should stay.

Priority order: fix the headline and subhead first (both are copy-only), then decide on pricing visibility, since that needs a business decision.
