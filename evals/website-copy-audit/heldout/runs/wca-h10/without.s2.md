# Homepage copy: Ember & Rye vs Quillbean and Northpour

## Summary

Quillbean is the competitor to worry about. Northpour is weak, but our headline has more in common with theirs than we'd like. Our best material (the Tuesday roast, the Wednesday dispatch, the named small team in Leeds) sits in a section below the fold, while the top of the page says things any coffee company could say.

## Where we're losing

**1. The headline says nothing ownable.** "Great coffee, delivered" doesn't mention offices, freshness or us. Quillbean's "Office coffee without the faff" names the audience and the benefit, and has a voice. Northpour's "Elevate your workplace coffee experience" is empty too, but at least it says "workplace".

**2. The subhead describes a form, not a product.** "Choose your beans, choose your frequency, and we take care of the rest" is true of every subscription service. Quillbean's subhead is nearly all mechanics: bag size, delivery day, roast to order, posted next morning. A buyer comes away knowing how it works. Ours leaves them guessing.

**3. Our strongest proof is buried.** "Roast Tuesday, ship Wednesday" is a concrete, checkable claim. "Packed by one of us" is something Northpour can't say. Both are below the fold, and neither is in the headline or subhead.

**4. Our button asks for commitment.** "Start a subscription" asks someone to sign up before they know the cost. Quillbean's "See plans and prices" lowers the stakes. Northpour's "Learn more" is the weakest of the three.

**5. Quillbean has an offer and we don't.** The free grinder loan above 3kg a month gives a reason to choose them. Nothing on our page works that way. That's a business decision, not a copywriting one (see below).

**Where we're fine.** We beat Northpour on specificity. Our "small team in Leeds" is more human than their "discerning office". We should keep that, not copy Quillbean's "faff" voice.

**A caution on freshness.** Quillbean roasts to order and posts the next morning. That is arguably fresher than our weekly batch, so we shouldn't claim to be fresher. Our angle is a predictable rhythm and real people. Without reviews or customer numbers, specific, checkable detail has to do the work social proof would normally do.

## What to change

### Headline and subhead (highest priority)

> **Office coffee, roasted Tuesday and shipped Wednesday**
>
> Fresh-roasted subscriptions for offices, from a small team in Leeds. Choose your beans and how often you want them. We roast every Tuesday, ship on Wednesday, and one of us packs your order.

This puts the freshness rhythm, the audience, the place and the people into the first screen. I wrote "roasted Tuesday and shipped Wednesday" on the assumption that everything shipping on Wednesday was roasted the day before. If some orders ship from earlier roasts, use "roasted the week it ships" instead.

### Button

> **Choose your beans**

This leads into the flow we already describe and avoids the commitment of "Start a subscription". If we can show prices before sign-up, "See plans and prices" is stronger, and Quillbean has already shown it works. I haven't written any prices, since I don't know ours.

### The section (tighten and move up)

> We roast on Tuesdays and ship on Wednesdays, so your coffee leaves us within a day of roasting. We're a small team in Leeds, and every order is packed by one of us. If something's not right, you'll hear back from a person who packed it.

Only keep the last sentence if it's true. If it isn't, cut it. The first sentence also depends on the Tuesday-to-Wednesday assumption above.

### Footer (low priority)

"Questions? Email hello@emberandrye.example" is fine. If a person replies, say so: "Questions? Email us and one of the team will reply."

## Decisions for the team

- **Do we want an offer?** Quillbean's grinder loan is a concrete reason to switch. We need something small and real that we can actually deliver, such as flexible pausing or a sample bag. I don't want to invent one in copy.
- **Can we show prices up front?** This is the biggest single lever on the button.
- **Which claims can we verify?** Every claim above should be something we can prove, since "no reviews yet" makes accuracy our main credibility asset.
- **Add proof later.** When we have customers or reviews, a line of social proof goes directly under the button. Until then, the Tuesday/Wednesday rhythm is our proof.
