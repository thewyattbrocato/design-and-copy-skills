1. **Hero is generic.** "A smarter way to manage your team" could be signed by any rival, and it doesn't say what the product is. The specifics (restaurants, schedules, swaps) only show up in the second sentence. A stranger skimming the headline wouldn't know it's scheduling software.

2. **"Request access" gives no idea what happens next.** Together with "Pricing on request", the reader can't tell whether this is a demo, a waitlist or a sales call. They also can't tell what it costs until the last line of the page, so many will just leave.

3. **The UK/Ireland limit is buried in the footer, and "labor" is the US spelling.** Anyone outside those markets finds out last. A UK or Irish manager sees "labor cost" on a page that otherwise says "rota", which makes the copy look unfinished. Make it "labour".

4. **Section 1 makes a claim the page never backs up.** "Schedules your staff will actually follow" promises something about staff behavior, and nothing nearby supports it. Section 3's heading, "Know your numbers", is also vague. The real point, projected labour cost before publishing, is only in the second sentence.
