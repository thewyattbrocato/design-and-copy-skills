Ranked, worst first. I left the tagline alone and didn't rewrite anything. I only had this one page of copy, so I couldn't check the request-access flow or anything after the click.

1. **The hero says nothing a rival couldn't say.** "A smarter way to manage your team" could sit on any workforce tool, and "smarter" gives a stranger nothing to hold onto. The specific part (schedules, swaps, labor costs) only arrives in the second sentence, after the reader has already moved on.

2. **"Request access" plus "Pricing on request" asks for commitment with no information.** A visitor can't tell whether this is a demo, a waitlist or a trial. They also can't tell what happens next or roughly what it costs, and a busy restaurant manager is likely to leave rather than find out.

3. **The promises have nothing behind them.** "Build next week's rota in minutes" and "Schedules your staff will actually follow" are outcome claims with no proof, example or customer voice anywhere on the page. "Publish it straight to your team's phones" doesn't show that staff will follow anything, so the headline promises more than the line under it delivers.

4. **Small things undercut trust and clarity.**
   - "Labor cost" is US spelling on a page that says "rota" and sells only in the UK and Ireland, so it reads as imported. The footer is the first place a reader learns the product is UK and Ireland only.
   - "Staff request a swap in the app" mentions an app that is never introduced. The reader doesn't know whether staff must install something.
