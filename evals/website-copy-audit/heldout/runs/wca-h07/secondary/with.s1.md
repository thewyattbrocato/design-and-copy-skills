1. **The hero opens with a generic claim and then lists features.** "A smarter way to manage your team" could describe any scheduling tool, and the sentence after it is three features joined by commas. A restaurant manager gets no specific pain or outcome in the first few seconds.

2. **The page asks for commitment without giving a reason.** "Request access" is the only call to action, pricing is "on request," and the UK/Ireland availability sits in the footer. Visitors can't tell whether they qualify, what it costs, or whether they'll wait for access, so most will leave rather than fill in a form.

3. **Two of the three feature headlines are vague or unproven.** "Schedules your staff will actually follow" promises adherence without saying how, and "Know your numbers" hides the most distinctive feature, seeing projected labor cost before publishing, behind an abstract label. The swap section is the strongest because it names a specific mechanic.

4. **Nothing shows the product or who uses it.** There's no screenshot, customer, restaurant type, or result, so a skeptical operator has nothing to check the claims against before requesting access.
