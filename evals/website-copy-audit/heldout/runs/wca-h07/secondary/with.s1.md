1. **The hero could describe any workforce tool.** "A smarter way to manage your team" says nothing specific, and the restaurant context doesn't arrive until the second sentence. The sentence after it is a feature list, so the headline is what a skimming visitor will remember, and it's generic.

2. **The main CTA asks for commitment and withholds the first thing a buyer wants.** "Request access" doesn't say what happens next, and pricing is "on request" in the footer. Availability (UK and Ireland) is also only in the footer, so a visitor elsewhere learns they're out of scope at the bottom of the page.

3. **Every claim is asserted and nothing is proven.** "Build next week's rota in minutes," "schedules your staff will actually follow," and "stay on top of labor costs" are promises with no customer count, example, screenshot, or number behind them. A skeptical restaurant manager has no reason to believe them.

4. **The strongest differentiator is the least developed.** Projected labor cost before publishing is the most distinctive feature here, but it's the third section, with a vague header ("Know your numbers") and a one-line body. It gets less space and weaker framing than the generic scheduling and swaps copy.
