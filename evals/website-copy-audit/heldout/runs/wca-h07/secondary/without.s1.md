1. **The hero opener is generic.** "A smarter way to manage your team" could describe almost any software, and it spends the most prominent line on that before getting to restaurants or the actual features. Leading with the specific job would show a restaurant manager faster that this is for them.

2. **Two gates stack on the call to action.** "Request access" implies a waitlist, and "Pricing on request" adds a second barrier. Operators comparing scheduling tools often bounce when they can't see cost or start without asking, so the page asks for the most commitment while giving the least information.

3. **Nothing backs the claims.** "Schedules your staff will actually follow," "in minutes," and "see projected labor cost" are all promises with no proof: no customer, no number, no screenshot of the rota or cost view. For a buyer who has been burned by scheduling tools before, the page reads as assertion.

4. **The Section 3 headline hides the value.** "Know your numbers" doesn't say what numbers, and the useful point, seeing projected labor cost before publishing, is in the body text. A manager skimming the headlines gets no reason to stop there.
