1. **The hero opens with a claim that could describe any scheduling tool.** "A smarter way to manage your team" says nothing specific, and a restaurant manager has to read to the end of the paragraph to learn what the product does.

2. **The page asks for a commitment without giving a reason to trust it.** "Request access" is the only call to action, and there are no customer names, restaurant counts, screenshots, or testimonials to back the claims. Visitors have little basis for trading their contact details.

3. **Section 3 undersells the strongest differentiator.** Projected labor cost before publishing is the most concrete and valuable feature here, but "Know your numbers" is vague and the body is one short line. It reads as an afterthought.

4. **The two qualifiers that decide whether someone keeps reading are in the footer.** The UK and Ireland availability and the "pricing on request" line are both easy to miss there, so a visitor outside those markets or looking for a price could spend time on the page before finding out it isn't for them.
