1. **The button asks for a commitment the page hasn't earned.** "Request access" doesn't say what happens next, and "Pricing on request" means a visitor can't judge cost before handing over details. That's the biggest drag on conversion.

2. **The hero opens with nothing specific.** "A smarter way to manage your team" could describe any scheduling tool, and the sentence closes on the boilerplate "all in one place." The restaurant-specific pain (last-minute swaps, labor overruns) doesn't show up until the second sentence.

3. **The strongest differentiator is under the weakest headline.** Projected labor cost before publishing is the reason a restaurant manager would switch, but Section 3's "Know your numbers" is vague and the point gets one line. It's the only section that speaks to the owner's budget, so it's underweighted.

4. **Availability is buried in the footer.** "Available in the UK and Ireland" decides whether a visitor can use the product at all, but it only appears after they've scrolled past the request button. Visitors outside those markets will hit the CTA and become dead-end sign-ups.
