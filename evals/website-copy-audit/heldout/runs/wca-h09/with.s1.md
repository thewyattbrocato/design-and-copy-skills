# Tidewell copy audit (hero, how it works, price, FAQ)

I found no blockers. Nothing here is false, and there's no fake urgency or trick flow. The copy is clear and plain-spoken, so I'd change little. The fixes below are mostly about price and one claim that needs checking.

## Findings, ranked

**1. Major: Price leaves the key question open (Price)**
> "Free for households of up to 4. Larger households are $2 a month."

- **Problem:** The reader can't tell if $2 is per household or per person, or who pays. They also can't tell how it's billed or how to cancel. Someone in a household of 5 is deciding whether this costs $2 or $10.
- **Fix:** State who pays and what $2 covers. If cancelling is simple, say so. Use the real answers, not my guesses:
  > "Free for households of up to 4 people. Larger households are $2 a month **[per household / per person]**, **[billed how, cancel anytime from where]**."
- Also confirm the currency. "$2" alone could be USD, AUD, CAD or NZD.

**2. Major: "Straight away" is a strong claim that needs a source on file (How it works, step 2; hero)**
> "Everyone sees the list update straight away." / "you see them as they're added."

- **Problem:** Shops often have poor signal. If an item added at home doesn't reach someone at the shop, or ticked items only vanish once they're back online, the central promise breaks at the moment it matters (step 3).
- **Fix:** Confirm how the app behaves offline and with slow signal. If the sync isn't instant in every case, soften the line to something true, such as "Everyone sees the list update" or "…as soon as they're online."

**3. Minor: The hero's closing sentences read like a disclaimer (Hero subhead)**
> "It works on iPhone and Android. There is no web version yet."

- **Problem:** The honesty is good, but four sentences before the button is a lot, and the platform note comes after "Everyone adds items…", which already repeats the headline. A desktop visitor learns the limit only by reading to the end.
- **Fix:** Shorten the subhead and move the facts into a line under the button:
  > Subhead: "Everyone adds items, and everyone sees them as they're added. Tidewell is a shared list for people who live together."
  > Under the button: "Free for households of up to 4 · iPhone and Android · No web version yet"
- This puts the price next to the decision, which also helps finding 1.

**4. Minor: Locale mismatch (How it works, step 3)**
> "Tick things off in the shop."

- **Problem:** "Tick" and "the shop" read British or Australian, while "$" suggests a US audience. If the audience is mixed, some readers will stumble. If it isn't, ignore this. It's a decision to make, not an error.
- **Fix:** Pick the market and make the vocabulary and currency match. For the US: "Check things off in the store."

**5. Minor: The button is generic (Hero)**
> "Download the app"

- **Problem:** It doesn't name the product or say that one tap finds your store. This is a small issue because the page has one clear action.
- **Fix:** "Download Tidewell". Add "for iPhone or Android" only if the button goes to a store chooser.

**6. Minor: The FAQ has one question and skips ones the page raises (FAQ)**
- **Problem:** The page raises "There is no web version yet" and "Larger households are $2" but never follows up. Readers will wonder about both.
- **Fix:** Add only questions you can answer from facts you have, for example "Who pays when a household has more than 4 people?" and "Will there be a web version?" Don't promise a web version unless it's planned and you're willing to say so.

**Also noticed:** Step 2 repeats "Everyone" from the hero, which is harmless. The page has no stated way to leave or delete a household. That's worth a line in the FAQ if it exists.

## What works (leave alone)

- **Headline:** "One grocery list for the whole household". A stranger knows what it is and who it's for.
- **Step 3:** "…so nobody buys the milk twice." It shows the benefit in a concrete moment.
- **Step 1:** "Invite the people you live with by link or code." It's short and plain.
- **Hero:** "There is no web version yet." Keep the honesty. Only its position changes (finding 3).
- **FAQ:** "Yes, a household can be one person." It's a straight answer.

## Fix order

1. Clarify who pays the $2, the billing and the currency (**small**, needs the founder's answers).
2. Verify the real-time and offline behavior, then adjust "straight away" if needed (**small**).
3. Trim the subhead and add the price and platform line under the button (**small**).
4. Decide the locale (shop/store, tick/check) and apply it consistently (**small**).
5. Add two or three FAQ answers drawn from facts you already have (**medium**).

**Not reviewed:** the app-store listing, onboarding screens and any emails. The promise on this page should match them.
