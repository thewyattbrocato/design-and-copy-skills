# Tidewell site audit: hero, how it works, price, FAQ

**Verdict:** No blockers. A stranger can tell what this is in one read. The weak spot is the price line, which leaves open questions about what a larger household pays and when. Most of the copy should stay as it is.

## Findings, ranked

**1. Price: who pays $2, and what happens at person five? (Major)**
> "Free for households of up to 4. Larger households are $2 a month."

Readers can't tell whether $2 is per household or per person, and "larger" is vague. A household of five also can't tell what happens when the fifth person joins. Does the list lock? Does the owner get a prompt? Who is charged? Nothing says how to stop paying or whether the free tier has other limits. Price copy that leaves the money question open reads as hiding something, even when it isn't.
**Fix:** Say "5 or more" and name who pays. Add one line on what happens at the limit. Confirm the facts in brackets before launch:
> "Free for households of up to 4 people. For 5 or more, it's $2 a month [per household, paid by the person who made it, cancel any time in the app: confirm each]."

**2. The hero never says it's free (Major)**
> Subhead: "...It works on iPhone and Android. There is no web version yet." / Button: "Download the app"

The price sits below the fold. A visitor deciding whether to download doesn't learn that most households pay nothing. This is also a fact you already have. It fits where the platform sentence already sits.
**Fix:** Add it to the subhead without lengthening it much:
> "Tidewell is a shared list for people who live together. Free for up to 4 people, on iPhone and Android. There is no web version yet."

**3. The FAQ is one question, and it skips the ones people will have (Major)**
> "Can I use it on my own? Yes, a household can be one person."

The answer is good. But a launch FAQ with a single entry leaves the money and membership questions unanswered. I can't write those answers because I don't have the facts. Questions to answer, once you confirm what's true:
- What happens when a fifth person joins?
- What if someone leaves the household?
- Can I be in two households?
- What can other members see?

**4. A ticked item "disappears" (Minor, but check the product)**
> "They disappear from everyone's list so nobody buys the milk twice."

Someone who ticks the wrong item, or ticks at the till and then needs it back, wants to know whether it's gone for good. Also check that "straight away" and "nobody buys the milk twice" hold with weak signal in a shop. If they don't always hold, soften the line. If they do, leave it.
**Fix if there's an undo or history:** add a clause, e.g. "...and you can bring one back if you tick it by mistake." Add that only if it's true.

**5. Mixed British and American wording (Minor)**
> "Tick things off in the shop" next to "grocery list" and "$2"

"Tick" and "in the shop" read British. "Grocery" and "$" read American. Pick the market you're launching in and match it, e.g. "Check things off at the store" for the US. If the audience really is mixed, leave it, since both read fine.

**6. The hero button doesn't help desktop visitors (Minor)**
> Button: "Download the app"

There's no web version, so someone on a laptop has nowhere to go from this button. I can't see what it does. If it only links to one store, add a store choice or a send-to-phone option. This is a product decision, not a wording one.

*Also noticed:* the hero subhead is four sentences, and its first clause nearly repeats step 2 ("see them as they're added" / "see the list update straight away"). It works, so I'd leave it.

## What works (leave alone)

- **"One grocery list for the whole household"**: plain and specific, and it names the category and the audience.
- **"There is no web version yet."**: admitting the limit up front saves visitors a wasted trip.
- **"Make a household. Invite the people you live with by link or code."**: it's a clear first step, and the fact that you can invite by link or code removes a hurdle.
- **"...so nobody buys the milk twice."**: it gives the reason in the reader's own terms.
- **"Yes, a household can be one person."**: short and direct.

## Fix order

1. Rewrite the price line and add the missing terms (**small**, but you need the facts confirmed first).
2. Put "free for up to 4" in the hero subhead (**small**).
3. Expand the FAQ to four or five real questions (**medium**, since it needs product answers).
4. Decide the British or American wording and apply it everywhere (**small**).
5. Check the hero button on desktop and the ticked-item behaviour (**medium**).

*Not reviewed:* the rest of the page, the app store listing, and the sign-up and first-run screens. *Needs confirming:* the $2 billing details, which I left as bracketed placeholders.
