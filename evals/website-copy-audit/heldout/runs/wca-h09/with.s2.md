## Findings, ranked

No blockers. There is no false claim, fake urgency or trick flow on this page. The problems are one major gap and a few smaller ones.

**1. Major. Price: "Larger households are $2 a month."**
- A reader with 5 or more housemates can't tell whether $2 is per household or per person, who pays, or how to stop. That is the decision this line affects, and the answer isn't beside it.
- "Larger" is also vague. It means 5 or more, but the reader has to work that out.
- Fix: say "5 or more", and state what $2 covers and how to cancel. I don't know those facts, so I've left placeholders.
- Rewrite: *"Free for households of up to 4 people. For 5 or more, it's $2 a month [per household / per person: confirm]. [Who pays, and how to cancel: one line.]"*

**2. Major. Hero: the best reason to care is buried.**
- The subhead says "Everyone adds items, and you see them as they're added", which describes a feature. The stake, "nobody buys the milk twice", only appears in step 3 of How it works.
- A stranger reads "shared grocery list", which many apps could claim. The milk line is the one that makes it Tidewell's.
- Rewrite: *"Everyone adds items and sees them as they're added, so nobody buys the milk twice. Tidewell is a shared list for people who live together. It works on iPhone and Android. There's no web version yet."*
- Keep step 3 as it is. The repeat there is fine.

**3. Minor to major, depending on audience. Voice and locale.**
- "in the shop", "straight away" and "Tick things off" read as British, but the price is in `$`.
- If your launch audience is American, these read slightly foreign. "Tick" in particular reads as "check off" to many US readers.
- If the audience is UK, Australian or similar, leave them. They suit the voice.
- Decide the audience first, then make the page consistent. Don't mix.

**4. Minor. Hero button: "Download the app"**
- You say there's no web version, so a desktop visitor who clicks this needs somewhere sensible to land.
- I can't see where the button goes. Check that it leads to both stores, or offers a link to send to a phone, rather than a dead end on desktop.
- If a store badge or "for iPhone and Android" sits beside it, the label is fine as it is.

**5. Minor. FAQ is a single entry.**
- The one answer is good, but a pricing-dependent product will get obvious questions. For example, what happens when a 5th person joins a free household?
- Only add entries you can answer from fact. I haven't written any, because I'd be inventing the answers.

## What works, so leave it alone
- "One grocery list for the whole household" is a plain headline a stranger understands in one read.
- "It works on iPhone and Android. There is no web version yet." Saying the limit up front stops disappointed clicks and builds trust.
- "Invite the people you live with by link or code." It's concrete and uses the reader's words.
- "so nobody buys the milk twice". It's vivid and specific. Use it more, not less.
- "Yes, a household can be one person." It answers directly with no hedging.

## Fix order
1. Price terms: per household or person, who pays, how to cancel, and "5 or more" (small, once you have the facts).
2. Move the milk line into the hero subhead (small).
3. Decide US or UK wording and make the page consistent (small).
4. Check the button destination on desktop and in each store (small).
5. Add FAQ entries for real questions you can answer, especially the 5th-member case (medium).

Not reviewed: the rest of the site, the store listings and the sign-up flow. Have someone confirm that "see the list update straight away" matches how the app behaves. It's a real-time claim, and I can't check it from here.
