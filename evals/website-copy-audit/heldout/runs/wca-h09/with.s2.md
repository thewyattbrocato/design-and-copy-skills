## Findings, ranked

**1. Price: the $2 is ambiguous (Major, Price)**
> "Larger households are $2 a month."

A reader with 5 people can't tell what they would pay. The line doesn't say whether $2 is per household or per person, or which currency (`$` could be USD, AUD, CAD or NZD). It doesn't say what happens when a free household adds a fifth member, such as whether it is blocked, locked, or asked to pay. It also doesn't say how to cancel. The pricing section is where a reader decides whether to commit, so this gap costs sign-ups.
**Fix:** state the unit, the currency, the over-limit behaviour and how to cancel. I can't supply these facts, so the rewrite below uses placeholders.

> Free for households of up to 4 people. Households of 5 or more are $2 a month [per household / per person? currency?]. [What happens when a fourth-person household adds a fifth.] [How to cancel.]

**2. Hero button leads desktop visitors to a dead end (Major, Hero)**
> Button: "Download the app" / "There is no web version yet."

On a laptop, a visitor can't download an iPhone or Android app and can't use Tidewell in the browser. The button doesn't say which platforms it covers, and the page doesn't say what a desktop visitor should do next.
**Fix:** name the platforms on the button, and give desktop visitors a route to the store. Only do this if it's true: a QR code, or "Open this page on your phone." Suggested label: **"Download Tidewell for iPhone or Android"**.

**3. "Free" is at the bottom of the page (Major, Hero → Price)**
> Hero subhead has no price. "Free for households of up to 4" appears only in the price section.

The strongest reason to try the app is hidden below the fold. Most households are 4 people or fewer, so for them the answer to "what does it cost?" is "nothing". That should be in the hero.

**4. The hero subhead does too many jobs and repeats step 2 (Minor, Hero)**
> "Everyone adds items, and you see them as they're added. Tidewell is a shared list for people who live together. It works on iPhone and Android. There is no web version yet."

It leads with a mechanism that "How it works" repeats ("Everyone sees the list update straight away"). It puts the product definition second. It also uses four sentences for what could be two.
**Fix**, using the founder's own words and adding the price from finding 3:

> Tidewell is a shared list for people who live together: everyone adds items and sees them as they're added. Free for households of up to 4. Works on iPhone and Android; there's no web version yet.

**5. Mixed regional language and an unspecified currency (Minor, How it works / Price)**
> "Tick things off in the shop", "straight away", "$2 a month"

"Tick", "shop" and "straight away" read British or Australian. `$` and "grocery list" read North American. Whichever market you're launching in, half the page will sound like someone else's. This also feeds finding 1.
**Fix:** pick the launch market and make the words and currency match. For example, a US launch would use "Check things off in the store".

**6. The product category drifts (Minor, Hero → How it works)**
> "One grocery list" → "a shared list" → "Add things as you think of them"

The headline says grocery list, but the subhead and steps describe a general shared list. A visitor can't tell whether it's only for groceries. If it is only groceries, say so in step 2 ("Add groceries as you think of them"). If it isn't, say what else it's for.

**Also noticed:**
- The FAQ has one question. If real users have asked about guests, two households, or who pays, add those answers. Don't add questions you don't have answers for.
- "Household" is the founder's word. It's consistent and clear here, so keep it. Check in user tests that people read it as "the people I live with".

## What works (leave alone)
- "One grocery list for the whole household": it's specific and plain, and a stranger understands it at once.
- "There is no web version yet.": it admits the limit up front, so nobody finds out after clicking.
- "…so nobody buys the milk twice.": it names the outcome in a concrete image.
- "Invite the people you live with by link or code.": it's clear, and it says how.
- "Yes, a household can be one person.": a direct answer to the real objection.
