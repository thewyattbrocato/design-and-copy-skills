**Findings, ranked.** I found no blockers. Nothing here is false or manipulative, and the page is honest to a fault. All the findings are Major or Minor.

1. **Major. Price: "Larger households are $2 a month."**
   - The reader can't tell whether $2 is per household or per person, or what a household of 5 pays. It's the only money line on the page, so a small gap here reads as a hidden cost.
   - The page also doesn't say whether the paid tier unlocks anything or whether the free tier has limits. Add that only if it's true.
   - Fix: "Free for households of up to 4 people. Larger households: $2 a month [per household / per person, confirm]."

2. **Major. The best objection-remover sits furthest from the button.**
   - Hero: "Download the app." The "free for up to 4" line only appears further down, under Price. Most households are 2–4 people, so most visitors are never told it's free at the point of decision.
   - Fix: put "Free for households of up to 4" directly under the button. This uses your own fact and adds nothing new.

3. **Major. The invite step doesn't survive "no web version."**
   - How it works: "Invite the people you live with by link or code." Hero: "There is no web version yet."
   - A housemate who taps a link on a phone, or on a laptop, has no stated path to the list. Do they need the app first? What does the link do?
   - Fix: add one clause to step 1 once you've confirmed the flow, for example "they'll be asked to install the app" or whatever is true. Also confirm that the Download button does something sensible on a desktop browser, such as store links or a QR code.

4. **Major, needs a source on file. "Everyone sees the list update straight away" / "you see them as they're added."**
   - It's the core promise and a strong one. Shops are exactly where signal is bad, and the page promises items vanish for everyone as you tick them.
   - If sync can lag or work offline, an absolute "straight away" will be tested on launch weekend. Confirm the behaviour with the team.
   - If it's reliably real-time, keep the wording. If not, soften it to "Everyone sees the list update as it syncs", or whatever is true.

5. **Minor to Major. Mixed dialect.**
   - "straight away", "in the shop", "Tick things off", "the milk" are British. "$2" and the iPhone/Android framing suggest a US reader, though your audience isn't stated.
   - A US reader hears "tick" and "in the shop" as slightly foreign. A UK reader sees a dollar price. Pick the market.
   - If US: "right away", "at the store", "Check things off."
   - If UK or international: show the price in the local currency.

6. **Minor. The subhead is doing four jobs, and the order buries the category.**
   - "Everyone adds items, and you see them as they're added." repeats "added". The sentence about the platform and the web caveat ends up in the middle of the value statement.
   - Rewrite: "Tidewell is a shared list for people who live together. Everyone adds items and sees them appear as they go on. Available on iPhone and Android, with no web version yet."
   - Keeping "yet" is fine only if a web version is really planned.

7. **Minor. The FAQ has one question, and the answer is flat.**
   - "Can I use it on my own? Yes, a household can be one person." The answer is fine and the question is good to include.
   - Likely questions it leaves open: what happens when a fifth person joins, whether housemates need to install the app, how to leave or delete a household, and how to undo an accidental tick (step 3 says items "disappear").
   - Add only the ones you can answer today.

*Also noticed:*
- The headline says "grocery list" while the subhead says "shared list". Fine, but make sure the product UI uses the same word.
- Step 3's "nobody buys the milk twice" is a benefit line, so keep it as it is.

**What works. Leave these alone:**
- "One grocery list for the whole household": plain, specific, and a stranger can tell what it is.
- "Tick things off in the shop. They disappear from everyone's list so nobody buys the milk twice." It states the outcome with a concrete example.
- "Add things as you think of them." It's in your customers' words.
- "There is no web version yet." Stating the limit upfront is a trust-builder, and the problem is only its placement (finding 6).
- "Yes, a household can be one person." It's direct and answers the question.
