## Findings

No blockers. Nothing on the page is false, unsupported or manipulative. One major gap in the price section is worth fixing before Friday.

**1. Major: Price section leaves the main questions open**
> "Free for households of up to 4. Larger households are $2 a month."

- **Who pays:** a reader can't tell whether $2 is per household or per person. If it's per household, they can't tell who pays.
- **What happens at the fifth person:** it isn't clear whether they get blocked, the list freezes, or the household gets prompted to pay.
- **Terms:** billing and cancellation aren't stated.
- A five-person flat will read this as a possible surprise charge.
- **Fix:** answer these in the sentence. I don't have the facts, so the placeholders are for the founder to fill in.

> "Free for households of up to 4 people. Add a fifth and it's $2 a month [per household / per person; who pays]. [Cancel any time / what happens if you stop paying]."

**2. Minor: Hero subhead buries the definition and hides "free"**
> "Everyone adds items, and you see them as they're added. Tidewell is a shared list for people who live together. It works on iPhone and Android. There is no web version yet."

- The first sentence repeats the "How it works" section. The actual definition comes second.
- The best fact, that it's free for most households, only appears further down the page.
- The "no web version yet" line is honest and should stay. It currently trails the sentence about platforms.
- **Rewrite** (same facts, reordered):

> "Tidewell is a shared list for people who live together. Everyone adds items and sees them as they're added. Free for households of up to 4. On iPhone and Android; no web version yet."

**3. Minor: "Tick things off" step doesn't say whether a mistake can be undone**
> "They disappear from everyone's list so nobody buys the milk twice."

- A misplaced tap makes an item vanish for the whole household.
- If there's an undo or a "bought" view, add half a sentence saying so. If there isn't, leave the line alone.
- I can't write this without knowing the product, so it needs a check against the app.

**4. Minor: Button doesn't match a page with no web version**
> "Download the app"

- A visitor on a laptop can't use the product from this page and has nowhere obvious to go next.
- If the button opens the App Store and Google Play, say so (for example "Download for iPhone or Android"). If it detects the device, leave it as is.
- Confirm the links are live before Friday.

**5. Minor: FAQ is one question and leaves out the likely hesitations**
> "Can I use it on my own? Yes, a household can be one person."

- The answer is good. Only one question is covered, though.
- Candidates, once you have true answers:
  - What happens at a fifth member?
  - Who pays?
  - Can I be in two households?
  - What happens when a housemate leaves?
  - Does it work with weak signal in the shop?
- Each needs a factual answer from the founder.

**6. Minor: Mixed regional wording**
> "in the shop", "Tick things off", "straight away", next to "$2" and "grocery list"

- If your market is the US, "store", "check off" and "right away" will read as more natural. If it's Australia or elsewhere, the current wording is fine.
- Pick one and apply it consistently, including in the app's own strings.

**Also noticed:** the hero doesn't say how this differs from sharing a notes or reminders list. You may not need it, but a stranger might ask.

## What works (leave alone)
- "One grocery list for the whole household": a stranger knows the product, the audience and the benefit.
- "so nobody buys the milk twice": a concrete outcome in the reader's own terms.
- "There is no web version yet.": plain and honest, and it saves a wasted click. Keep it, just move it (finding 2).
- "Invite the people you live with by link or code.": clear and short.
- "Yes, a household can be one person.": a direct answer that removes a worry.

## Fix order
1. Spell out the price terms: who pays, what happens at a fifth member, billing and cancellation (small, once the founder confirms the facts).
2. Reorder the hero subhead and add "free for up to 4" (small).
3. Confirm the button destination and make its label match the platforms (small).
4. Add 3–5 FAQ entries with true answers (medium).
5. Settle regional wording and check whether ticking an item off can be undone (small).
