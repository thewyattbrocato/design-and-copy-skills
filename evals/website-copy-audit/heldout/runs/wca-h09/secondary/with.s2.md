**Audit: Tidewell homepage copy (hero, how it works, price, FAQ)**

**Findings, ranked**

1. **Major. Price section, "Larger households are $2 a month."** The reader can't tell what they're paying for. Is $2 per household or per extra person? Monthly billing, or is there a trial? Can they cancel? "Larger" also doesn't say where the line is. The price is the decision point, so these gaps cost sign-ups or create support tickets after they happen. *Fix:* state the threshold and the unit, then state billing and cancellation. Facts to confirm are in the rewrite below.

2. **Major. Button, "Download the app."** It doesn't say which app. An Android visitor on iPhone-only copy (or a desktop visitor) can't tell whether this works for them until they click. Combined with finding 3, the page ends on a button that may be a dead end. *Fix:* name the platform on each button.

3. **Major. Subhead, "It works on iPhone and Android. There is no web version yet."** This is the most important limit on the page, and it sits in the middle of a paragraph about the product. It belongs next to the button, where the decision happens. Honest disclosure is a strength; the placement is the problem.

4. **Major. Solo use is answered only in the FAQ.** The subhead says "people who live together," so a single person may decide it isn't for them and leave before reaching the FAQ. The FAQ answer is correct and useful, so it should be in the hero too. *Fix:* add one clause to the subhead.

5. **Minor. Subhead repeats How it works.** "Everyone adds items, and you see them as they're added" is said again in step 2 ("Everyone sees the list update straight away"). The hero should say what the product is and who it's for. The mechanics belong in the steps.

6. **Minor. "Everyone sees the list update straight away" is a real-time claim.** That's fine if it's true, but it needs a basis on file (for example, sync latency in testing). If it's usually a few seconds, "straight away" overpromises. I can't verify this from the copy.

7. **Minor. Terms drift: "household," "people who live together," "whole household," "the people you live with."** It reads as one idea, but four phrasings make a reader wonder if they mean different things. Pick one and reuse it.

**What works (keep these)**

- "One grocery list for the whole household." Specific, the category is obvious, and it's the right headline.
- "Tick things off in the shop. They disappear from everyone's list so nobody buys the milk twice." This is the strongest line on the page: a concrete moment the reader recognises.
- "There is no web version yet." Honest, short, and useful. Keep it verbatim; only move it.
- "Yes, a household can be one person." Direct and correct.

**Rewrites (flagged lines only)**

- **Subhead** (finding 4, 5):
  > Tidewell is a shared list for the people you live with, including if that's just you. Available on iPhone and Android.

- **Buttons** (finding 2):
  > Download for iPhone · Download for Android

- **Under the buttons** (finding 3):
  > No web version yet.

- **Price** (finding 1). The bracketed items are facts the founder must supply; I haven't filled them in:
  > Free for households of up to 4 people. Households of 5 or more pay $2 a month, billed [monthly / per household / per extra person — confirm]. Cancel any time [confirm].

- **"Straight away"** (finding 6): keep if it's true. If sync takes a few seconds, use "within seconds" instead, and only if that's accurate.

**Fix order**

1. Clarify the price terms (unit, threshold, billing, cancellation). *Small effort, but needs the founder's answers first.*
2. Name the platform on the download buttons. *Small.*
3. Move the "no web version" line next to the buttons. *Small.*
4. Add the solo-use clause to the subhead. *Small.*
5. Confirm the real-time claim, then keep or soften "straight away." *Small.*

**Verdict on launch:** No blockers on this copy. The price terms and download buttons should be fixed before Friday; the rest is polish.
