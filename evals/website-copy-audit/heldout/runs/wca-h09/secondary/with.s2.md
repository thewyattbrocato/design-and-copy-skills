## Findings

**1. Price doesn't say what currency or unit it's in.** Blocker until confirmed.
- Quote: "Larger households are $2 a month."
- The copy is British ("straight away", "shop"), but the price is in dollars. It also doesn't say whether $2 is per household or per person.
- A UK reader can't tell what they'll pay. If the currency is wrong, the page states a wrong price.
- Fix: confirm the currency, amount and unit before Friday. Rewrite: "Free for households of up to 4. Households of 5 or more pay [amount and currency] a month, [per household / per person]."

**2. The recurring charge has no stated way out.** Blocker unless cancellation is shown where people pay.
- Quote: "Larger households are $2 a month."
- This is a subscription, and nothing on the page says how it renews or how to stop it. A household deciding whether to add a fifth person can't see the exit.
- Fix: put the renewal and cancellation route next to the price, not only in terms. Rewrite: "[Renews monthly. Cancel any time in ___. Confirm the real route.]"

**3. The line that decides whether you can use it is buried.** Major.
- Quote, end of the subhead: "It works on iPhone and Android. There is no web version yet."
- A visitor reads the whole pitch before learning they need a phone. A laptop visitor can't use the product at all, and that should be clear before they click.
- Fix: move the platform sentence to sit directly under the button and cut it from the subhead. Rewrite (under the button): "For iPhone and Android. There's no web version yet."

**4. The button doesn't say what it gets you.** Major.
- Quote: "Download the app"
- It doesn't name a platform, so a laptop visitor clicks and gets nothing, and Android users can't tell they're covered.
- Rewrite: "Get Tidewell for iPhone or Android." If you have store badges, use them alongside.

**5. The subhead repeats the how-it-works section.** Minor.
- Quote: "Everyone adds items, and you see them as they're added."
- Step 2 says the same thing: "Everyone sees the list update straight away." The hero spends its second sentence on a feature the next section already covers.
- Rewrite: "Tidewell is a shared list for people who live together." Let step 2 carry the live update.

**6. "Straight away" is a timing claim.** Minor, needs a source on file.
- Quote: "Everyone sees the list update straight away."
- If updates depend on a connection, someone on patchy signal will see a delay. This is probably true in normal use, so keep it, but check it before you publish it as stated.
- Optional softer line if it isn't reliable: "Everyone sees the list update as soon as they're connected."

**Also noticed:** "Free for households of up to 4" should be checked too. Is free permanent, or is there a trial or a catch? If there is, it belongs beside "Free".

## What works

- "One grocery list for the whole household" names the category and the reader's problem without hype. Keep it.
- "Invite the people you live with by link or code." It says how the product works, in concrete terms. Keep it.
- "They disappear from everyone's list so nobody buys the milk twice." It leads with the outcome. Keep it.
- "Yes, a household can be one person." It answers the question in its first word. Keep it.
- "Free for households of up to 4." Clear threshold. Keep it once finding 1 confirms the facts behind it.
