The hero and steps are close. The biggest risks are the pricing wording, which is ambiguous, and a few product claims that need checking before launch.

## Rewritten copy

**Hero**
- **Headline:** One grocery list for the whole household
- **Subhead:** Everyone in the house adds to the same list, and changes show up right away. Available on iPhone and Android. Not on the web yet.
- **Button:** Download the app

**How it works**
1. **Start a household.** Invite the people you live with by link or code.
2. **Add things as you think of them.** Everyone sees the change right away.
3. **Tick things off at the shop.** They disappear from everyone's list, so nobody buys the milk twice.

**Price**
- Free for households of up to 4.
- Households of 5 or more pay $2 a month.

**FAQ**
- **Can I use it on my own?** Yes. A household can be just you, and it's free.

## What I changed and why

- **The hero subhead is doing three jobs.** I cut "Tidewell is a shared list for people who live together," which repeats the headline, and moved "not on the web yet" to the end as a plain fact.
- **"Everyone sees it update" appears in both the hero and step 2.** I kept the claim in step 2 and shortened the hero to avoid saying it twice.
- **"Straight away" is British/Australian and "grocery" is American.** The page mixes both. I used "right away" and kept "shop" and "tick," since the founder's body copy uses them. Pick one variety for the whole site.
- **The price line had unclear terms.** "Larger households" doesn't say whether the $2 is per household or per person, or what happens at exactly 5. The rewrite assumes per household. Change it if that's wrong.

## Things to confirm before Friday

1. **Pricing:** Is the $2 per household or per person? Is the free tier permanent or a trial? Is there an annual option? Is "$" the right currency for every market you're launching in?
2. **Real-time claim:** "Changes show up right away" is a product promise. Test it on both platforms, including what happens offline or on a weak connection. If it's only "within a few seconds," say that instead.
3. **Platform button:** "Download the app" is ambiguous for desktop visitors, who can't install it. Consider App Store and Google Play badges so people go to the right store.
4. **FAQ depth:** One question won't cover the usual launch objections. Good candidates are: Is it free? Who can see my list? What happens if someone leaves the household?
