**Findings, ranked**

1. **Major. PRICE, "Free for households of up to 4. Larger households are $2 a month."** A reader can't tell whether $2 is per household or per person, whether it's billed monthly or yearly, whether a child counts toward the 4, or whether "Free" lasts. Price is the decision most likely to produce a surprise charge and a distrustful review. Rewrite below. The bracketed part needs the founder's answer before launch.

2. **Major. Subhead, last sentence, "It works on iPhone and Android. There is no web version yet." and the button, "Download the app."** The platform limit sits in the paragraph, away from the button. A visitor on a laptop reads the headline, clicks the button, and only then learns they can't use the product. The button also doesn't say which app or store it means, on a page that serves two platforms. Rewrite below.

3. **Major. HOW IT WORKS, "Invite the people you live with by link or code."** This is the first real step for a household, and it doesn't say what the invitee needs. Do they need the app already installed? Does the link open the app store? With no web version, a link that opens a browser page would be a dead end. Rewrite below. The founder needs to confirm the answer.

4. **Minor. Subhead, "Everyone adds items, and you see them as they're added." then "Everyone sees the list update straight away."** The first two sentences say the same thing, and the one that says what Tidewell is ("Tidewell is a shared list for people who live together") comes third. Rewrite below.

5. **Minor, check the claim. HOW IT WORKS, "They disappear from everyone's list so nobody buys the milk twice."** If ticked items are removed, the copy is accurate. If they're crossed off or shown as bought, "disappear" is wrong and a reader who wants to see what was bought will be surprised. "Nobody buys the milk twice" also depends on everyone being online and synced. Keep the line if the product works this way. This is a check on the product, not a rewrite.

**Blockers:** none found. No claims needing a source, no urgency, no testimonials, and no hidden cancellation. The price terms are the closest to a problem, and they are Major.

**What works (keep as written)**

- "One grocery list for the whole household." One idea, in the reader's words.
- "Tidewell is a shared list for people who live together." It says what the product is. Move it up, as in finding 4.
- "Add things as you think of them." Concrete and easy to picture.
- "Tick things off in the shop." The clearest line on the page. The outcome is what matters to a shopper.
- "Yes, a household can be one person." It answers the real objection for solo users in few words.

**Rewrites** (flagged lines only)

- **Finding 1, PRICE:** "Free for households of up to 4 people. Households of 5 or more pay $2 a month, per household [billed monthly, confirm]. [Confirm: does Free have an end date?]"
- **Finding 2, under the button:** "Available on iPhone and Android. No web version yet." If there is no planned date, "No web version." is the more honest line.
- **Finding 2, button:** Two buttons, "Get it on the App Store" and "Get it on Google Play" [store links needed]. If there's only one button for now, "Download for iPhone or Android" is accurate.
- **Finding 3, invite line:** "Invite the people you live with by link or code. [Confirm: what do they need to join, the app installed first?]"
- **Finding 4, subhead:** "Tidewell is a shared list for people who live together. Anyone adds an item, and everyone sees it straight away."

**Fix order**

1. **Price terms** (medium effort; the founder needs to answer the bracketed questions). Stops the surprise-charge problem.
2. **Platform line and store buttons** (small; needs store links). Stops visitors clicking into a dead end.
3. **Invite requirements** (small; needs a yes/no from the founder on whether invitees need the app first).
4. **Subhead** (small; copy change only). Puts the category first and removes the repeat.
5. **Verify "disappear"** (small; check the product behaviour, change the word only if it's wrong).
