**Findings**

1. **Major. Price section, "Larger households are $2 a month."** The price is the decision point, and the reader can't tell what they'd pay. Is $2 per household or per person? Is it billed monthly, and can they cancel? "Larger" is also vague: the free tier is "up to 4", so the paid tier should start at 5 in the same words. Fix: state the threshold and the unit, and confirm the terms with the owner before launch.

2. **Major. Hero button, "Download the app."** It doesn't say which store, and the page says there's no web version. A desktop visitor clicks and hits a dead end, and the platform limit sits at the end of the subhead, where it's easy to miss. Fix: name the platforms on the button and move the web note next to it.

3. **Minor. Hero subhead, "Everyone adds items, and you see them as they're added."** This repeats "Everyone sees the list update straight away" in How it works, and the product name arrives mid-sentence. The subhead is also doing four jobs (what it does, who it's for, platforms, web limit). Fix: tighten it and let the How it works section carry the mechanics.

4. **Minor. FAQ, "Yes, a household can be one person."** The answer is correct but reads as a rule rather than a reply. It's also the only FAQ on the page, so it looks like a placeholder. Fix: a shorter answer, and add the next question real visitors will ask (probably "Is there a web or Android version?") once the owner confirms the answer.

5. **Minor, needs a check. "Everyone sees the list update straight away."** This is a product claim. Grocery shops often have poor signal, so if the list only syncs when online, "straight away" will be wrong for the exact moment the feature matters most. Fix: test it in a shop-like connection, and add a qualifier if the sync is delayed.

**What works**
- "One grocery list for the whole household": clear category, clear audience, no hype. Keep it.
- "Tick things off in the shop. They disappear from everyone's list so nobody buys the milk twice." This is the strongest line on the page: a concrete benefit in a situation the reader recognises. Keep it.
- "Make a household. Invite the people you live with by link or code." Plain, sequential, and tells the reader exactly what to do first.
- "Free for households of up to 4": the free tier is stated plainly. Keep the wording, only fix the paid side (finding 1).
- The page makes no urgency, scarcity or social-proof claims. Keep it that way.
