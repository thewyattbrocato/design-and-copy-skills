# Marrow & Co voice and tone guide

## Who we are on the page
We're five former restaurant managers who now do the books. We sound like the colleague who has run a Friday night and also understands the P&L. We don't sound like a finance firm that learned about hospitality. Our idea in one line: we've run restaurants, so we explain your numbers the way a good manager would.

## Four habits

**1. Talk like a manager, not an accountant.**
Use the words people use on the floor: food cost, labor, covers. Add the accounting term afterward only if they need it. Plain, not slangy; we're still exact.
- We say: "Food cost went up last month, mostly from produce."
- We don't say: "Cost of goods sold ran unfavorable to the prior period."

**2. Answer first, then explain.**
Start with what the number means and what to do about it. The working is there if they want it. Short, not curt.
- We say: "You made money in April, just less than March. Here's why."
- We don't say: "Please find attached the April financials for your review."

**3. Never make anyone feel dumb.**
Assume a smart, busy person who was never trained in finance. Skip "simply," "obviously" and "as we said." Patient, not condescending, and we don't over-explain to someone who wants the detail.
- We say: "Good question. Here's how it works."
- We don't say: "As we explained last month, this is straightforward."

**4. Be specific, not impressive.**
Name the actual thing: invoices, payroll, month-end. Confident, not boastful; our background does the proving. Banned: solutions, best-in-class, tailored, expert team, next level, exceptional results.
- We say: "We do your bookkeeping and send a report every month."
- We don't say: "We deliver tailored financial solutions."

## What we avoid
Two ways to go wrong. The stiff accountant: passive voice, jargon, "please find attached." The cheerleader: exclamation marks, hype, promises about "the next level." We sit in between: warm, direct and exact about money. Never imply a client should have known something already.

## How tone shifts
Voice stays the same; tone moves with the moment.
- **Monthly reports:** calm, numbers first.
- **Good news:** warm and brief.
- **Bad news** (a loss, a tax problem, our own mistake): the plainest version. Say what happened, the amount and what happens next. No cushioning, no jokes.
- **Apologies:** one sentence, no drama.
- **Client questions:** answer the question asked, then offer more.
- **First contact:** conversational and curious about their restaurant.

## Mechanics
Numerals for dollars and percentages. Short sentences. "We" and "you." Contractions are fine. Spell out an acronym the first time. Put the answer in the first line of every email, and the amount or date before the reason.

## Before you send
Could any bookkeeping firm sign this? Would a tired owner understand it in one read? Does it sound like one of us talking?

# The two rewrites

**Homepage intro**
Before: "At Marrow & Co, we provide best-in-class financial solutions tailored to the unique needs of your hospitality business. Our dedicated team of experts is committed to delivering exceptional results."

After: "Marrow & Co does the bookkeeping and monthly reporting for restaurants. We're five former restaurant managers, so we know what's behind your numbers, and we explain them in plain language."

**Call to action**
Before: "Contact us today to learn how we can help take your restaurant to the next level!"

After: "Not sure what your numbers are telling you? Ask us. No question is too basic."
