# Marrow & Co: Voice and Tone Guide

## Who we sound like
We're five ex-restaurant managers who are good with numbers. We've closed out a night, chased a food cost problem, and made payroll in a slow month. On the page, we sound like that person: someone who's been in your kitchen and will tell you straight what the numbers say.

## Our voice (always the same)

**Plain.** Short words and short sentences. If a client would have to Google it, we explain it or cut it. "Prime cost" is fine once we've said what's in it. "Optimize" is never fine.

**Specific.** We name the real thing: food cost, payroll, a slow Tuesday, the monthly P&L. Phrases like "exceptional results" could sit on anyone's site. Specifics could only be on ours.

**Kind, never soft.** We never make anyone feel dumb, and we never dodge bad news. We say it clearly, then say what to do next.

## Our tone (changes with the moment)

- **Website:** Warm, confident, brief. Start with the owner's problem, not our credentials.
- **Monthly reports:** Calm and clear. What happened, why, and what to watch.
- **Bad news:** Direct and steady, with no cushioning and no alarm. *"Labor ran 34% of sales, up from 29%. Here's what changed."*
- **Client questions:** Patient. No question is basic. Answer first, then give context.
- **Chasing paperwork:** Friendly and specific. Make it easy to act on, because they're probably mid-service.

## Swap list

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| Financial solutions | Bookkeeping and monthly reports |
| Best-in-class | Say what the client actually gets |
| Leverage insights | Use what the numbers show |
| Take it to the next level | Name the level: "know your real food cost" |
| As you may be aware | Just say it |

## Rules of thumb

1. Write it the way you'd say it at the bar after close, then tidy it up.
2. One idea per sentence.
3. Use "you" and "we," not "clients" or "our team of experts."
4. Explain a term the first time it appears, in half a sentence.
5. Cut adjectives that praise us (dedicated, exceptional, tailored). Facts do that job better.
6. Use exclamation points rarely, and never to manufacture excitement.
7. Never imply the reader should already know something.

## Two quick tests
- **Could a competitor paste this on their site?** If yes, rewrite it.
- **Would a tired owner reading at 11 p.m. get it on the first pass?** If no, simplify.

## The rewrites

**Original:**
> "At Marrow & Co, we provide best-in-class financial solutions tailored to the unique needs of your hospitality business. Our dedicated team of experts is committed to delivering exceptional results."

**Rewrite:**
> "Marrow & Co does bookkeeping and monthly reporting for restaurants. We're five former restaurant managers, so we know what a slow Tuesday and a bad food cost week feel like. Every month, we show you where your money went and explain it in plain language. No jargon, and no such thing as a dumb question."

**Original:**
> "Contact us today to learn how we can help take your restaurant to the next level!"

**Rewrite:**
> "Not sure what your numbers are telling you? Tell us what's confusing, and we'll talk it through in plain English."

**What changed:** The rewrites say what you do (bookkeeping, monthly reporting) instead of "solutions." They swap "experts" for the fact that you've run restaurants. They replace "exceptional results" with what clients actually get. The call to action starts from the owner's real question instead of a vague promise.

One thing to check: I only used what you told me. If you have a concrete detail, like a typical turnaround time or a specific report clients love, adding it to the first rewrite would make it even harder to copy.
