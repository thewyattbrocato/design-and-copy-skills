**No, a full rewrite isn't necessary.** The statement already does the job:

- It names a specific group, night-shift nurses who can't sleep during the day.
- It names the alternatives they'd otherwise use, generic blackout curtains and sleep apps.
- It gives a difference you can check, which is one kit built around a night-shift schedule with a setup checklist.
- It says plainly that you haven't run clinical studies.
- A competitor couldn't truthfully put their name on it.
- It matches what you sell and tested well with the nurses you interviewed. A rewrite would risk losing that.

**One thing is worth fixing:** "turn **any** bedroom into a place you can sleep at noon." That promises more than you can back up. A curtain set, a door seal and a sound machine won't work for every room, and "any" is the one word a skeptical nurse or a regulator could push on. Your own checklist is "for your own room", so I'd match that:

> "For night-shift nurses who struggle to sleep during the day, Dayrest is a set of blackout-and-white-noise kits (curtain panels, a door seal and a small sound machine) that turn **your** bedroom into a place you can sleep at noon. Unlike generic blackout curtains or sleep apps, Dayrest is sold as one kit designed around a night-shift schedule, with a setup checklist for your own room. We have not run clinical studies; our claim is practical, not medical."

If your marketing lead wants to change something, I'd work on voice or channel-specific versions, like a shorter one-liner for ads. Those can be built on this statement without replacing it.
