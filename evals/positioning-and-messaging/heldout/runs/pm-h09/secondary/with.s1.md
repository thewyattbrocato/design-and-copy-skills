Your positioning is already solid—no full rewrite needed. It has what works: specific audience, clear what it is, real differences you can point to, and you've tested it. Don't throw that away.

**One thing to tighten:** "blackout-and-white-noise kits" is the weak spot. It's grammatically clunky and doesn't sound like how a nurse would describe it. Say it simpler: "a sleep kit with blackout curtains, a door seal, and a small sound machine." This is what's actually in the box, and it's clearer.

**One thing to strengthen:** "We have not run clinical studies; our claim is practical, not medical." This is honest, but it reads defensive. Flip it: "We don't claim medical proof—we're built around what works for night-shift schedules in practice." You're being clear about what you are without apologizing for it.

Everything else—the audience specificity, the kit-as-one-thing angle, the setup checklist, the differentiation from generic products—is already doing the work. Your marketing lead may want to flex, but resisting the impulse to rewrite will serve you better. Positioning that tested with real customers is rare; keep it.
