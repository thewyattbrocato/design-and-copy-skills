No, a full rewrite isn't necessary. The statement does what a positioning statement should:

- **Audience:** it names a specific group in a specific situation, night-shift nurses who can't sleep during the day.
- **Alternatives:** it names what they'd use instead, generic blackout curtains and sleep apps.
- **Difference:** it says what's different, which is one kit built around a night-shift schedule, with a setup checklist for your own room. Each part is something you actually sell, so it can be checked.
- **Honesty:** it says plainly that you have no clinical studies. A rival couldn't copy it without changing the facts.

It also tested well with the nurses you interviewed. Rewriting it would throw away that evidence.

I'd change one phrase: "turn **any** bedroom into a place you can sleep at noon." You can't back up "any". A shared room or a door that won't seal might not work, and the checklist is written for each person's own room. The fix is small:

> "For night-shift nurses who struggle to sleep during the day, Dayrest is a set of blackout-and-white-noise kits (curtain panels, a door seal and a small sound machine) that turn **your** bedroom into a place you can sleep at noon. Unlike generic blackout curtains or sleep apps, Dayrest is sold as one kit designed around a night-shift schedule, with a setup checklist for your own room. We have not run clinical studies; our claim is practical, not medical."

Your marketing lead may have a real concern about the tone or the length. If so, that can be handled in the public-facing copy. The last sentence reads like a guardrail for your team, so you could keep it internal and use a shorter version on the site. That wouldn't change the positioning itself.
