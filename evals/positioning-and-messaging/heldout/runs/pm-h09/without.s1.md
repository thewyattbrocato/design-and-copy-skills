No, a full rewrite isn't necessary. The statement has a specific audience, a concrete product, a real differentiator and an honest limit on its claims. It also tested well with your nurses and matches what you sell. A rewrite risks losing those things, so I'd make three small edits.

**What I'd fix**

1. **"turn any bedroom into a place you can sleep at noon."** This is the one real problem. "Any bedroom" is a promise you can't keep, because some rooms have odd windows, thin walls or loud neighbors. It also sits badly next to your own "practical, not medical" disclaimer. I'd say it helps turn *your* bedroom into a dark, quiet place to sleep.
2. **"a set of blackout-and-white-noise kits."** The plural contradicts your differentiator, "sold as one kit." I'd make it singular.
3. **"sleep at noon."** Keep it as a vivid image, but it works better once the claim is softened to "helps."

**Revised version**

> For night-shift nurses who struggle to sleep during the day, Dayrest is a blackout-and-white-noise kit (curtain panels, a door seal and a small sound machine) that helps turn your bedroom into a dark, quiet place to sleep at noon. Unlike generic blackout curtains or sleep apps, Dayrest is sold as one kit designed around a night-shift schedule, with a setup checklist for your own room. We have not run clinical studies; our claim is practical, not medical.

**What I left alone**

- The audience, the three kit items and the "one kit plus checklist" contrast are accurate and tested well.
- The clinical-studies line is honest, so I kept it. If this statement is internal, you could move that line into public-facing copy instead. It's a guardrail, not positioning.

When you talk to your marketing lead, I'd ask what specific problem they see. If they have evidence the nurse framing or the differentiator isn't landing, that's worth testing. Otherwise, targeted edits are enough.
