No, a full rewrite isn't necessary. The statement is already doing the job a positioning statement should do, and a from-scratch rewrite risks losing what tested well with your nurses.

**What works and should stay**
- **Specific audience:** "night-shift nurses who struggle to sleep during the day" is a real, recognizable person.
- **Clear category and contents:** it names the three items and the checklist, and you've confirmed the kit includes all of them.
- **Real differentiation:** one kit designed around a night-shift schedule, versus generic curtains or apps. The comparison is to categories, not named competitors, so it's safe.
- **Honest limits:** "practical, not medical" is a strength. It keeps you out of unsubstantiated health-claim territory, and I'd keep it.

**What needs fixing (two small things)**
1. **"a set of blackout-and-white-noise kits"** is a grammar slip. You sell one kit, and the next sentence says "one kit." It should be singular.
2. **"turn any bedroom"** is an absolute you can't back up. You haven't tested every bedroom, and the sentence is otherwise careful about claims. "Your bedroom" keeps the promise and drops the overreach. It also pairs better with "a setup checklist for your own room."

**Revised version**

"For night-shift nurses who struggle to sleep during the day, Dayrest is a blackout-and-white-noise kit (curtain panels, a door seal and a small sound machine) that turns your bedroom into a place you can sleep at noon. Unlike generic blackout curtains or sleep apps, Dayrest is sold as one kit designed around a night-shift schedule, with a setup checklist for your own room. We have not run clinical studies; our claim is practical, not medical."

**For your marketing lead:** if they have a specific problem with it, such as tone or a gap in the audience, that's worth discussing. Without a concrete problem, a rewrite is just change. A better use of their time is testing taglines or ad copy built on this foundation.
