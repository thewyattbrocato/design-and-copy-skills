# Tidewell message brief

**One-liner**
Tidewell is laundry management software for small commercial laundries. It tracks every customer's bags and linen loads from drop-off to delivery, and texts the customer when the order is ready for pickup.

**Controlling idea**
Every bag, tracked from drop-off to delivery. If a request or a headline doesn't help a small laundry know where a load is, what to charge for it, or when to tell the customer, it isn't part of this launch.

**Who it's for**
Wash-and-fold shops and hotel and spa linen contractors with 3 to 25 employees. They run many customers' loads at once, and each customer may be priced differently. [assumed: the best fit is a shop that loses track of orders or prices by hand today. Check this against the nine pilot shops.]

**Who it's not for**
Shops that want payroll in the same tool, or a live link to their accounting software. Tidewell has neither. It exports CSV.

**What we're up against**
Most shops today use one of three things:
- **Paper tickets.** They're free and everyone knows them.
- **A spreadsheet.** It's flexible and already on hand.
- **A point-of-sale system built for retail.** It rings up a sale at a counter.

We say "instead of paper tickets, a spreadsheet, or a retail register." We don't name specific products, and we don't claim any of these fails. Our claim is what Tidewell does that they weren't built to do.

**Why we're different**
1. **Built by people who ran a laundry for six years.** The two founders did the work themselves. Proof: their history, which anyone can check. What it lets a shop do: use software shaped around how a laundry runs, not adapted from another trade.
2. **One barcode tag follows each order, and the customer gets a text at pickup-ready.** Proof: the feature itself, which can be demoed. What it lets a shop do: answer "where's my order?" without digging through tickets. [unproven against retail POS systems: check what the ones your prospects use actually do before saying they can't.]
3. **Pricing by weight or by piece, with a rate card for each customer.** Proof: the feature itself. What it lets a shop do: charge a hotel and a walk-in differently without re-figuring each job. [same check against retail POS.]
4. **Tablet at the counter, phone for drivers, works offline and syncs later.** Proof: demoable. What it lets a shop do: keep working when the connection drops.

**Objections and honest answers**
- *"Does it do payroll or connect to our accounting software?"* No payroll. No accounting integration yet; it exports CSV. Don't promise a date.
- *"What if the internet goes down?"* It keeps working offline and syncs later.
- *"Who else is using it?"* Nine shops, in a pilot. We have no case studies yet, so don't imply otherwise.

**Proof on hand**
- Nine shops in the pilot.
- Founders with six years running a laundry.
- The four features above.

We have no customer quotes, no time-saved figures and no case studies. Until we do, nobody on the team says "saves hours," "shops love it," or "trusted by." [add before launch: one real sentence from a pilot shop, and one number measured in the pilot, with how it was measured.]

3. Customers get a text when their order is ready.
4. Priced by weight or by piece, with a rate card for each customer.

We can add "nine shops are using it in our pilot" wherever a number helps. Say "pilot" every time.

**Words**
- **Use:** bags, loads, linen, drop-off, pickup-ready, rate card, wash-and-fold, tag (for the barcode tag).
- **Avoid:** solution, platform, streamline, seamless, all-in-one. They fit any product, and "all-in-one" is untrue for us.
- **One name per thing:** always "tag," never "label" or "barcode sticker." Always "rate card," never "price list" or "pricing profile."

**Variants**
The claim stays the same. Only the emphasis changes.
- **Owner:** per-customer rate cards, and knowing where every load is.
- **Counter staff:** the tag, the tablet, and the pickup-ready text, so fewer "is it ready?" calls. [assumed: that calls are a pain point. Confirm with the pilot shops.]
- **Hotel and spa linen contractor:** per-customer pricing by weight or piece, and driver tracking from the phone.
