# Tidewell Message Brief

## What we are

Tidewell is software for small commercial laundries. It tracks each customer's bags and linen loads from drop-off to delivery using a barcode tag. It prices jobs the way the shop charges, by weight or by piece, with a rate card for each customer. It also texts the customer when an order is ready for pickup. It runs on a tablet at the counter and a phone for drivers, and it keeps working offline and syncs later.

## Who it's for

Owners and managers of small commercial laundries with 3 to 25 employees. There are two kinds of shop, and they talk about their work differently:

- **Wash-and-fold shops.** Their customers drop off bags and pick them up. The words they use are *bag*, *pickup* and *per pound*.
- **Hotel and spa linen contractors.** They serve accounts on a route. The words they use are *load*, *delivery*, *rate card* and *per piece*.

Use each group's own language when writing for them. The product is the same for both.

## What we're up against

Our main competitor is what shops already use: paper tickets, a spreadsheet, or a point-of-sale system built for retail. None of these is a bad choice, and they're familiar and already paid for. They also share one weakness. A laundry job doesn't end at the register. It sits in the shop for a day or more, and it has to be washed, found, priced correctly and delivered. Paper and spreadsheets can't follow a job through those steps. A retail POS handles the sale and then loses track of the job.

So we're not asking shops to replace a rival product. We're asking them to change a habit. Our messaging should respect that and never mock the old way.

## Why we're different

1. **Built by people who ran a laundry.** Our two founders ran one for six years. They built Tidewell around problems they had themselves.
2. **It follows the job, not just the sale.** Each bag or load has a barcode tag, so the shop can see where it is from drop-off to delivery.
3. **Pricing matches how laundries charge.** Weight or piece, with a rate card for each customer, no workarounds.
4. **It works when the Wi-Fi doesn't.** The counter tablet and driver phones keep working offline and sync later.

## What we say about ourselves

Keep to these four. They are all true today.

1. **"Know where every bag and load is."** (Barcode tracking from drop-off to delivery.)
2. **"Price every job the way you actually charge."** (Weight or piece, rate cards for each customer.)
3. **"Customers get a text when their order is ready."** (Pickup-ready texts.)
4. **"Built by people who ran a laundry for six years."** (Founder story. Say it plainly and don't dress it up.)

Offline support is a strong proof point and can follow any of these lines, especially in copy for drivers.

## What we don't say yet

We're early. Honesty here protects trust with shops that will talk to each other.

- **No results claims.** We have no data on time saved, errors reduced or revenue recovered. Don't use phrases like "save hours a week" or "cut lost bags."
- **No customer quotes or case studies.** We don't have any, so we don't imply them.
- **Be accurate about the pilot.** Nine shops are using Tidewell in a pilot. We can say that. We shouldn't call it "trusted by" or "loved by" anyone.
- **Accounting.** There's no integration yet, only CSV export. Say "exports to CSV" and don't say "works with" any accounting product.
- **Payroll.** Tidewell doesn't do payroll. If a prospect asks, say so directly. Don't dodge the question.

## Tone

Plain, practical and a little dry, like an owner talking to another owner. Use short sentences and the shop's own vocabulary. Avoid "revolutionize," "seamless" and "all-in-one." Explain the product by describing a bag coming in and going out.

## When someone asks

- **"Why not just use what we have?"** If it's working, we'll say so. Tidewell is for the point where you can't tell where a job is or what to charge.
- **"Does it connect to our accounting software?"** Not yet. It exports to CSV today.
- **"Who else uses it?"** Nine shops are in our pilot now, and we're still collecting what we learn from them.
