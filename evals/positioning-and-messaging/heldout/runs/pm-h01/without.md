# Tidewell Message Brief

## What we are

Tidewell is software for small commercial laundries: wash-and-fold shops and hotel and spa linen contractors with 3 to 25 employees. It tracks each customer's bags and linen loads from drop-off to delivery with a barcode tag. It prices jobs by weight or by piece, using per-customer rate cards. When an order is ready, it texts the customer.

It runs on a tablet at the counter and a phone for drivers. It works offline and syncs later.

**One-line version:** Tidewell keeps track of every bag and load in a small laundry, from the counter to the customer's door.

## Who it's for

Owners and managers of small commercial laundries. They run the counter, the wash floor, and the delivery route, often all in the same day, and they don't have an IT person. There are two main groups:

- **Wash-and-fold shops** that take in customer bags and return them clean.
- **Hotel and spa linen contractors** that handle recurring linen loads on negotiated rates.

Both need to know where each job is, what it costs, and when it's ready. Their pricing is often specific to the customer.

## What we're up against

Our main competitor is not another product. It's the way these shops already work:

- **Paper tickets.** They're cheap and familiar, and they're easy to lose or misread. They also can't tell a customer anything.
- **Spreadsheets.** They're flexible, but someone has to maintain them, and they don't follow a job through each stage.
- **Retail point-of-sale systems.** They're built to sell items at a counter, not to price a bag by weight or apply a different rate card to each customer.

None of these is broken enough to force a change. Our job is to show that a tool built for laundries is better than something that mostly works.

## Why we're different

**We've done this work.** Our two founders ran a laundry for six years. They built Tidewell around problems they dealt with themselves.

**It's built for the job.** Barcode tracking, weight and piece pricing, and per-customer rate cards are the core of the product, not add-ons to something generic.

**It works where laundries work.** The tablet at the counter and the phone in the driver's hand both keep working without a connection and sync when it returns.

## What we say about ourselves

These are the claims we make. Everything in them is true today.

1. **Built by people who ran a laundry.** Our founders ran one for six years.
2. **Every bag and load tracked, drop-off to delivery.** Barcode tags follow each job through the shop.
3. **Priced the way you price.** Charge by weight or by piece, with a rate card for each customer.
4. **Customers get a text when their order is ready.** Fewer "is it done yet?" calls.
5. **Works offline.** Counter tablet and driver phone sync when they reconnect.

## Where we are, and what we don't claim

We are launching with **9 shops using Tidewell in a pilot**. That is accurate, and we can say so plainly.

We do **not** yet have:

- Case studies
- Measured results, such as time saved or errors reduced
- Customer quotes

So please don't use phrases like "saves hours every week," "trusted by laundries everywhere," or "proven." Describe what the product does, not outcomes we haven't measured. When we have real numbers and permission to quote pilot shops, this brief will be updated.

Two things Tidewell doesn't do, which we should state plainly if asked:

- **No payroll.**
- **No accounting software integration yet.** Today, data leaves Tidewell as a CSV export.

Don't describe Tidewell as "all-in-one" or imply it replaces the shop's accounting or payroll tools. It covers the work of getting laundry in, through, and back out. Say "yet" for the accounting integration only if the team has agreed that it's planned.

## Tone

Plain, practical, and direct, the way someone who has worked a laundry counter would talk. Use the words owners use: bags, loads, pickup, route. Avoid startup jargon, and don't oversell.
