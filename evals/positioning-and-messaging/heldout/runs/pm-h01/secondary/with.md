# TIDEWELL MESSAGE BRIEF

**What it is**

Tidewell is operations software for commercial laundries. It tracks each customer's bags and linen loads from drop-off to delivery using barcode tags, automatically texts customers when their load is ready for pickup, and handles flexible pricing—by weight, by piece, or custom rate cards per customer. It runs on a tablet at the front counter and a phone for drivers, works offline, and syncs when you're back online.

**Who it's for**

Wash-and-fold shops, hotel and spa linen service contractors, and commercial laundries with 3 to 25 people. Shops where someone—usually the owner or manager—spends significant time each day tracking which customer load is where, confirming what they ordered and what they owe, and manually calling or texting to tell them when it's ready. Shops that have outgrown paper tickets and spreadsheets but don't need a full enterprise system. Shops losing time and customers to the friction of manual tracking.

**What they use now**

Most commercial laundries operate on paper or spreadsheets. A paper-ticket shop writes load details on a ticket, sticks it in a rack or box, and that becomes the record—easy to lose, hard to find, and only visible to whoever is holding the ticket. A spreadsheet shop types in customer name, load type, pricing, and status, updating it constantly as loads move through the laundry—but it's slow, data gets out of sync, and customers still need a manual call or text. Some use a retail point-of-sale system, which handles the sale but doesn't understand laundry workflow. None of these tell you where a specific customer's load is right now with one glance. None automatically tell the customer when their load is ready. So the owner does it manually: calls, texts, managing expectations, or letting loads sit until someone asks. On busy days, this is where the shop gets stuck.

**What's different**

Tidewell automates the two biggest time sinks: tracking and notification. Barcode tags mean every bag or load is in the system from the moment of drop-off. When it's ready, an automated text goes to the customer—no phone call, no manual message. Pricing is flexible: by weight, by piece, or per-customer rate cards, and the whole shop sees the same rates. Drivers scan bags on a phone, counter staff use a tablet, everything syncs later if there's no internet right now. That last part matters for shops without reliable connectivity. The software was built by people who ran a commercial laundry for six years, so the workflow matches how laundries actually work.

What it doesn't do: payroll or direct accounting integration (CSV export only for now). It's not built to be everything. It's built to cut the most time waste out of a laundry's day.

**Why it matters**

A laundry that knows where every load is and tells customers automatically moves faster, loses fewer customers to frustration, and recovers hours every week. For a 10- to 15-person shop, that time is money.

**Who it's not for**

Large laundries with dedicated dispatch teams or complex accounting needs. Shops happy with their current system. Laundries that need payroll and full accounting inside the software.

**Where we are**

Nine laundries are using Tidewell in a pilot. We have no case studies, no time-saved numbers, and no customer quotes yet. We built this based on running a laundry ourselves, and we're learning from the shops using it now.

**How to talk about Tidewell**

Talk like someone who has run a laundry: clear, practical, honest about what it does and doesn't do. Respect for the customer's time and money. No jargon, no hype, no "solution" or "platform" language. Be direct about where we are—nine pilots, still building, learning from customers. Every feature you mention should solve an actual laundry problem, not an imaginary one. If someone asks about payroll or accounting, say we don't do it yet, and ask what matters most to them first.
