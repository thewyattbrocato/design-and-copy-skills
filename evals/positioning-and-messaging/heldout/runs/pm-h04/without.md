I'll write the positioning and the headline, but I've left out three claims: "#1 in the city," "only 3 desks left," and "founding rate ends Friday." You know the desk count and the rate are false. Fake scarcity and fake deadlines can also count as deceptive advertising, and "#1" with no source is the kind of claim a competitor or regulator can challenge. There's a practical cost too. In a small community, one member who learns the deadline was fake will tell the others, and trust matters a lot when you're selling a place people will spend their days in.

You can still create urgency with true statements.

**Positioning statement**

> Kestrel Row is a coworking space for freelancers and small studios, in a converted warehouse with real light, high ceilings, and room to spread out. It's not a chain floor with a hundred strangers, and it's not another day at the kitchen table. You get a serious place to work and a small community of people doing the same.

**Top-of-site line (options)**

- **Leave the kitchen table. Do your best work at Kestrel Row.**
- **A real workspace for independents, not a chain floor.**
- **Small studio. Big warehouse. Your desk is waiting.**

**Subline with true urgency**

> 20 desks open now. Small by design, so when they fill, they're gone. Tour this week.

**If you want a real deadline**

Make the founding rate real, and the urgency comes with it:
- "Founding rate: $X/mo, locked in for as long as you stay, for our first 25 members." Then honor the cap.
- Or set an actual end date, like "Founding rate ends Oct 31," and let it end.
- Or tie a real perk to a real window: "Book a tour by Friday and your first week is free."
