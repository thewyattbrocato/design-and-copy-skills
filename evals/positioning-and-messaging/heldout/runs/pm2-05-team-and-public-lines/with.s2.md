**Words:** Use "fix," "repair," "part," and the model number. Avoid "circular economy," "sustainability journey," and "built to die" in customer copy. Those are internal words.

**Rule for the knockoff argument:**
Stock a part if it gets the appliance working and keeps it working, and if we describe it honestly. Cheap isn't the test, and neither is "not the original brand." A knockoff that fails in two months sends the customer back to buying new, which is the outcome we exist to prevent.

Three questions for any disputed part:
1. Does it fit and fix the problem? If not, no.
2. Is it likely to last, judged by our own return and repeat-failure data and not by a hunch? [add: return rate by part, repeat-order rate for the same repair]
3. Is it clearly labeled as original or aftermarket, with the real tradeoff stated? If we can't say it honestly on the page, we don't sell it.

We don't have data on whether knockoffs fail more often, so that is the first thing to find out. Until then, this rule keeps the argument about evidence and not about whose values are stronger.
