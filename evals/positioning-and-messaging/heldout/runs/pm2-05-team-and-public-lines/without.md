# For the team

**Why we exist.** We want fixing things to be normal. Appliances shouldn't be built to die, and a broken dishwasher shouldn't mean a landfill trip. Everything we do serves one outcome: **the customer's appliance works again and stays working.**

**What customers want.** They want the dishwasher working by the weekend. They don't care about our mission, and they shouldn't have to. If we keep the repair promise, the mission takes care of itself.

**How we decide.** For any stocking or priority question, ask:

1. **Does it make the repair succeed the first time?** The right part, clear fit info, a video, and fast shipping all count.
2. **Does it make the repair last?** A part that fails in three months is a failed repair. It also pushes the customer toward replacing the appliance, which is the opposite of our mission.
3. **Can we stand behind it?** If we wouldn't be comfortable with the customer finding out exactly what they got, we don't sell it.

**The knockoff question.** "It sells" isn't a reason on its own. The rule:

- **Stock a non-OEM part** if it performs and lasts about as well as the original, and the page says clearly what it is. Cheaper parts that work make repair more affordable, which serves the mission.
- **Don't stock it** if it's a known short-lived part, or if its return and failure rate is meaningfully worse than the OEM equivalent. A sale that becomes a return or a dead appliance costs us money and trust.
- **When unsure,** stock it on a trial basis. Track returns and complaints per part, and drop it if the numbers are bad. That settles the argument with data instead of opinions.

**Limits we're upfront about.** Some manufacturers won't sell to resellers, so we don't carry those brands. We say so plainly rather than implying we cover everything.

**What we already promise.** Ships within a day from Ohio, a repair video on every part page, and 30-day returns. Protect these before adding anything new.

I haven't seen your return or failure data, so the "worse than OEM" threshold is yours to set. Pick a number now, before the next argument.

# Homepage line

**Recommended:**

> **Fix it yourself. Parts ship within a day, and every one comes with a repair video.**

It's built around the weekend repair, and each claim is one you already keep.

**Alternatives:**

- "Broken dishwasher? The part ships within a day, and we'll show you how to put it in."
- "Get your appliance working again. Fast shipping, repair videos, 30-day returns."
- "The right part, a video of how to fit it, and a month to send it back."

I left the mission out of the headline on purpose. It works better on an About page or in a short line near the footer, something like "We believe fixing things should be normal." On the homepage, customers need to see quickly that you'll get them fixed.

I'd avoid promising a "by the weekend" delivery in the line. Shipping within a day doesn't guarantee arrival by Friday, and a missed promise would hurt more than the line would help.
