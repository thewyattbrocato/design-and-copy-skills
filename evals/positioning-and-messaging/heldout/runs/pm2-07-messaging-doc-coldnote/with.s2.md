# Coldnote messaging doc

## Who it's for
Food producers who keep product in coolers and freezers and check them today with a paper log and a dial thermometer: creameries, butchers, small caterers, farmers-market vendors. [assumed: the best fit is an owner who is often away from the cooler, such as overnight. Confirm with the nine pilot kitchens.]

**Not for:** anyone who needs a certified food-safety system, anyone who needs readings below -25°C, or anyone who needs more than a log and an alert.

## The line
**Coldnote texts you when your cooler leaves the range you set, and keeps the log, so you're not relying on a dial thermometer and a paper sheet.**

Use this wording everywhere. Buyer versions change the emphasis, not the claim.

## Versions by buyer
- **Creamery:** "The alert came at 3 a.m. and the walk-in was at 46°F. We moved the dairy before it turned." (Marisol Quen, owner, Quen Family Creamery.) Lead with this quote. Mention the $79 hub if the walk-in is metal.
- **Butcher:** Your cooler holds your stock. Set the range, and Coldnote texts you when a reading leaves it. The log builds itself, so there's no clipboard to fill in.
- **Caterer:** You're not always in the kitchen. The text reaches you wherever you are, and the log is there when you need it.
- **Market vendor:** One $29 sensor and $12 a month. [unconfirmed: whether it works at a stall without Wi-Fi. Sensors need Wi-Fi, so pitch the home cooler or freezer until we know.]

## The awkward bits
- **Cold limit:** "Sensors are rated to -25°C. Below that we can't guarantee them." Say it up front to anyone with a deep freezer.
- **Metal walk-ins:** "Metal walls often block Wi-Fi. If yours does, you'll need our $79 hub inside the cooler." Quote the cost before the buyer finds it.
- **Compliance:** "Coldnote is not a certified food-safety system and we make no compliance claim." Never imply otherwise, and never say "inspector-approved."
- **Inspector log format:** The export matches the log forms used in Marlow and Dunmore counties. Other counties get a plain PDF. Say "laid out like," not "accepted by."
- **Monthly fee:** It's $12 per location per month on top of $29 per sensor. For one location that's $173 in year one. The battery lasts about 14 months.
- **Pilot size:** "Nine kitchens have used it since spring." Don't say "trusted by" or "hundreds."

## Company bio, 150 words
Coldnote makes a small wireless temperature sensor and an app for food producers who keep product in coolers and freezers: creameries, butchers, small caterers and farmers-market vendors.

Most of these businesses still keep a paper log and check a dial thermometer by hand. Coldnote replaces that routine.

The sensor sends readings over Wi-Fi, and the app texts the owner when a reading leaves the range they set. It also keeps the log, which exports as a PDF laid out like the health inspector temperature log forms used in Marlow and Dunmore counties; other counties get a plain PDF.

Sensors cost $29 each, service is $12 a month per location, and the battery lasts about 14 months.

Coldnote is not a certified food-safety system and makes no compliance claim.

It is built by two people in Madison and has been in a pilot with nine kitchens since spring.
