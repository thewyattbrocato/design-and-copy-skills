# Coldnote messaging guide

## Who it's for
Owners of small food businesses who keep product in a cooler or freezer. Today they check a dial thermometer by hand and write it on a paper log. [assumed: the owner is both the buyer and the person who does the checking.]

**Not for:** anyone who needs a certified food-safety system, freezers colder than -25°C, or coolers with no Wi-Fi.

## How we describe it
**The line:** "Coldnote texts you when your cooler drifts out of range, and keeps the log for you."

**Category:** wireless temperature monitoring for food producers (a sensor plus an app).

**Versus:** the dial and the paper log. Both only help if someone is standing there to look.

**Test for features and headlines:** does it help the owner find out about a cooler's temperature, or show a record of it? If not, it isn't Coldnote.

## Version for each buyer
- **Creameries and butchers (walk-ins):** "Find out when it happens, not in the morning." Lead with the Quen quote. Raise the $79 hub early, because metal walk-ins often need it.
- **Small caterers:** "Stop the thermometer round." The log exports as a PDF. In Marlow and Dunmore counties it is laid out like the inspector's temperature log form.
- **Farmers-market vendors:** Lead with price: $29 a sensor, $12 a month. Pitch only cold storage that sits on Wi-Fi, such as home or commissary coolers. Sensors need Wi-Fi, so a stall or vehicle cooler isn't covered. [unproven: any use away from Wi-Fi.]

## What we say about the awkward bits
- **Compliance:** "Coldnote is not a certified food-safety system, and we make no compliance claim. It records temperatures and texts you. Check with your county on what it needs from you." Never write "inspector-approved" or "compliant."
- **Inspector format:** "In Marlow and Dunmore counties, the export follows the inspector's log form. Elsewhere you get a plain PDF."
- **Cold limit:** "Rated down to -25°C. Below that, we don't guarantee it."
- **Metal walk-ins:** "Metal walls often block Wi-Fi. If yours does, add our $79 hub inside the cooler."
- **Evidence:** "Nine kitchens have used it in a pilot since spring. We don't have case studies yet."
- **Size:** "We're two people in Madison."

**150 words**
Coldnote makes a small wireless temperature sensor and an app for food producers who keep product in coolers and freezers: creameries, butchers, small caterers and farmers-market vendors. Most of them still keep a paper log and check a dial thermometer by hand. Coldnote takes over the checking, so nobody has to remember. A $29 sensor goes in the cooler; the app texts the owner when a reading leaves the range they set and keeps the log, which exports as a PDF. The service is $12 a month per location, and the battery lasts about 14 months. Coldnote is not a certified food-safety system and makes no compliance claim. It is a two-person company based in Madison. Nine kitchens have been using it in a pilot since spring, including Quen Family Creamery, where a 3 a.m. alert showed a walk-in at 46°F and the owner moved the dairy before it turned.
