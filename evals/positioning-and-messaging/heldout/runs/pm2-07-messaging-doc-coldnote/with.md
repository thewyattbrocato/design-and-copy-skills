# Coldnote messaging brief (launch draft)

## Who it's for
Owners of small food businesses who keep product in a cooler or freezer and now check a dial thermometer by hand and write it in a paper log. That means creameries, butchers, small caterers and farmers-market vendors.

**Not for:** anyone who needs a certified food-safety system or a compliance claim, or anyone whose freezer runs below -25°C.

## How we describe it
**One-liner:** Coldnote is a wireless temperature sensor and app that texts you when your cooler or freezer leaves the range you set, and keeps the log.

**Controlling idea:** Between your hand checks, Coldnote watches the cooler and texts you when it drifts. Every variant below repeats this claim. Only the emphasis changes.

**Words:** Say cooler, freezer, log, alert, text. Don't say "solution", "smart" or "compliance".

## By buyer
- **Creameries:** Lead with Marisol Quen's quote (below). Walk-ins are usually metal, so plan on the $79 hub.
- **Butchers:** One sensor per cooler or freezer at $29 each, plus $12 a month per location. Check that the freezer stays at -25°C or warmer.
- **Small caterers:** Lead with the log. In Marlow and Dunmore counties it exports on a layout like the inspectors' form. Elsewhere it's a plain PDF.
- **Market vendors:** Sensors need Wi-Fi, so pitch it for the places they store product, such as a kitchen or unit with Wi-Fi. [check: whether any pilot kitchen used it at a stall without Wi-Fi. Until then, don't promise stall use.]

## The awkward bits
- **Wi-Fi and metal walk-ins:** "Metal walls often block the signal. If yours does, you need our $79 hub inside the cooler."
- **-25°C limit:** "Rated down to -25°C. Below that we don't guarantee it."
- **Compliance:** "Coldnote is not a certified food-safety system, and we make no compliance claim. It keeps a log and sends alerts."
- **County formats:** "Marlow and Dunmore get the inspector-style layout. Everyone else gets a plain PDF."
- **Evidence:** "Nine kitchens have used it since spring. We have no case studies or survey numbers yet."
- **Size:** "We're two people in Madison."
- **Cost:** "$29 a sensor, $12 a month per location, and the battery lasts about 14 months."

## Company bio, 150 words
Coldnote is a small wireless temperature sensor and an app for food producers who keep things in coolers and freezers: creameries, butchers, small caterers and farmers-market vendors. Most of them still keep a paper log and check a dial thermometer by hand. Coldnote does the watching between those checks. The owner sets the range they want. When a reading leaves it, the app sends a text, and it keeps the log. The log exports as a PDF, laid out like the temperature log forms health inspectors use in Marlow and Dunmore counties, or as a plain PDF anywhere else. The sensor costs $29 and runs about 14 months on a battery. The records and alerts service is $12 a month per location. Coldnote is not a certified food-safety system and makes no compliance claim. Two people in Madison build it; nine kitchens have used it in a pilot since spring.
