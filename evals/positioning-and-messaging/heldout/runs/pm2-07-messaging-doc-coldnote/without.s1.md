# Coldnote messaging guide

## Who it's for
Food producers who keep product in coolers and freezers: creameries, butchers, small caterers and farmers-market vendors. Most keep a paper log and check a dial thermometer by hand. They are our competition, and we are not asking them to replace a system.

## How we describe it
**One line:** Coldnote is a $29 wireless sensor and an app that texts you when your cooler or freezer drifts out of range, and keeps the log for you.

**Pricing:** $29 per sensor, $12 a month per location, and a $79 hub where needed. The battery lasts about 14 months.

## Versions by buyer
- **Creameries:** "If the walk-in warms up at 3 a.m., you hear about it at 3 a.m." Lead with the Quen quote.
- **Butchers:** "Set the range for your case or walk-in. Coldnote texts you when a reading leaves it, so you don't rely on checking the dial."
- **Small caterers:** "Every cooler and freezer gets a log without anyone filling in a clipboard. Export it as a PDF when you need it."
- **Farmers-market vendors:** "Know your storage cooler is holding between markets." Sensors need Wi-Fi, so pitch the cooler or freezer at home or in the commissary. Don't promise stall use.

## The awkward bits
- **Not a certified system.** Say it early: "Coldnote is not a certified food-safety system, and we make no compliance claim. It's a sensor, an alert and a log."
- **Inspector forms.** The PDF is laid out like the temperature log forms used by health inspectors in Marlow and Dunmore counties. Elsewhere it's a plain PDF. Never say "inspector-approved."
- **Metal walk-ins.** They often block Wi-Fi, so those locations need the $79 hub inside the cooler. A walk-in with one sensor costs $29 + $79 up front, plus $12 a month.
- **Cold limit.** Sensors are rated to -25°C and are not guaranteed below that. Don't pitch them for ultra-low freezers.
- **Proof is thin.** We have 9 pilot kitchens, no case studies and no survey numbers. Say "in a pilot with nine kitchens," not "trusted by."
- **We're two people.** Say it plainly and let it read as direct access.

## What we can back up today vs. what we still need

| We can say | We can't say yet |

| Quen Family Creamery's approved quote | Percentages, savings or "prevents spoilage" |
| Alerts by text when a reading leaves the owner's range | Compliance, certification or inspector approval |
| Marlow and Dunmore log layout | Layouts for other counties |
| Prices, ~14-month battery, -25°C rating | Reliability claims beyond the pilot |

**Still needed:** a published case study (Quen is the obvious first), a few more approved quotes, pilot numbers we can verify (alerts sent, for example), and a list of which counties' forms we could match next.
