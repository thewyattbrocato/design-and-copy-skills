# Coldnote Messaging Doc

## Who it's for
Food producers who keep product in coolers and freezers: creameries, butchers, small caterers and farmers-market vendors. Most keep a paper log and check a dial thermometer by hand. Coldnote is not for anyone who needs a certified food-safety system.

## How we describe it
**One line:** Coldnote is a small wireless sensor and an app that texts you when your cooler or freezer goes out of range, and keeps the log for you.

**Price line:** $29 per sensor, $12 a month per location. The battery lasts about 14 months.

## By buyer
- **Creameries:** Lead with Marisol Quen's quote. Many creameries have metal walk-ins, so mention the $79 hub early.
- **Butchers:** Position it as a replacement for the hand-checked dial thermometer. Freezers and walk-ins are the use case. Sensors are rated to -25°C, and walk-ins will likely need the hub.
- **Small caterers:** Stress the text alert and the automatic log, so there's no clipboard to keep up.
- **Farmers-market vendors:** Stress the low entry cost: $29 for a sensor. Don't promise it works at a stall until we settle the Wi-Fi answer (see below).

## The awkward bits

| If they ask | We say |
|---|---|
| Is it compliant or certified? | "Coldnote isn't a certified food-safety system, and we make no compliance claim. It alerts you and keeps a log." |
| Will inspectors accept the log? | "In Marlow and Dunmore counties, the PDF is laid out like the temperature log forms inspectors use. Elsewhere you get a plain PDF." |
| What about my deep freezer? | "Sensors are rated to -25°C. Below that, we don't guarantee them." |
| My walk-in is all metal. | "Metal walls often block Wi-Fi. Those locations need our $79 hub inside the cooler." |
| Where's the proof? | "Nine kitchens have used it in a pilot since spring. We don't have published case studies or survey numbers yet." |
| Who are you? | "Two people in Madison." |
| What does it really cost? | "$29 per sensor, plus $12 a month per location. Walk-ins that need it add a $79 hub." |

Never say or imply "compliant," "inspector-approved," or "guaranteed to protect your product."

## What we can back up today vs. what we still need

**Can back up now**
- Prices, battery life (about 14 months), and the -25°C rating.
- Text alerts when a reading leaves the owner's range, and the log.
- The Marlow and Dunmore PDF layout.
- Nine pilot kitchens since spring.
- Marisol Quen's approved quote. Use it word for word: "The alert came at 3 a.m. and the walk-in was at 46°F. We moved the dairy before it turned." (Marisol Quen, owner, Quen Family Creamery)

**Still need**
- Case studies and any pilot numbers, such as alerts sent or how long sensors have been running.
- More approved quotes, especially from butchers, caterers and market vendors, so each buyer message has a voice behind it.
- Field confirmation of battery life. The pilot has run roughly six months, so the 14-month figure hasn't been seen end to end.
- An answer for market stalls and other places without reliable Wi-Fi. The fact sheet only says sensors need Wi-Fi.
- Log layouts for more counties.

## Company bio (150 words)
Coldnote makes it easier for small food producers to keep an eye on the coolers and freezers their business depends on. Our product is a small wireless temperature sensor and an app, built for creameries, butchers, small caterers and farmers-market vendors. Most of them still keep a paper log and check a dial thermometer by hand. With Coldnote, the app texts an alert when a reading leaves the range the owner sets, and it keeps the log, which exports as a PDF. In Marlow and Dunmore counties, that PDF is laid out like the temperature log forms health inspectors use there. Sensors are $29 each, and the records and alerts service is $12 a month per location. Coldnote is not a certified food-safety system and makes no compliance claim. We are two people based in Madison, and nine kitchens have used Coldnote in a pilot since spring.
