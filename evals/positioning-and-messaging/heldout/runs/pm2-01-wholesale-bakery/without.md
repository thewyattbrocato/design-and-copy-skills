**Why this should read as established**
- **Specifics, not claims.** The 36-hour ferment, the 6am delivery, and the six-day schedule do the work that "years of experience" would do. I left out founding dates and "family tradition" lines because they'd be invented.
- **No apologizing for being small.** "Two-person bakery" and "we make three breads and nothing else" read as focus, not as a limitation.
- **The minimum stated plainly.** A clear policy sounds like a business that has done this before.
- **The competition is implied, not named.** The "after your rush has started" line makes the contrast without naming the big supplier.

**Optional additions**
- The tire shop could be a nice detail ("baked in a unit behind a tire shop"). It adds character, but it can also undercut the established tone. Whether to include it depends on how much charm you want.
- Once you have a couple of cafés on board, add a line like "Baking for [Café Name] and [Café Name]." Nothing builds trust faster.
- A "Call us to set up a tasting" call to action under the paragraph would give buyers an easy next step.
