**One-line description**

> Country sourdough, seeded rye, and milk buns, baked by two people and at your door before 6am, six days a week.

Shorter alternative:

> Wholesale bread for cafés and restaurants, baked small and delivered before you open.

**About paragraph**

> Marrow Lane Bread is two bakers, Dessa and Calloway, working out of a unit behind a tire shop. We make three breads and make them properly: a country sourdough with a 36-hour cold ferment, a seeded rye, and a milk bun built for sandwiches. Every loaf is baked by one of us, and every delivery arrives before 6am, six days a week, so your bread is on the counter when you unlock the door. Our minimum is 20 loaves per delivery. If you're tired of anonymous bread that shows up mid-morning, we'd love to bake for you.

**Why it reads as established**

- **Specifics carry the credibility.** The 36-hour ferment, the 6am promise, and the three-bread menu all show a bakery that knows what it does. Vague claims like "artisan" or "passion for quality" sound newer, not older.
- **The small size is a selling point.** "Two bakers" tells a café owner exactly who is responsible for their bread. The tire shop line adds character, and you can cut it if you'd rather not have it.
- **I left out things you can't back up yet.** That means "years of experience," "trusted by local cafés," and "family tradition." Customers can check those, and a false one costs more than it helps.

**Two things to check**

- I wrote "every loaf is baked by one of us" and "before 6am." Make sure both are true on your worst day, because they're promises.
- I only hinted at the industrial supplier ("anonymous bread that shows up mid-morning"). Naming them or being sharper could put off cafés that use them. You may prefer to leave that line out.

Once you have your first few accounts, ask them for a one-sentence quote. A single real line from a café owner will do more for trust than any wording on the page.
