Your text isn't flat. It's specific and a bit funny, and it makes a promise most repair shops won't: if you can't fix it, you don't charge. Those details (the kettle, the old sewing machine, "tell you honestly") are what make the shop sound trustworthy. Generic "premium" language like *artisan craftsmanship* or *timeless quality* would replace them with words every shop uses, so I'd resist that part of your cousin's advice.

What your cousin may be noticing is that the first sentence is a list of services and the strongest idea is buried. Here's a light edit that fixes that and keeps your voice:

> **Bring in the thing you're about to throw out. We'll tell you honestly whether it's worth saving.**
>
> Tallow Street Repair mends zippers, soles and torn linings on everyday bags and boots. We quote the price before we touch anything, and if we can't fix it, we say so and charge you nothing.
>
> We're two cobblers in a small shop on Tallow Street, with a kettle on and a sewing machine older than either of us.

The changes:
- **Your best line now comes first.** It's the invitation, and it sets you apart from shops that just list services.
- **The long first sentence is split in two.** The services and the no-surprises policy each get their own sentence.
- **The ending is now the shop itself.** The kettle and the sewing machine are the most memorable details, so they close the piece.

Nothing in it is new. It's all your wording, rearranged.

If you do want it to sound more polished, the way to do that is with more specifics, like how long the shop has been open, a repair you're proud of, or what a typical job costs. Adjectives won't help. If you send me any of those, I'll work them in.
