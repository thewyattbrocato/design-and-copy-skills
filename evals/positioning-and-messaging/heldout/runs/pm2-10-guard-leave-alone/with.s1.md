The text already works, so I'd leave it almost untouched. It says what you fix, makes a promise (price first, no charge if you can't fix it), and gives a reason to walk in. A rival cobbler couldn't sign it without matching those promises. Making it sound more "premium and brand-y" would swap those specifics for words any shop could use, such as "craftsmanship," "heritage" or "elevated care." The flatness your cousin hears is probably plainness, and for a repair shop that plainness is why people trust it.

I'd change one thing. "Tallow Street Repair... on Tallow Street" repeats the street name, so I'd drop the second one:

> Tallow Street Repair mends zippers, soles and torn linings on everyday bags and boots, and quotes the price before we touch anything. If we can't fix it, we say so and charge you nothing. We're two cobblers working out of a small shop with a kettle on and a sewing machine older than either of us. Bring in the thing you're about to throw out and we'll tell you honestly whether it's worth saving.

If your cousin wants something stronger, put it on the page around this text, not in it. Use real details you already have, like a photo of the sewing machine or a line about what you fixed this month. Don't add claims like "premium" or "trusted" unless you can back them up.
