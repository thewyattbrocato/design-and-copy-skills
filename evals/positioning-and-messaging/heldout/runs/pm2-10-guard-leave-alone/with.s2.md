I'd leave it as it is. It's already specific, and "punching it up" would make it worse.

It says what you fix (zippers, soles, torn linings on everyday bags and boots). It names the alternative, which is throwing the thing out. It makes two promises a customer can hold you to: the price comes before the work, and there's no charge if you can't fix it. A generic premium repair shop couldn't truthfully put its name on it. Words like "artisan", "heritage" or "elevated" could sit on any rival's site, and they'd bury the kettle and the old sewing machine, which are the parts people will remember.

If your cousin still thinks it feels flat, the likely cause is the layout around it, such as a photo of the bench or a visible price list, not the wording.
