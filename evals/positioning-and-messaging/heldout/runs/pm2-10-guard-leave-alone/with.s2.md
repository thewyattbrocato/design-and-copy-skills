I wouldn't punch this up. It already does what about text is supposed to do, and your cousin's "premium and brand-y" would make it worse.

It says what you fix (zippers, soles, torn linings on bags and boots). It makes two promises a customer can hold you to: the price comes before the work, and you charge nothing if you can't fix it. It also tells people what to do next: bring in the thing you're about to throw out. A bigger shop could not truthfully put its name on this, because the two cobblers, the kettle and the old sewing machine are yours alone.

"Premium" wording like *artisan*, *heritage craftsmanship* or *restoring your treasured pieces* is what any repair shop could say. It would also drop the honesty that sets you apart.

The text is fine to publish as it is. If your cousin wants something to change, the only place I'd touch is the first sentence: "everyday bags and boots" is fine, but if you also repair other things (belts, jackets, wallets), add them. Customers search for what they own, so naming more items helps more than polishing the tone.
