I wouldn't punch this up. It already works, and "premium and brand-y" would make it worse.

It says what you fix, and it makes two promises a customer can check: you quote the price first, and you charge nothing if you can't fix it. The last line also speaks to someone about to throw something out. A bigger repair shop couldn't truthfully put its name on it. Words like "premium", "craftsmanship" or "heritage" would fit any shop, and they'd bury those details.

I'd keep it as written:

> Tallow Street Repair mends zippers, soles and torn linings on everyday bags and boots, and quotes the price before we touch anything. If we can't fix it, we say so and charge you nothing. We're two cobblers working out of a small shop on Tallow Street, with a kettle on and a sewing machine older than either of us. Bring in the thing you're about to throw out and we'll tell you honestly whether it's worth saving.

Your cousin may be reacting to how it looks on the page rather than to the words. A short headline above it would help, and so would a photo of the shop or the sewing machine. For the headline, you could use "Bring in the thing you're about to throw out."
