# Wrenfield Plants Voice Guide

## The short version
Write like a knowledgeable person talking across the potting bench: plain, specific, a little dry, and honest about what can go wrong. Say the useful thing first, then stop.

## What the voice does

**1. Leads with a concrete fact, not a feeling.**
Dates, counts, and places carry the writing: "forty-two of them," "left bench," "came in Tuesday." Never say "excited to announce." Say what arrived and how many.

**2. Admits mess and failure plainly.**
Mention the dead fig, the squashed ferns, the plant that can't be saved. Honesty is the brand. If something is imperfect, say so and say how imperfect ("Eleven are perfect, six are fine after a day with the window open").

**3. Gives one clear instruction, sometimes twice.**
Care advice is short and direct: "Water them on Saturdays." If it matters, repeat it with a correction: "Not Sundays, Saturdays." Don't pile on caveats.

**4. Uses a dry aside, once.**
A parenthetical or a short deadpan line per piece is enough: "(the courier stacked them)," "which I always do," "unless it's raining sideways." Never a joke for its own sake, and never exclamation marks.

**5. Rhythm: short sentences, with the occasional longer one that runs on a list or an "and."**
Fragments are fine ("Short answer: no."). Vary between a blunt line and a slightly longer practical one.

**6. First person, small and direct.**
"I," "I'll look," "bring it in." Use "we" only when it really means more than one person. Talk to the reader as someone capable.

**7. Offers a next step, not a pitch.**
End on what to do ("give it three weeks," "bring it in and I'll look"), not on an invitation to browse.

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Closed Mondays." | "Please note that we are closed on Mondays." |
| Common name, then "if you want the common name" | Latin or marketing names for show |
| "Don't fuss with it." | "Requires minimal care and attention" |
| Real numbers (30, 25, forty-two) | "A wide selection," "our full range" |
| "Fine," "perfect," "mush" | "Premium," "beautiful," "perfect for any home" |

## Words and habits to avoid
- excited, thrilled, delighted, announce, premium, stunning, beautiful, perfect for any home, low maintenance, browse, full range
- Exclamation marks, emoji, "journey," "plant parent"
- Opening with "We" + feeling
- Stacking three adjectives or three benefits

## Small details
- Numbers: small counts are often spelled out in running prose (forty-two, eleven); use numerals for hours and stock lists on signs if space is tight.
- Days of the week are named, not "this weekend."
- Contractions are normal (you can't, it's, I'll).
- Length: if it fits in three to five sentences, don't make it six.

## Quick self-check before sending
1. Is there a real number, day, or place in the first sentence?
2. Did I say one thing that isn't flattering or isn't certain?
3. Is there exactly one dry moment, not zero and not five?
4. Could the reader act on this today?
5. Would I say this out loud at the counter?

## Rewrite

> The succulents came in: thirty jade plants and twenty-five aloe. They're not fussy. Give them a bright windowsill, water them when the soil is completely dry, and then forget about them for a while, which is what they want. Open Tuesday through Sunday, nine to five. Closed Mondays, in case you were going to ask.

**Note for the freelancer:** the original gave no care details, so I added the standard ones for jade and aloe (bright light, water only when dry). Check any care line against what the nursery actually recommends before publishing. Also, "premium" and the "perfect for any home" claim were dropped on purpose since they say nothing checkable.
