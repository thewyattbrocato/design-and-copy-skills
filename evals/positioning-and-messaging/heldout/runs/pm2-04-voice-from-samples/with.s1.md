# Wrenfield Plants voice guide

*Inferred from four things the owner wrote. Where the guide says "I", the owner is the one speaking.*

**The voice in one line:** a nursery owner telling you what's on the bench, what to do with it, and not to worry too much.

## Five habits

**1. Facts first, with real numbers.**
Say what arrived, how many, and what state it's in. Counts and days replace adjectives. Don't turn this into a spec sheet.
- Say: "Eleven are perfect, six are fine after a day with the window open."
- Don't say: "A wonderful selection in excellent condition."

**2. Admit the limits.**
Say what went wrong, what won't work, and that the plant business is hard. Don't be gloomy about it. The owner killed a fig, and the courier squashed the ferns. Both are stated flatly and nobody apologizes at length.
- Say: "Short answer: no, you can't save it."
- Don't say: "Unfortunately, we're sorry to say it may be difficult to revive."

**3. Instructions are short, then you're let off the hook.**
Give the one or two things that matter, then tell the reader to relax. Don't pile on care tips.
- Say: "Keep it out of direct sun and don't fuss with it."
- Don't say: "Here are 10 tips to help your plant thrive!"

**4. One dry aside per piece, at most.**
The humor comes from precision or a small correction, usually in parentheses or a repeated phrase. It never comes from exclamation marks or puns. If a piece has no natural aside, leave it out.
- Say: "Water them on Saturdays. Not Sundays, Saturdays."
- Say: "(the courier stacked them)"
- Don't say: "Leaf us a message!"

**5. Treat the reader as a capable adult.**
Give the common name when it's useful, offer to look at the plant in person, and don't sell. The owner doesn't tell people a plant is "perfect for any home". They say what it needs and let the reader decide.
- Say: "If it's still mush, bring it in and I'll look."
- Don't say: "Visit us to browse our full range!"

## Mechanics

- **Person:** "I" for the owner, "you" for the reader. Avoid the corporate "we" and "our".
- **Sentences:** short, with contractions. Fragments are fine ("Not Sundays, Saturdays.").
- **Numbers:** spell them out in prose (forty-two, eleven, thirty). Hours are "nine to five". Use digits only on signs where space is tight.
- **Plant names:** use the common name, and add the Latin one only if it helps. The owner says "Boston fern, if you want the common name".
- **Punctuation:** no exclamation marks. Parentheses are fine.
- **Never use:** "excited to announce", "premium", "beautiful", "perfect for any home", "low maintenance", "browse our full range", "available now", "selection".
- **"Perfect" is allowed only literally**, as in "eleven are perfect", meaning a count of undamaged plants.

## Tone by surface

| Surface | Adjust |
|---|---|
| Newsletter | The most room. Open with what happened this week, then what to do about it. One aside. |
| Signs | Shortest version. Fact, then one dry clause at most. |
| Customer replies | Straight answer first, then steps, then an offer to help in person. If someone's plant has died, be kind by being clear, not by softening. |
| Anything about money, hours, or returns | Plainest version. No jokes. |

## Rewrite

**Original:**
> We are excited to announce a new shipment of premium succulents has arrived at our nursery. Our selection includes 30 jade plants and 25 aloe, available now. These beautiful plants are perfect for any home and are low maintenance. Visit us this weekend to browse our full range, open 9 to 5 Tuesday through Sunday.

**In the owner's voice:**
> The succulents came in [day]: thirty jade plants and twenty-five aloe. Give both a bright window and then mostly leave them alone. Water when the soil is dry all the way down, not before. Closed Mondays, open nine to five every other day, so come by this weekend.

## Things to check

- **[day]:** the owner usually names the day things arrive, so fill it in.
- **The care line:** I wrote it from general knowledge of jade and aloe, not from anything the owner said. The owner should confirm it matches what they'd tell a customer.
- **The aside:** I left it out because the facts gave nothing natural to riff on. Don't add one just to hit the quota.
