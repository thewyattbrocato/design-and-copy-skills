# Wrenfield Plants voice guide

*Inferred from four things the owner wrote. Where this guide and a new draft disagree, check the four samples.*

**The voice in one line:** a person who grows plants, tells you straight what happened and what to do, and is slightly dry about it.

## Five habits

**1. Say what actually happened, including the bad parts.**
Open with a specific fact from the nursery, even an unflattering one. The owner killed a fig, the courier squashed the ferns. This builds trust, so don't turn it into a bit. One admission per piece, then move on.
- We say: "The ferns came in Tuesday, a bit squashed (the courier stacked them)."
- We don't say: "We're thrilled to share some exciting new arrivals!"

**2. Give the exact number, in words, with no adjectives.**
Counts are specific and stated flat. Spell numbers out in prose: forty-two, eleven, three weeks, nine to five. Split counts by condition when it matters ("Eleven are perfect, six are fine after a day with the window open"). Don't say "premium", "beautiful" or "a wide selection".
- We say: "Forty-two of them, and they're fine."
- We don't say: "A fantastic range of healthy plants."

**3. Verdict first, then the steps, then an offer.**
For a question, give the short answer, then what to do with a timeframe, then "bring it in and I'll look". Care instructions are short and a little bossy. If something matters, repeat it with the specific detail.
- We say: "Water them on Saturdays. Not Sundays, Saturdays."
- We don't say: "We recommend establishing a consistent watering routine."

**4. One dry aside per piece, and the joke is a fact.**
The humor comes from a true detail, usually in parentheses or the last clause: "unless it's raining sideways". It never comes from exclamation marks, puns or "plant parent" talk. If nothing funny is true, skip the aside.

**5. Plain names, no hype, no fuss.**
Use the common name (the owner always offers it). Reassure by telling people to leave the plant alone: "don't fuss with it". Plants are never "perfect for any home" or "low maintenance". Say what light and water they want.

## Tone by surface

- **Newsletter:** the fullest version. A short story or fact first, then stock, then the one instruction that matters.
- **Customer reply:** the plainest. Answer first. If the news is bad, say so without cushioning, then give a next step and the offer to look at it in person.
- **Signs:** the shortest. Hours and one real fact, with at most one aside. "Closed Mondays." is a complete sentence.

## Mechanics

- First person singular ("I"), contractions, and fragments are fine.
- No exclamation marks. Never "excited to announce", "premium", "perfect for", "low maintenance", "browse our full range" or "visit us".
- Name the day ("Tuesday", "Saturdays"), not "recently" or "soon".
- Hours are written "nine to five, Tuesday to Sunday".

## Watch for

Don't copy the fig or the courier into every issue. Those are anchors for the *kind* of opening: a true, small, specific thing. Each issue needs its own. If the freelancer doesn't know the real detail, they should ask the owner and not invent one.
