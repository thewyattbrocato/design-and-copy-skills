# Wrenfield Plants voice guide

This guide is inferred from your four samples. If a rule below doesn't sound like you, tell the freelancer.

**The voice in one line:** a nursery owner talking across the bench. They're honest about what they know, specific about the plants, and they don't oversell.

## Five habits

**1. Open with what happened or the answer, not a greeting or an announcement.**
Say the thing: what arrived, what died, what the answer is. Don't warm up. Don't go so blunt that it's cold. There's always a human detail somewhere (the courier stacked them).
- Say: "The ferns came in Tuesday, a bit squashed."
- Don't say: "We are excited to announce a new shipment."

**2. Exact numbers, spelled out, with the bad news included.**
Use real counts ("forty-two", "eleven are perfect, six are fine after a day with the window open"). Admit the flaw before the customer finds it. Never use "a variety of", "a wide selection", or "premium".
- Say: "Eleven are perfect, six are fine after a day with the window open."
- Don't say: "Our high-quality ferns arrived in great condition."

**3. Admit failure and limits plainly.**
Mistakes are fine to mention and make the advice more believable. Don't be self-deprecating for laughs, and don't dwell on it. One sentence, then move on.
- Say: "I killed a fiddle-leaf fig in March, so I'm not going to pretend any of this is easy."
- Don't say: "Plant care can be tricky, but we're here to help!"

**4. Short, firm care instructions, with permission not to fuss.**
Give one or two instructions, concrete enough to act on (a day, a place, a length of time). Then tell them to relax. Repeat a rule if it matters ("Not Sundays, Saturdays").
- Say: "Keep it out of direct sun and don't fuss with it."
- Don't say: "For best results, ensure optimal light conditions and monitor regularly."

**5. Dry humor, one touch at a time.**
A parenthetical, a deadpan aside, or an exaggeration like "raining sideways". Use one per piece, at most. Never use exclamation marks, emoji, or a joke that explains itself.
- Say: "Boston fern, if you want the common name, which I always do."
- Don't say: "Fern-tastic news, plant lovers! 🌿"

## Tone by situation

- **Signs:** the shortest version. Fact, then the exception. Use "Closed Mondays", not "We are closed on Mondays."
- **Newsletter:** the full voice, in first person singular ("I"). It's one person writing, even though the business has a name.
- **Replying to a worried customer with a dying plant:** direct and kind. Give the verdict first ("Short answer: no"), then what to do, then an offer to look at it. Don't soften the bad news into mush.
- **Complaints, refunds, anything where someone is upset:** drop the jokes entirely. Plain and helpful.

## Mechanics

- Spell out numbers in running text (forty-two, eleven). Use numerals for times and prices if clearer ("9 to 5" on a sign, "nine to five" in a sentence).
- Use the common name, and the Latin name only if the customer needs it.
- Contractions are fine. Sentence fragments are fine ("Not Sundays, Saturdays.").
- Words to avoid: *excited to announce, premium, perfect for any home, low maintenance, beautiful, browse our range, curated, journey, plant parent.*

## Before sending, check

1. Does it start with a fact?
2. Is there at least one real number or specific detail?
3. Is there anything I'd be embarrassed to say out loud at the counter? Cut it.
4. Is there a flaw, limit, or honest "it depends" where one is true?
5. Could another nursery have written this sentence? If so, make it more specific to Wrenfield.
