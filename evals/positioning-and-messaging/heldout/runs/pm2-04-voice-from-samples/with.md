# Wrenfield Plants voice guide

*Inferred from four of the owner's own pieces: a newsletter intro, a newsletter item, a customer reply and a shop sign. If something here clashes with how the owner actually talks, they win.*

**The voice in one line:** a nursery owner telling you what's on the bench and what to do about it, with no sales pitch.

## Four habits

**1. Counts and facts instead of adjectives.** Say how many, which, and when. Don't call plants "beautiful" or "premium."
- We say: "Forty-two of them, and they're fine."
- We don't say: "A wonderful selection of healthy plants."

**2. Say what happened, including the bad parts.** Mishaps get one plain sentence, often in the same breath as the good news. Don't dramatize them or hide them.
- We say: "The ferns came in Tuesday, a bit squashed (the courier stacked them)."
- We don't say: "We're thrilled to share that our ferns have arrived!"

**3. Give the instruction, then stop.** Care advice is short, specific and a little bossy. Give one rule, not a list of caveats. Don't add reassurance after it.
- We say: "Water them on Saturdays. Not Sundays, Saturdays."
- We don't say: "For best results, we recommend a consistent watering schedule."

**4. Dry, not jokey.** The humor is understatement and the odd aside, such as "unless it's raining sideways" or "which I always do." Use at most one per piece, and never explain it. No exclamation marks, puns or emoji.

## Where the limit is
- **Blunt, not cold.** The reply that says "no, you can't save it" also says what to do next and offers to look at the plant. Give bad news straight, and always give the next step.
- **Dry, not sarcastic.** The owner never makes fun of the customer.

## How it flexes
- **Signs:** shortest. A fact, then at most one dry clause.
- **Newsletter:** first person ("I"), conversational, a few short paragraphs. Open with something that happened, not a greeting.
- **Customer replies:** answer first ("Short answer: no"), then the steps, then the offer. When someone is upset about a dying plant, drop the humor and keep the practical help.

## Mechanics
- First person singular: "I," not "we are excited."
- Numbers are written out in words: forty-two, eleven, nine to five. Use digits only when words get awkward.
- Use the common name, and the Latin name only if it helps.
- Short sentences, with the odd fragment. Parentheses are fine for asides.
- **Never use:** "excited to announce," "premium," "perfect for any home," "beautiful," "browse our full range," "low maintenance" as a bare claim. If a plant is easy, say what you do with it.

## The test before sending
1. Does it contain a number, a day or a plant name that someone could check?
2. Is there exactly one instruction, and is it specific?
3. Could any garden center send it unchanged? If so, rewrite it.

# The rewrite

> The succulents are in: thirty jade plants and twenty-five aloe. Both are easy, which is not the same as no work. [Add the owner's one watering rule here, e.g. "Water when the soil is dry. Not before."] Come by this weekend. We're open nine to five, Tuesday through Sunday, closed Mondays.

**Before you send it:**
- I didn't invent any care advice. The bracket needs the owner's actual rule, since that's the line that will sound most like them.
- If there's something true about how the shipment arrived (the day, the condition, the courier), add one sentence about it in the style of habit 2. That is the quickest way to make it sound like the owner, but I had no facts to base it on.
