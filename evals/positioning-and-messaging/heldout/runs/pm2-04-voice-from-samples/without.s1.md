# Wrenfield Plants voice guide

## The short version
Sound like a knowledgeable person talking over the bench, not a business making an announcement. Be specific, be honest, be brief, and be a little dry.

## What your writing does

**1. Leads with the real thing, not the greeting.**
Your intros start with what happened (a dead fig, a delivery), never "We're excited to…". Open with a fact or a admission.

**2. Uses exact numbers and details.**
"Forty-two of them," "eleven are perfect, six are fine," "three weeks," "Saturdays." Never "a variety," "a wide selection," or "many." If you know the count, say it. Spell out numbers in running prose in the newsletter ("forty-two"); digits are fine for hours on signs.

**3. Admits flaws plainly.**
The courier squashed the ferns. You killed a fig. Bad news gets one calm sentence, then the fix. Don't spin it, and don't grovel.

**4. Gives instructions as short, flat commands.**
"Water them on Saturdays." "Don't fuss with it." "Give it three weeks." No "we recommend" or "you may wish to."

**5. Repeats to correct, for emphasis.**
"Not Sundays, Saturdays." "Short answer… Long answer…" A quick self-correction or two-part structure is a signature move. Use it sparingly, once per piece at most.

**6. Dry humor, never jokes.**
"Unless it's raining sideways." "Which I always do." The humor sits in a precise, slightly deadpan detail. No exclamation marks, no puns, no emoji.

**7. Offers a person, not a policy.**
"Bring it in and I'll look." First person singular ("I"), even though it's a business. Use "I" for the owner's voice; use "we" rarely.

**8. Plain words, with the common name.**
Say "Boston fern," "jade plant," "mush." Skip "premium," "beautiful," "perfect for any home," "curated," "low maintenance" (say what the care actually is).

## Sentence mechanics
- Short sentences, mixed with the occasional longer one that carries a parenthetical or a tacked-on clause.
- Parentheses for dry asides.
- Fragments are fine ("Closed Mondays.").
- Commas and periods. No exclamation marks, rarely a dash.
- Paragraphs are 3–5 sentences.

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "The ferns came in Tuesday, a bit squashed." | "We're thrilled to announce our new fern arrivals!" |
| "Don't fuss with it." | "Minimal care is required." |
| "Closed Mondays." | "Please note we are closed on Mondays." |
| "If it's still mush, bring it in." | "Please don't hesitate to contact us." |

## Checklist before sending
- Does the first sentence say something that actually happened?
- Is there at least one exact number or day?
- Is any sentence doing nothing but sounding friendly? Cut it.
- Any adjectives like *beautiful, premium, perfect*? Replace with a fact.
- Is there one dry detail, and not more than one?
- Could the owner say this out loud at the bench without cringing?

## Note for the freelancer
Don't invent facts to be charming. If you don't know the care needs, the count, or what went wrong with a delivery, ask. The voice depends on the details being true.

## Rewrite

> The succulents came in Tuesday: thirty jade plants and twenty-five aloe. They're on the front bench. Both want a lot of light and very little water, so water them every two weeks or so and then leave them alone. Open Tuesday through Sunday, nine to five. Closed Mondays.

**Notes on the rewrite:** I made up two things you'll want to check: "came in Tuesday" and "front bench," and "every two weeks" for watering. The original paragraph had no arrival day, location, or care info, so replace them with the real ones. I also dropped "premium" and "beautiful" because they say nothing, and left out a joke rather than force one. If something went slightly wrong with the shipment, or there's a real quirk about these plants, that's where your dry detail would go.
