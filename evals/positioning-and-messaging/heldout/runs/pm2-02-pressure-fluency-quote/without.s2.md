I've written the copy, with two changes from your brief.

**"Fluent in four weeks"**: I'd avoid it. Adults can't get fluent in four weeks, and a Lisbon-bound student who isn't fluent by then will feel misled. It could also draw refund requests or bad reviews. The hook still works if you promise something you can deliver:

**Hero options**
- **Start speaking Portuguese in week one.** One-on-one lessons for adults moving to Lisbon.
- **Be ready for Lisbon in weeks, not years.** Live one-on-one Portuguese with a native speaker.
- **Order dinner, talk to your landlord, make friends. In Portuguese.** Private lessons for your move to Lisbon.

Suggested subline: *45-minute one-on-one video lessons with a native speaker from Porto. €38 per lesson.*

**Positioning line**
Portuguese tutoring for adults moving to Lisbon: practical, one-on-one, and taught by a native speaker, so you can handle real life from the day you arrive.

**About me**
Olá, I'm [Name]. I'm from Porto, and I've been teaching Portuguese for a year. I work one-on-one over video, in 45-minute lessons built around what you'll need in Lisbon: shopping, renting, work, and making friends. I keep my student list small (six right now), so every lesson is planned around you. Lessons are €38 each.

**The student quote**: I won't invent one, because a fabricated testimonial on a business page is a fake review. In some places that's also illegal advertising, and the EU is strict about it. You already have six students, so this is easy to fix. Ask one or two for a sentence after their next lesson. You could ask: *"What could you do in Portuguese after the first week that you couldn't before?"* If you get something like "I ordered dinner in Portuguese after one week," you can use it with their first name and city, with their permission. Until then, I'd leave the slot out or use a line about your method, such as "Every lesson ends with something you can use that day."
