I've written all three, with two changes. I didn't use "get fluent in four weeks" or an invented student quote. Both would be false claims to people paying you, and fluency in four weeks isn't something a prospect can reasonably read as "it varies". A made-up testimonial with a name and city is also fake social proof, which can get you in trouble with consumer-protection rules in the EU. You can get the same hook without either.

**Hero**

> **Order dinner in Lisbon in your first week. Hold a real conversation in your first month.**
> One-on-one Portuguese lessons over video with a native speaker from Porto. Built for adults moving to Lisbon.
> [Book your first lesson]

If you want the four-week hook in there: "Your first real Portuguese conversation in four weeks." That's a claim you can stand behind. "Fluent" isn't.

**Positioning line**

> Portuguese for people who are about to live it. Practical, European Portuguese, one-on-one.

Alternative: "Learn the Portuguese you'll need on day one in Lisbon, not the Portuguese in a textbook."

**About me**

> Olá, I'm [Name]. I'm from Porto, and Portuguese is my first language. I've been teaching adults for about a year, one-on-one over video, and I focus on the situations you'll face when you arrive in Lisbon: the café, the landlord, the pharmacy, the Finanças office. Lessons are 45 minutes and tailored to you. I work with a small number of students at a time, so you get real attention, not a template.

**Pricing line**

> €38 per 45-minute lesson. No packages required.

(Only keep "no packages required" if it's true.)

**Testimonial**

You have 6 students, so ask two of them for a sentence or two. Something like "What could you do in Portuguese after the first few weeks that you couldn't before?" usually gets you a quote as good as the one you imagined, and it's real. Until then, leave the section out. An empty testimonial block looks better than a fake one that someone eventually spots.
