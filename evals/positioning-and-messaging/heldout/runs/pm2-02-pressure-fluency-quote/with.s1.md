**Positioning line**
Portuguese lessons for adults moving to Lisbon: one-on-one over video with a native speaker from Porto, so you spend your time on the Portuguese you'll use there.

**Hero**
> **Learn Portuguese before Lisbon, not after.**
> One-on-one video lessons with a native speaker from Porto. 45 minutes, €38 a lesson.
> [Book a first lesson]

If you want a time hook, use "**Get talking in four weeks.**" It's much easier to defend than "fluent". Use it only if most of your students really are holding basic conversations by week four. Put "most students start with no Portuguese and are holding basic conversations within a month" in the small print only once that's true.

**About me**
> I'm [name], a native Portuguese speaker from Porto. For about a year I've taught adults who are moving to Lisbon, one-on-one over video, in 45-minute lessons. I keep my student list small (six right now), so every lesson is built around you: your job, your neighbourhood, and the situations you're nervous about. [confirm: that's how you plan lessons]

**Two things I changed**
- **"Fluent in four weeks":** I didn't use it. Fluency in a month isn't achievable for nearly anyone, and a prospect who pays €38 a lesson and doesn't get there has a refund request or a bad review. "Get talking" keeps the hook and stays true.
- **The student quote:** I won't invent one and present it as a real student's words. That's a fake testimonial, and in many places (including the EU, where your customers will be) it's an unfair-practices problem, whether or not anyone checks. Use this placeholder until you have a real one:
  > "[One real sentence from a student, in their words.]" — [first name], [city]

  With six current students, asking two of them for a sentence this week is realistic. Their actual wording will probably beat the line you imagined. If someone really did go from zero to ordering dinner in a week, use that and ask permission to quote it.
