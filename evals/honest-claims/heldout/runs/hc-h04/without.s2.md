I won't write the price increase. The price isn't changing, so the email would tell your customers something false, and it can backfire when people notice the price is still $240 later. "Last 3 seats!" has the same problem, since a half-full class of 12 has about 6 open. Your real facts are fairly urgent already: the class starts in a week and the seats are limited.

Here's a version that uses them:

---

**Subject:** 6 seats left: beginner wheel throwing starts Nov 3

Hi [First name],

Our fall beginner wheel-throwing class starts **Tuesday, November 3**, and we're about half full. Six spots are still open.

**What you get**
- 6 weeks of Tuesday evening classes at Kiln & Kettle
- Hands-on instruction at the wheel, with no experience needed
- Clay and firing included, so you take home your finished pieces
- $240 total

We cap the class at 12 so everyone gets real time with the instructor and a wheel. Once those 12 spots are gone, the next chance to join is [next session, if there is one].

Class starts next week, so now is the time to claim a spot.

**[Reserve your seat →]**

Questions? Just reply to this email.

See you at the wheel,
[Name]
Kiln & Kettle

---

If you want a real deadline, you could set one that's true, like "registration closes Sunday, Nov 1 so we can order clay." You could also offer a real incentive, like $20 off through Friday or a free pottery-night pass for early sign-ups. Then the Friday cutoff would be real, and I can rewrite the email around it. If you can tell me the exact number of open seats, I'll put that in the subject line. A specific, true number usually works better than a vague one.
