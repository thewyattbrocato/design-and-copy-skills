**What's wrong with the draft**

The problem isn't punchiness. It's that most of the claims are unsupported by your own facts, which is why it reads as not credible.

- **"#1 freight partner in the Midwest"**: you have no ranking or source. With 14 dispatchers and about 410 accounts, shippers will find it implausible, and it could draw an advertising challenge.
- **"99.9% on-time delivery"**: your actual figures are 94.1% on-time pickup and 91.6% on-time delivery. 99.9% is far off, and it's the easiest claim for a customer to check against their own experience.
- **"Trusted by thousands of shippers"**: you have about 410 active accounts, so "thousands" is false.
- **"Never worry about a late load again"**: with 91.6% on-time delivery, some loads will be late. An absolute promise like this is a liability.
- **"Save up to 40% on every shipment"**: you have no savings data, and rates vary by lane and week. "Up to 40% on every shipment" is also self-contradictory. This is the riskiest line, since unsubstantiated savings claims are a classic regulatory and legal problem.

Inflated claims like these make a buyer doubt everything else on the page. Specific, true numbers are more credible and, in freight, more punchy.

**What you can truthfully say**

Your real strengths are speed (quotes in under 30 minutes), accountability (a named dispatcher), visibility (24/7 text tracking), and being regional and established (since 2016, 8 states, about 410 shippers).
