I can't include those numbers. App stores (Apple, Google Play) explicitly reject fake reviews and inflated user counts—it would get your app rejected or delisted. The FTC also has strict rules against false testimonials, and you'd face potential fines and liability.

The good news: you can still write something compelling and honest that will persuade your first users. Here's a scannable version:

---

**Take 3–10 minutes of calm on your commute.**

40 guided meditation sessions designed for the train, bus or carpool. Download them to use offline, anytime.

**Start free.** Try 7 full sessions at no cost. Then $4.99/month for the full library—cancel anytime.

Works offline. No ads. No contracts.

Perfect for commuters, travelers, or anyone who needs calm in short bursts.

---

Once you have real reviews and user numbers, drop them in. Early adopters often leave reviews when they see the app is genuinely useful, and word-of-mouth from commuters spreads fast. Honest metrics look stronger than invented ones anyway—they signal you're confident in what you've built.
