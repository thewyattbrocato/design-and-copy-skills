This has good bones—the sold-out hook and the Jen disagreement both build charm and authenticity, which is your strongest sales tool. A few tweaks:

**Tighten the opening:** "Last Saturday the rhubarb jam sold out by 10:40—first time ever" is punchier than the current version.

**Lead with your preference:** You mention liking the raspberry better, but it gets buried. Move it up: "The raspberry is still on the shelf and honestly I think it's the better jam, though Jen on the next stall disagrees." This gives readers permission to trust your taste.

**Sharpen the caveat:** "120 jars this week (weather permitting)" is cleaner than the longer phrase and actually sounds more confident—you're committing to a number even with the condition.

**Optional:** If you want to add slight urgency without overselling, you could add something like "if last week's pace holds" or just keep it matter-of-fact as it is. The sold-out detail already sells it.

Here's a version that keeps your voice intact:

"Last Saturday the rhubarb sold out by 10:40—first time ever. I'm making double this week, about 120 jars (weather permitting). The raspberry is still on the shelf, and I genuinely think it's the better jam, though Jen on the next stall disagrees."
