You've got great bones here—the sold-out detail is your hook, and the Jen comment gives you personality. A few tweaks to lean into the selling without losing that authentic voice:

**Rework the middle sentence:**
Instead of "I'm making double this week, about 120 jars, though the rhubarb is at the mercy of the weather so don't hold me to it," try something like:

"I'm doubling the batch to 120 jars this week—rhubarb's in high demand—though fair warning, the weather might have other plans."

This flips the scarcity into a feature (high demand) while keeping the honest caveat.

**Punch up the raspberry pitch:**
Right now "still on the shelf" is a bit passive. Try:

"The raspberry's still available and honestly might be the better jar—Jen disagrees, but then again, she sells strawberry."

The callback to Jen as a rival seller adds humor and makes it feel like a real recommendation, not just inventory clearing.

**Full version:**
"Last Saturday the rhubarb sold out by 10:40—something that's never happened before. I'm doubling this week to 120 jars, though the weather might have other plans. The raspberry's still available and honestly might be the better jar—Jen on the next stall disagrees with that take, but then again, she sells strawberry."

Keeps you sounding like you while creating a bit more momentum and personality. Does that direction feel right?
