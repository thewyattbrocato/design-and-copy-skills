Your page is flat in tone, not in content. The content is the strong part. It's specific, it states the price and time, it says up front when a piano may need two visits, and it makes a clear promise about repairs. Most tuner sites don't do any of that, so I wouldn't "punch up" by adding adjectives. That would weaken what's working.

**What it needs**
1. **A first line that says why you.** "I tune pianos" is a job title. Lead with what a customer gets, like straight answers or no surprises.
2. **A person in it.** After 22 years, there must be a reason you do this and a piano you remember. One real detail would do more than any polish.
3. **Your promises as the main point.** "I'll tell you before I start" and "no commission" are your selling points, but they're buried mid-paragraph. Pull them out.
4. **A stronger call to action.** "You can book by..." is passive. Make it the closing line.
5. **Proof.** Even one name of a church hall or school, or a short quote from a customer, would help. Ask permission first.
