Your copy is already good: plain, specific, and honest. The problems are a few contradictions and one misplaced section.

**What to fix**

1. **"Back at your door in about a week" is misleading.** Your own steps say 2-3 days for the box, then transit to you, then 5 business days after receipt, then return transit. That's closer to two to three weeks door to door. Customers who read "a week" will email you on day 8. Either say "ships back within 5 business days of arrival" (true and still good) or give the full range.
2. **The serrated price is under "What we can't do," but you can do it.** Move it into pricing. The section should only hold real limits.
3. **Is the box free?** The headline says "the box we ship you," and the button says "Get a shipping box." Nowhere does it say whether the box or the outbound label costs anything. Say so either way, because people will wonder.
4. **"Drop the box at any carrier location" is probably wrong.** A prepaid label usually works with one carrier. Name the carrier, or say "drop it at any [carrier] location."
5. **Grammar:** "We sharpen each one... and it's back" mixes singular and plural.
6. **Worked total.** "$9 a knife, $12 flat" makes people do math. One example line (six knives = $66) removes the question.

**The quotes placeholder** has to be real before launch. Don't let the designer ship it as-is or fill it with invented ones. If you have no customers yet, cut the section rather than fake it.

**Check before it goes to the designer**
- Is `hello@sharpmail.example` a placeholder? `.example` domains don't work.
- Do you want a line on how to pack the knives safely? It's a common worry with mail-in sharpening, and one sentence in step 2 would cover it.
- "Chipped tips" became "chips" in my version. Keep "tips" if you only mean tip damage and will handle chips elsewhere on the blade.
