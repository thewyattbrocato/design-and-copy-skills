**No, those numbers don't show the program worked.** They show that people finished it, liked it, and passed its quiz. Your director will likely want to know whether agents handle support work better afterward, and none of these measures answers that.

**What your numbers do and don't show**

- **12/12 completion** shows the course is doable. It doesn't show anyone learned anything.
- **4.6/5 survey** measures reaction. Post-course surveys are also biased upward: people who finish tend to rate highly, and the survey is usually asked right when the course ends.
- **91% quiz** shows the agents retained the material in the course setting. It doesn't show they can apply it on live tickets. Quizzes also tend to be easier than real cases.
- **No comparison.** Without a baseline (previous cohort, agents who haven't taken the course yet, or their own pre-course performance), you can't tell whether any change came from the course.
- **Sample size.** With 12 people, one or two unusual agents can move averages a lot.

**What would show impact**

Pick a few metrics you already track and compare the new cohort to a baseline at about 30, 60, and 90 days:

- Time to proficiency: when agents first hit your target on handle time, QA score, or escalation rate, compared to earlier hires.
- Quality: QA scores, first-contact resolution, reopen rate, CSAT on their tickets.
- Efficiency: average handle time and escalations to tier 2, adjusted for ticket mix if possible.
- Retention: whether agents stay past 90 days. This matters because onboarding cost is often a big part of the business case.

If you can't get a clean comparison group, a before/after comparison against the last several cohorts is still much better than nothing. Say that's the method you're using.

**What to tell your director now**

You don't need to wait for 90-day data to report something useful. Something like:

> "The first cohort of 12 completed the two-week onboarding. Participants rated it 4.6/5 and averaged 91% on the final quiz, so the content is landing and the format is workable. These are early signals, though, not proof of impact. To judge whether it changes performance, I'm tracking [time to proficiency, QA score, FCR, escalation rate] for this cohort against [previous cohorts / a baseline], with 30/60/90-day checkpoints. I'll have a first readout on [date]."

That keeps you from overclaiming now and gives her a clear answer on when she'll get the real evidence.
