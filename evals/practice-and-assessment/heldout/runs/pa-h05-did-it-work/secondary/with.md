**Short answer: no, it isn't enough.** Those three numbers show the cohort finished, liked the course, and could answer quiz questions on the last day. They don't show that agents handle tickets differently afterward, which is what your director is asking about.

**Why each number doesn't prove it**
- **12 of 12 completed:** This measures attendance, not learning.
- **4.6/5 survey:** This measures reaction. It can flag dissatisfaction, but a high score doesn't show the skill transferred.
- **91% quiz:** This is same-day recall. If the questions test definitions or policy wording, it overstates what agents can do. Even good scenario questions don't show they apply the material to a live ticket.

**What to tell your director**

> The first cohort of 12 agents completed the two-week onboarding, rated it 4.6/5, and averaged 91% on the final quiz. That tells us the course was well received and the material was learned by the end. It doesn't yet show that agents perform better on the job. We'll know that by [date], when we compare [metric the team already tracks, e.g., first-contact resolution or QA score] for the cohort against [baseline period] and against [comparison group, if one exists].

**Before you report a result**
1. **Pick the metric the team already tracks** for support quality, and the observable behavior behind it. Write down what success looks like before you pull the data.
2. **Get a baseline.** Pull the same metric for [N] months before the cohort started. Don't invent one.
3. **Measure later, not on the last day.** Check at 30 and 60 days, when agents are using the skill on real tickets.
4. **Add a comparison** if you can, such as agents who haven't taken the course yet or a later cohort. Without one, you can't separate the training's effect from everything else that changed.
5. **Audit a sample of live tickets** against a short rubric, such as "checked [X] before offering [Y]," scored by two reviewers.
6. **Ask the 12 agents and their leads** what they still get stuck on, and which job aids they actually use. If the answer is "I know, but…", the cause may be tools, escalation paths, or targets, and more training won't fix it.

**Notes**
- With 12 agents, one bad week can move the numbers a lot. Report individual patterns with caution and avoid overclaiming.
- If the metric doesn't move, check the cause (tools, process, incentives, time pressure) before adding training.
