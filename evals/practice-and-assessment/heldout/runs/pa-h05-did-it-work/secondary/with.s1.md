**No, that isn't enough to show the program worked.** Each number measures something other than what your director is asking about:

- **Completion (12 of 12)** shows people attended. It doesn't show they can do the job.
- **Survey (4.6/5)** shows they liked it. The survey is a satisfaction measure, so it can flag unhappiness but can't show learning or behavior change.
- **Quiz (91%)** shows they could answer questions on the last day of the course. It doesn't show they can handle a live ticket weeks later, and it can't show the program caused the result.

Also, with 12 agents and no comparison group, you can't separate the program's effect from the people you hired, the queue they were given, or the season.

**What would show it worked:** the metric support already uses, compared before and after.

1. **Name the outcome the program was meant to change.** For example: time to proficiency, first-contact resolution, QA score on a specific ticket type, escalation rate, or [your team's metric]. If the onboarding was meant to fix a specific problem, such as wrong refunds or long escalations, use that.
2. **Get a baseline.** Pull [metric] for the agents hired before the program, over the same period after their start dates.
3. **Measure the new cohort at fixed points** (for example, day 30, 60, 90) on that metric, from your ticketing or QA system.
4. **Compare against a reference.** Use the prior cohort, or agents hired in the same window who did not take the course, and note the difference and its size.
5. **Check the cause before you credit the course.** If the metric is low because of a tool problem, an unclear escalation process, or staffing, more training won't fix it, and you should say so.
6. **Ask a few agents what they actually do differently** on the tickets where the course should matter.

**What to tell your director now:**

> "The last cohort of 12 completed the two-week onboarding. Learners rated it 4.6/5 and averaged 91% on the final quiz. Those numbers tell us the course was well received and the content landed on the day. They don't yet tell us whether agents handle tickets better on the job. I'm measuring [metric] at day 30, 60, and 90 against [baseline/comparison group], and I'll have results by [date]."

That answer is accurate, doesn't overclaim, and gives a date. I don't have your ticket data, so the metric, baseline, and dates above are placeholders you'll need to fill in.
