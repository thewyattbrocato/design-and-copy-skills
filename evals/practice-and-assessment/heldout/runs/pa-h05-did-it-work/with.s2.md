**Short answer: no, it isn't enough.** Those three numbers show the course was delivered and that people liked it. They don't show that agents handle tickets better.

**What each number tells you**
- **12 of 12 finished:** Completion is mostly a sign that the course was required and scheduled. It says nothing about whether the training worked.
- **4.6 out of 5 on the survey:** This measures satisfaction. It can flag unhappiness, but it can't show that anyone learned or changed what they do.
- **91% quiz average:** This is one same-day score with no baseline. Without a pre-course score or a comparison group, you can't tell whether the course taught them or they already knew the material. A quiz also only shows what people can recall or recognize. It doesn't show what they do with a live customer.

**What to tell your director**
> "The cohort completed the program and rated it highly, which tells us it was well received and delivered as designed. It doesn't yet show that it changed performance. To show that, I'm tracking [metric you already use, e.g. first-contact resolution, QA score, escalation rate, handle time] for these 12 agents at [30/60/90 days] and comparing it with [the prior cohort / tenured agents at the same tenure]. I'll report back on [date]."

That is honest and it gives her something concrete. Telling her "the program worked" on this evidence would be overclaiming.

**What to do next**
1. **Pick the metric the team already tracks.** Choose one or two tied to what the course was meant to change, such as QA score, reopen rate, escalations or time to independent handling. Write down what you expect to see before you pull the data.
2. **Find a comparison.** The best option is the previous cohort's numbers at the same weeks of tenure. Their onboarding, ticket mix and season will differ, so call it a rough comparison and don't claim it proves the course worked. With 12 agents, report counts ("9 of 12 met the QA standard by week 6"), not percentages.
3. **Check behavior directly.** Have a lead or QA reviewer score a sample of each agent's real tickets or calls against a short rubric in weeks 3–6. Add a short scenario check a week or more after the course, with mixed cases, to see what stuck. A same-day quiz can't show that.
4. **Ask the agents and their leads.** Ask a few agents which parts they actually used and which they didn't. Ask their leads where the new agents still get stuck. That tells you what to fix in the course.
5. **Show the survey beside the results.** Report it as "rated 4.6/5" next to the performance data, not as proof on its own.
