**No, it's not enough to show the program worked. It's good evidence that the program was delivered and well received.**

**What you have**
- **Completion (12 of 12):** Participation. If the course was mandatory, this says little about quality.
- **Survey (4.6/5):** Agents liked it. That doesn't mean it changed how they work. Check whether the survey was anonymous, since new hires tend to rate generously.
- **Quiz (91%):** Agents retained the material right after the course. With no pre-test, you can't tell how much they learned versus already knew. A high average can also mean the quiz was easy.

These are reaction and learning measures. Your director is asking about results: do agents perform better on the job because of it? Nothing here speaks to that. There's also no comparison group, such as earlier cohorts, and 12 people is a small sample.

**What to tell her**
Don't overclaim. Something like:

> "The first cohort completed the program at 100%, rated it 4.6/5, and averaged 91% on the final quiz. That tells us the program is well-received and the content is landing. It doesn't yet show on-the-job impact, so I'm tracking [metrics] for this cohort against [baseline] at 30/60/90 days and will report back."

That's more credible than "it worked," and it shows you know what evidence would settle it.

**What would show it worked**
Compare this cohort to the previous onboarding cohort(s), or to a similar group of new hires. Use whichever of these you already track:
- **Time to proficiency:** weeks to reach target handle time or quality score.
- **Quality and customer metrics:** QA scores, CSAT, first-contact resolution.
- **Escalation rate:** how often new agents hand off tickets.
- **Retention:** 90-day attrition, if that's a problem for you.
- **Manager assessment:** a short structured check-in at 30 days on whether agents were prepared.

If you can still get it, ask for the same metrics from the previous cohort now. That gives you a baseline without waiting.

**Caveats to state up front**
- With 12 agents, small differences won't be statistically meaningful. Present the results as directional.
- Other things may have changed at the same time, such as ticket mix, tooling, or seasonality.
- For future cohorts, add a short pre-test and ask a few behavior-based survey questions, like "I feel able to handle X without help."
