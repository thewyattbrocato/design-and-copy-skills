**No, that isn't enough to show the program worked.** It shows people finished it and liked it. It doesn't show they do the job differently or better.

**What each number tells you**
- **12 of 12 completed:** This mostly reflects that the course is mandatory, so it says little about learning.
- **4.6 out of 5 survey:** This shows agents were satisfied. It can flag unhappiness, but a high score doesn't show they learned anything.
- **91% quiz average:** The quiz was taken right at the end, so it only shows short-term recall. It also has no baseline. You don't know what agents would have scored before the course, or whether the questions test judgment or just policy wording.
- **Sample and comparison:** Twelve people with no comparison group means you can't separate the course from the agents' prior experience or from good coaching.

**What to tell your director**
> "The first cohort completed the program and rated it highly, and quiz results were strong. That tells us the program is well received. To show it works, I'm tracking whether the cohort performs better on the metrics we already use. I'll report on that at [30/60/90 days]."

Don't claim more than that now. If she asks for a verdict today, say "promising, not yet proven."

**What to gather next**
1. **Pick the metrics you already track,** such as [time to first independent ticket], [CSAT on their tickets], [escalation or reopen rate], and [QA score]. Name which behavior the course was meant to change and which metric reflects it.
2. **Compare against a baseline.** Use the previous cohort's numbers at the same tenure, or the current cohort versus agents hired before the program. Use [your baseline figures]. I haven't assumed any.
3. **Re-check on a later day.** Give a short scenario-based quiz about 2–4 weeks later, with new situations rather than the original questions. Retention after a gap is better evidence than a same-day score.
4. **Observe real work.** Have a lead score 3–5 live tickets or calls per agent against a short rubric of the key behaviors.
5. **Ask agents what they use.** Interview 3–5 of them about which parts of the course they applied and which they didn't, and what got in their way. If a tool or process is the blocker, more training won't fix it.
