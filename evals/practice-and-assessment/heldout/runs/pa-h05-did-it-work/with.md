**Short answer: no, not as proof the program worked.** What you have shows the course was completed and liked. It doesn't show agents do the job better.

**What your numbers show**
- **12 of 12 finished.** This is completion. A cohort of 12 that is probably required to finish will complete almost no matter how good the course is.
- **4.6/5 survey.** This shows people were satisfied. It can flag unhappiness, but it doesn't show anyone learned or changed behavior.
- **91% quiz average.** This shows knowledge right after the course, with no baseline. You can't tell what they knew before, whether the quiz tests judgment or just recall, or whether anything stuck. A single same-day score also can't show retention.

**What to tell your director**
Something like: "The rollout went well. All 12 agents finished, they rated it highly, and they scored well on the final quiz. Those are good early signs that the course is usable and understood. They don't yet show it improved support performance, so here's how I'll measure that over the next [X weeks]."

Then propose the evidence that would show it worked:

1. **Pick the metric you already track that onboarding should move.** Examples are time to independent handling, first-contact resolution, QA scores, escalation rate, reopen rate, and CSAT on their tickets. Name the specific behavior behind each one, such as "correctly escalates billing disputes."
2. **Compare against a baseline.** Use the previous cohort's numbers at the same tenure (weeks 2, 4 and 8), or [current baseline] if you have it. With 12 people this is directional, not statistical, so say so.
3. **Re-test later.** Give a mixed set of scenario questions with changed details about 1–2 weeks after the course and again around 30 days. Compare it to the day-one score to see what stuck.
4. **Look at actual work.** Have a lead or QA reviewer score a sample of each agent's real tickets against a short rubric.
5. **Ask agents and their leads where they got stuck.** Find out which parts they used on the job and which they didn't. Five or six conversations will tell you more than the survey average.
6. **Check the cause of any gaps.** If agents struggle because of a tool, a vague process, or time pressure, more training won't fix it. Flag those to the person who owns them.

**Caveats to state up front**
- Cohort size is 12, so be careful about claims.
- If the quiz is mostly recall questions, rewrite some as "what would you do next?" scenarios for future cohorts.
- I haven't seen the course, the quiz, or your metrics, so the placeholders above need your real numbers.
