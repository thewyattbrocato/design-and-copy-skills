**No, that isn't enough to show the program worked. It's a good start, though, and you can say so honestly.**

**What you have and what it shows**
- **12 of 12 finished:** people stayed through the course. It says nothing about whether they do the job better.
- **Survey of 4.6/5:** people were satisfied. A survey like this only flags unhappiness. It can't show that anyone learned or changed what they do.
- **Quiz average of 91%:** this is the closest to evidence. But it's a single end-of-course score taken right after the material. It shows recall that day, not that agents handle real tickets better. It also has no baseline, so you can't tell whether they'd have scored similarly before the course.

**What to tell your director**
> "The first cohort's early signals are positive: 12 of 12 finished, they rated it 4.6/5, and the final quiz average was 91%. Those show the course is well received and people learned the content. They don't yet show better performance on the job. To check that, I'll track [metric you already report, e.g. QA score, reopen rate, time to independent handling] for this cohort at [30 and 60 days], compared with [previous cohort or current baseline] at the same tenure. I'll also have the quality team score a sample of each agent's real tickets against a short rubric."

**What to collect so you can make the claim**
1. **Pick the metric now, before you look at results.** Choose one your team already tracks, and write down what "worked" would look like. Use a placeholder like [target] until you have a real baseline. Don't invent one.
2. **Compare against a baseline.** Use the previous cohort or current new hires at the same tenure. With 12 people and no control group, this is suggestive, not proof. Note differences like ticket mix, team lead or tooling changes.
3. **Observe real work.** Have QA score [number] of each agent's tickets in weeks 3–4, using a rubric fixed in advance.
4. **Retest on a later day.** Give a mixed set of new scenarios about a week after the course, and again at about a month. If scores hold up on changed scenarios, that's much stronger than 91% on the same day.
5. **Ask the agents.** Which parts did they use in the first weeks? What do they still not know how to do? Ask the stronger and weaker performers both. This shows which parts of the course are working and which need fixing.
6. **Report small numbers as counts.** Say "9 of 12", not "75%".

If the metric moves in the right direction, you can say the program *is associated with* improvement. If it doesn't, the same data will show where to fix the course. It's also worth checking whether the problem was a tool or process issue that no course could fix.
