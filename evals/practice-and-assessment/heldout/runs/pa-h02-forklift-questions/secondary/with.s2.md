# Forklift Safety Check: New Hire Quiz

Answer each question based on what you'd do on the floor. Answers and feedback are below the questions.

**1. Pre-shift check.** You start your shift and test the truck. The forks, tires, brakes, lights, and hydraulics all pass, but the horn makes no sound. What do you do?

- A) Operate it carefully. You only need the horn at blind corners, so report it at the end of your shift.
- B) Tag the truck out, don't operate it, and report the horn to [supervisor name].
- C) Try the horn again once you're in the aisle. If it works there, carry on.
- D) Keep working and ask a coworker to call out at blind corners for you.

**2. Going down a ramp.** You're taking a loaded pallet down a ramp to the dock. Which way do the forks point?

- A) Downhill, in the direction you're traveling.
- B) Level with the ramp, so the load sits flat.
- C) Uphill, so you drive down the ramp in reverse with the load facing up the slope.
- D) Either direction works, as long as the mast is tilted forward to keep the load steady.

**3. Blocked view.** You're carrying a pallet down an aisle and the load blocks your view of the path ahead. What's the best move?

- A) Keep going slowly and have a coworker call out what's ahead.
- B) Raise the forks higher so you can see over the top of the load.
- C) Lean out of the side of the cab and look ahead while you drive forward.
- D) Drive in reverse so you can see where you're going.

**4. Speed.** You're bringing a pallet from a clear aisle into a freezer chamber. What's your maximum speed once you're inside the chamber?

- A) 5 km/h
- B) 8 km/h
- C) 10 km/h
- D) 12 km/h

**5. Pedestrian.** In an aisle, a worker is walking toward you and hasn't looked your way. What do you do?

- A) Sound the horn and keep moving at 8 km/h. They'll hear you and step aside.
- B) Stop, make eye contact, and wait until they've seen you and moved clear.
- C) Speed up to clear the end of the aisle before they reach it.
- D) Slow down and drive past close to them, since the aisle is wide enough.

**6. Passenger.** A coworker asks for a ride on your truck to the dock. They say it's only 20 m. What do you tell them?

- A) Yes, if the distance is short and you go slowly.
- B) No. Passengers are never allowed on the truck.
- C) Yes, as long as they stand on the back step and not on the forks.
- D) Yes, if you clear it with [supervisor name] first.

**7. Capacity plate.** A pallet you need to move is a little heavier than the capacity shown on the mast's plate. What do you do?

- A) Lift it slowly with the mast tilted back. It's only a little over.
- B) Lift it. The plate is only a guideline, and other drivers do it too.
- C) Don't lift it. Report it to [supervisor name].
- D) Lift it with a coworker steadying the other side of the load.

**8. Blind corner.** You're driving down an aisle toward a blind corner at 8 km/h. What do you do?

- A) Keep 8 km/h. The aisle limit still applies.
- B) Sound the horn, keep 8 km/h, and take the corner wide.
- C) Slow to a crawl without sounding the horn, since no one is visible.
- D) Sound the horn and slow to a crawl before the corner.

---

## Answer Key

**1. B.** Tag the truck out and report it.
- A: A failed check means the truck is out of service now, not at the end of the shift. Blind corners are exactly where you'd need the horn.
- C: A failure that comes and goes is still a failure. Passing once in the aisle doesn't clear the truck.
- D: Leaning on a coworker doesn't fix the truck. It still fails its check and must be tagged out.

**2. C.** Forks uphill, driving down in reverse, with the load facing up the slope.
- A: With the forks pointing downhill, nothing holds the load back on the forks. If it shifts, it slides forward and off.
- B: The rule is forks uphill on a downhill run. Level doesn't apply here.
- D: The direction is set, not optional. Loaded mast tilt is slightly back, not forward.

**3. D.** Reverse, so you can see where you're going.
- A: A coworker's call-outs depend on their sight and their timing. You still can't see the path yourself.
- B: Travel is with forks 10 to 15 cm off the floor. Raising them breaks that rule and still leaves the load in front of you.
- C: Leaning out doesn't clear a load that blocks the view. You're just partly out of the cab with the load still in the way.

**4. A.** 5 km/h. Once you're inside the freezer chamber, the chamber limit applies.
- B: 8 km/h is the aisle limit. Once you cross into the chamber, the limit drops. Check which zone you're in as you enter it.
- C and D: Both are over both limits. A clear path doesn't raise either one.

**5. B.** Stop, make eye contact, and wait until the worker has seen you and moved clear.
- A: A horn is not eye contact. Pedestrians have right of way, and you make contact before you move near one.
- C: Speeding up brings you closer to them sooner. It doesn't clear anything.
- D: Pedestrians have the right of way, so passing close is the risk, not the solution.

**6. B.** No. Passengers are never allowed, for any distance.
- A: Distance and speed don't change the rule. No passengers, ever.
- C: Where they stand doesn't matter. No one rides on the truck.
- D: A supervisor's approval doesn't change the rule. The right answer is to say no and let your coworker walk.

**7. C.** Don't lift it, and report it to [supervisor name].
- A: The plate shows the maximum. "A little over" is still over.
- B: The plate is the limit, not a suggestion, and other drivers doing it is not a standard.
- D: Steadying the load doesn't change its weight relative to the plate.

**8. D.** Sound the horn and slow to a crawl before the corner.
- A: The speed limit is a maximum, not a target. At a blind corner, you slow to a crawl.
- B: The horn helps, but 8 km/h is still too fast for a corner you can't see around.
- C: Seeing no one doesn't mean no one is there. You need both the horn and the crawl.

---

**Notes:** Replace [supervisor name] with the real reporting contact before the session. For a real check of retention, give these again a week later with the scenarios changed (different loads, aisles, and coworkers) and count how many people answer correctly.
