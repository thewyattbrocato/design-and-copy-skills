# Forklift Safety: Post-Session Quiz

Answer each question by choosing one option. Bracketed items are site-specific details to fill in before the quiz is given.

**1. Travel height.** You need to drive an empty truck to the charging area. The forks were left at floor level from your last task. What do you do before moving?
- A. Keep the forks at floor level, since that is the lowest point.
- B. Raise the forks to 10 to 15 cm off the floor, then travel.
- C. Raise the forks to shoulder height so you can see over them.
- D. Start driving, since the forks are already in position.

**2. Mast tilt.** You have a loaded pallet and are traveling down an aisle. How should the mast be set?
- A. Tilted forward, to hold the load on the forks.
- B. Tilted slightly back.
- C. Upright, since tilt does not matter at low speed.
- D. Tilted forward only when turning.

**3. Speed in the chamber.** You are driving in the main aisle at the 8 km/h limit. You enter a freezer chamber. What is your speed limit now?
- A. Keep 8 km/h, since you are still on the same route.
- B. Drop to 5 km/h when you enter the chamber.
- C. Drop to 5 km/h only if the truck is loaded.
- D. Speed up to clear the chamber quickly.

**4. Blind corner.** You are approaching a blind corner in an aisle.
- A. Sound the horn and slow to a crawl before turning.
- B. Keep aisle speed and take the corner at a normal pace.
- C. Slow to a crawl without the horn, since the aisle is quiet.
- D. Sound the horn only if you see someone first.

**5. Passengers.** A coworker asks for a ride to the far end of the dock. The trip is about two minutes, and she says she will hold the overhead guard. What do you do?
- A. Give the ride, since it is short and she will hold on.
- B. Give the ride, as long as you drive at 5 km/h.
- C. Decline. No passengers are allowed, whatever the distance.
- D. Give the ride on the overhead guard, which is built to take weight.

**6. Heavy load.** A pallet looks heavier than the capacity plate allows. What do you do?
- A. Lift it slowly with the mast tilted back.
- B. Lift it if you are the only one checking.
- C. Do not lift it. Report it to [supervisor name] following [site load-handling procedure].
- D. Lift it, since the plate is only a guideline.

**7. Pedestrian.** You are driving toward a pedestrian pushing a cart in a cold-storage aisle, and you are behind schedule.
- A. Sound the horn and keep going; they will step aside.
- B. Pass closely, since you have the aisle.
- C. Stop, make eye contact with the pedestrian, and let them pass before you move near them.
- D. Wave from a distance and continue at speed.

**8. Blocked view.** You are carrying a load, and it blocks your view ahead.
- A. Drive forward slowly, leaning to look around the load.
- B. Sound the horn and drive forward.
- C. Drive in reverse, looking back over your shoulder.
- D. Raise the load higher so you can see over it.

**9. Ramp with a load.** You are carrying a load down a ramp.
- A. Forks point downhill, with the load toward the bottom.
- B. Forks point uphill.
- C. Forks level, regardless of the slope.
- D. Orientation does not matter if you go slowly.

**10. Pre-shift check.** During your pre-shift check you find a hydraulic leak on your assigned truck.
- A. Wipe up the leak and operate the truck, since it is small.
- B. Tag the truck out, report it to [supervisor name], and do not operate it.
- C. Operate it carefully and mention the leak at the end of the shift.
- D. Move it to another aisle, then report it later.

---

## Answer Key and Feedback

**1. B.** Travel height is 10 to 15 cm off the floor.
- A: Forks at floor level can catch on floor joints and debris. Set travel height before moving.
- C: High forks raise the center of gravity and block your forward view. Travel height is low.
- D: The height left by the last task is not the travel height. Check and reset it before moving.

**2. B.** Carry a load with the mast tilted slightly back.
- A: Forward tilt can let the load slide off. Tilt back when loaded.
- C: An upright mast gives the load no hold. Tilt back when loaded, at any speed.
- D: Tilt is a travel setting, not a turning setting. Keep it tilted back while carrying.

**3. B.** The chamber limit is 5 km/h, and it applies as soon as you enter.
- A: The chamber limit is lower than the aisle limit. Slow down before the entry.
- C: The chamber limit applies whether or not you are loaded.
- D: Speed limits are maximums, not targets. Drive at the lower limit inside.

**4. A.** At a blind corner, sound the horn and slow to a crawl.
- B: You cannot see who is around the corner, so aisle speed risks a collision.
- C: The horn warns anyone around the corner. Use both the horn and the slow speed.
- D: The horn is for blind corners whether or not you can see anyone. Sound it every time.

**5. C.** No passengers, at any distance or speed.
- A: A short trip still counts as carrying a passenger, and the rule does not allow it.
- B: Speed does not change the passenger rule.
- D: No part of the truck is built to carry people. Decline the ride.

**6. C.** Do not lift an overweight load. Report it.
- A: Tilting back does not make an overweight lift safe. Do not lift it.
- B: Having someone watch you does not make the lift allowed. Follow the capacity plate.
- D: The capacity plate is the maximum load. Do not lift above it.

**7. C.** Pedestrians have right of way. Stop, make eye contact, and let them pass.
- A: The horn does not give you the right of way. Stop and wait for the pedestrian.
- B: Passing close to a pedestrian does not show you have given them right of way.
- D: Speed around pedestrians gives them no time to see you. Stop and make eye contact first.

**8. C.** If the load blocks your view, drive in reverse.
- A: Leaning out does not show you what is ahead. Reverse so your view is clear.
- B: The horn does not clear your view. Reverse.
- D: Raising the load higher adds instability and does not fix the blocked view. Reverse.

**9. B.** On a loaded ramp, the forks point uphill.
- A: Pointing the forks downhill puts the load on the wrong side of the truck. Forks point uphill.
- C: The forks need to be oriented for the slope, not level. Point them uphill.
- D: Slow speed does not replace the correct orientation. Forks point uphill.

**10. B.** Anything failing in the pre-shift check means tag the truck out and report it.
- A: Wiping up a leak does not fix the cause. Tag it out.
- C: Operating a leaking truck puts others at risk. Tag it out now, not at end of shift.
- D: Moving the truck does not tag it out or report the fault. Do both now.

---

**Notes for the trainer:**
- Items 1, 4, 7, and 9 are good candidates for a short retrieval set the next day, mixed with items from other topics, before the full quiz is repeated later in the week.
- Items 5 and 6 test a rule with no judgment call. Keep them, but expect new hires to get them right after one session.
