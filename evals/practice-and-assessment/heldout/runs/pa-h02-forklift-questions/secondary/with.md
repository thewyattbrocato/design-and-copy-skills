# Forklift Safety Check: New Hires, Kestrel Ridge Cold Storage

Give this after the session, with the session's rules visible if the learner needs them. Answers and feedback follow the questions.

**1.** You are driving a loaded pallet down the loading ramp. What do you do?

A. Drive forward, with the forks pointing downhill.
B. Drive in reverse, with the forks pointing uphill.
C. Drive forward, with the forks pointing uphill.
D. Drive in reverse, with the forks pointing downhill.

**2.** You are carrying a load through the aisle at 8 km/h and reach a blind corner. What do you do?

A. Keep your speed, since the aisle limit allows 8 km/h.
B. Sound the horn, slow to a crawl, and then take the corner.
C. Speed up slightly so you clear the corner before anyone steps out.
D. Stop completely and wait for someone to come around first.

**3.** You are driving in an aisle at 8 km/h and enter a freezer chamber. What speed applies?

A. Stay at 8 km/h, since the aisle limit was already set.
B. Drop to 5 km/h or below.
C. Go 8 km/h, since the chamber trip is short.
D. Drop to 3 km/h, the slowest speed the truck can run.

**4.** Your load is tall enough that you cannot see the path ahead. What do you do?

A. Lean out and steer past the load while moving forward slowly.
B. Raise the load higher so you can see over it.
C. Drive in reverse, so the load is behind you.
D. Ask a coworker to ride on the back of the truck and call out directions.

**5.** A coworker asks you for a ride on your forklift to the far end of the dock, about 20 meters away. What do you do?

A. Agree, since it is a short trip and the truck is moving slowly.
B. Agree, but have them stand on the back step.
C. Refuse, since forklifts never carry passengers.
D. Agree, if they hold onto the mast.

**6.** A pallet's weight is not listed, but it looks heavier than the capacity plate on your mast allows. What do you do?

A. Lift it slowly, since the plate is only a guideline.
B. Lift it, but keep the mast fully upright.
C. Leave it. Do not lift it, and report it to [supervisor or site contact].
D. Lift it partway to test the weight, then decide.

**7.** You are driving through an aisle and a worker is walking across ahead of you. What do you do?

A. Sound the horn and keep moving, since the worker should hear you.
B. Drive around them quickly, since you have right of way in the aisle.
C. Stop, make eye contact with the worker, and wait until they are clear.
D. Slow down, but continue if the worker is not looking at you.

**8.** During your pre-shift check, you find a hydraulic fluid leak under the mast, and the brake feels soft. What do you do?

A. Note both problems, then use the truck carefully for the shift.
B. Tag the truck out of service and report both problems to [supervisor or site contact].
C. Ask a coworker to use the truck, since the leak is small.
D. Fix the leak yourself, then run the truck normally.

---

## Answers and feedback

**1. B.** Driving in reverse with the forks uphill keeps the load stable on the way down.
- A: Forward with forks downhill puts the load ahead of you, where it can slide off the forks. *Better move:* Drive in reverse, with the forks uphill.
- C: Forks uphill is right, but driving forward on the way down leaves the load pointing the wrong way for control. *Better move:* Keep the forks uphill and reverse down.
- D: Reverse is right, but downhill forks let the load slide toward the truck. *Better move:* Keep the forks uphill.

**2. B.** A blind corner is where people step out unseen, so you warn them and slow before you reach it.
- A: The 8 km/h aisle limit is a maximum, not a target. At a blind corner, you cannot see who is there. *Better move:* Slow and sound the horn first.
- C: Speeding up reduces the time you have to react to someone who appears. *Better move:* Slow to a crawl and sound the horn.
- D: Stopping is safer than speeding, but you do not need to stop if you can see enough after slowing. *Better move:* Sound the horn and slow to a crawl, then proceed carefully.

**3. B.** The freezer chamber limit is 5 km/h, lower than the aisle limit.
- A: The 8 km/h limit applies to aisles only. Entering a chamber changes the limit. *Better move:* Drop to 5 km/h as you enter.
- C: Trip length does not change the limit. *Better move:* Drop to 5 km/h for the whole trip inside the chamber.
- D: Slowing below 5 km/h is allowed, but the rule is a maximum, so 3 km/h is not required. You are not wrong to go slower, but the rule to remember is 5 km/h. *Better move:* Drop to 5 km/h or below.

**4. C.** Reversing puts the load behind you, so you can see the path you are traveling.
- A: Leaning out while moving forward is unsafe, and it does not solve the blind spot. *Better move:* Drive in reverse, so the load is behind you.
- B: Raising the load to see over it makes the truck less stable. *Better move:* Drive in reverse with the load low.
- D: Riding on the truck is not allowed, and it puts a passenger at risk. *Better move:* Drive in reverse.

**5. C.** Forklifts never carry passengers, even for a short trip.
- A: A short trip still counts as carrying a passenger. *Better move:* Say no, and walk them to the dock or [site transport option].
- B: A step or ledge still carries a person on the truck. *Better move:* Say no.
- D: Holding the mast does not make it safe, and the truck is not designed for riders. *Better move:* Say no.

**6. C.** If the load is heavier than the capacity plate allows, you do not lift it.
- A: A plate sets the maximum load, not a guideline. Lifting above it risks tipping the truck. *Better move:* Do not lift it, and report it.
- B: Keeping the mast upright does not change the load limit. *Better move:* Do not lift it.
- D: Lifting partway still puts the truck and the load at risk. *Better move:* Do not lift, and report it.

**7. C.** Pedestrians always have right of way, so you stop, make eye contact, and let them pass.
- A: The horn warns but does not give you the right to proceed. The worker may not see or hear you. *Better move:* Stop, make eye contact, and wait.
- B: Pedestrians have right of way, so you do not pass them quickly. *Better move:* Stop, make eye contact, and wait until they are clear.
- D: Not making eye contact is the problem. Assuming the worker will stay out of your way is unsafe. *Better move:* Stop, make eye contact, and wait.

**8. B.** A leak and a soft brake are both reasons to take the truck out of service.
- A: A leak and a brake problem both affect whether the truck can be stopped or controlled safely. *Better move:* Tag it out and report both.
- C: Passing an unsafe truck to a coworker puts them at risk too. *Better move:* Tag it out and report.
- D: Repairs are not your job at this stage, and the truck should not run until it is fixed. *Better move:* Tag it out and report.

---

**Notes**
- The bracketed reporting contact and any site transport options are placeholders. Fill them in before using the quiz.
- For retention, give the same questions again at the end of the first week, with the options shuffled. Do not treat one same-day pass as proof that the rules are learned.
