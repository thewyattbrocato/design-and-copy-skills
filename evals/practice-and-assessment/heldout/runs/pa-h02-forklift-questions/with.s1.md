These are 8 scenario questions, one for each rule in the session. I assumed the hires are new to forklifts and will take the quiz right after the session with no time limit. Each wrong answer has its own feedback that names the mistake.

---

**1. Travel position**
Hasan has picked up a loaded pallet and needs to cross the warehouse floor. How should he travel?

- A. Forks about chest height to clear bumps, mast upright
- B. Forks 10 to 15 cm off the floor, mast tilted slightly back
- C. Forks scraping the floor, mast tilted forward to pin the load down

**Answer: B.**
- **A:** A high load makes the truck top-heavy, and the first sharp turn or bump could tip it. Carry the load low, 10 to 15 cm up, so the center of gravity stays low.
- **B:** The low fork height keeps the truck stable, and the backward tilt cradles the load so it can't slide off the forks.
- **C:** Forks on the floor can catch a seam or debris and stop the truck suddenly, and a forward tilt pushes the load off the forks. You were right to want the forks low. The target is 10 to 15 cm, tilted slightly back.

---

**2. Speed limits**
Priya is driving an empty aisle toward the freezer chamber and wants to get the pallet in quickly. What does she do at the chamber door?

- A. Keep going at 8 km/h until she reaches the pallet position
- B. Slow to 5 km/h on entering and hold it inside
- C. Drop to 5 km/h only if she sees someone else inside

**Answer: B.**
- **A:** 8 km/h is the aisle limit, and inside the freezer it's too fast. Chamber limits are lower, so the limit changes at the door.
- **B:** The freezer limit is 5 km/h the whole time you're inside, whether or not anyone is in view.
- **C:** Slowing down only when you see people means you're relying on seeing them in time. You were right to think about people, but the 5 km/h limit applies inside the chamber regardless.

---

**3. Blind corners**
Marcus is approaching a blind corner in the aisle. What does he do?

- A. Sound the horn, then take the corner at normal aisle speed
- B. Slow to a crawl and sound the horn
- C. Slow to a crawl and stay quiet so he doesn't startle anyone

**Answer: B.**
- **A:** The horn warns people, but at full speed you can't stop if someone steps out. You need both the horn and a crawl.
- **B:** The horn tells anyone around the corner you're coming, and the crawl gives you time to stop if someone appears.
- **C:** Crawling is half of it. A quiet truck gives a person around the corner no warning, so sound the horn too.

---

**4. Passengers**
Dev's feet hurt and he asks to ride on your truck for the short trip to the dock. What do you do?

- A. Let him stand on the pallet and drive slowly
- B. Say no. He walks, and you drive on alone.
- C. Let him ride if he holds on to the mast

**Answer: B.**
- **A:** A person on the load can fall under the truck or be crushed if you brake or turn. A slow speed doesn't change that.
- **B:** The rule has no exceptions. A short trip or a sore foot doesn't change it.
- **C:** Holding the mast doesn't make it safe. The rule is no passengers, ever, however they hold on.

---

**5. Capacity plate**
The label on a pallet shows a weight above the maximum on your mast's capacity plate. What do you do?

- A. Lift it just off the floor to see if the mast copes
- B. Leave it on the floor and don't lift it
- C. Lift it, but drive at the slower freezer speed

**Answer: B.**
- **A:** Even a short lift can overload the mast or tip the truck forward, and you only find out when it fails. A test lift is already a lift.
- **B:** The plate gives the maximum load. If the load is heavier, you don't lift it.
- **C:** Driving slowly doesn't reduce the weight, and the overload risk is the same at any speed. [Add who they should tell about an overweight pallet.]

**6. Pedestrians**
Sam is driving through an aisle. A picker ahead is crossing, looking down at a handheld scanner. What does Sam do?

- A. Sound the horn once and keep rolling, since she's been warned
- B. Stop, make eye contact, and move on only once she has seen the truck
- C. Speed up and pass behind her before she steps farther across

**Answer: B.**
- **A:** She may not have heard the horn over the noise in the building, and a person looking at a scanner may not react. Pedestrians have right of way, so you have to confirm she has seen you.
- **B:** Eye contact is how you know she has seen the truck. Until then, she goes first.
- **C:** Speeding past could end in a collision, and the pedestrian has right of way. Wait for her.

**7. Blocked view on a ramp**
Lena has a tall load that blocks her view straight ahead. She needs to take it down a ramp. How does she go?

- A. Forward, leaning to look around the load, forks pointing downhill
- B. In reverse, with the forks pointing uphill
- C. Forward, sounding the horn the whole way down

**Answer: B.**
- **A:** Leaning to see around the load still leaves you blind in front, and forks pointing downhill can let the load slide off. Stop relying on a gap in your view.
- **B:** Reversing means you can see where you're going. On a ramp with a load, the forks point uphill so the load stays on them.
- **C:** The horn warns people, but it doesn't let you see them. When the load blocks your view, drive in reverse.

---

**8. Pre-shift check**
During the pre-shift check, Omar's brakes feel soft. Forks, tires, horn, lights and hydraulics all look fine, and the shift starts in a few minutes. What does he do?

- A. Drive carefully and report it at the end of shift
- B. Tag the truck out and report the problem
- C. Test it with a light load first, and tag it out only if it still feels soft

**Answer: B.**
- **A:** Driving carefully doesn't fix brakes that may not stop the truck, and the next driver could use it without knowing. Anything failing means the truck is tagged out.
- **B:** One failed item is enough. Tagging it out stops anyone driving it, and reporting it gets it repaired.
- **C:** Testing with a load means finding out the brakes are bad while carrying a pallet. Tag it out first, before driving.
