These are 8 scenario questions, one per topic in the session. I assumed the hires take them right after the session with the session rules at hand. Each wrong answer has a one-line explanation, so the quiz also works as feedback.

---

**1. Carrying height**
Priya has picked up a loaded pallet and is driving down an aisle. How should she carry it?

A. Forks all the way down on the floor, mast upright, so the load is as low as possible
B. Forks 10 to 15 cm off the floor, mast tilted slightly back
C. Forks 10 to 15 cm off the floor, mast tilted slightly forward to press the load down
D. Forks about knee height, mast upright, to clear anything on the floor

**Answer: B.**
- A: Forks dragging on the floor catch on cracks and debris and can stop the truck suddenly. Keep the forks 10 to 15 cm up.
- C: A forward tilt lets the load slide toward the forks' tips. Tilt slightly back when loaded.
- D: Higher than 10 to 15 cm raises the load's center of gravity and makes the truck less stable.

---

**2. Freezer speed**
Marcus is driving an empty truck down the aisle at 8 km/h toward the freezer chamber door. The door is open, and he wants to get through quickly so the cold air doesn't escape. What should he do?

A. Keep 8 km/h, because that's the speed limit for the whole building
B. Speed up through the doorway, then slow down inside
C. Slow to 5 km/h before he enters the chamber and stay at or below it inside
D. Use 5 km/h only when loaded, and 8 km/h when empty

**Answer: C.**
- A: 8 km/h is the aisle limit. Inside the freezer chambers the limit is 5 km/h.
- B: Speeding at the door is where he is least able to see and stop. A few seconds of open door isn't worth the risk.
- D: The 5 km/h limit applies inside the chambers whether the truck is loaded or empty.

---

**3. Blind corner**
Aisha is approaching a blind corner. She can't see around it. What does she do?

A. Sound the horn, then continue at her normal speed because anyone nearby has been warned
B. Slow to a crawl and sound the horn
C. Slow to a crawl but stay quiet, so she doesn't startle anyone
D. Stop and wait until someone on the other side waves her through

**Answer: B.**
- A: The horn warns people, but at full speed she still can't stop in time if someone steps out.
- C: Without the horn, a pedestrian around the corner has no warning at all.
- D: Waiting for a wave isn't the procedure. People may not see her, and others may not see the wave. Horn and a crawl are her job.

---

**4. The offered ride**
It's the end of a long shift. Your coworker Tomás asks for a lift on your empty truck to the far dock. He says he'll stand on the lowered forks and hold the mast. What do you do?

A. Say no. The truck carries no passengers, and he can walk or wait
B. Agree, since the truck is empty and the forks are lowered
C. Agree if he holds on tightly and you drive slowly
D. Agree only for a short distance

**Answer: A.**
- B: An empty truck and lowered forks don't change the rule. A person on the forks can fall or be crushed.
- C: Holding on doesn't make it safe. A sudden stop or a bump can still throw him off.
- D: The risk starts as soon as the truck moves with a person on it. Distance doesn't matter.

---

**5. The heavy pallet**
Jin is about to lift a pallet. The capacity plate on the mast shows the truck's maximum load, and the pallet's label shows a heavier weight. What does Jin do?

A. Lift it a few centimeters to see how the truck handles it
B. Lift it and drive at a crawl
C. Not lift it, leave it where it is, and tell [supervisor/lead]
D. Lift it, but only with the mast tilted back as far as it goes

**Answer: C.**
- A: A test lift can tip the truck or strain the hydraulics, and he'd find out when it's too late.
- B: Driving slowly doesn't fix a truck that's overloaded. It can still tip.
- D: Tilting back doesn't increase capacity. If the load is heavier than the plate allows, don't lift it.

---

**6. The pedestrian**
Lena is driving down an aisle with an empty truck. A coworker wearing headphones is walking toward her in the same aisle. What does Lena do?

A. Sound the horn and keep driving, since she is the one moving and he can see the truck
B. Move over to the far side and drive past slowly
C. Wave and keep driving so he knows she has seen him
D. Stop, make eye contact, and move only once he has seen her and passed or stopped

**Answer: D.**
- A: Pedestrians always have right of way, and a horn can't be heard through headphones.
- B: Driving past without confirming he's seen her risks him stepping into her path.
- C: A wave tells him nothing. She needs eye contact before moving near him.

---

**7. Blocked view**
Omar has stacked a pallet so high that he can't see over it. The route ahead is a flat aisle. What does he do?

A. Drive forward slowly and lean out to check the aisle
B. Drive forward and sound the horn constantly
C. Drive in reverse, looking where he's going
D. Raise the load a bit so he can see underneath it

**Answer: C.**
- A: Leaning out still leaves a blind spot directly ahead where a person could be.
- B: The horn doesn't give him sight. A person he can't see could still be right in front of the forks.
- D: Raising the load takes the forks above the 10 to 15 cm travel height and makes the truck less stable.

---

**8. The ramp**
Sofia is carrying a loaded pallet down a ramp. How should she travel?

A. Forward, with the forks pointing downhill, so she can see where she's going
B. Forward, with the forks pointing uphill
C. In reverse, with the forks pointing downhill
D. In reverse, with the forks pointing uphill

**Answer: D.**
- A: With the load pointing downhill, it can slide off the forks.
- B: This isn't possible going downhill. If she faces forward downhill, the forks point downhill.
- C: Reversing down the ramp puts the forks uphill, so downhill forks mean she's facing the wrong way.

---

**9. Pre-shift check**
During the pre-shift check, Dev finds a small hydraulic leak under the mast. The truck still drives and lifts fine, and the others are in use. What does he do?

A. Wipe up the leak and use the truck, since it works
B. Use it for light loads only
C. Tag it out and report it
D. Mark where the leak is and keep an eye on it during the shift

**Answer: C.**
- A: Wiping hides the evidence. A leak can lead to a failure under load.
- B: A light load doesn't guarantee the hydraulics will hold. A failing truck is a danger at any weight.
- D: Watching it during the shift leaves the problem for the next driver to find. Anything that fails the check gets tagged out and reported.

---

Two notes:
- Use these at the end of the session, then ask the same eight again tomorrow without the answers, with changed details (different names, different loads). That later, changed version is the better evidence they've learned it.
- The speed limits, 10 to 15 cm height, and tag-out rule are all from your session notes. I added no new numbers, and the one placeholder is the person to tell in question 5.
