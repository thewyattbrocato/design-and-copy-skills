**Do B, but change it so the team lead's load drops.** A 40-question multiple choice test can't show whether a rep can handle an objection on a call.

**Why not A:** Objection handling is a spoken skill. It means hearing "your price is too high" or "I need to talk to my spouse" and responding in real time. A multiple choice test measures recognition, not that. A rep can pick the right answer and still freeze on a call. A same-day score also can't show the skill will last. Auto-scoring scales well, but the score doesn't tell you what you need to know.

**Why B works:** It has reps produce the response and get feedback right away. It also gives you observed performance, which is the only real evidence for this skill.

**Making B fit a stretched team lead**

- **Use pairs or trios for most of the practice.** The lead runs one live role-play per rep, about 20 minutes each, and reps practice the other rounds with each other. That's roughly 5 hours of lead time for 15 reps, not 15. Reps use a one-page rubric and a short script card of objections, so peer rounds still give feedback.
- **Give the lead a short rubric.** Use 3 to 4 observable items, for example: acknowledges the concern before answering, asks one clarifying question, responds to the actual objection, and proposes a next step. Feedback then takes two minutes, not ten.
- **Put a worked example first.** Show a recorded or live good call before reps start, so they aren't discovering the skill from scratch.
- **Use the prep time to practice.** Reps should draft and say their responses aloud, not reread materials.
- **Bring it back later.** One session can't certify the skill. Add a short follow-up on a later day with mixed objections and changed details, such as a different customer or a new twist on the objection. That follow-up is your mastery check.

**Handling your VP**

Scale is a fair concern, but a test that scales and measures the wrong thing doesn't help. You can offer A's strengths in a smaller role: a short, scenario-based quiz (a named customer, a specific objection, "what do you say next?") as pre-work or a later retrieval check. Don't make it the main assessment. For the VP, tie success to a metric you already track, such as [current conversion rate on objection calls] or [ramp time to first close]. Compare reps who went through B with earlier cohorts, or with whoever skipped a round, once you have a few weeks of data.

**Two things to settle before you start**

- Define what "good" sounds like, with the rubric and a few example responses. Without that, feedback will vary by person.
- If reps are failing on calls because of pricing, lead quality, or a confusing process, training won't fix that. Check this first.
