**Do B, but cut the team lead's load so it's workable.**

**Why not A:** A multiple-choice test measures whether a rep can recognize the right answer to an objection. It doesn't measure whether they can say it, live, to a skeptical homeowner. For objection handling, that gap is the whole job. A also uses the full 2 hours on a weak signal. Its scalability is real, but it scales a measurement that doesn't predict performance.

**The real cost of B:** 15 reps × 60 minutes of role-play is about 15 hours of team lead time, plus feedback. That's heavy but finite, and it's spread over a week or two rather than all at once. You can shrink it:

- **Have the lead run only one or two of the three role-plays per rep.** Reps can do the others in triads (seller, buyer, observer) using a short rubric and a script of the top 5–6 Halvorsen objections, such as cost, "I'll wait for rebates," roof concerns, and "I need to talk to my spouse." That brings the lead's time down to roughly 5–8 hours.
- **Make the lead's session the final one.** Reps arrive having already practiced, so the lead's feedback goes to refinement instead of basics.
- **Add a short knowledge check, 10 questions at most, before the role-plays.** It's auto-scored, so it covers the part of A your VP cares about (scale, a results page, a record). It also catches reps who don't know the product facts, so the lead doesn't spend live time on that.
- **Consider recording the role-plays.** The lead can then review a few at 1.5× speed instead of attending all of them.

**For your VP:** the scalable piece is still there, as a pre-check and a rubric-scored record for each rep. The live practice is what makes new reps better at objections, and 15 reps is small enough to do it properly. If the lead's time truly can't stretch, borrow a senior rep or a manager to cover some of the sessions before falling back to A alone.
