**Do B, with changes so it doesn't swamp the team lead. Don't do A as the objection-handling assessment.**

**Why not A.** Objection handling is something reps *say* live. A multiple-choice test checks whether they can recognize a good response. It doesn't check whether they can produce one, and a score of 34/40 can't show that. It also only measures what's easy to auto-score, which is policy and definition recall. It scales, but the thing it scales is the wrong evidence.

**Why B, and the fix for the lead's time.** Role-play is the right activity because it's the actual skill. But 15 reps × 3 × 20 min = 15 hours of live time. Ways to cut that:

1. **Run it in triads.** Rep, customer, observer, rotating. The lead floats and gives feedback to one group at a time, or only on the final round. Live lead time falls sharply, and reps get more practice reps, since observers also learn.
2. **Give the observer a short rubric.** Three or four things anyone could score, such as: acknowledged the concern before answering, asked one question to find the real objection, gave a specific response, and proposed a next step. Peers can give "quick feedback" with that.
3. **Use the lead for the last round only.** Reps do rounds 1–2 in triads. Round 3 is with the lead as the check, scored on the same rubric.
4. **Use a small job aid.** Put the top objections and the company's approved facts (pricing, incentives, financing terms: [fill in from your materials]) beside them during prep. Reps shouldn't have to memorize facts they'll look up on the job.
5. **Stagger cohorts** (e.g. 3 groups of 5) so the lead isn't on for two hours straight.

**What to keep from A.** A short auto-scored quiz (10 scenario questions, not 40 definition questions) can work as prep or a follow-up. Each question would be a named rep and a customer's actual line, asking "what do you say next?" That satisfies the VP's wish for scale and a results page, and it gives the lead a list of who struggled with what before the role-plays start.

**Making the case to your VP.** B's weakness is that it needs the lead's time. The triad design addresses it. Also, "scales" isn't worth much if the result is a test score that doesn't predict performance on calls. If you can, compare [current close rate or objection-stage drop-off] for this cohort with earlier cohorts after 30 to 60 days. That's better evidence than either option's completion numbers.

**One caveat.** One session on one day doesn't establish that reps can do this. Plan a short repeat a few days or a week later, with different objections and customer details, even if it's only 10 minutes in pairs. That later session is the real check.

I made two assumptions: the reps are new to your product's objections, and the team lead is the only person who can coach. If a senior rep or two could take on observer or coach roles, the lead's load drops further.
