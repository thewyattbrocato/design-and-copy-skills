I'd go with B, but change it so the team lead isn't running all of it.

**Why not A:** Objection handling is something reps have to do live, under pressure, and a multiple-choice test only checks whether they can recognize a good answer. A rep can score 95% and still freeze when a prospect says "my neighbor's panels never paid off." A results page also gives the VP a number that looks like readiness without measuring it. A does scale, but it scales a measurement of the wrong thing.

**The real cost of B:** 15 reps × 60 minutes of live role-play is about 15 hours of team lead time, plus feedback. That's a lot for someone who's stretched, but it's spread over the cohort, not one day. Here are three ways to cut it:

1. **Peers play the prospect.** Run the role-plays in triads: one rep sells, one plays the customer from a scripted objection card, and one observes with a simple rubric (acknowledged the concern, asked a question, reframed, asked for next step). The team lead sits in on only the third round, or on one round per rep. That cuts their time by roughly 60–70%.
2. **Record the sessions.** Reps record role-plays on a video call. The team lead reviews a few minutes each, async, and gives written feedback. This is less effective than live feedback, but it's much cheaper.
3. **Use a short auto-scored check for facts only.** Ten questions on pricing, incentives, financing, and net metering, done as pre-work. This gives your VP the scalable piece and a results page. It also frees the live time for practice instead of explaining facts.

**What I'd tell the VP:** "The test tells us who knows the answers. The role-plays tell us who can deliver them. We're keeping a quick auto-scored knowledge check for scale, and using the live time on the skill that affects close rates."

If the team lead truly can't spare any time, a fallback is peer-only role-plays with the rubric, plus the team lead reviewing the two or three reps the rubric flags as weakest. It's still better than a 40-question test.
