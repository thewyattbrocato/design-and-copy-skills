**Go with B, but don't let it land entirely on the team lead.** An MC test checks whether reps can recognize the right answer. Objection handling is a live skill: reps have to respond under pressure, without a menu. A test would mostly tell your VP who memorized the playbook.

The lead-time concern is real, though. Three live 20-minute rounds with feedback for 15 reps is roughly 19 hours of lead time. Cut that with three changes:

1. **Peer-run role-plays.** Pair reps (or group them in threes) and give each one a shared objection card and a short scoring rubric: did they acknowledge, ask a question, answer with the approved proof point, and close the next step. Reps score each other.
2. **Lead joins one round per rep.** The lead runs or observes one live round and gives feedback on it. That's about 25 minutes per rep, or about 6 hours total, spread across the onboarding window.
3. **Record the peer rounds** if you can, so the lead can spot-check a few later and calibrate the peer scoring.

This also gives your VP something measurable. The rubric scores are a scalable, auditable record of who can handle objections, which is more useful than a test score.

If the lead truly can't give even one live round per rep, the fallback is to have a senior rep or a previously certified rep run the live rounds, with the lead reviewing a sample. Don't fall back to the MC test as the only measure of this skill.
