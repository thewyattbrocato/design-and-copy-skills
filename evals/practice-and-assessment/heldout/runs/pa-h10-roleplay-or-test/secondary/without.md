**Go with B, but change the format so the team lead isn't running all 45 role-plays alone.**

Objection handling is a performance skill. A 40-question test can show that a rep knows the right response to "your panels are too expensive," but not whether they can say it under pressure on a live call. Reps who score well on A could still freeze on the phone. The VP's scaling concern is fair for knowledge checks, though, so keep A as a short supplement, not the main event.

**Cost of B as written:** 3 role-plays × 20 min × 15 reps is about 15 hours of team-lead time, plus feedback. That's the real constraint, so cut it down:

1. **Peer pairs for most reps.** Pair reps and have them run two of the three role-plays with each other, using a one-page scoring rubric (did they acknowledge, ask a clarifying question, respond with proof, close the loop). Peers can do this without the lead.
2. **Lead runs one live round per rep.** That's 15 × 20 min, about 5 hours, and it's the round that counts. The lead gives the quick feedback there.
3. **Group the live rounds.** Have the lead run 3–4 reps at a time with the others observing and scoring. That cuts the lead's time further and the observers learn from each other's objections.
4. **Add a 10–15 question quiz** (the auto-scored piece your VP likes) as a gate before the role-plays, to confirm reps know the product facts and objection frameworks. It's cheap and it takes the VP's concern seriously without making it the whole test.

If the VP still pushes back, the pitch is: A tells you who memorized the answers, B tells you who can use them on a call. Ask the team lead how many hours they can give before you commit, since that number decides how much of step 1 and step 3 you need.
