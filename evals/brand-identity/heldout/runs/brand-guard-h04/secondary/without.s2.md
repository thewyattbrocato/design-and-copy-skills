I'd keep the core identity. The sign, bags, and receipts are doing work you can't buy from a designer: 41 years of recognition on one corner. A rebrand puts that at risk to fix a feeling, and the gradient icon is a poor fit for a hardware store whose look is safety orange, heavy type, and black.

The marketing hire may still be right that something needs updating, so I'd split the question:

- **Is the look hurting business?** Ask a few regulars and some newer customers what they think the name and sign say about the store. If people say "solid, reliable, old-school," that's an asset. If newcomers say it looks like it's closed or they didn't realize it was open, that's a real problem worth solving.
- **Where does "dated" actually show up?** If the weak spots are the website, Google listing, social posts, or email, fix those. Those are the places where a modern look helps, and they don't touch the sign.
- **What would a refresh cost?** Replacing the sign, vehicles, bags, and receipt stock is expensive and hard to undo. Updating digital assets is cheap and reversible.

If you want to modernize without losing the identity, I'd keep the slab serif and the orange-and-black palette, perhaps refine the letterforms or spacing, and skip the gradient entirely. Test any change on the website or a bag run before it goes on the building.

So: not a rebrand, but a targeted look at the digital presence, and a quick check with customers before deciding anything about the physical signage. If you tell me what the marketing hire's concern is based on (feedback, competitor comparison, sales data), I can help you weigh it.
