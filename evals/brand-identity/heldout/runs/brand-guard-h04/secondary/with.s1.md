**I wouldn't rebrand on the "looks dated" argument alone.** A heavy all-caps slab in safety orange is a strong, distinctive mark, and it fits a hardware store. It's also what 41 years of customers have learned to find on a corner and trust on a bag. "Dated" is a judgment about style. Recognition is what you actually have.

**Find out what problem the hire is trying to solve.** Ask them to name it specifically. Common answers have different fixes:

- **Weak online presence or discovery:** fix the website, Google Business profile, and photos. The logo is rarely the bottleneck.
- **Worn or faded signage:** repaint or re-fabricate the same design. That's maintenance, not a rebrand.
- **Want to attract younger DIY customers:** a refresh in the same spirit (cleaner kerning, a web-friendly version, a modern secondary font for social and ads) can reach them without losing the sign.
- **Feeling small next to big-box stores:** that's a positioning question. A new font doesn't answer it.

**On the gradient icon, I'd push back hard.** Gradients don't survive the places this mark has to live: receipt thermal printers, one-color stamps, embroidery on work shirts, a single-color sign, a photocopied flyer. Flat safety orange is the feature that makes the mark read as hardware. A gradient would make it look like a tech startup.

**Costs of a full rebrand are real:** new sign fabrication, bag and receipt stock, vehicle graphics, website and social updates, and the risk that regulars who know you by the sign wonder what changed. Only some of that comes back as new customers.

**A cheap way to settle it:**
1. Mock up the current mark, a clean modern-sans version, and a refined version of the slab, on a bag, a receipt, a sign, and a phone screen.
2. Show them to 20 or so regulars and ask which one they'd trust for a plumbing fitting or a bag of concrete. Also ask what, if anything, they think looks dated.
3. Let the results decide. If regulars are fine with it, the question is about marketing channels, not the logo.

My recommendation: keep the mark. If the hire can point to a concrete problem the logo causes, make a light refinement that keeps the slab, the caps, and the orange, and put the rest of the effort into the channels where new customers actually find you.
