**Recommendation: don't rebrand. Refine the mark you have.**

Your mark is already doing the hard job. Forty-one years of recognition in one town is hard to buy back, and a heavy all-caps slab in safety orange on white is a sign-and-bag tradition that reads as "hardware" at a glance. Nothing in your description says it misleads, fails at small sizes, or resembles another business. "Looks dated" is a taste judgment, and novelty alone isn't a reason to replace a mark people know.

The two specific proposals don't hold up:

- **A gradient icon is an effect, not an idea.** It usually looks muddy in one color, on receipts, on a fax, or on a stamp, and it fails at the 16-32 px sizes an app icon or favicon needs.
- **A modern sans-serif would erase the thing people recognize.** Plenty of hardware and lumber signage uses bold slab serifs for the same reason yours works: it looks sturdy and permanent.

**What I'd do instead:**

1. **Redraw, don't replace.** Keep the slab, the caps, and the orange. Tune letter spacing by eye, sharpen any worn corners, and make sure the letters stay open at small sizes.
2. **Make a simplified small version** for favicons, social avatars, and the app icon: heavier, fewer details, perhaps just a bold "P" in a block. This addresses the real digital problem without changing the sign.
3. **Test the current mark first.** Put it at 32 px, in solid black, and reversed on a dark background. Where it fails in those tests, that's what to fix.
4. **Ask customers before deciding.** Show regulars the current mark next to the proposed one and ask which feels like Pinegate. I can't tell you the result in advance, and that answer is the evidence that should drive the decision.

**What would change my view:** the mark fails at digital sizes even after refinement, it's confusable with another local business, or the store's offer has shifted so far that the name and look no longer describe it. Any of those would justify a fuller rethink.
