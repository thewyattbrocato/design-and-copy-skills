**I wouldn't rebrand. I'd refresh what you have and leave the identity alone.**

**Why keep it**
- **People already know it.** After 41 years, the orange slab-serif name is how the town finds you. "Looks dated" isn't a reason to throw that away. A rebrand makes sense when a mark misleads, fails at the sizes you need, is easily confused with a competitor's, or blocks where the business wants to go. I haven't seen any of those here.
- **It fits the trade.** A heavy slab serif in all caps and safety orange suggests sturdy tools, signage and workwear. A generic modern sans-serif could belong to a fintech app or a meal kit.
- **It already works in one color.** You have the orange on white and a plain black version for receipts. A gradient icon would turn muddy on a receipt, a stamp, an embroidered apron or a one-color sign. Gradients also aren't an idea. They're a style that will look dated in a few years too.

**What to do instead**
1. **Redraw the wordmark cleanly.** Tune the spacing and fix any uneven edges from decades of reproduction. Keep the same orange, the same slab and all caps.
2. **Add a small-size version.** A heavier, simpler mark for favicons, social avatars and phone screens. This is probably what your marketing hire actually needs, and it can be a solid "P" in the same slab or a tight "PINEGATE" block. It shouldn't be a gradient.
3. **Write down the rules.** Cover the orange, the black and a reversed white-on-dark version, minimum sizes, and horizontal and stacked lockups. Right now the identity lives only in people's habits.
4. **Modernize the surroundings.** Use cleaner layouts, better photography, and a modern sans-serif for body text and the website, next to the slab logo. Most of what feels dated is usually the layout and materials, not the logo.

**How to settle it with your hire**
Ask 20 to 30 customers, at the register or in a short survey, what comes to mind when they see the sign and bag. If many say "reliable" or "the local place," the recognition is worth more than a fresh look. If younger or online customers don't see you as a hardware store at all, that's a real problem. Even then, fix it with the refresh above and a stronger online presence, not a new logo.

I'm assuming you're a local, trusted, mostly in-person business. If you're planning a big move online or a second location in a new area, tell me, because that could change this advice.
