**I'd say no to a full rebrand. A modest refresh might be worth considering.**

**Why not rebrand**
- **Forty-one years of recognition is the asset.** In a local business, people don't have to read the sign to know it's you. A new look throws that away, and a new logo can't buy it back.
- **The identity suits a hardware store.** A heavy slab serif in safety-orange says sturdy, practical, and trustworthy, which is what customers want from a hardware store. Safety-orange on white is also distinctive. Most competitors use red, blue, or green.
- **"Looks dated" isn't a business problem by itself.** Ask what the problem is: Are you losing younger customers? Is online search or social media underperforming? Are the signs or bags faded and worn? If your marketing hire can't point to a specific loss, the case is weak.
- **A modern sans-serif with a gradient icon is the generic look.** It's what every new app and startup uses. It would make you look like everyone else, and a gradient is hard to reproduce on a sign, a bag, or a receipt.

**What I'd do instead**
- **Tidy up what you have.** Redraw the wordmark cleanly as vector files, fix the spacing, and lock the exact orange value. Make sure it's consistent across the sign, bags, website, and social media. Old brands often look dated because of inconsistent or poorly reproduced logos, not because of the design.
- **Modernize the surroundings.** Update the website, Google Business profile, photography, in-store signage, and social presence. That's where "dated" usually shows up.
- **Optionally, add a simple secondary mark.** A small icon, such as a monogram "P" in the same slab and orange, would work for social avatars and app icons. It would extend the brand without replacing it.

**How to handle your hire**
Ask them what specific problem the rebrand would solve and how you'd measure it. If they have real evidence, like customer feedback or trouble with online visibility, take it seriously. If it's mostly taste, a refresh channels their energy without risking what you've built.

It's your call, but the heritage is worth more than a trend, and the trend will date faster than the slab serif has.
