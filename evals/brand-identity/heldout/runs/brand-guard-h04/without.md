My recommendation is not to do a full rebrand. A modest refresh might be worth it, but only for specific, practical reasons.

**Why I'd keep it**
- **Recognition is the asset.** After 41 years, people recognize the orange slab serif before they read it. A rebrand throws that away and gets you nothing in return.
- **It fits the category.** Heavy slab serif in safety orange reads as sturdy, local, and trustworthy, which is what people want from a hardware store. "Dated" and "established" are often the same thing, and here that works in your favor.
- **Your customers aren't the audience for a modern look.** A gradient icon and a generic sans-serif would make you look like every app and startup. The corner hardware store with a distinctive sign stands out.
- **Rebranding costs more than the design fee.** You'd pay for new signage, bags, vehicles, uniforms, receipts, and web and social profiles. You'd also confuse some longtime customers.

**What the marketing hire may be right about**
"Dated" often means the execution is tired, not the identity. Check for these:
- Is the logo crisp at small sizes, such as on a phone, a social avatar, or a favicon? A name in a wide slab serif can be hard to read when it's tiny.
- Do you have a compact version, such as a "P" monogram or a stacked layout, for square spaces?
- Are the colors consistent everywhere, or has the orange drifted over the years?
- Is the website, social media, or in-store signage looking neglected? That's usually what actually makes a business feel dated.

**What I'd do**
1. Keep the wordmark, the typeface, and the orange. Have a designer clean up the files and spacing and write down the exact colors.
2. Add a simple square or monogram version for digital use.
3. Put the marketing hire's energy into the website, Google Business profile, social media, and in-store displays.
4. Leave the gradient out. It's a trend that will look dated sooner than your slab serif will.

Ask the marketing hire what problem a rebrand would solve, and what number would show it worked. If the answer is "it looks old," that's taste, not a business case. If it's something concrete, like "we're losing younger customers online" or "the logo is unreadable on mobile," you can fix that with a refresh and keep the identity.
