My recommendation is no full rebrand. Do a light refresh only if there's a specific problem to fix.

**Why I'd keep it**
- **Recognition is your asset.** After 41 years, people recognize the slab serif in safety orange without reading it. A rebrand throws that away, and a new brand has to earn it back.
- **It fits the category.** A heavy slab serif says sturdy, no-nonsense, and been here forever. That's what people want from a hardware store. "Dated" and "established" are often the same thing seen from two sides.
- **A modern sans-serif plus a gradient icon is the generic look.** It's what every app, startup, and DTC brand uses. It would make you look like everyone else, and it works against a local store's biggest advantage, which is that it's been there a long time.
- **Switching has real costs.** You'd need a new sign, bags, receipts, vehicles, shelf tags, and web and social profiles. Gradients also reproduce badly on cheap signage, one-color bags, and receipt printers. Your plain black version works well because it's simple.

**When a change would make sense**
- You're losing customers, especially younger ones, and you can tie it to how the brand looks. Ask the marketing hire for evidence beyond "looks dated."
- You're changing what the business is, such as moving into a different market or opening in new towns where the name isn't known.
- The current logo has practical problems. Examples are a poor online or social presence, a bad small-size version, or a low-resolution file.

**What I'd do**
1. Keep the name, the slab serif, and the orange.
2. If marketing wants something to work on, have them clean up the existing logo. That means redrawn vector files, consistent spacing, an app-icon or social-avatar version (maybe a bold "P" in the orange), and a tidier website. That gets you "modern" without losing the recognition.
3. Ask the new hire what problem the rebrand solves and how you'd measure it. A good marketer should have an answer. If they don't, that tells you something.

You know your customers better than I do. If foot traffic and loyalty are healthy, the brand is doing its job.
