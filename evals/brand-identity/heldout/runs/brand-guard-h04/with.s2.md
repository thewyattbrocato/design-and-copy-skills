**Verdict: don't replace it. Keep the mark and make small fixes where it falls short.**

**Why**
- Forty-one years of local recognition is the asset. A heavy slab serif in safety orange reads as "hardware" and "dependable" to people in your town. "Looks dated" is a taste judgment, not evidence that the mark fails.
- Safety orange on white is also distinctive. A modern sans-serif and a gradient icon are what most new businesses and apps use, so you'd swap something recognizable for something generic.
- A gradient icon is the weakest part of the proposal. It usually turns to mud in one color, and you already need a one-color version for receipts. It also gets hard to read at favicon size.

**What I'd change instead (redraw, not replace)**
1. **Tune the lettering.** Clean up the spacing, even out the stroke weights, and make one custom detail, such as a cut or a reshaped letter, so it looks designed rather than typed.
2. **Make a small-size version.** Use a heavier, slightly looser cut for a favicon, social avatar and receipt header. Check that it reads at 16–32 pixels.
3. **Fill the gaps in the set.** Make a stacked version and a symbol-only version (a "P" or a "PG" cut from the same slab). Also make a reversed version, white on orange or on black, for dark grounds.
4. **Add a few system rules.** Fix the orange as one exact value, pick one supporting typeface for body text, and set layout rules. That makes the brand feel current without touching the name.

**Test before deciding**
Ask 20–30 customers at the counter, or run a short poll on your social pages. Show them the current logo next to a modern sans-serif mock-up. Ask which one says "Pinegate" and which one they'd trust to know their hardware. I won't predict the result, but if people pick the old one, that settles it with your marketing hire on evidence rather than opinion.

**When replacing would be right**
- The mark fails at needed sizes and can't be fixed by redrawing.
- It's confusable with another local business.
- You're changing what the business is, for example moving online or toward a different customer.
