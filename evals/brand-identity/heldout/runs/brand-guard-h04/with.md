**Don't rebrand. Refine what you have.** "Looks dated" isn't a reason to replace a mark that a whole town recognizes. A heavy slab serif in safety-orange on white fits a hardware store. It reads as sturdy and established, which is what you want people to think. A modern sans-serif and a gradient icon would make you look like every other new business, and you'd lose 41 years of recognition for nothing.

**Evidence**
- Everyone in town knows the sign and bags. You'd be giving up that recognition for novelty.
- The meaning still fits. Slab serif, caps and safety-orange suit the trade.
- Gradients are the weak part of the proposal. They turn to mud in one color, on receipts, on a stamp and on a small favicon. Your plain black version works because the mark is flat and solid. A gradient icon would break that.
- Hardware is a trade where plain and trusted wins. Big-box competitors already look "modern."

**What I'd actually do (a refresh, not a replacement)**
1. **Redraw the wordmark cleanly.** Fix the spacing by eye, sharpen the edges, and make sure it holds up at small sizes. Add one custom touch, such as a cut or a reshaped letter, so it isn't just a stock font.
2. **Make a heavier, simplified small version for icons and favicons.** "PINEGATE" in full caps is wide, so a compact monogram, such as a "P" in the same slab, would work for avatars, social media and the website tab.
3. **Keep the orange exactly as people know it.** Define it once (hex, Pantone) and use it consistently on a white ground and reversed on dark.
4. **Set up the versions:** horizontal, stacked, symbol-only, plus orange, black and white. Add clear-space and minimum-size rules.
5. **Modernize around the mark, not in it.** Use cleaner layouts, better photography, a consistent bag and signage system and a good website. That is where "dated" usually comes from, not the logo.

**Next test, before anyone decides**
Ask 20 to 30 customers, at the counter or in a quick bag insert, what they think of when they see the sign. Also ask the marketing hire what specifically feels dated. If it's the website or the layouts, those can be fixed without touching the logo. If real evidence shows the logo fails somewhere, such as in a size or on a surface, fix that one thing.

- If you ever do want a fresh look, the safer way is a time-boxed campaign variant, with the core mark staying recognizable.
