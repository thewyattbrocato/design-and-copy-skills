I'd refine it. Keep the dog, and have a professional redraw it.

**Why:** The dog is the one thing you have that a new clinic can't buy. Twelve years of neighborhood recognition is brand equity. Your partner's real concern is that the logo looks amateur, and that can be fixed without throwing out the dog. The wobbly sketch and the mismatched rounded font cause most of the amateur look. The dog's concept doesn't.

**What I'd do:**

1. **Hire a logo designer for a redraw, not a redesign.** Give them the original sketch and say: "Keep this dog's pose, the one raised ear, and the personality. Clean up the linework and make it work at every size." Expect a few hundred dollars to a couple thousand, depending on who you hire.
2. **Keep the lopsided charm on purpose.** The raised ear is the memorable part. If the redraw turns it into a symmetrical, generic dog, you've lost what you were trying to keep. Tell the designer to treat that as a requirement.
3. **Simplify the line.** Use one consistent stroke weight, with no sketchy overlapping lines. A little hand-drawn warmth is fine, but it should look deliberate.
4. **Replace the font.** Pick a typeface that suits the cleaned-up dog, such as a friendly, sturdy sans-serif or a soft slab. The font is the cheapest way to look more professional.
5. **Ask for a full set of files.** You need vector files, one-color and reversed versions, a small icon version (just the dog) for social media and signs, and a horizontal and stacked layout. With three locations you'll use all of these.
6. **Add the sketch's story.** Something like "Since 2014" or a short line on your website about the kid who drew it turns the logo's history into a selling point. It also helps your partner, because the new locations get to borrow the original's credibility.
7. **Roll it out in stages.** Start the new logo at the two new locations and update the original clinic's signs, forms, and website over the following months. Neighbors will see the same dog, just tidier, and won't think you've changed.

**To bring your partner on board:** Present the redraw as the clean, professional logo they want. Show them a side-by-side of the old and new versions so they can see that the dog looks better and is still recognizable. If your partner still wants to drop the dog after that, ask the designer for one fully new concept to compare. I'd bet the redrawn dog wins.

**One caveat:** Ask your friend and their kid (now an adult) before you commercialize the drawing further. It's a courtesy, and you also want to be sure about the ownership of the original artwork. A short written note saying you have permission to use and adapt it is enough. It's also a nice gesture to credit them in the "story" version.
