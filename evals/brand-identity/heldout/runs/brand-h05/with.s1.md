**Refine it. Keep the dog and redraw it cleanly.**

**Why:** The neighborhood already knows the dog, and that recognition took twelve years to build. A new logo would throw it away just as you open in two new areas, where you want the clinic to feel like the same trusted place. Your partner's real complaint is probably that it looks amateur and won't hold up on signs, scrubs, and small screens. A redraw fixes that without a replacement.

**What I'd do:**

1. **Trace the dog and keep what makes it that dog.** That means the pose, the head tilt, and especially the one ear up. I'd redraw it as one confident, even-weight line, or as a solid shape, with the wobble smoothed out but a little hand-drawn warmth left in. If it ends up looking like stock clip art, the redraw has failed.
2. **Make a heavier small version.** A pencil sketch has thin lines that vanish at 16–32 pixels, on a favicon, social avatar, or stitched on a shirt. The small version gets fewer details and thicker strokes. The dog alone, without the name, is your icon.
3. **Check it in black on white, then reversed on dark.** If the dog reads in both, color is a later choice. I'd keep whatever color people already associate with the clinic.
4. **Keep the rounded font's friendliness but fix the lettering.** Re-space it by eye and make one small custom tweak, so it isn't a stock font. The dog and the letters should have the same line weight.
5. **Build three lockups.** The three are horizontal, stacked, and dog-only. Each location name goes on a separate line under the main mark. The logo itself stays identical across all three clinics, which is what makes them feel like one family.

**Test it before you commit.** I don't have evidence that the dog is as recognized as you feel it is, so check. Show 10–15 clients the old and redrawn versions and ask which they'd say is "our clinic." Your partner can see for themselves whether the redraw keeps the recognition.

**To get your partner on board:** Present the redraw as the clean, professional logo they asked for, not as a compromise. Show it side by side with the old one, on a building sign, a business card, and a phone icon. A replacement is only warranted if the dog turns out to be confusable with another local clinic's mark or can't be made to work small. Neither seems likely from what you've described.
