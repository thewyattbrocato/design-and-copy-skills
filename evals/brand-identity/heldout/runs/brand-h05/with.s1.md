**Refine it. Keep the dog.**

Your partner is right that three locations need a cleaner, more consistent look. That's a reason to redraw the dog, not to replace it. Twelve years of neighborhood recognition is the most valuable thing the logo has, and you can't buy it back. The wobble is also part of why people remember the dog. A vet clinic that feels warm and personal beats one that feels like a corporate chain, especially when you open new locations.

**What I'd do**

1. **Redraw the same dog.** Keep the pose, the head tilt, and the one ear up. Trace it as a solid shape with a confident, even line. The ear and the tilt are what people recognize, so they stay exactly as they are. I'd keep a trace of the original wobble in the line so it still looks hand-drawn, just steadier.
2. **Make a heavier small version.** A pencil sketch has thin lines that vanish at favicon, social avatar, and embroidered-scrub sizes. Draw a bolder, simpler dog for those uses. It should be a separate drawing, not the full logo shrunk down.
3. **Fix the type, not the dog.** The rounded font is the most dated part, and it's probably a stock font. Pick a sturdier rounded or semi-rounded face with the same friendliness. Match its weight to the dog's line, and adjust one letter so it's yours. Check that the name reads well on its own.
4. **Build the full set.** That means horizontal, stacked, and dog-only versions, each in one color, in white on dark, and in full color. Add a short guideline sheet so the new locations can't drift. Include a plain rule for signs, uniforms, and the website.
5. **Keep your current colors** if the old logo had a recognizable one. Change one thing at a time.

**How to settle it with your partner**

Don't argue taste. Run a quick test. Show 15–20 clients the old and new marks with the clinic name covered, and ask what they recognize. If most people say "that's the dog from [clinic]," you have your answer. If almost nobody does, I'd reconsider, but I doubt that's what you'll find.

I'd also give your partner the thing they want: a fully polished, consistent identity across all three locations. The only thing that stays is the one element customers already love.
