**Refine it.** Keep the dog.

Here's why, and what I'd do.

**Why refine**

- Twelve years of neighborhood recognition is brand equity you can't buy. People already associate that one-eared dog with your clinic and with trust.
- A vet clinic sells warmth and care, not corporate polish. A hand-drawn dog does that job better than a slick geometric mark would.
- With three locations, a distinctive mark matters more. Generic "clean professional" vet logos (paw print, silhouette, a heart) are everywhere, and yours isn't.
- Your partner's real concern is probably that the logo looks amateur and won't scale across signage, web, and uniforms. That's a production problem, and you can fix it without replacing the character.

**What I'd do**

1. **Hire a logo designer to redraw the dog, not reinvent it.** The brief is: "Keep the pose, the single raised ear, and the personality. Clean up the line, make it work as vector art." Expect a few hundred to a couple thousand dollars for a solid freelancer.
2. **Keep the wobble, but make it deliberate.** Smooth the line weight and fix the proportions, but keep a slightly hand-drawn feel. A cleaner pencil-style or brush-style line reads as intentional, not sloppy.
3. **Replace the font.** The rounded font is probably the part that looks dated. Pair the dog with a more current typeface that's still friendly. This gives your partner most of the "professional" look they want.
4. **Build a real system.** Get a one-color version, a small icon-only version (just the dog) for social avatars and app icons, and a stacked and horizontal lockup. Add a consistent color palette. This is what makes three locations look like one brand.
5. **Credit the artist.** Tell your friend's kid, who is now an adult, what you're doing. Ask them to be part of it, or at least send them the result. It's a good story for your website and the new locations. Also check that you have their permission to use and modify the drawing, since a quick written note now avoids ownership questions later.
6. **Transition gently.** Put "Same dog, fresh look" on the new signage and social posts so the neighborhood sees an upgrade, not a replacement.

**How to pitch it to your partner**

Don't argue "sentiment versus professionalism." Say: "We're not throwing out our most recognizable asset. We're making it look as good as it deserves to, and building a system around it." Show them a few examples of well-known brands that modernized a hand-drawn mascot without dumping it.

If the designer's refined version comes back and you both still dislike it, then you can talk about replacement. But I'd bet it won't come to that.
