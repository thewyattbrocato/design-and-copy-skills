**Refine. Keep the dog and redraw it.**

The dog is the one asset you can't buy. Twelve years of neighbors recognizing it is recognition you'd have to spend years and money to rebuild with a new mark. A "clean, professional" look is a drawing problem, and a redraw solves it without replacing the mark.

**What I'd do:**

1. **Redraw the dog as a flat vector.** Keep the pose and the one ear up, since that's the gesture people remember. Remove the wobble, make the outline consistent, and drop any sketch texture or hairlines.
2. **Make a simplified small version.** Fine detail will vanish at sign or favicon size, so make a heavier version with fewer lines for those uses.
3. **Redraw the wordmark, don't just retype it.** Keep the rounded character, but set the spacing by eye and adjust one or two letters so it's custom and matches the dog's weight.
4. **Pick one solid color** that's close to the original if there is one, and use it reversed on dark backgrounds. Keep effects like gradients and shadows out of the core mark.
5. **Build a lockup set for all three locations:** horizontal, stacked, and symbol-only, with a minimum size. Location names go on a separate line, not inside the mark, so the shared brand stays consistent.

**Evidence and the next test:**
- I'm assuming the current logo isn't misleading or hard to read at the sizes you use. If it is, that changes the answer.
- Before you commit, show the redrawn dog next to the old one to 10 to 15 longtime clients and ask who it is. If they recognize the new one as the same clinic, you're done. If not, the redraw has drifted too far.
- Check the redraw at 32 pixels and in one color. If it reads there, it will read on the sign and the website.

**Assumptions:** I don't know your current colors, the exact sketch file, or whether you have a brand guide. Also, a trademark search should happen before you put the name on new signs, since a shared name across locations is a larger investment. That's a lawyer's call, not a design one.
