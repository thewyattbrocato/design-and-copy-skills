**I'd refine it and keep the dog.** Redraw the sketch as a clean vector mark, keep the pose and the up ear, and pair it with a more professional wordmark.

**Why refine:** The dog is the one part of the brand your neighbors already recognize, and it still fits a vet clinic. The problem is execution: wobbly lines, a sketch that won't reproduce small, and probably no reliable one-color version. Those are fixable without losing what people know. Replacing it would trade a known asset for a generic one, and a clean stock-style dog would look like every other clinic.

**What I'd do:**

1. **Redraw the dog in clean, solid shapes.** Keep the silhouette, head tilt, and the raised ear. Simplify the interior details. Judge it in solid black first, then at 32 px (sign and favicon size), then reversed on dark.
2. **Make a heavier small version** for the smallest uses, such as the website favicon and a phone-screen avatar, where fine lines would disappear.
3. **Rework the name, don't replace it.** Keep the rounded feel, but have a designer adjust a few letters so it looks custom and matches the dog's weight. Make sure the name reads clearly in a glance on a sign.
4. **Build a simple system for three locations.** The dog is the shared mark, the clinic name is the shared wordmark, and each location gets a small line of text beneath it (for example, "Northside"). That lets the partner's "one brand" goal and your "keep the dog" goal both work.
5. **Test it with real people.** Show the old sketch and the refined version to a dozen regulars and staff, and ask which one they'd trust with their pet. Put both on the sign mockup for the new locations. Use the answers to decide, not my opinion.

**Two things to check before you commit:**

- **Ownership of the original drawing.** A child drew it, so the copyright may belong to the child or their family, not the clinic. Get a written release or permission from the family before you use a redraw publicly. This is a question for a lawyer if it is not clear.
- **Trademark.** I can't tell you the dog is unique. Ask a trademark attorney to run a search before you register the mark, especially since you're expanding.
