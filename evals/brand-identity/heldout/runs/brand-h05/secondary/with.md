**Refine. Keep the dog and redraw it.**

The dog is the asset. The neighborhood already recognizes it, and a dog for a vet clinic is about as literal and correct as a mark gets. What's wrong is the execution: the wobbly line, the inconsistent weight, and the hand-drawn feel that reads as a school project next to a professional partner's expectations. Those are fixable without losing the image people know.

**What I'd do:**

1. **Trace the dog as a clean vector, keeping the exact pose.** The raised ear is the signature, so keep its angle and silhouette. Smooth the outline, make the stroke weight consistent, and remove any sketch-only detail. Don't add features, a stethoscope, or a heart. Those would be a second idea.

2. **Make a simplified small version.** Strip interior lines (eye, nose, and so on) and keep the head silhouette and the ear. Test it at about 32 px. If the eye or any hairline disappears, drop it from the small version.

3. **Test in solid black first, then reversed.** If the silhouette doesn't read in one color on white and on a dark background, fix the shape before picking any color.

4. **Redraw the wordmark, don't just reuse the rounded font as-is.** Keep the rounded character so it still feels like your clinic, but adjust spacing by eye and reshape at least one letter so the lettering matches the dog's weight and tone.

5. **Build a small lockup set for three locations:**
   - Horizontal (dog + clinic name) for signs and letterhead
   - Stacked for vehicles, windows, and social
   - Dog only for avatars, favicon, and collars
   
   Put the location name (for example, "Oak Street") as a separate line under the wordmark, not inside the mark. That gives you one identity with three addresses.

**What to show your partner:** the redrawn mark alongside the original, both on a sign mockup and a 32 px icon. The comparison makes the case better than an argument does.

**The test that settles it:** show the old and new versions to 10 to 20 regular clients and ask which one is the clinic. If most pick the new one, you have evidence the refinement holds recognition. If they don't, adjust the drawing rather than replacing it.

**Before rolling it out to new locations:** get a basic trademark search on the redrawn mark. Your existing use gives you some footing, but a search is cheap compared with rebranding three locations later.

I worked from your description, not the actual sketch, so the redraw itself still needs to be done from the original file.
