**Refine.** Redraw the dog as a clean vector and keep the one-ear-up pose, the proportions and the friendly expression. Don't replace it.

**Why:** Your neighborhood already knows the dog. That recognition is the most valuable thing the clinic owns, and a new mark would start from zero at three locations. The wobble is what needs fixing. The asymmetric ear is what people remember, so keep it. A clean redraw of the same silhouette can look professional without looking like a different clinic. I haven't seen the sketch, so this assumes the pose and silhouette are distinct enough to carry over.

**What I'd do:**

1. **Redraw the dog as flat, solid vector art.** Keep the pose and silhouette, and simplify the interior lines. Drop any shading or sketch texture.
2. **Set the name in a custom-adjusted rounded face.** Keep the rounded feel you have now, but tune the spacing by eye and reshape one letter so it isn't an untouched stock font.
3. **Make the lockups.** Horizontal (dog left, name right), stacked, and dog-only. Set a minimum size and clear space for each.
4. **Make a small-size version.** Use a heavier, simpler dog head for favicons, collar tags, and avatars. Hairlines will disappear at 16–32 px.
5. **Test in black and white first.** Check the solid-black version, then the reversed version on dark. Pick the color last and keep the original brand color if it still works.
6. **Build a short system for the three locations.** Define the color roles, one type pairing, and the dog as the graphic device. Each location gets a name line under the same mark, so the partner's "one brand" goal is met without losing the dog.

**Test before you commit:** Show the redrawn dog, without the name, to 10–15 longtime clients and ask what clinic it belongs to. If most of them recognize it, the refinement worked. If not, the redraw is too far from the original, so adjust it. I can't give you a recognition figure from my side. That has to come from asking your clients.

**Notes:**
- Check that the name is clear to use at the new locations, and get a trademark search if you plan to register the mark. I can't confirm that from here.
- The one trade-off: a faithful redraw will still look a little folksy. If your partner wants something sleeker, the answer is a tighter redraw of the same dog, not a new dog.
