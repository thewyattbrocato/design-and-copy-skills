**Refine it.** I'd keep the dog and have a designer redraw it as a clean vector illustration.

- **The dog is your equity.** Neighbors already recognize it, and that recognition is hard to buy. A generic "clean professional" mark is what most vet clinics look like, so you'd be trading something distinctive for something interchangeable.
- **The problem is execution, not concept.** The wobbly lines make it look amateur, but that's fixable. Keep the pose, the single raised ear, and the overall silhouette. Smooth the lines, make the stroke weight consistent, and simplify the details so it works at small sizes on a sign, a collar tag, or a phone screen.
- **Three locations need one recognizable anchor.** A redrawn dog gives all three sites a shared identity. A new abstract mark would have to earn recognition from scratch in every neighborhood.
- **Pair it with a more refined wordmark.** Keep the rounded feel of the name, but use a cleaner version of that font or a slightly more polished one. That addresses your partner's "professional" concern without discarding the dog.

Before you pay for the redraw, check the rights. The kid who drew it owns the copyright unless it was assigned to the clinic in writing, and twelve years later that's worth fixing. Ask the family for a simple signed release or assignment. It's a short document, and it protects you when you're putting the logo on three buildings and a website.

Ask the designer for a full-color version, a one-color version, and a reversed version for dark backgrounds. Test the dog at the smallest size you'll use, like a business card corner or a social avatar. If it still reads there, you're done.
