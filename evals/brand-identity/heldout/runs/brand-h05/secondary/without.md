**Refine it.** Keep the dog and redraw it as clean vector art. Don't replace it.

Your partner is right that the current logo doesn't look professional, but the problem is execution, not the concept. Recognition is your biggest asset. People already know that dog, and a new mark with no connection to it would start from zero in a neighborhood where you've been known for twelve years. Two new locations need a logo people can spot from across a parking lot, and this one already does that.

What I'd do:

1. **Hire a designer for a redraw, not a new concept.** Ask for a vector version that keeps the pose, the proportions, and the raised ear. Smooth the wobbly lines and set the proportions evenly. Most of the charm is in the silhouette, so keep that and clean up everything else.
2. **Pair it with a refined version of the rounded type.** Keep the same family or something close, and tighten the spacing and weight so it reads as a deliberate lockup.
3. **Build a simple system for three locations.** A primary lockup, a stacked version, a dog-only icon for signage and social, and a one-color version. Make sure the dog still reads at favicon size and on a van.
4. **Test it with the neighborhood before you commit.** Show the new version next to the old one and see if people recognize it. If they do, you've kept the equity. If they don't, adjust.

Two small things to handle along the way:

- **Credit the kid.** Something like "Original sketch by [name], age [x]" on the About page or in the clinic lobby would mean a lot to the family, and it's a good story for the new locations.
- **Check the ownership.** Since a child made the original, confirm with the family that they're fine with a professionally redrawn version being the official mark. It's almost certainly a yes, but it's worth a short conversation before you print signage in three places.
