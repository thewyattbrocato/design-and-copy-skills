**Refine it.** I'd keep the dog and have a designer redraw it as a clean vector mark.

- **The dog is your equity.** Neighbors already recognize it. A new professional logo would make you look like a new business to the people who already trust you, and a new clinic that opens two locations needs recognition more than a clean slate.
- **Professional doesn't require losing the character.** Most of what reads as "wobbly" is line quality, not the idea. A good designer keeps the silhouette, the up ear, and the pose, then draws them with confident, consistent strokes and a simple color palette.
- **The rounded wordmark is a good match.** Keep that typeface family, clean it up, and pair it with the dog. That gives you a system your partner can scale across three signs, vehicles, and forms.

How I'd do it:

1. **Check ownership first.** Confirm the clinic has the rights to the drawing. Twelve years on, a handwritten agreement may not exist. If it's unclear, get a simple written assignment from the family. It's also a nice gesture to credit the kid who drew it.
2. **Commission a redraw, not a new logo.** Ask for 2–3 versions that keep the ear and pose, in a primary lockup (dog + name) and a standalone dog mark for the new locations.
3. **Test it at real sizes.** Check it at favicon size, on a van, and in black and white. The wobbly sketch probably fails at small sizes, which is a reason to refine it, not drop it.
4. **Show the neighborhood.** Put the old and new side by side in a few spots and see what people say before you commit. If they say "that's our dog," you've kept what matters.

Your partner's goal of a consistent, professional system across three locations is reasonable, and this gets them there while keeping the mark people already know.
