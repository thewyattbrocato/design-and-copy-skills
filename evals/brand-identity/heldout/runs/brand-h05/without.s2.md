**Refine it.** Keep the dog and clean it up. Don't replace it.

Your partner is right that three locations need a consistent, professional mark. But the neighborhood already knows the dog, and that recognition took twelve years to build. A new logo would throw it away just as you open two new locations, which is when you most need people to connect the new sites to the clinic they trust. A hand-drawn dog with one ear up is also more distinctive than most vet logos, which tend to be generic paw prints and silhouettes.

Here is what I'd do:

1. **Hire a designer to redraw the dog, not reinvent it.** Brief them that the one raised ear, the wobbly hand-drawn warmth, and the pose all stay. They should clean up the line weight, fix proportions only where needed, and turn the sketch into a proper vector file. Hand-drawn character is fine in a logo, but the current pencil sketch probably doesn't scale well. It likely blurs on signage and disappears on small things like a favicon, a tag, or an embroidered scrub top.
2. **Replace the font, but keep the rounded feel.** A modern rounded typeface will look more polished and still feel friendly. This is probably where most of your partner's "professional" look comes from.
3. **Build a small system, not just one file.** You need a horizontal layout, a stacked layout, a dog-only icon, a one-color version, and a reversed version for dark backgrounds. Each location can carry its name under the same mark.
4. **Show it as an evolution.** Say "same dog, fresh look" on social media and in the new waiting rooms. That uses the familiarity as an asset, and regulars will feel the clinic is growing rather than changing.

To get your partner on board, show them a mockup rather than arguing the point. Ask the designer for a cleaned-up dog beside the current one, plus a sign and a business card. Most people who want "clean and professional" are objecting to the rough execution, not to having a dog. If they still want something entirely new after seeing it, the argument for replacing it is that the old mark can't be made to work. I don't think that will be true here.

One caveat: if the sketch has any quirks you love, such as the wobble or a particular eye, tell the designer up front. Those are the details that vanish in a "cleanup" if no one says to protect them.
