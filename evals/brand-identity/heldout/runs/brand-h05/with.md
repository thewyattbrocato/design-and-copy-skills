**Refine it. Keep the dog.**

Twelve years of neighborhood recognition is the most valuable thing the logo has, and you're about to open two locations where that recognition will carry over. A clean, generic replacement would throw it away just when you'll be introducing the brand to new neighborhoods through the people who already know it. Your partner is right about one thing, though: a wobbly pencil sketch won't hold up across three locations. The way to settle that is to fix the execution and keep the dog.

**What I'd do**

1. **Redraw the dog as a clean, bold, solid-color shape.** Keep what makes it recognizable: the pose, the one ear up, and the slightly lopsided charm. Replace the pencil texture and thin, uneven lines with confident, even strokes. It should look like the same dog, only drawn by someone with a steady hand. Don't over-polish it. A perfectly symmetrical corporate dog would lose the personality people remember.
2. **Make a simplified small version.** Pencil detail disappears at favicon, social avatar and stamp sizes. Draw a heavier, simpler dog for those, not the full logo shrunk down.
3. **Test it in one color.** It should read as solid black on white and reversed on a dark background. That covers signage, scrubs, embroidery, receipts and vaccine tags.
4. **Tidy the lettering without changing its temperament.** The rounded font is a good fit for a friendly clinic. Keep the rounded feel, but set the name properly: tune the letter spacing by eye and make one small custom touch, such as a rounded terminal or a tail on a letter, so it isn't just a stock font. Make sure the dog and the lettering have matching weight.
5. **Build lockups for three locations.** You'll need a horizontal version, a stacked version and a dog-only version. Put each location name on a separate line under the logo, not inside it, so the mark stays the same everywhere.
6. **Keep your current color unless it fails.** If the old logo has a recognizable color, carry it over. Change it only if it's muddy or doesn't hold up on dark backgrounds.

**How to settle it with your partner**

Show them the refined dog next to the original and a generic replacement on real surfaces: the storefront sign, a website header, a phone icon and a scrub top. Then ask 10 to 15 longtime clients which one they'd recognize as your clinic. I wouldn't predict the result, but I expect the refined dog will win, and then the decision is made on their answers and not on my opinion or your partner's.

**When I'd change my answer**

I'd replace the dog if the testing showed it can't be redrawn to read at small sizes without losing what makes it recognizable, or if it looks too much like another local clinic's mark. Neither seems likely from what you've described.
