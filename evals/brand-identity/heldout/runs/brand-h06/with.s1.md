**Assumptions:** The mark is a flat, single-color stack of three books that forms an S. The name is "Southbank Reading Room". I haven't seen the artwork, so the proportions below are guidelines to check against it.

**Master set (build these first)**
- **Symbol only:** the S-stack on its own.
- **Horizontal lockup:** the symbol on the left. The name sits to its right, vertically centered on the stack. The name is one line, or two lines ("Southbank" / "Reading Room") if the stack is tall.
- **Stacked lockup:** the symbol centered above the name.
- **Colors:** every version works as solid dark on light, solid light on dark, and in one flat color. Draw the three books so they are separated by gaps, not hairline outlines. That way the S still reads when the mark is small or printed in one ink.
- **Clear space:** use the height of one book spine on all sides.
- **Tagline:** if you ever add one (e.g. "A neighborhood library"), keep it as a separate line outside the mark.

**1. Website header (wide, short)**
- Use the horizontal lockup, set on one line.
- Keep the height small, around 40–56 px on desktop. The symbol should be about as tall as the cap height of the name plus a little more.
- Put the name in a medium-to-bold weight. Match its stroke weight to the book edges so the symbol and the lettering feel like one piece.
- On mobile, switch to the symbol plus a shortened name, or the symbol alone. Don't scale the full lockup down until the name is unreadable. Keep the name at 14 px or larger.
- If the header sits over a photo or a dark band, use the reversed (light) version.
- The favicon is the symbol only, redrawn heavier. Make the gaps between books wider and drop any fine detail so it reads at 16–32 px. Don't just shrink the full logo.

**2. Square social avatar**
- Use the symbol only. Platforms crop avatars to a circle, and the name would be unreadable at that size.
- Center the S-stack with generous padding. The symbol should fill about 55–65% of the width, so a circular crop doesn't clip the corners of the stack.
- Use a solid background in your brand color with the symbol in a light color, or the reverse. Avoid gradients and photos.
- If the stack is much taller than it is wide, check the crop. Nudge the symbol so it's centered by visual weight, not by its bounding box.
- Export at 1024×1024 so it stays sharp when downscaled.

**3. Narrow bookmark**
- Use the stacked lockup, or a vertical arrangement, since the format is tall and thin.
- Put the symbol near the top at about 60–70% of the bookmark's width. Put the name below it in two or three short lines, centered:
  - "Southbank"
  - "Reading"
  - "Room"
- Don't rotate the horizontal lockup sideways. Rotated text is harder to read and looks off when the bookmark is lying on a page.
- Leave empty space below for a quote, a due date or a hole punch. Keep the logo away from the punch hole and the trim edge, at least 3 mm.
- Print in one or two colors. It's a good place for the single-color version of the mark.

**4. Canvas tote bag**
- Use the stacked lockup, centered on the front of the bag. It's the most flexible shape, and a tote is closer to square than wide.
- Print it in one color of ink, usually dark on natural canvas. Screen printing handles flat shapes well and handles fine detail poorly, so use the heavy-gap version of the mark.
- Size it large: about 20–25 cm wide on a standard tote, placed in the upper-middle of the bag, not at the very top where the straps pull.
- Keep the clear space generous. Canvas texture will soften small gaps, so check that the gaps between books are wide enough (roughly 2 mm or more at print size).
- Optional: a second, symbol-only print on the back or a small corner, as a quiet alternate.

**Quick checks before you finalize**
- Print the symbol in solid black at about 16 px. The S should still be recognizable.
- Check the reversed version on your darkest brand color.
- Read the name aloud and upside down to check the lettering doesn't look like anything else.
- Test the avatar in a circular crop.

**Notes**
- If your current mark has thin lines or a gradient, redraw it flat and heavier for the small versions (favicon, avatar). That's the one change worth making.
- The tote and bookmark are print jobs, so ask your printer for their minimum line weight and ink count before the artwork is final.
