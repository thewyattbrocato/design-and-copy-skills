**Partly true, but "just how embroidery works" doesn't explain the whole problem.** Embroidery has real physical limits. The logo was probably designed for screens and print, and nobody adapted it for thread or for a 32px icon. Both problems are fixable, and for a small firm the fix is one simplified mark, not a full rebrand.

**Why this happens**

- **Embroidery:** Thread is roughly 0.4 to 0.5 mm wide, and stitches pull the fabric, so strokes thinner than about 1 mm fill in and merge. Small text is worse. Lettering much under about 6 mm (1/4") tall turns into a solid blob. Fine detail that looks crisp on screen has no physical equivalent in thread. Digitizing (converting artwork to stitch paths) can help, but it can't create detail the thread can't hold.
- **32px favicon:** At that size, a thin line or small text covers only a fraction of a pixel. The browser blends it with the background, so you get gray. The fix is a different drawing, not a smaller copy of the main logo.

**What to do**

1. **Make a simplified "small-size" version of the mark.** Keep only the core shape or monogram. Thicken strokes, remove fine lines, and drop the tagline. Use high contrast and a solid silhouette. Test it by viewing it at 32px and at 1 inch on a shirt.
2. **Use that simplified mark for the favicon.** Export a 16×16 and 32×32 `.ico` or PNG, plus a 180×180 Apple touch icon. Adding an SVG favicon is optional. Check it in a browser tab.
3. **Create an embroidery version for the polos.** Remove the tagline or make it a bold, large sans-serif at roughly 6 mm or taller. Limit the design to two or three thread colors, avoid gradients and tiny gaps, and make lines at least about 1 mm.
4. **Ask the embroiderer for a sew-out sample before the full run.** This is a stitched test on the actual fabric. It's standard practice and costs little. Ask them what minimum line width and text height they recommend for your garment.
5. **Keep the full logo for the website and PDF deck.** Those are fine. The simplified versions are for small-size and stitched use.
