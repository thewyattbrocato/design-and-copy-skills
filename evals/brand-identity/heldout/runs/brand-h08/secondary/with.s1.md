**The designer is partly right.** Embroidery can't reproduce fine lines or small text, because thread has width and the stitches can't be placed more precisely than that. But the melted tagline and the smudge are symptoms of using the website artwork unchanged. A stitch-ready version is a standard step, and it's usually a small job.

**Why it happens**

- **Embroidery:** Thread is roughly a millimeter wide, and fabric can shift and pucker during stitching. Hairlines and tight gaps fill in, and small letters lose their counters (the enclosed spaces in letters like "e" and "a"). Embroiderers generally need minimum stroke widths and minimum letter heights. Ask yours for their numbers; they vary by shop and fabric.
- **Favicon:** At 32px, a detailed logo has only a few pixels per detail. Anti-aliasing turns thin strokes and similar colors into gray. A shrunk logo is never a good favicon.

**What to do**

1. **Make a stitch version.** Ask the designer for a simplified copy of the mark with:
   - Strokes heavier than your embroiderer's stated minimum (plan on roughly 1 mm or more, and confirm).
   - No hairlines, gradients, or fine interior detail.
   - Fewer colors, ideally 1–2 thread colors.
2. **Drop or enlarge the tagline on apparel.** If it has to stay, make it a bold, large line, and check that it's legible at the planned size. Otherwise, leave it off polos.
3. **Make a dedicated favicon.** Use a bold single shape or simple monogram, solid and high-contrast, with no tagline and no thin lines. Export 16, 32, and 180px (Apple touch icon), plus an SVG if your site supports it. Test it in a real browser tab in light and dark mode.
4. **Get a sample before the full order.** Have the embroiderer stitch one test polo at the real size. This is the only reliable proof, and it's cheap compared with a run of shirts.

**Next step for the designer:** send them the three failures above and ask for a "stitch-ready" mark and a separate "icon" mark, plus a sample stitch-out before the order goes out. If they push back on simplifying, the stitch sample will settle it.
