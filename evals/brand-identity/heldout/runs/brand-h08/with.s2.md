Partly true. Embroidery does have hard physical limits, but your designer is using that as a reason to stop. The real fix is a simplified version of the logo for each small use, and that's a small job. I haven't seen your logo, so the specifics below are what usually goes wrong, not a diagnosis of your file.

**Why it happens**

Your website and PDF deck are screens and vector print, which can render hairlines and tiny type at almost any size. The favicon and the polos can't.

- **Favicon:** A 32px tab icon (often shown at 16px) has about 1,000 pixels to work with. A full logo shrunk into it turns fine detail and a tagline into gray blur. The favicon needs its own drawing, not a shrunken logo.
- **Embroidery:** The image is built from thread, which has real thickness. Lines thinner than about 1mm (roughly 0.04") fill in with neighboring stitches. Text under about 5–6mm (¼") tall closes up, so a small tagline usually can't be legible. Gradients, shadows and subtle tints can't be stitched at all. They get flattened into a few thread colors.

So your designer is right that embroidery can't reproduce the artwork as it is. They're wrong if they mean you're stuck with the melted result. The standard answer is an embroidery-specific version of the logo.

**What to do**

1. **Make a small-size version of the mark.** Draw it heavier and simpler: thicker strokes, fewer details, flat solid colors, and no tagline. Keep the same shape and color so people still recognize it.
2. **Favicon:** Use only the strongest part of the logo, such as a monogram or the single main shape, as a solid shape on a plain background. Check it at 16px and 32px, in light and dark browser themes.
3. **Embroidery:**
   - Send the embroiderer the simplified vector file (SVG, AI or EPS), not the deck PDF or a PNG.
   - Ask them to **digitize** it and send a **stitch-out sample** for approval before they make more polos. Digitizing is the step where the artwork is converted into stitch paths, and a good digitizer can often rescue a design.
   - Drop the tagline from the polos. If you want it, put it on a separate line at a larger size, or leave it for print materials.
4. **Write a one-page rule sheet.** It should list the full logo for web and print, the simplified logo for small and stitched uses, and the symbol alone for favicons and avatars. Add a minimum size for each. This also gives you an answer next time someone asks for the logo on a mug or a pen.

**Scope:** This is roughly one designer revision, with maybe a digitizing fee from the embroiderer (often a one-time cost of a few dozen dollars). It isn't a rebrand, and I wouldn't change the main logo, since it works where it's used now.
