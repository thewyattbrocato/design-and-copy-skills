No, it isn't "just how embroidery works." Embroidery does have hard physical limits, but your designer is describing the problem as if it were unavoidable. It isn't. You have one logo file doing three jobs it wasn't drawn for.

**Why it happens**

- **Favicon (32px, shown at 16px in many tabs):** The full logo shrunk down has fine lines and detail packed into about 1,000 pixels. Anything thinner than roughly 2px blends into its neighbors and turns gray. Browsers also don't give you a lockup, they give you a tiny square.
- **Embroidery:** The logo is built from thread, not pixels. A needle makes a hole, and thread has real thickness, about 0.3–0.4mm. In practice:
  - Lines thinner than about 1mm (roughly 1.5pt) merge or disappear.
  - Text under about 5–6mm (¼ inch) tall is unreadable. A small tagline is the usual casualty.
  - Gradients, shadows, and tiny gaps between shapes either get lost or flatten into mush.
  - Fabric pulls and puckers around dense stitching, which makes fine detail worse on a polo.

**What the designer got right and wrong**

- Right: embroidery can't reproduce a web-quality logo as is. Some simplification is physically required.
- Wrong: treating that as the end of the conversation. The standard fix is a small-size version of the mark, and a designer should supply it. Having only one full-color horizontal logo is a gap in the deliverables, not a law of nature.

**What to do (a small job, not a big project)**

Ask for four files, drawn as flat, solid shapes:

1. **Favicon / symbol-only mark.** Draw it for 16–32px, not a shrunken logo. Use only the strongest shape, such as a monogram or the main symbol, with heavy strokes and no fine lines. Check it at actual size in a real browser tab.
2. **Embroidery version.**
   - Simplified, with thicker lines and wider gaps between elements.
   - Limited to 1–4 solid thread colors, with no gradients.
   - Drop the tagline. Put the firm name in bold lettering only if it's tall enough to stitch, and leave the tagline off the polos.
   - Have it digitized (converted into a stitch file) by an embroidery shop, and ask for a sew-out sample on a polo before the full order. Many shops do the digitizing for a modest fee.
3. **One-color version**, black and reversed (white on dark). It helps with the stitching and with stamps, signs, and dark backgrounds.
4. **Tagline as a separate line**, not locked inside the logo, so it can be dropped wherever it's too small to read.

**Judging the result**

Look at each version at the smallest size it will actually appear: 16–32px on screen, and the real stitched size on a polo. If it reads there, it's done. You'll also want a clear minimum size written down (for example, "don't embroider below 2 inches wide").
