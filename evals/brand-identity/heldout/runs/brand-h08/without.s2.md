Your designer is partly right. Embroidery does have hard physical limits, but the fix is usually a simplified version of the logo made for small sizes, not a different logo.

**Why it happens**

- **Favicon:** At 32px (often shown at 16px) you have roughly 1,000 pixels to work with. Thin strokes, small text, and subtle gradients average into gray. Your logo looks fine on the website and in the deck because it's drawn at a size where those details survive.
- **Embroidery:** Thread is physical. A stitch line is about 0.3 to 0.4 mm wide, and the needle punches holes that pull the fabric. In practice:
  - Lines thinner than about 1 mm (roughly 0.04") fill in or vanish.
  - Text shorter than about 5 to 6 mm (roughly 0.2") tall usually isn't legible, and small serif or thin-stroke type is the worst case.
  - Gaps between elements need about 1 mm or more, or the threads blend together.
  - Gradients, shadows, and photo-style detail can't be stitched at all. Each one becomes a flat color.
  - Stretchy polo knit makes all of this worse.

So "just how embroidery works" is true of your current artwork, but it's not an excuse. Every professional brand has a small-use or simplified version of its logo for exactly this reason.

**What to do (a small project)**

1. **Make a simplified mark.** Ask your designer for a version with thicker strokes, fewer fine details, more spacing, and no tagline. It should be a single-color or flat-color version.
2. **Favicon:** Use only the icon or monogram, not the full logo or wordmark. Check it at 16px and 32px, and export it as SVG plus PNG, or ICO.
3. **Embroidery:**
   - Drop the tagline from polos, or stitch it only if it can be at least 6 mm tall in a bold sans-serif. Otherwise leave it off.
   - Use the simplified mark at about 3.5 to 4" wide on the chest.
   - Have the embroiderer re-digitize it. A good digitizer adjusts stitch type and density for the fabric. A "sew-out" sample, which is a test stitch before the full order, usually costs $25 to $75 and is worth it.
   - If you already have the digitized file, ask the vendor whether it was auto-converted from your JPG or PNG. That's often the cause of melted lines.
4. **Write it down.** One page covering three versions (full logo, simplified, icon only) and where each one is used is all the "brand system" a firm your size needs.

**Questions for your designer**

- "Can you produce a small-size version of the logo with thicker strokes and no tagline?"
- "Can you supply a flat, simplified vector for the embroiderer?"

If they refuse, or say embroidery just can't be done well, I'd get a second opinion. This is routine work, typically a few hours of design time, and I'd expect most of the cost to be the new embroidery digitizing and sample.
