Your designer is half right. Embroidery does have physical limits, but the fix is a simplified version of the logo for small and stitched uses. That is a normal, small job, and a good designer would have prepared it before the polos were ordered.

**Why it happens**

Both failures come from the same cause. The full logo was drawn for a screen or a page, where detail costs nothing. Small and physical uses don't have that room.

- **Favicon:** At 32px (and often 16px on the tab itself) you have about a thousand pixels. Fine lines, a wordmark and small shapes blur into gray. Shrinking the full logo can't fix this.
- **Embroidery:** Thread is about 0.3–0.4 mm wide, and stitches sit on stretchy fabric that pulls and puffs. Lines thinner than about 1 mm fill in, gaps narrower than about 1.5 mm close up, and lettering shorter than about 5 mm (roughly ¼ inch) turns to mush. Gradients, shadows and hairlines don't convert at all, because thread is one flat color per area. A tagline at body-text size is nearly always past that limit.

So the physical limit is real, but only for the full logo. The designer's shrug is wrong because the logo can be adapted.

**What to do**

Ask for a small set of variants, not a redesign. The logo itself stays recognizable.

1. **Favicon / symbol-only mark.** Use one bold shape, such as a monogram or the logo's main symbol, in solid color. Make it heavier than the original and drop the wordmark and tagline. Check that it reads at 16px, then export it as SVG, 32px and 180px PNG, and `.ico`.
2. **Embroidery version.** Use a flat, one-to-three-color version with thicker strokes and wider gaps. Set a minimum letter height of about 5–6 mm, and drop the tagline from the chest logo. If the tagline matters, put it on the back of the collar or on a sleeve at a larger size. Have a digitizer, who is a specialist in turning art into stitch files, produce the file from this version. Auto-converted artwork is a common cause of melted lines. Ask for a test stitch-out on a scrap before you reorder.
3. **A one-page rule sheet.** It lists which version goes where: full logo for web and print, simplified for stitching, symbol for tabs and avatars. It also gives minimum sizes, so this doesn't happen again.

For a firm your size, this is a few hours of designer time plus a digitizing fee, usually well under a few hundred dollars. The existing polos are probably not salvageable, but ask the embroiderer whether they will redo them if the problem was in their digitizing and not just in the artwork.
