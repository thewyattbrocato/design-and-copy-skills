**Verdict: replace the lockup, but keep what's good.** I'm judging from your description and haven't seen the logo. The check I'd run is to put it in black on white at 1 inch wide, then reversed on a dark photo. I expect it to fall apart both ways.

## What's going on

**1. It stacks five ideas.** The mark has a script wordmark, a wheat stalk, a banner, a gold outline and a tagline. Say it aloud and it's "a script name *and* wheat *and* a banner *and* a border *and* a slogan." A mark should be one idea you can say in one sentence.

**2. Every piece is the bakery stock choice.** Wheat is the category's default icon. Brown script, cream-and-pink and gold are the default palette. The tagline is the default sentiment. Each piece is individually reasonable, which is why together they read as "every other bakery." Nothing in it says Crumb & Co. in particular.

**3. It won't hold up small.** The wheat has fine detail and the tiny-caps tagline is a hairline. The gold outline is a thin line, and gold is hard to print consistently. On a box corner, an Instagram avatar or a sign seen from across the street, those details disappear. What's left is a brown scribble on a pink blob.

**4. The tagline is locked inside the mark.** It can't be dropped on small applications, and it's generic copy. "Baked fresh with love" is something nearly every bakery says.

**5. The name has an unused idea.** "Crumb" is a specific, tactile word, and nothing in the logo uses it.

## What I'd change

- **Pick one idea.** I'd go with the name itself: a wordmark with one custom touch, such as a ligature, a distinctive "&", or a single cut or crumb-shaped detail. Skip the wheat. If you love the wheat, it can become a one-color secondary pattern for packaging, not part of the mark.
- **Draw the wordmark properly.** Use a sturdier script or a characterful serif, not an untouched stock script. Tune the spacing by eye so "Crumb & Co." reads in a second, with weight even enough to survive small sizes.
- **Drop the banner and the gold outline.** The shape behind the logo is the main thing making it look like a template. Let the wordmark sit directly on the surface.
- **Make the tagline a separate, optional line.** Better still, rewrite it so it sounds like you.
- **Choose one distinctive color, and choose it last.** Dark brown is fine as a base, but pair it with one color other local bakeries aren't using, not cream-and-pink. Gold should be a foil or print finish on boxes, not part of the mark.
- **Make a small version.** Make a heavier, simplified monogram (for example "C&Co" or just the "&") for the Instagram avatar, box seals and favicon. Don't shrink the full logo.
- **Build a system around it.** Pick a consistent color role, one packaging pattern (the wheat could go here), a photo style and layout rules. That's what makes a bakery recognizable with the logo covered.
