# Why it looks like every other bakery

**Verdict: the mark is generic by construction. I'd replace the composition and keep a couple of cues.** That rests on your description and on people telling you it looks generic. I haven't seen the file, and I don't know how recognized it is locally. Ask ten regulars to sketch it from memory. If few can, you have little equity to protect.

**What's going on**

1. **It's the category's stock kit.** Script wordmark, wheat stalk, cream and pink, gold outline, and a "baked with love" tagline are the default bakery vocabulary. Each element is individually fine. Together they say "bakery" and nothing about Crumb & Co.
2. **It stacks four or five ideas.** The mark has script, wheat, banner, outline and tagline. Try saying it in one sentence without "and". You can't, so nothing leads.
3. **The wheat is a plain picture of the trade.** It has no link to your name, your bread, or your shop.
4. **It's built for one big, full-color version.** The tiny-caps tagline, the thin gold outline and a script over a pink banner will all fail at Instagram avatar size (about 30 px). On kraft boxes, gold on cream or pink also has little contrast. Foil or gold ink is a separate cost.
5. **The tagline is locked into the mark.** It's illegible small, and it makes the whole logo one inflexible block.
6. **The wordmark probably isn't custom.** A script face used as-is looks like everyone else's.

**What I'd change**

1. **Pick one idea, and build it from your name.** "Crumb" is the most ownable word you have. For example, a wordmark where the "C" of Crumb has a bite taken out, or the ampersand drawn as a loaf scored on top. In one sentence: *"Crumb & Co. is the name with a bite taken out of it."* Drop the wheat, the banner and the outline.
2. **Make the wordmark the logo, with one custom move.** Use a sturdy, warm face, serif or soft slab, instead of a thin script. Redraw one letter or the ampersand. Check it read aloud, stacked, and cropped to a circle.
3. **Test it solid black on white, then at 32 px, then reversed.** If it only works in color or with gold, it isn't done.
4. **Make the tagline a separate, optional line.** Keep it out of the mark and use it on boxes and signs only. I'd also tighten it, because "baked fresh with love" is a phrase every bakery uses.
5. **Keep dark brown, and put the distinctiveness in the system.**
   - Use one bold, unexpected accent in place of cream and pink.
   - Use a single graphic device, such as the bite or a scoring pattern, as box tape or a pattern.
   - Take photos from one consistent angle.
   - Cover the logo: if the box, sign and feed still look like Crumb & Co., it's working.
6. **Deliver a set of versions, not one file.**
   - Horizontal and stacked lockups.
   - A symbol-only version: the bitten "C" or the ampersand loaf, drawn heavier for the avatar and favicon.
   - One-color and reversed versions, for stamps, bag stickers and the dark shop sign.

**Next tests**
- Put the current logo and two rival bakeries' logos side by side with the names covered. If you can't tell them apart, that confirms the problem.
- Show 5–10 customers the new version against the old one, and ask which they'd recognize on a sign across the street.

Two assumptions: Crumb & Co. is a local, independent shop, and the brown is your only color with real equity. If customers know and love the wheat, tell me. I'd then keep it as a secondary graphic and still simplify the lockup.
