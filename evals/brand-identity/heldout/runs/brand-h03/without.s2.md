**Why it looks like every other bakery**

Your logo is a stack of the most common bakery signals, and each one is individually fine:

- **Script wordmark in brown.** Script says "homemade and warm," and so does brown. Thousands of bakeries use both.
- **Wheat stalk.** It's the default bakery icon. It says "bread" but nothing about you.
- **Cream and pink banner.** The pastel banner is a stock "artisan" shape, and the palette is the usual soft-bakery one.
- **Gold outline around everything.** It adds fussiness without adding meaning. It also tends to disappear at small sizes and print badly.
- **"Baked fresh with love since our first loaf."** Every bakery says fresh, love and baked. The line could belong to any of them.

Together that makes five generic choices and no distinctive one. There's also a practical problem: a script wordmark, a drawing, a banner, an outline and a tagline are five layers competing in one lockup. That's hard to read on an Instagram avatar, and it's hard to emboss or stamp on a box.

**What I'd change**

1. **Find one thing that's only yours and build around it.** What do you actually bake? What's the story behind "Crumb & Co."? A signature product, a neighborhood detail, or the founders' background gives you something to draw on. Right now nothing in the logo comes from you.
2. **Use the name.** "Crumb" is a great word. A custom ampersand, or a crumb-shaped mark or texture, would be more ownable than wheat. Wheat can go.
3. **Cut layers.** Drop the banner and the gold outline. One strong wordmark with room around it will outperform the stack.
4. **Replace script with something with character.** Options are a chunky serif, a quirky hand-lettered face, or a confident sans with one custom detail. Whichever you pick, keep it legible at 40px.
5. **Rethink the palette.** Keep one warm anchor (your brown is fine) and pair it with a surprising accent, such as tomato red, deep green, mustard or blue. Pink and cream are the genre default. If you want gold, use it as a foil or print finish on boxes, not as an outline.
6. **Move the tagline out of the logo.** Keep the logo to the name and mark. Put a line in your copy, on packaging, or on the sign, and make it specific. For example, name what you bake or when the ovens open, instead of "baked with love."
7. **Design a system, not one lockup.** You need a primary logo, a compact mark for the Instagram avatar and stamps, and a one-color version for boxes and the sign. Test all three at tiny and huge sizes.

**Before you redesign**

Put your logo next to five local competitors and see how many of the five signals each one shares. Whatever the others do, pick a direction that's clearly different. Also keep in mind that customers saying it "looks like every other bakery" is about the visuals. It doesn't mean the bakery itself is generic, so the redesign should bring out what's actually different about you.
