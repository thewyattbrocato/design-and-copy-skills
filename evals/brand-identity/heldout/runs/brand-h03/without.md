**Your logo is a collection of bakery clichés.** Each element is individually reasonable, but together they're the default bakery kit:

- **Script wordmark in brown:** the "artisan" signal that thousands of bakeries use.
- **Wheat stalk:** the most common bakery icon there is. It says "bakery" without saying anything about *you*.
- **Cream and pink banner:** the soft, pastel look is the category default.
- **Gold outline around everything:** this is a "premium" signal, but it's decoration. It also tends to disappear at small sizes and on kraft boxes.
- **"Baked fresh with love since our first loaf":** every bakery says "fresh" and "love." It's also too long to read in tiny caps.

There's also a structural problem. The logo has five or six elements competing at the same level (wordmark, icon, banner, outline, tagline), and none of them leads. That's why it's forgettable. It's also probably why it works poorly across your three uses. A sign needs to read from across the street, an Instagram avatar is about 110px, and a box needs to stamp or print in one color. Fine detail like a thin outline and tiny caps fails at all of these.

**What I'd change**

1. **Find the one distinctive thing and build around it.** What's actually specific to Crumb & Co.? Maybe it's the ampersand, the word "crumb" (a texture, a scoring pattern, a torn edge), a signature product, or your neighborhood. Right now nothing in the logo comes from that.
2. **Cut it down to one idea.** Keep one strong mark and drop the banner, the outline, and probably the wheat. A confident wordmark, plus a single simple symbol if you want one, will beat the layered version.
3. **Replace the generic script.** Either commission or customize a wordmark, or pair a characterful serif or hand-lettered "Crumb" with a plain "& Co." The ampersand is your best asset for a custom detail.
4. **Rethink the palette.** Dark brown is fine as an anchor. Swap cream and pink for something with a point of view, like a bold tomato red, a deep green, or a mustard, and keep one accent color. Use gold only as a print finish on special packaging, not in the logo.
5. **Move the tagline out of the logo.** Keep a lockup that works without it. Use the tagline separately on packaging, and shorten it to something that sounds like you, such as "Baked this morning." The "since our first loaf" line is also a bit odd if you're a newer business.
6. **Test it at the extremes.** The logo should work as a one-color stamp on a kraft box, at 48px as an Instagram avatar, and in a sign mockup viewed from 30 feet away. If it fails any of these, simplify further.

Don't throw away your existing equity. If customers already recognize the brown and the name, keep the name and the brown and rebuild the rest.
