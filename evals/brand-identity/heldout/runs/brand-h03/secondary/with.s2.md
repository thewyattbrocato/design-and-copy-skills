**What's going on:** Each element is the bakery default: a wheat stalk, a brown script, a cream-and-pink banner, and a gold outline. Any one of them is fine, but together they say "bakery" and nothing about Crumb & Co. The mark also stacks at least four ideas, so no single one registers. The gold outline and banner make it read as a label, and the tiny-caps tagline will vanish on a shop sign or an Instagram avatar. I'm working from your description, not the file, and I've assumed a neighborhood bakery with a friendly, slightly premium tone.

**What I'd change:**

1. **Make the name the mark.** "Crumb & Co" already has something distinctive: the ampersand. Draw a custom ampersand, or reshape one letter, and let that be the logo. That's the one idea. Drop the wheat, the banner and the outline.
2. **Go solid and flat.** One color, probably the dark brown, on cream. Keep pink and gold for packaging and seasonal accents, not the core mark.
3. **Move the tagline out of the mark.** Set it as a separate line under the logo on boxes, and drop it from the sign and avatar. "Baked fresh with love since our first loaf" is also long, so consider a shorter line for the sign.
4. **Test it in the places it has to work:**
   - Solid black on white at about 32px (the Instagram avatar size).
   - Reversed on the dark shop sign.
   - Read aloud, to check that nothing unintended comes through.

**Evidence to get next:** Ask ten regulars to name the bakery after seeing the mark for three seconds, with the old and new versions side by side. That's the only real check on whether it's distinctive, and I can't assert it from here.

**Assumptions and trade-off:** I assumed you're not tied to the current colors and that no trademark issues stand in the way of a custom ampersand. The trade-off is that a minimal mark is less charming on its own, so the warmth has to come from packaging and photography.
