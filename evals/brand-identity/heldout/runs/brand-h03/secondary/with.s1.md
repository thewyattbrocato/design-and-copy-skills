**What's going on:** Each piece of the logo is a bakery cliché, and together they cancel out any single idea. Script wordmark, wheat stalk, ribbon banner, cream-and-pink, and gold outline are the default kit for bakeries, so the mark reads as the category rather than as Crumb & Co. The effects make it worse. The gold outline and the tiny tagline are the first things to disappear on a shop sign, a box corner, or an Instagram avatar, so what's left is the generic part.

**What I'd change:**

1. **Pick one idea.** Right now it's "wheat, and banner, and script, and gold, and we're fresh." Choose the strongest asset you already have. The name is the obvious candidate: "Crumb" is a specific, ownable word, and the "&" could carry the mark. Other options are a drawn crumb or loaf shape, or a detail from your own shop (your oven, your signature bake). The mark should be explainable in one sentence without "and."

2. **Cut the decoration.** Drop the wheat stalk, the banner, the gold outline, and the tagline from the mark. Keep the wordmark and redraw it with one custom adjustment, such as a reshaped letter or a custom ampersand, so it isn't a stock script font.

3. **Make the core mark flat and solid.** Test it solid black on white at the smallest real size (a 32px avatar, a box label), then reversed in cream on dark brown for the sign. If it fails either test, simplify again before choosing color.

4. **Set color last.** Keep the dark brown as the core. Cream can be the ground, and one accent color can be chosen for a reason. Pink and gold are the parts most shared with other bakeries, so I'd drop them from the mark and use them, if at all, in packaging graphics.

5. **Use the tagline as a separate line.** On boxes, put "baked fresh with love since our first loaf" in its own line or on the inside flap. Keep it out of the lockup used on the sign and Instagram.

6. **Build a system around the mark.** The mark alone won't separate you from other bakeries. Add one repeated device, such as a crumb-texture pattern on tissue or a specific photo style, so the brand is recognizable even with the logo covered.

**Refine or replace:** If customers already recognize the sign and boxes, keep the dark brown script, since that's your equity, and strip everything else. If the logo is still new, a clean redraw is the better move. I'm working from your description, not the files, so I can't check the actual letterforms.

**Next test:** Mock the stripped version on a box label, the sign, and an Instagram avatar, then show it to a handful of regular customers, cover the mark, and ask what they remember about the shop. That tells you more than my opinion.
