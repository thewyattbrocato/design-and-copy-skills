**What's going on:** Every element is the bakery default. Script wordmark, wheat stalk, ribbon banner, gold outline, and a sentimental tagline are the pieces most bakery logos already use. Each one is fine alone, but together they say "bakery" and nothing about Crumb & Co. The logo is built from category signals, not from anything specific to your shop. It also has too many small parts. Wheat stalks, hairline outlines, and tiny caps disappear on a shop sign seen from across the street or an Instagram avatar, so what survives is the generic shape.

**What I'd change:**

1. **Start from the name.** "Crumb" is your best asset and the logo ignores it. Try a mark built on the crumb itself: a crumb-texture pattern, a loaf cross-section, or an ampersand redrawn from bread shapes. Any one idea that only fits your shop is better than five that fit any bakery.

2. **Cut to one mark.** Choose one graphic element and drop the rest. The banner and gold outline should go first. Then check the mark at 1 inch and at sign size.

3. **Replace the stock script.** Script is the most common bakery typeface. Use a distinctive serif, a bold sans with a quirk, or hand-lettering that's specific to you. The "&" is a good place to show character.

4. **Pick a color that isn't brown, cream, pink, and gold.** Those four are the bakery palette. One strong, unexpected color, like a tomato red, deep green, or cobalt, will stand out on a shelf and in a feed. Keep brown as a secondary if you like it for warmth.

5. **Drop the tagline from the logo.** "Baked fresh with love since our first loaf" is the most generic line in the design, and it's too small to read anyway. Keep it as copy on the box if you want it. If you do want a line in the mark, make it specific to you, like a signature loaf, a street, or a year.

**How to test the new version:**
- Cover the name. Does the mark still suggest *this* bakery, or just "bakery"?
- Shrink it to a 32px favicon and print it in one color. Is it still recognizable?
- Show it to five people for five seconds, then ask what they remember. If they say "bakery" and not "Crumb & Co," keep working.
