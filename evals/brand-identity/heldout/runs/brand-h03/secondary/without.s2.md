Your logo is made entirely of bakery-category signals, so it reads as "bakery" before it reads as *Crumb & Co.* Script, wheat, cream-and-pink, gold, and a sentimental tagline are the default kit for the category. Each piece is fine alone, but together they describe any bakery in town, and the brand has nothing that belongs only to you.

The fine details also work against you on a sign and on Instagram:
- **The gold outline and the tiny-caps tagline** will disappear at distance and at avatar size. They're decoration that only shows up up close.
- **The wheat stalk and banner** are the most common bakery icons, so they add clutter without adding recognition.
- **The script wordmark** is hard to read on a shop sign from across the street, and it's the most common bakery typeface.

**What I'd change:**

1. **Start from something true about you.** Find one thing only you have: the founder's name, your street or neighborhood, a signature bake (the cardamom bun, the sourdough with a particular scoring), your oven, or a deliberately odd name origin. Build the brand around that. A bakery with a specific story has less need for a wheat stalk.

2. **Cut the props.** Remove the wheat, the banner, and the gold outline. Make the logo a single flat shape or a single word with one simple device.

3. **Make the name the logo.** Set "Crumb & Co." in a bold, characterful typeface, ideally a sturdy sans or a heavy serif, with a custom ampersand or one letter drawn differently. A distinctive ampersand is a cheap, memorable signature. Script is the bakery default, so avoid it.

4. **Pick one color that isn't brown, cream, pink, or gold.** Deep tomato red, ink blue, or a single bright color on kraft paper would stand out on a shelf of cream-and-brown boxes. Use one color for the logo and one neutral for everything else.

5. **Replace or drop the tagline.** "Baked fresh with love since our first loaf" is generic and unreadable at sign size. If you keep a line, make it specific: your hours, your neighborhood, or a phrase only you would say. Otherwise cut it.

6. **Test it at three sizes.** Check it on the shop sign from across the street, as a 40px Instagram avatar, and printed on a box in one color. If it only works at full size with all the details, simplify again.

A quick check on the current version: cover the name and show the mark to a few people who've never heard of you. If they guess "bakery" but not *Crumb & Co.*, the mark isn't doing its job.
