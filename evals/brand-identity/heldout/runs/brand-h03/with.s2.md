**Why it looks like every other bakery**

The logo is a stack of the category's most common signals. Script lettering, a wheat stalk, a pastel banner, a gold outline and a "baked with love" tagline are each on many bakery logos, and together they are the default bakery look. Nothing in it says Crumb & Co. specifically. The problems:

- **Too many ideas.** Wordmark, wheat, banner and frame each compete. The mark can't be said in one sentence without several "and"s.
- **The wheat is the trade's stock icon.** It tells people you sell bread, which they already know. It says nothing about you.
- **It depends on fine detail and color.** The thin gold outline, the cream-on-pink banner and the tiny-caps tagline will vanish at Instagram avatar size. On the shop sign and boxes they only survive with foil or careful printing. In one color or reversed on kraft or dark stock, the banner and outline turn to mud.
- **The tagline is locked into the logo.** It is unreadable small, and it forces the whole lockup to be used even where there's no room.
- **The script is probably an untouched stock script.** If so, anyone can set the same letters.

**What I'd change**

I'm assuming Crumb & Co. is a small local bakery, warm and handmade, with no wide recognition yet. That makes replacing the mark reasonable, but this is an assumption, not tested. Asking ten regulars what they remember about the logo would give you real evidence.

1. **Pick one idea built from your name.** For example, a custom ampersand drawn as a scored loaf, with one slash cut through it. You'd say it in one sentence: "The & is a loaf with a score mark." It works as the Instagram avatar and as a stamp on boxes.
2. **Drop the wheat, banner and outline.** Keep the dark brown, which is a good choice. Choose cream or pink as a background color, not as a shape inside the logo.
3. **Customize the wordmark.** Keep it a script if you like, but redraw it with one change. That could be a connected "Co.", a cut in the "C", or a swash that becomes the ampersand. Tune the spacing by eye.
4. **Make three lockups.** Make a horizontal one for the sign, a stacked one for boxes, and the ampersand alone for the avatar and favicon. Draw a heavier, simplified version for small sizes.
5. **Make the tagline optional.** It should be a separate line that you use on boxes and menus, never part of the logo. Set it in readable type, not tiny caps. I'd also test a less generic line. "Baked with love" is also what everyone says.
6. **Build a small system.** Use the score mark as a repeating pattern on tissue paper and box lids, with cream, pink and brown as the color roles. Then someone could recognize you even with the logo covered.

**Test before committing:** Check it solid black on white, then reversed on dark, then at 32 pixels wide. If it reads in all three, add color last.
