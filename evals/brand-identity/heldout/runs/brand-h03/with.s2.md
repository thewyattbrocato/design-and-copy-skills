## What's going on

Your logo uses every standard bakery signal, and none of them belongs only to you.

- **Wheat stalk:** this is the trade's stock icon. It tells people "bakery" but not "Crumb & Co."
- **Script wordmark in an untouched stock style:** it is readable, but it's the default for "warm and handmade".
- **Cream and pink banner with a gold outline:** this is more decoration on top of the script and the wheat. A thin gold outline also disappears at small sizes. On an Instagram avatar or a stamped box it turns to mush.
- **Tagline in tiny caps under the mark:** it's locked into the logo, so it shrinks to nothing on most surfaces. The wording ("baked fresh with love") is also one of the most common bakery lines.
- **Too many ideas:** the script, wheat, banner, outline and tagline each compete. You can't say the mark in one sentence without "and", so people remember none of it.

If you put another bakery's name on it, nobody would notice. That is what "looks like every other bakery" means.

## What I'd change

I'm assuming you're a neighborhood bakery that is warm, a little playful and handmade. I'm also assuming the logo has to work on a sign, a box lid and a small Instagram avatar. I haven't seen the logo itself, only your description.

1. **Pick one idea.** Your name already has one: **the ampersand.** "Crumb & Co." as a wordmark with a custom-drawn ampersand. For example, an ampersand with a bite taken out of it, or one whose loop reads as a soft knot of dough. In one sentence: "the ampersand with a bite out of it." Your wordmark becomes ownable because of that one detail.
2. **Draw the wordmark properly.** Keep the brown and the warmth, but use a sturdier, heavier letterform than a thin script. Adjust the spacing by eye and make one custom change (the ampersand). It should read in a second, even on a box side or a sign seen from across the street.
3. **Drop the wheat, banner and gold outline.** The core mark should be flat and solid, with no effects. Wheat can survive as a pattern on tissue paper or box interiors, which is a better job for it.
4. **Make the ampersand the symbol.** It becomes the avatar, stamp, sticker and favicon on its own. That gives you a small version that isn't the full logo shrunk down.
5. **Make the tagline a separate, optional line.** Use it on boxes and menus, not inside the logo. Consider rewriting it too, since "baked fresh with love" could be any bakery's line.
6. **Choose color last.** Keep dark brown as the anchor, but add one color that rival bakeries in your area don't use, instead of cream, pink and gold. Check what the shops near you use before picking.

## How to test it

- Draw it in solid black on white, then reversed on dark brown, then at 32 px. It should still read.
- Put a rival bakery's name in your logo. If it still fits, it's still too generic.
- Ask 10 to 15 regular customers which bakery they'd say it belongs to. I can't tell you how recognizable your current logo is, so that test is the evidence for how much of it to keep.

I'd redraw rather than just tweak, because the problem is that the logo has no single distinctive idea, not that any one detail is off. I can sketch the ampersand wordmark and symbol as SVG, with black, reversed and small versions, if you'd like.
