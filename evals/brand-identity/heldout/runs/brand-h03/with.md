**Why it looks like every other bakery:** the logo is a stack of category signals, and none of them belongs only to Crumb & Co.

- **Wheat stalk:** this is the bakery stock icon. It says "bakery" and nothing about you.
- **Script wordmark in brown:** this is the default bakery typeface and color, and it's probably untouched stock type.
- **Cream-and-pink banner:** this is the default soft-bakery palette.
- **Gold outline:** this adds a fourth layer on top of the other three.
- **Tagline in tiny caps:** this is boilerplate wording that fits any bakery. It's also locked inside the mark, so it's unreadable at small sizes.

That makes at least four ideas competing in one mark: wheat, script, banner and outline. Cover the name and it could be any bakery, and a rival's name would fit it just as well. I haven't seen the actual file, so this is based on your description. I'm also assuming the goal is warm, handmade and neighborhood-friendly.

**What I'd change (rebuild the mark, keep the name):**

1. **Pick one idea, stated in one sentence without "and".** Make it specific to *Crumb & Co.* The name gives you material that no other bakery has: the crumb, or the ampersand. For example, a custom ampersand with a scored-loaf cut through it, or a single crumb shape as the dot or counter in the wordmark.
2. **Drop the wheat, banner and outline.** Each one is a separate idea or effect. The mark should work as a flat, solid shape.
3. **Customize the wordmark.** Use a sturdier typeface, or a script with one custom change such as a ligature, a cut, or a reshaped "C" or "&". Make it readable in a second.
4. **Remove the tagline from the mark.** Keep it as an optional separate line, and rewrite it in your own voice. "Baked fresh with love" is what every bakery says.
5. **Build three versions.** Make a horizontal lockup for the sign and boxes, a stacked one for packaging and a symbol-only version for the Instagram avatar. Check each in solid black, reversed on dark, and at about 32 px. The current mark has hairlines and a small tagline that would die at avatar size.
6. **Choose color last.** Keep dark brown if you like it, since it's fine on its own. Pair it with one distinctive accent instead of the cream, pink and gold set. Pink and gold are probably the biggest source of the "generic" feel.
7. **Build a small system so you aren't relying on the logo alone.** Define a color role, one graphic device (for example, the scored-loaf cut as a pattern on boxes), a photo style, and a layout rule.

**Refine or replace?** Replace, but only because the evidence supports it. People already tell you it's generic, and it doesn't separate you from rivals. If customers recognize it strongly in your neighborhood, you might keep a cue or two, such as the brown. I don't know whether they do, so ask a handful of regulars which part of the logo they'd pick out.

**Next test:** put the new mark next to the logos of the two bakeries closest to you, then cover the names. If yours is still easy to tell apart, it's working.
