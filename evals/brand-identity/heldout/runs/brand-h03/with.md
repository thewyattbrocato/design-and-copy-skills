**What's going on:** the logo is made of the bakery category's default parts, and it has too many of them. I'm working from your description, not the artwork.

- **Stock ingredients.** A script wordmark, a wheat stalk, a cream-and-pink banner, brown and gold: each is the first choice for a bakery. Together they read as "a bakery" and not as Crumb & Co.
- **Too many ideas.** Script, wheat, banner, outline and tagline each ask for attention, so none of them wins.
- **It fails at small sizes.** A thin gold outline, a detailed wheat drawing and tiny-caps text turn to mush on an Instagram avatar or the corner of a box. They also don't survive one-color printing, like a stamp or a sticker.
- **The tagline is inside the mark.** "Baked fresh with love" is also what every bakery says, so it adds nothing distinctive.
- **Nothing is yours.** Your name has a good hook, the crumb, and the design never uses it.

**What I'd change** (assuming a small, independent, warm and handmade bakery):

1. **Choose one idea.** For example, "a single crumb breaking off the ampersand," or "a C with a bite taken out." If you need "and" to describe the mark, it's still two ideas.
2. **Cut the wheat, banner and outline.** Wheat is the trade's stock icon, and the banner and outline only add decoration.
3. **Redraw the wordmark.** Use a sturdier, heavier letterform with one custom detail, like the crumb on the ampersand. Check that it reads in a second and works upside down.
4. **Make it work solid black on white first.** Then check it reversed on dark and at 16–32 px. Add color last.
5. **Pick a color that leads.** Cream, pink, gold and brown is the category palette. Keep the dark brown as the text color if you like it, and add one distinctive ground color that other bakeries nearby aren't using.
6. **Make three versions.** Use horizontal for the shop sign, stacked for boxes, and symbol-only (the crumb or the bitten C) for the Instagram avatar and favicon.
7. **Make the tagline a separate, optional line.** It should never be locked into the logo. I'd also rewrite it to say something only you could say.
8. **Extend it beyond the logo.** A scatter of crumb dots as a box pattern, one photo style and a fixed layout would make you recognizable even with the logo covered.

**Refine or replace:** I'd replace it. "Looks like every other bakery" is a reason to replace, and the issue is the whole concept, not a detail. Keep the name and, if you want continuity, the brown. Before you commit, ask a handful of regulars which bakery they think of when they see the logo. That will tell you more than my opinion.
