Going only on your description (I haven't seen the logo), the problem is probably that every element is a standard bakery signal, and together they say "bakery" without saying "Crumb & Co."

**What's going on**

- **Each choice is the category default.** Script wordmark, wheat stalk, brown, cream and pink, and a banner are what people expect from a bakery, so the logo reads as a template.
- **Nothing in it comes from your name or your shop.** Wheat could belong to any bakery. "Crumb" is a distinctive word, and the logo doesn't use it.
- **Too many layers.** Wordmark, illustration, banner, outline and tagline are five things competing. A logo with that much stacked together has no clear focal point.
- **It won't survive small sizes.** Fine script, thin gold outlines and tiny-caps taglines turn to mush on an Instagram avatar, a box lid or a sign seen from across the street. You use it in three very different places, and each needs a different scale.
- **The tagline is filler.** "Baked fresh with love since our first loaf" is generic. Nearly every bakery says something like it, and tiny caps under a logo mostly go unread.

**What I'd change**

1. **Pick one idea and build the logo around it.** The obvious one is the crumb. That could be a simple mark, a torn-bread shape, or the ampersand made from bread-like forms. It's ownable in a way wheat isn't.
2. **Cut the banner, outline and wheat.** The wordmark plus one small mark is enough. Gold outlines in particular look dated and print badly on kraft or cardboard.
3. **Replace the script with a distinctive wordmark.** Either a characterful serif with some warmth, or a hand-lettered wordmark made specifically for you, not a stock script font. Make "&" the one flourish.
4. **Rethink the palette.** Keep one or two colors that you can own, such as a strong tomato red, a deep green or a butter yellow, plus a neutral. Dark brown on cream and pink is the most common bakery combination. Pick colors that look different on a shelf of competitors.
5. **Build a small system, not one lockup.** You need:
   - a full logo for signage and boxes
   - a compact mark for the Instagram avatar and stickers
   - a one-color version for stamps and tape

   Test each at small size and in one color.
6. **Move the tagline out of the logo.** Use it as separate copy on boxes or in your bio. Better still, replace it with something specific to you, such as a signature item, a neighborhood or a baking method.

**A quick test:** cover the name and put your logo next to five local bakeries. If you can't tell which is yours, it needs to change. After the redesign, check that someone can pick yours out in a squint at thumbnail size.
