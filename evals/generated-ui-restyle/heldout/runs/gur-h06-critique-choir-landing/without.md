## What gives it away

**Visual tells**
- Dark slate background with a purple/indigo/pink palette. This is the strongest tell.
- A blurred glowing orb behind the hero, gradient-clipped headline text, and a CTA with a colored glow shadow.
- A pill badge with a sparkle icon above the H1.
- Glassmorphism cards (`bg-white/5 backdrop-blur`) with gradient icon tiles and a `hover:-translate-y-1` lift.
- Everything is centered, with a symmetrical three-card feature row.

**Copy tells**
- "Supercharge," "all-in-one platform that empowers," "streamline," "focus on what matters most," "Lightning-fast," "Effortlessly." None of it says what Stavewise does that a shared Google Drive doesn't.
- The icons don't mean anything. Sparkles stands for attendance, a shield stands for "insights," and a lightning bolt stands for sending files.
- There's no picture of the product anywhere.

**Content problems that matter more than style**
- **"Trusted by 1,800+ choirs worldwide" next to a 38-school pilot.** If that number isn't real, fix it today. It's the one thing here that could actually hurt you, and teachers talk to each other.
- **"AI-powered."** Many teachers are wary of AI. If there's no real AI in the product, drop the claim. If there is, say what it does.
- The audience matters too. Choir directors are busy and skeptical, and they're often on school laptops. A neon dark-mode look reads as crypto or dev tooling, not as something built for a rehearsal room.

## What I'd change first, in order

1. **Fix the social proof.** Replace it with something true and specific, like "Piloting in 38 schools," and add one real quote from a director with their name and school.
2. **Rewrite the headline around your actual pitch.** For example: *"Know who's learned their part before the concert."* Follow it with a subhead that lists the real jobs: hand out parts and practice tracks, take attendance, see who's behind. Cut the badge entirely.
3. **Replace the three cards with a real screenshot.** Show the screen where a director sees each section and who hasn't practiced. That's your best asset, and nothing on the page shows it yet. Then turn the features into plain rows with concrete descriptions, like "Send each section its own part and track," not icon cards.
4. **Then change the look.** Use a light, warm background, one accent color, and a typeface with some character (a serif for headings suits music). Remove the orb, the gradients, and the glass. Anything that looks like sheet music or a rehearsal folder would feel more like your product than a glow effect.
