## What gives it away

**Visual tells (almost all of them are here)**
- Dark `slate-950` background with a purple/indigo/pink palette. This is the default look of AI-generated UIs.
- The blurred purple blob behind the hero (`blur-3xl` circle).
- A gradient-clipped headline, a pill badge with a sparkle icon, and a glowing purple button shadow.
- Glassmorphism cards (`bg-white/5 backdrop-blur`) with gradient icon tiles and a `hover:-translate-y-1` lift.
- Everything is centered, and the layout is a hero followed by a 3-up icon grid.
- The icons are decorative rather than meaningful. Sparkles stands for attendance, Shield for insights, and Zap for parts. Nothing connects them to the features.

**Copy tells**
- "Supercharge," "intelligent," "all-in-one platform that empowers directors to streamline," and "focus on what matters most."
- "Lightning-fast," "Smart," "Effortlessly," and "Concert-ready insights." Any SaaS product could use these. Your real selling point is buried in the third card: knowing who hasn't learned their part before the concert.
- "AI-powered" in the badge. Unless AI is a real part of the product, it will make skeptical teachers trust you less.

**Missing**
- There's no product, no screenshot, and nothing visual about music or a rehearsal.
- There's no human voice, such as a director quote or a school name.

## The biggest problem: the trust line

"Trusted by 1,800+ choirs worldwide" doesn't match 38 pilot schools. Music teachers talk to each other, and one who asks around will find out. If the number isn't verifiable, remove it before anything else. "In pilot at 38 schools" is true, specific, and works better with this audience.

## What I'd change, in order

1. **Fix the claims.** Replace the 1,800 line with the real number. Drop the "AI-powered" badge unless you can say what the AI does.
2. **Rewrite the headline around the job.** For example: *"Know who hasn't learned their part, before the concert."* Follow it with one plain sentence: *"Hand out parts and practice tracks, take attendance in one tap, and see each singer's progress by section."*
3. **Replace the blob with a real screenshot.** Show the roster or section view with a few singers flagged as not ready. That image will do more than the rest of the page combined.
4. **Change the palette and mood.** Use a light, warm background, one accent color, and a typeface with some character. A serif for headings would fit. Remove the gradients, blur, glow, and glass. Directors are often on school laptops or projectors, where dark neon washes out.
5. **Make the features concrete.** Say "Send each section its own part and practice track," "Take attendance from your phone in under a minute," and "See who's behind, by section." Show a small screenshot or notation-style detail for each one, and drop the icon tiles.
6. **Add real proof.** Use a short quote from a pilot director (with permission) and a few school names.

Steps 1 and 2 take about ten minutes and fix most of what your cofounder is reacting to. Steps 3 to 5 are what make it look designed by someone who knows choirs.
