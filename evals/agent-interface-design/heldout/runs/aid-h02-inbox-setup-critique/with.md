**Short version:** the screen asks for a lot of preferences but never says what the assistant may do while you're away or what it will never do. Replying to clients is the risky part, and nothing limits it.

*Assumption: Quill Mail can archive and send email on a freelancer's behalf when they aren't looking, and a wrong reply to a client costs real money or goodwill.*

## What I'd change

**1. "Enable AI" on by default is a blanket grant.**
- It covers sending mail to your clients, and the screen states no maximum.
- Replace it with a one-line promise at the top: *"While you're away, Quill will sort your mail and write draft replies. It will never send anything without your OK, and it will never delete mail."*
- Auto-sending can come later as an opt-in (see 6). Until then, rename **Reply** to **Draft a reply**.

**2. The Autonomy slider (1–10, default 7) should go.**
- Nobody knows what a 7 means.
- It also conflicts with the dropdowns. If autonomy is 7 and invoices are "Ask me," which one wins?
- A high default also pushes the assistant toward acting more than the person agreed to.
- The per-category choices already express autonomy, so use those and drop the slider.

**3. Twelve dropdowns on the first screen is too much to ask.**
- Ask only what gates safe operation. Start every category on "Ask me" or "Draft," and let people tune after they've seen it work.
- The two Archive defaults are reasonable because archiving is reversible. But cold pitches can hide real leads, and freelancers live on leads. Archived mail needs a visible "Archived by Quill" list, with the doubtful items first.
- "Ignore" and "Archive" are unclear. Does ignore leave the mail in the inbox? Say so in the option label.

**4. Nothing is restated or tested before it goes live.**
- Before "Finish," show how the assistant read the setup, with examples from the person's own recent mail. For instance: *"This invoice from Dana → I'd draft a reply and ask you. This newsletter → I'd archive it."*
- Let them fix any example right there. A dry run on real mail is the best onboarding you can give this product.

**5. "Describe your tone of voice" is a blank box that most people can't fill in.**
- Offer to learn from their sent mail, then show a sample draft they can approve or correct.
- Make the field optional, so it doesn't block setup.

**6. "Finish" starts everything at once.**
- Rename it to something like **Start in review mode**.
- After a run of clean results the person can see, offer a wider scope as a proposal, for example "Send routine meeting confirmations on your behalf." Never widen it silently, and give any auto-send a hard cap (count per day and allowed recipients). I'd set the starting values by testing, not guessing.

**7. The grey footer hides the controls that matter most.**
- "Settings > Advanced > Assistant" is three levels deep, so pause, off and undo are effectively buried.
- The setup screen should say where the person will see the assistant working: a status home showing running or paused, the last action, and what it did.
- Pause and off should be one tap from there, with no reason required. Undo should be reachable from each item.

## Sketch of the revised screen

> **Let's set up your assistant**
> While you're away, Quill sorts your mail and drafts replies. It never sends without your OK and never deletes anything.
>
> **Start with these (change anytime):**
> Newsletters → Archive · Cold pitches → Archive · Everything else → Draft or ask me
> *[Edit all 12 categories]*
>
> **Your tone** (optional): *[Learn from my sent mail]* or describe it
>
> **Here's what I'd do with your last mail:** *(real examples, each correctable)*
>
> **[Start in review mode]**
> You'll see everything Quill does on the home screen, where you can pause, turn off or undo any action.

## What already works

- Starting most categories on "Ask me" is the right instinct.
- Archive is a reasonable thing to automate for newsletters.
- The friendly title is fine.
