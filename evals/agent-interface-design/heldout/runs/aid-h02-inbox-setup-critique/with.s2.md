**Assumption:** "Reply" sends real email from a freelancer's address to clients, so the worst case is a wrong or tone-deaf message to a paying client that can't be unsent. Most of my feedback follows from that.

## What works
- Most categories default to Ask me, which is the right cautious start.
- Newsletters and cold pitches default to Archive. That's cheap to reverse, so it's a good place to be bold.

## Main problems

**1. The slider and the dropdowns contradict each other.**
- If a category is set to Reply, what does autonomy 7 add? If a category is set to Ask me, does 7 override it?
- Nobody can predict what "7" does, and the number is a vibe, not a limit.
- Cut the slider. The per-category rules are the real controls.

**2. "Reply" has no cap and no safety net.**
- Nothing limits how many replies it sends, to whom, or how fast. "Enable AI" is one grant for a send-on-your-behalf agent.
- Make Reply mean "draft for my approval" at first. Add auto-send later, per category, after a run of drafts the user approved unedited.
- If auto-send stays, give it a hold of a few minutes, a daily cap, and exclusions such as new contacts and anything mentioning money.

**3. Twelve dropdowns up front is a chore.**
- Most people will click through, so the defaults become the real setup, and they haven't understood them.
- Scan the inbox and propose the rules: "I found 38 newsletters and 11 invoices. Here's what I'd do." Ask for corrections only.
- Show a short list at setup, and defer the rest.

**4. There's no preview before it goes live.**
- Add a dry run on the user's last couple of weeks of mail: "Here's what I would have done." This is the best trust-builder you have.
- Restate the rules in plain words with three real examples before Finish, for example: "A client email asking for a quote → I'll draft a reply and ask you."

**5. The tone box is a blank page.**
- Freelancers won't know how to describe their voice. Offer to learn it from their sent mail and show a sample draft to approve.
- Make the box optional, and don't block Finish on it.

**6. "Ignore" is ambiguous.** It could mean "leave it in my inbox" or "hide it". Use "Leave in inbox" so it's clear nothing happens.

**7. The footer hides the controls that matter.** "Settings > Advanced > Assistant" is three levels deep, and a freelancer who just watched it mishandle a client email needs Pause in one tap. After setup, the user needs:
- A status home showing on or paused, the last action, and what it's waiting on. Pause should be one tap from there.
- A daily digest of what it did, with the doubtful items first and an undo on each.
- "Not this one" and "never do this for this kind" on every action.
- Archived cold pitches visible for a while, since some of them are real leads.
- Off and delete options that need no reason.

## Suggested shape

1. **Title and one-line promise:** "Quill will sort and draft while you work. It won't send anything without your OK, and it never deletes."
2. **Review the proposed rules:** the 3–5 categories it found most often, each with a plain-language rule. The rest go on a "More categories" link, defaulting to Ask me.
3. **Optional tone step:** "Learn from my sent mail", or write your own.
4. **Preview:** "Here's what I'd have done this past week." Then Finish, with "Pause anytime from the Quill icon" under the button.

**Before launch**, I'd want to know whether auto-send exists at all in v1. If it doesn't, drop "Reply" as a mode and call it "Draft reply". That removes your biggest risk with no real loss, because freelancers mostly want the drafts. I haven't assumed any numbers about volume or accuracy, so test the thresholds above with real users.
