You don't need a values argument. Make the asymmetry concrete with his own numbers.

**1. The payoff is asymmetric.** Automation's upside is capped at the time it saves. Its downside is capped only if you build caps. Suppose a typical customer spends $4k/month on ads and the owner saves 3 hours/week, about $600/month at $50/hour. One bad week of runaway or misallocated spend could burn $1,000 to $2,000, and a customer who sees that is likely to churn. Plug in your real figures, but the shape holds: a month of savings can be erased by one incident, and nothing offsets that unless you cap the downside.

**2. Small accounts can't tell you whether the agent is working.** A small shop might get 50 to 200 conversions a month, which is too few to separate a good spend decision from noise. If the agent has full control on day one, it optimizes against that noise, and you won't know whether results improved or would have happened anyway. You need a baseline or holdout before you can judge the agent, and you can't build one while it's spending freely.

**3. "Handle everything" was a request for less work, not for spend authority.** Interview answers describe what people want in the abstract. What customers will delegate is what they've seen work. Ask him what the interviews say about the moment they'd hand over the wallet. Most small-business owners say they want to be hands-off, then want approval rights when a number moves. You can deliver "handle everything" as an outcome without giving up control on day one.

**4. Staged autonomy gives you the data you need to price the risk.** Propose this:
- **Stage 1 (shadow, ~4 weeks):** The agent recommends and logs what it would have done. Measure how often the owner would have approved, and compare its predicted ROAS to realized ROAS.
- **Stage 2 (approve-to-act):** One-tap approval for each change. Measure the approval rate and the error rate.
- **Stage 3 (bounded autonomy):** The agent acts within hard limits: daily and monthly spend caps, a ROAS floor, a max change per day, and a kill switch.
- **Stage 4 (full control):** Only after stage 3 shows a low error rate over a meaningful sample.

Each stage produces the error-rate data needed to justify the next one. Full control on day one means you skip the measurement that would tell you whether it's safe.

**5. Frame it as a shipping decision, not a no.** "We'll ship full control in 90 days, once shadow mode shows the agent's decisions match what owners approve at X% and its ROAS beats the holdout." That gives the CTO a date and a target, and it's a question he can actually act on.

For the one-line version: *"Full control day one means our downside is uncapped and we can't tell if it's working. Staged control means we find out the error rate before customers pay for it."*
