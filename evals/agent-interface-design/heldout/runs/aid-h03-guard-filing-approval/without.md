I'd go with approval by exception, not approval of everything.

**Why not approve all 60 a week**
- Approving 60 filings a week turns into rubber-stamping within a couple of weeks. You'd get the cost of the review without the protection, and the one bad filing would get approved along with the rest.
- The actions are reversible. Moving and renaming loses nothing, and nothing goes out to anyone. A wrong filing is an annoyance, not a disaster.

**What I'd build**
1. **Auto-file the confident cases.** That means a sender or email address you've mapped to a client, or a client name or ID that clearly matches in the PDF.
2. **Queue the uncertain ones for your approval.** That covers new senders, no match, two possible clients, and unreadable PDFs. This is probably 10–20% of receipts, and it's the part where your judgment matters.
3. **Keep an action log.** Record the original filename and location, the new path, the time, and why it picked that client. Add a one-command undo. That's what makes auto-filing safe.
4. **Never overwrite.** If the destination filename already exists, add a suffix or send it to the review queue.
5. **Send a short daily or weekly digest.** List what was auto-filed, grouped by client, so you can skim it in a minute and catch mistakes.

**Start strict and loosen over time.** For the first week or two, run it in approve-everything mode. Track how often you'd have disagreed. If it's near zero for the high-confidence category, switch that category to auto. This also gives your cofounder evidence rather than an argument.

**One thing could change my answer.** If any client folders are shared with the clients themselves, a misfile could show one client's financial documents to another. That's the real risk here, and it isn't reversible once someone has seen the file. In that case I'd keep a stricter bar. For example, auto-file only on an exact sender match, and queue everything else. If the folders are private to you, the lighter approach is fine.
