**No, not as proposed.** A streak of approvals doesn't show the review is unnecessary. It shows the autopilot has been right so far. The review exists for the run where it's wrong, and that run is what a streak can't predict. Money that's sent to employees the next day is hard to recover, and these customers are small enough that one bad run (a doubled shift, a wrong rate, an overtime error) is a serious problem for them.

Product's premise, "they obviously just click through," is a real finding, but it points to a different fix. If people approve without reading, the review isn't doing its job, and removing it just hides that.

**What I'd do instead:**

1. **Make the review show only what needs attention.** Most runs have nothing unusual. Put the review on the exceptions: hours well above that employee's usual, a new employee, a changed pay rate, overtime, a total that differs from the last run by more than a set percentage, or a timesheet that was edited after submission. If nothing trips, the review is a short summary. If something does, the customer has to look at that line.

2. **Offer auto-send as an opt-in, with guardrails.** An owner can turn on auto-send for runs that pass those checks. It should be:
   - revocable at any time,
   - paused automatically when any check trips,
   - announced with a summary email or notification before the bank submission cutoff, with a cancel option.

3. **Force a review periodically.** For example, every tenth run, the first run after any change to employees or rates, and any run after a failed or cancelled auto-send.

4. **Measure the right thing.** A streak of approvals doesn't tell you whether the approvals were careful. Track how often people edit a run after opening the review, how often they reverse a sent run, and how often payroll corrections happen after auto-send versus manual review. Those numbers will show whether the review is working or whether it's theater.

**What would change my view:** data showing that exception-only review catches the same errors as the full review, and that customers with auto-send have no more corrections than customers who review everything. If product has that evidence, auto-send for clean runs with a cancel window becomes reasonable. "Three approvals in a row" isn't that evidence.
