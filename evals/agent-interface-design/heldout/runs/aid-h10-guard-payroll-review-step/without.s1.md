**I wouldn't do it as proposed.** Dropping the review for everyone with three straight approvals is the wrong rule. A narrower version of the idea is worth doing.

**Why the proposal fails**
- **Three approvals in a row doesn't show the runs were correct.** It shows the customer clicked three times. Fast approval is just as consistent with "the screen is useless" or "they trust it blindly" as with "the output was fine."
- **The risky runs are the unusual ones.** Autopilot is most likely to be wrong when something changes: a new hire, a termination, a raise, a missing timesheet, new bank details. A "streak" rule removes the check at those moments, because the streak was built on ordinary runs.
- **Mistakes are expensive for this customer base.** With about 8 employees, one wrong or duplicate payment is a large share of the payroll. Small businesses have little slack to absorb it. Support also says the money is hard to pull back once it lands, so the review is your last cheap checkpoint.
- **You carry the downside.** Without the review, "I never approved that" becomes a much stronger complaint. Check with legal and finance who bears the loss and the fees.

**What I'd do instead**
1. **Make the review conditional on anomalies.** If the run matches the previous one (same people, same rates, total within a set tolerance, no bank-detail changes, no new or terminated employees), approve it automatically or with a one-tap confirm. If anything differs, require the full review and highlight the differences.
2. **Make it opt-in.** Offer "auto-approve routine runs" to customers who have approved three in a row, and let them turn it on. Don't enable it for them.
3. **Add a cancel window.** If you can hold submission until the bank cutoff, send a "Your payroll goes out at 3pm. Cancel or review" notice. That recovers most of the safety the review gave you at a fraction of the friction.
4. **Fix the screen.** If people click through, the review shows too much that isn't useful. Show a "what changed since last run" summary instead of the full table.
5. **Test it before rolling out.** Run a holdback group and track payment errors, corrections, reversals, support tickets, and how often anomaly flags catch real problems. Expand only if error rates hold.

This gives Product most of what they want. Routine runs get less friction, and the review stays in place for runs where something changed.
