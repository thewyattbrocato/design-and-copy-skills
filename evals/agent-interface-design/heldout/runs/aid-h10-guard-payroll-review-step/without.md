**No, not as proposed.** A modified version could work.

**Why "three approvals in a row" is the wrong trigger**
- It shows the autopilot was right the last three times. It doesn't show this run is right. With weekly pay, three runs is only three weeks.
- The review matters on the rare run that differs: a new hire, a terminated employee still on the list, a changed rate, a missing or doubled timesheet, or a typo in hours. Those are also the runs where a customer who has been clicking through will keep clicking.
- Errors are asymmetric. Skipping the review saves a few seconds a run. A bad run sends real money out the next day, and it's hard to recover. For an 8-person business, one bad run is a large share of their cash. It also creates tax and filing problems, and awkward conversations with employees.
- Removing the screen removes the customer's explicit authorization. That raises liability and compliance questions you should check with legal, since you'd be initiating the transfers. Today you can say the customer approved the run.

**What I'd do instead**
1. **Make the review conditional, not removed.** After a streak, auto-send only when the run is routine. That means the same employees, rates within a small tolerance, hours within the usual range, no new bank details, and a total within X% of the recent average. Any deviation brings back the full review with the differences highlighted.
2. **Make it opt-in.** Offer "Auto-approve routine runs" as a setting, not a silent change. The customer then consents to it, and you aren't deciding for them that they don't care.
3. **Add a safety net.** Send a notification with a summary and a "hold this run" window before the bank cutoff. This is cheap and recovers most of the value of the review.
4. **Keep hard stops.** Always require review for the first run after any change to employees, pay rates, or bank accounts, and for totals above a cap.

**To settle the argument with data**
- Find how often approved runs are later corrected, reversed, or generate a support ticket. If the rate is near zero even for click-through customers, that supports a conditional skip. If it isn't, that settles it.
- Pilot the conditional version with a small group before rolling it out. Track post-send corrections and complaints.

**For Product:** the "they obviously just click through" observation is valid, and the review screen may have too much friction on routine runs. The fix is to make routine runs lighter and flag the unusual ones. Removing the check entirely for everyone with a streak doesn't do that.
