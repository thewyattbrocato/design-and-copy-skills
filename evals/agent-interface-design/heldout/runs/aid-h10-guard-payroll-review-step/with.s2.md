**No, not as proposed.** Three approvals in a row doesn't show the customer checked the runs. It shows they clicked three times. The reasoning also doesn't match the risk. A payroll run sends money, and Support says it's hard to pull back. The rule you'd be removing is the only point where a person can catch a mistake before it becomes one.

**Why the trigger is wrong**
- **Clicking through is a different thing from the run being right.** The runs that matter are the ones that differ from the last three: a new hire, a terminated employee, a rate change, a missing or duplicated timesheet. Those are exactly the runs where a customer on a streak would no longer see the screen.
- **Small businesses feel errors more.** With about 8 employees, one wrong amount is a visible share of someone's paycheck and of the owner's cash. The owner is usually also the person who would have to handle the fallout.
- **The cost is lopsided.** The review costs the customer a few seconds. A bad auto-sent run costs a clawback, an angry employee, and possibly a fee or a compliance issue. I'm not putting numbers on that, but the direction is clear.
- **Nobody would catch it afterward.** If the plan is "the customer will notice the next day," the money has already moved.

**What I'd do instead**
1. **Keep review as the default and make it faster.** Show what changed since the last run: new or removed people, rate changes, hours that differ a lot from the usual, and the total. A run that matches the usual pattern becomes a quick confirmation. That addresses the "they just click through" complaint without removing the check.
2. **Make skipping review an opt-in that the customer proposes to themselves.** After a streak of clean runs, offer it: "Send future runs automatically when they match your usual pattern." Show the rule in plain words with examples of what would and wouldn't trigger it. Don't turn it on silently.
3. **Cap it.** Auto-send only when the run is within stated limits, such as the same people, no rate changes, and a total within a set range of recent runs. The limits are an assumption for you to set. Any run outside them goes back to full review.
4. **Add a hold before the money moves.** Send a "this run goes out at X unless you stop it" notice with a visible cancel. This only works if your bank cutoff leaves a real window, so check that with Support or your payments team.
5. **Make it easy to turn off, with no reason required.** Put the control on the status screen and in every auto-send message.
6. **Report each auto-sent run.** Include the people, amounts and anything unusual, with the unusual items first.

**Before anyone builds this, ask**
- What share of past runs had a correction or a complaint, and how many were caught at the review screen? I don't have that data. It's the number that would settle the argument.
- How long is the window between "Approve" and funds being committed? That sets what a hold can do.
- Who bears the cost of a bad auto-sent run: the customer, or Bluepeak?

If Product's real goal is fewer clicks, the lighter review in step 1 gets most of that. Dropping review entirely only makes sense if the data shows the review screen almost never catches anything and the hold in step 4 is workable.
