**Don't publish as written.** The arithmetic has three problems, and the annual offer has a claim problem.

**1. Agency is priced per page, but the load comes from check frequency.**

Max load per tier, in page-checks per month:

| Tier | Price | Max page-checks/mo | Price per 1,000 page-checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | 100 | n/a |
| Starter | $9 | ~430 (100 pages weekly) | ~$20.90 |
| Team | $19 | 150,000 (5,000 daily) | ~$0.13 |
| Agency | $249 | 72,000,000 (100,000 hourly) | ~$0.003 |

Agency gives 24× the checks of Team (hourly vs. daily) and costs about 37× less per check. If crawling is most of your cost, a full Agency account can cost far more than $249 to serve. Team is also fine on the page count, so a Team account that runs daily on its full 5,000 pages is the buyer to watch. Fix this by pricing check frequency, or by capping hourly checks, or by pricing Agency per check-hour, not only per page. Without your crawl cost per check, I can't say how big the loss is. Compare your cost per 1,000 page-checks to the last column.

**2. Free and Starter nearly duplicate each other.**

Both are 1 site and 100 pages. Starter adds weekly checks instead of monthly, and "1 user" (likely the default anyway). A solo buyer with one site has little reason to pay $9 for that. Either make Free clearly narrower (for example, no alerts or export, if the product has those) or make Starter add something a single-site owner actually values. A cheap tier must truly lack what the next one adds.

**3. Team is the tier your buyers fit.**

Freelancers with 2 to 5 client sites land at Team: 5 sites, 5,000 pages, daily checks, $19. That is the core of the page. The price per site is $3.80 for Team and $4.98 for Agency, so the jump from Team to Agency is steep for a mid-size buyer (6 to 49 sites) who has no tier in between. Check whether you have such buyers before adding a tier.

**The annual offer.**

- "55% off, for a limited time" that has run for two years is not a time-limited offer. Remove the "limited time" wording, or state the annual price as the standing price. Fair-persuasion and honest-claims both cover this.
- Annual at 55% off means a customer pays about 5.4 months of monthly price for 12 months of service. Annual only beats monthly revenue if the average monthly customer would leave within about 5 months. Check your retention data. A standing annual discount of about two months free (≈17%) is a common default; that gives Starter $90, Team $190, Agency $2,490 per year.

**Recommended changes before Monday:**

- Price or cap check frequency on Agency (or make hourly a premium add-on).
- Make Free and Starter clearly different, or cut Starter.
- Replace the annual offer with a standing discount sized from your retention data, and drop the "limited time" line.

**Assumed:** Crawl cost is the main cost, as you said. Most Team buyers use far less than their page limit, which I haven't verified. Free users cost little per month. I used no competitor prices.

**Would change if:** Crawl cost per 1,000 checks is near zero (the Agency risk shrinks a lot). Your data shows most buyers hit their page limit, which would make the Team-to-Agency gap a bigger problem. Monthly retention is short, which would make the annual discount sensible.
