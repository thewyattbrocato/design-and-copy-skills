I'd charge per teacher in three tiers set by active student count. That's per student in spirit, but the bill is predictable.

| Tier | Group | Price (assumed) | Teachers | Monthly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | up to 12 active students | $6 | ~360 | $2,160 |
| Studio | 13 to 30 active students | $9 | ~405 | $3,645 |
| Large | 31+ active students, plus what large studios need (see below) | $19 | ~135 | $2,565 |
| **Total** | | | | **$8,370** |

Today's total is 900 × $9 = $8,100, so this is about 3% more revenue with no one lost. The middle 45% see no change. The smallest teachers pay a third less, and the biggest pay about twice as much.

**Why this unit:**
- **Per student** tracks the value a teacher gets, because their income grows with their student count. You can measure it, and it doesn't shrink as you grow.
- **Per lesson booked** is the one to avoid. Bills swing month to month, so teachers can't predict them. It also taxes the behavior you want, which is using the app more, and it gives a teacher a reason to book lessons off the platform.
- **Pure per-student metering** makes the bill change every time a student joins or leaves. Bands give the same fairness with one number to remember.

**Details that matter:**
- Count a student as active if they had a lesson in the last 60 days. That stops teachers parking inactive students to stay in a lower band.
- A tier needs something the one below lacks, or it's just a bigger quota. The Large tier should hold what big studios actually want, such as multiple teachers on one account, or a billing and attendance report. It should also include faster email support. They email you the most, and this makes that cost visible and paid for. Solo and Studio get help docs and normal-speed email.
- Don't move the 135 big teachers from $9 to $19 overnight. Tell them now, keep them at $9 for a stated period (say 90 days), and ship the Large-only features before the new price starts. Move the smaller teachers' price down immediately, since there's nothing to resist there.
- Watch large-teacher churn for the first two months. If more than about 4 of the 135 leave, Large at $19 is bringing in less than it costs. In that case, drop it to $15.

Assumed: the prices are placeholders I picked, since I have no evidence of what your teachers would pay. Server and support cost is flat per teacher, as you said, with the extra support load from big studios. Your goal is fairness at roughly neutral revenue, not maximum revenue. Teacher counts come from your 40/45/15 split of 900.

Would change if:
- **Large-tier price:** Ask 8 to 10 big studios what they'd pay and why. If most say $25 or more, raise it. If they cluster near $12, hold it at $15.
- **Solo-tier price:** If most small teachers use the app for only a few students and are price-sensitive, go to $5. That drops revenue to about $8,010, so you'd need the Large price to offset it.
- **One flat price:** If big studios don't use the app any differently from small ones, a flat price is defensible and the tiers add complexity for little gain.
