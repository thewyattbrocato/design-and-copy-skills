I'd charge per teacher in three tiers, based on active student count.

| Tier | Active students | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | up to 12 | $7/mo |
| Studio | 13-30 | $12/mo |
| Pro | 31+ (unlimited) | $24/mo |

If nobody churned, that comes to about $10.6k/month against $8.1k now (360×$7 + 405×$12 + 135×$24), roughly 30% more. Treat the prices as starting points to test, since I'm guessing at willingness to pay.

**Why not per lesson:** It's the worst of the three. Your costs don't scale with lessons, and the bill would swing with holidays, sick weeks and summer. Teachers would also feel taxed for doing their job. Per-lesson pricing makes sense for a marketplace that takes a cut of payments, and you aren't one.

**Why not pure per-student:** It tracks value fairly well, but the bill changes every time a student joins or quits. Teachers hate that, and you'd spend support time on billing questions. Tiers give you most of the fairness with a predictable bill.

**Why tiers fit your situation:**
- Your tier boundaries match your segments, and the big studios are the ones who email you most. A higher price for that group also pays for the extra support.
- A teacher with 55 students probably earns a few thousand dollars a month from teaching. $24 is about 1% of that, so there may be room to go higher, maybe $29.
- Small teachers get a price cut, which fixes the unfairness that's bothering you.

**Details that matter:**
- **Define "active":** For example, a student with a lesson booked in the last 60 days. Otherwise teachers will archive students to stay under a threshold, or you'll end up arguing about stale rosters.
- **Use soft limits:** If someone goes over their tier, show an upgrade prompt rather than locking them out mid-term.
- **The middle tier is the risk:** It's 45% of your customers, and they'd see a 33% increase. Consider $10 instead of $12 if you want less churn risk. The difference is only about $800/month at your current numbers.
- **Don't raise prices on existing customers all at once:** Put new signups on the new plans first. Keep current teachers at $9 for 6-12 months, or move only the ones who would pay less. Then watch conversion and churn before you migrate the rest.
- **Annual billing:** Offer roughly two months free. It helps cash flow and reduces churn.

If you can pull the actual distribution of students per teacher, I'd check how many teachers sit at 10-14 and 28-34. Many teachers clustered just below a threshold means it should move.
