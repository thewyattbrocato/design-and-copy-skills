**I'd charge per teacher, in three tiers set by active students: $7, $12 and $22 a month.** All three prices are starting points for you to test, not measured numbers.

| Tier | Who it's for | Active students | Price | Teachers (of 900) | Monthly revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | Part-timers and new teachers | up to 12 | $7 | 360 | $2,520 |
| Studio | Established solo teachers | 13–30 | $12 | 405 | $4,860 |
| Pro | Large studios | 31+ | $22 | 135 | $2,970 |
| **Total** | | | | | **$10,350** |

Today's revenue is 900 × $9 = $8,100, so this is about 28% more if nobody leaves. Plausible ranges are $6–8, $10–14 and $18–30.

**Why tiers by student count, not the other two**
- **Per lesson booked** punishes the product for working. Bills would swing with holidays and exam seasons, and teachers can't predict what they'll pay.
- **Per student** makes teachers hesitate to add students. It also gives you a fiddly monthly bill that moves every time a student joins or drops, for a product that costs you the same to run either way.
- **Student-count tiers** track the value a teacher feels, are easy to predict, and you can measure them. Count students with a lesson in the last 60 days, so a dormant roster doesn't push someone up a tier.

**Why the Pro price can be higher**
- Pro is the group whose support cost is highest, since the big studios email you the most. Give them priority support, which is a real thing that Starter and Studio lack. If you have staff or multi-teacher accounts, put those here too.
- Assumed: a teacher with 30 students at about $40 a lesson grosses roughly $4,800 a month. $22 is about 0.5% of that, so it is a small cost for them.

**Sanity check on churn**
- The new prices leave you $2,250 a month above today's $8,100.
- That cushion covers losing about 187 Studio teachers (46% of the group) before you fall below current revenue. The 360 Starter teachers moving down to $7 costs you $720 a month, and that is already counted in the table.
- This is a fairness fix and a cheaper way in for new teachers, so I'd make it the first change.

**Existing customers**
- Everyone stays at $9 for 6 months from the announcement (assumed; pick the date). That gives you six months to see how teachers react before the new prices bite.
- Starter teachers drop to $7 immediately.
- Studio teachers move to $12 after the 6 months, which is a $3 rise. Pro teachers move to $22 after the 6 months, which is a $13 rise. Tell Pro teachers first and add the support benefit when you do.
- Give a student-count grace period (say 30 days over a limit) so nobody is bumped up a tier for a single busy month.

**What to watch**
- Cancellations in the Studio and Pro groups over the first 90 days.
- How many Starter teachers sit at 10–12 students. If many cluster just under the limit, the 12-student line is in the wrong place.
- Reverse or soften the Pro price if more than about 15% of that group cancels or complains. At that point you are losing more than the extra $13 brings in.

I wouldn't add a free tier. Every free teacher would cost you the same servers and support as a paying one, and $7 is already a low entry price.

**Assumed:** your costs are flat per teacher, teachers' lesson prices are as above, and the mix of 40/45/15% holds.

**Would change if:**
- A handful of calls with Pro studios show they'd balk at $22. Ask what they'd pay and why, then watch what they actually do.
- Your support cost per Pro teacher turns out to be many times the others. Then a higher Pro price or a support add-on makes more sense.
- Usage is more even across teachers than you think. Then a single flat price of about $10–11 would be simpler and nearly as good.
