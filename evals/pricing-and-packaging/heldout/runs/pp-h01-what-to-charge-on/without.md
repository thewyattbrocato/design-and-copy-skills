I'd charge per teacher in three tiers, based on active student count.

**Why not the other two**
- **Per lesson booked:** Bills swing month to month, and teachers feel it as a tax on their income. Summer and holidays make it worse. It's also the hardest to forecast for you.
- **Pure per student:** It's fair in theory, but a bill that changes every time someone enrolls or quits is annoying. It also gives teachers a reason to avoid adding students to the app.
- **Tiers:** Bills stay predictable, small teachers pay less, and big studios pay more. Your three groups (under 12, 12 to 30, over 30) already suggest the tiers.

**Starting prices**

| Tier | Active students | Price | Teachers (of ~900) | Monthly revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | up to 12 | $6 | ~360 | $2,160 |
| Studio | 13 to 30 | $12 | ~405 | $4,860 |
| Pro | 31+ | $22 | ~135 | $2,970 |

That comes to about $9,990 a month, against $8,100 now. It's roughly 23% more if nobody leaves. The Starter cut costs you about $1,080, and the Studio and Pro increases more than cover it. This ignores churn, so treat it as a ceiling.

**Details that matter**
- **Count "active" students**, meaning those with a lesson in the last 60 days or so. Otherwise teachers will feel punished for keeping old students on the roster.
- **Use a soft limit.** If someone hits 13 students, give them a grace period and a nudge to upgrade instead of a hard wall.
- **Give the top tier a reason to cost more than size alone.** Your big studios email you the most, so put priority support there. Multi-teacher features or bulk messaging would also fit.
- **Grandfather existing Studio and Pro teachers** at $9 for a few months, with a clear heads-up. The Starter cut can start right away, and it will win goodwill.
- **Check the real distribution before you lock in the cutoffs.** If many teachers sit at 11 to 13 or 29 to 31, the lines will cause friction. Move them to a gap in your data.

I'm working from your 40/45/15 split and nothing else, so the revenue numbers are only as good as that. The prices are a starting point to test, not a final answer. If you can pull a student-count histogram, I can help tune the tier edges and prices.
