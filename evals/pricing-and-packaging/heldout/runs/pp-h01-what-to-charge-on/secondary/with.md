**Recommendation: charge per active student, in two plans split by who buys.** Per-lesson pricing punishes teachers for being busy, and flat pricing ignores the gap you already see. Active students are predictable for the teacher, easy for you to measure, and track both the value they get and how much they use the app.

- **Solo, $9/month (unchanged):** one teacher login, up to 12 active students. The 40% under 12 keep their price, so the largest group sees no change.
- **Studio, $14/month base:** includes up to 30 active students, multiple teacher logins, shared rooms or calendar, and priority support. Each student above 30 adds $0.50.

Priority support is the part that addresses your cost problem: the big studios generate the email, so the plan that includes the expensive support is the one they pay for.

**Arithmetic (prices and student averages are my assumptions, not your data):**

| Group | Teachers | Now | Proposed | Monthly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Under 12 (Solo) | 360 | $9 | $9 | $3,240 |
| 12–30 (Studio) | 405 | $9 | $14 | $5,670 |
| 31+ (Studio, assumed avg 50 students) | 135 | $9 | $24 ($14 + 20 × $0.50) | $3,240 |
| **Total** | **900** | **$8,100** | | **$12,150 (+50%)** |

**Assumed:** the $14 Studio base and $0.50 overage are guesses at what the mid group will pay; I have no willingness-to-pay data. The 31+ group averages 50 students. Costs per account are roughly equal, as you said, but I don't have the numbers, so I haven't checked that the Solo price covers its cost.

**Would change if:**
- **The Studio features don't matter to the 12–30 group.** Many of them may be solo teachers with 20 students who don't need multiple logins. Then the $5 gap has no reason behind it, and the Studio plan needs a feature that group actually asks for. Check this before launch by asking 10–15 teachers in that band what they'd pay for.
- **Teachers at 12–13 students hit a $5 cliff.** Either raise the Solo cap to about 15, or switch Solo to $9 plus a small per-student charge above 12.
- **Support tickets from large studios cost more than the overage covers.** Then price the overage higher or limit priority support to a set number of hours.
- **Many 12–30 teachers refuse the increase.** Keep them at $9 for a stated period, and watch churn in that group for 60–90 days before deciding whether to hold or reverse.
