I'd charge **per teacher, in tiers based on active students**. Don't bill per lesson, and don't bill strictly per student.

**Why this shape**

- **Per lesson** is the worst fit. Booking volume swings week to week, so teachers can't predict their bill, and it charges them more for being busy. Your costs don't scale with lessons anyway.
- **Pure per student** is fair in principle, but it makes the bill hard to predict and invites teachers to undercount. Tiers keep the fairness and make the price easy to understand.
- **Your costs are mostly flat per account**, so the tiers can't be justified by server cost. The honest justification is value (a 55-student studio gets more from the product) and support load (big studios email more). Say that to customers. Don't claim the tiers track infrastructure cost.

**A starting structure** (numbers are placeholders to test, not a researched price)

| Tier | Active students | Price/mo | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | up to 15 | $6 | Core scheduling |
| Studio | 16 to 40 | $12 | Core plus priority email support |
| Studio+ | 41+ | $25, or quote | Priority support, onboarding call, multiple calendars or staff if you have them |

Define "active student" clearly, for example a student with a lesson in the last 60 days or on the roster and not archived. Otherwise you'll get disputes.

**Rough revenue check** using your distribution, with 40% Solo, 45% Studio, 15% Studio+:

- Current: 900 × $9 = **$8,100/mo**
- Proposed: 360 × $6 + 405 × $12 + 135 × $25 = **~$10,400/mo** (about +28%)

The numbers depend on where the breakpoints fall within your 12 to 30 group, so check them against your real student counts before deciding.

**Things to watch**

- **Small teachers see a cut from $9 to $6.** That's most of your base, so it's the group that matters for the fairness goal, and it's a real revenue give-up (~$1,100/mo). It's the price of making the bill feel fair. It could be partly offset by a price increase on the Studio tier, which is already a good deal for them.
- **Don't raise prices on existing customers overnight.** Grandfather current teachers for 3 to 6 months, or move them to a tier only when their student count crosses a boundary.
- **Put the support cost into the top tier.** Your big-studio support load is the real cost driver, so charge for priority support instead of absorbing it.
- **Offer an annual discount** (around 2 months free) to smooth cash flow and lower churn.
- **Test before rolling out.** Show the new pricing to 20 to 30 teachers across tiers, or to new signups only, and watch churn in the small-teacher group in particular.
