**Recommendation: charge by active roster size, in three bands, with the band set by the number of students a teacher has active.** Don't charge per lesson, because a teacher's bill would swing with their busy and slow months. Per-student billing is the fairest on paper, but it makes every new student a price change, and the 55-student teacher would pay far more than support costs justify.

| Band (active students) | Share of 900 | Teachers | Price (assumed) | Monthly revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solo (up to 12) | ~40% | ~360 | $9 | $3,240 |
| Studio (13–30) | ~45% | ~405 | $15 | $6,075 |
| Studio+ (31+) | ~15% | ~135 | $29 | $3,915 |
| **Total** | | ~900 | | **$13,230** vs $8,100 now (+63%) |

With 10% of accounts lost at the new prices, the total is about $11,900, still +47%. Those churn and conversion rates are guesses, not data.

- **Solo stays at $9.** The 40% who are smallest keep the price they already know, and it removes the complaint that small teachers subsidise large ones.
- **Existing teachers stay at $9 for a stated period, such as six months,** then move to their band. That delays the revenue above, so count it as a second-half-of-year number.
- **The 12/13 and 30/31 edges are cliffs.** A teacher at 13 students pays $6 more than one at 12. If that causes complaints, use a base of $9 plus a small per-student charge above 12 instead.
- **Studio+ may need its own pricing.** The 30+ group is where support email concentrates. If those accounts are businesses with several teacher logins, they are a different buyer. Offer them a studio plan priced per teacher seat, not a higher single-teacher band.

Assumed: current server and support cost per account is roughly the same across bands, as you said; the 30+ group's extra support load is not yet costed; band prices are starting guesses with no willingness-to-pay data behind them.

Would change if: support cost for the 30+ group is much higher than $29 covers (raise that band or move studios to seat pricing); teachers at 13–30 students churn at a much higher rate after the rise (go back to a smaller step for that band); or a short survey of about 15 teachers per band shows they would not pay the proposed amounts.
