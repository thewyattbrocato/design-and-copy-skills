I'd charge per teacher, in three tiers keyed to **active students**. Per-student billing penalizes growth, and per-lesson bills are unpredictable for teachers and hard for you to forecast. Tiers match your cohorts and are simple to explain.

| Tier | Active students | Price/mo | Teachers (est.) | Monthly revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | Up to 12 | $7 | ~360 | ~$2,520 |
| Studio | 13 to 30 | $12 | ~405 | ~$4,860 |
| Studio Plus | 31+ | $25 | ~135 | ~$3,375 |
| **Total** | | | ~900 | **~$10,755** (vs. $8,100 now, about +33%) |

Why these choices:

- **Solo drops to $7.** That's the fairness fix you want, and a lower entry point helps acquisition. It costs about $720/mo, which is acceptable.
- **Studio goes to $12**, a meaningful increase for the middle group. Your largest cohort is the most likely to churn, so watch it closely.
- **Studio Plus carries a higher price for a real cost.** Your big studios generate the most support volume, so make it visible in the tier: priority support, or a support SLA. That makes the price easier to defend than a bare number.
- **Count active students**, meaning students with a lesson booked in the last 30 days or a non-archived roster entry. Teachers who pause a student shouldn't pay for them.

Before you roll it out:

1. **Grandfather existing teachers.** Give 60 to 90 days' notice and keep current pricing until then, or let them lock in for a few months. Email the big studios personally first; they're the most likely to push back and the most valuable to keep.
2. **Check the numbers against your data.** The 40/45/15 split is by teacher count, so confirm the revenue math with actual account data. The tier boundaries at 12 and 30 are where your cohorts naturally break, which is convenient.
3. **Test the price before committing.** A short survey or a few teachers on the new tiers will show how sensitive the 12 to 30 group is. Van Westendorp-style questions work well here.

If you want a single line for the pitch: "You pay for the students you teach, not a flat fee, and small teachers pay less."
