I'd charge by **active students, in three tiers**, not per student or per lesson.

| Tier | Active students | Price/mo | Teachers (approx.) | Monthly revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | up to 12 | $5 | ~360 | $1,800 |
| Studio | 13–30 | $12 | ~405 | $4,860 |
| Studio+ | 31+ | $25 | ~135 | $3,375 |
| **Total** | | | ~900 | **~$10,035** (vs. $8,100 today, about +24%) |

**Why tiers over per-student:** Pure per-student pricing (say $1 each) is the most "fair" on paper, but it makes a 6-student teacher's bill swing with enrollment and seasons, and it makes teachers hesitate to add students. Tiers are predictable, and a teacher can tell which one they're in without a calculator.

**Why tiers over per-lesson:** Lesson counts are the hardest metric to explain and the most volatile. Your costs don't scale with lessons anyway.

**Why these breakpoints:** Your costs are roughly fixed per account, but support load grows with account size. The top tier is where the emails come from, so pricing it higher and attaching something real to it (a support SLA, priority email, or a faster response time) ties the price to the cost you actually have. Charging the 31+ group more is defensible because they get more value from the scheduling and bear more of the support load.

**Details that matter:**
- **Define "active."** Count students with a lesson in the last 60–90 days, not everyone ever entered. Otherwise teachers who archive students get penalized, and that will generate complaints.
- **The Solo price cut is the easy part.** The 40% under 12 are the ones most likely to churn over price, and the cut is a clear improvement for them. Revenue rises mostly from the top two tiers.
- **Grandfather existing teachers** for 60–90 days with written notice, or let them keep $9 for a while. Price changes land badly when they're sudden.
- **Check the support claim before setting the top price.** Pull ticket counts by account size. If the big studios generate a lot more tickets, $25 may still be low; if not, $20 is enough.
- **Offer an annual discount** (around 2 months free) to smooth cash flow.

The numbers above are estimates from your rough split, and I can't see your churn or support data. The biggest unknown is how many Solo teachers leave at $5 versus stay at $9, but the change only needs a small loss in that group to still be worth making.
