**Recommendation: your partner is right. Raise the price, and test it rather than guess.**

A three-month waitlist for a year, near-total close rate and zero pushback all show demand well above your capacity at $1,800. A price that clears the market would produce some "no"s and a short queue. Neither is happening.

**Step 1: Raise new quotes to $2,500 now.** Treat this as a test, not a final answer. Quotes already sent and projects already booked stay at $1,800. Give it about 8–10 quotes.

**Step 2: Read the results.**
- Close rate stays above ~70%: you were well under the market. Test $3,200–3,500 next.
- Close rate falls to about 40–60%: this is probably close to the peak. Your queue shortens, but revenue per slot rises.
- Close rate below ~30%: step back to about $2,100–2,200.

**The arithmetic (capacity is the constraint):** You can only deliver a fixed number of videos a quarter, so lost deals cost little. If you deliver N videos and the close rate falls from ~95% to 60%, you still fill your slots as long as the waitlist holds.
- At $2,500, you break even on revenue even if you lose about 28% of the volume you'd have taken, and you can lose much more than that before capacity goes unfilled.
- With a three-month backlog, you'd have to lose most of your demand before a slot sat empty.

The risk is much smaller than it feels. The worst case is a few weeks of shorter queue, which you can correct by moving the price back down.

**Why the buyer can afford it:** Your buyers are software companies. Their alternatives are a mid-size studio or agency, or a freelancer plus review cycles. A 60–90 second explainer at agency rates typically costs several times $1,800 (assumed, so check it). Your price is probably being compared with their internal cost and time, not with other studios. Ask two or three recent clients what they'd otherwise have paid. That's the cheapest evidence you can get, and it may settle the argument with your partner.

**Fear of finding out:** The test is small and reversible, and each quote is a single data point. Not testing has a real cost: with three people and a fixed capacity, every project at $1,800 may be leaving $700+ on the table. Over a year, that's likely tens of thousands of dollars.

**Packaging, only if the test shows the pushback is real:** Don't add tiers yet. If some buyers balk, the natural split is by scope, such as a base video at $2,500 and a version with extra revisions, a cutdown for social or localized versions at a higher price. That serves buyers who value more, and doesn't just lower the price for those who don't.

**Assumed:** Capacity is your real limit (not sales effort), your costs are mostly your own time, repeat or referral clients make up much of your demand, and agency prices for similar work are well above $1,800.

**Would change if:** Most of your business comes from one or two repeat clients who'd leave over any increase (then raise for new clients only), or your waitlist is long because of slow delivery and not high demand (then fix throughput first), or early quotes at $2,500 meet real resistance (then use the table above).
