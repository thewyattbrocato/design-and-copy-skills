**Raise the price on new quotes, in steps, and treat the first step as a test. A 100% close rate and a three-month waitlist both mean you're underpriced.** Your partner is probably right. When almost nobody hesitates, you haven't found the top of what buyers will pay. Right now your calendar is rationing demand, and your price isn't.

**The arithmetic**

| New price | Rise | Share of deals you can lose before revenue per quote falls below today |
|---|---|---|
| $2,400 | +33% | 25% |
| $3,000 | +67% | 40% |
| $3,600 | +100% | 50% |

The real cushion is bigger than this table shows. You're capacity-bound, so lost deals mostly shorten the waitlist, and you still fill the calendar. You could lose half your quotes at $2,400 and stay booked, just with a shorter queue. That makes the downside small.

**How to run it**

1. **Quote $2,400 to every new inquiry from now on.** Clients who already have a quote or a booked slot keep $1,800. Say so in writing, with the date their slot is held until.
2. **Watch the next 8–10 quotes.**
   - If you still close about 80% or more, go to $3,000 for the next batch.
   - If you close roughly half, you're near the top of the range, so hold there.
   - If the waitlist drops under about a month and closes fall off, step back slightly.
3. **Ask every buyer what they were comparing you to.** Ask a few of your past clients too. Their answers tell you what an explainer is worth to them, such as agency quotes, the cost of making it in-house, or what the video does for their signups. That value is what you price against, not your hours.
4. **Optionally, sell the queue.** Some clients will pay extra to skip a three-month wait. A priority slot at a premium is a tier for a real buyer group, and it's worth adding once the base price settles.

Don't answer "how do we feel about it?" with a discount, and don't wait to feel ready. The test is reversible: you can drop back to $1,800 on the next quote.

**Assumed:** your capacity is the binding limit (that's what the waitlist suggests), and software-company buyers get real business value from the video. I haven't assumed any competitor prices, and I don't know your cost per video or your monthly capacity.

**Would change if:** your quotes mostly come from referrals or repeat clients who are loyal to the $1,800 price. Then the new-buyer close rate will understate how they'd react, so keep existing clients at $1,800 for a stated period and test on cold inquiries. Also, if you want to stay on the waitlist deliberately for brand reasons, a smaller step makes sense.
