Your partner is probably right. A three-month waitlist, a near-100% close rate, and no one ever pushing back are the three classic signs of underpricing. A healthy close rate for custom creative work is more like 30-50%. At 90%+, you're leaving money on the table. For software companies, 60-90 second custom explainers commonly go for several thousand dollars and up, so $1,800 is probably well below market.

**Why the test is less scary than it feels**

You have a waitlist, so a price increase is close to a free experiment. If the higher price loses you some deals, the waitlist gets shorter and you do less work per dollar. If it loses none, you've been giving away money for a year. Doubling the price and losing half your clients leaves revenue the same with half the workload. Neither outcome is a disaster.

**What I'd do**

1. **Raise the price for new quotes only.** Honor existing quotes and anyone already on the waitlist, so no one feels burned.
2. **Make a real jump, not a nudge.** Go to $3,000-3,500 rather than $1,900. Small bumps teach you nothing because no one will react to them.
3. **Quote it flatly.** State the number with no apology, no explanation, and no discount.
4. **Watch the next 8-10 quotes.**
   - Closing 70% or more means you're still too low, so go up again.
   - Closing 40-60% means you're near the right range.
   - Closing under 30% means you've overshot, so step back a bit.
5. **Raise again until you see real resistance.** The point where people start saying no is the market price, and you've never actually seen it.

**Ways to make it easier**

- **Add tiers.** For example, a base package, one with extra revisions and cutdowns for social, and a rush option. Most buyers pick the middle one, and the tiers anchor your price as a considered choice.
- **Charge for the things that cost you time**, such as extra revision rounds, scripting, and voiceover.
- **Pick a number you can defend.** If your partner is nervous, agree on a floor in advance, such as "if we close under 30% over 10 quotes, we drop back."

Pricing is the cheapest lever you have, and you have a cushion that most studios don't. I'd raise it this week.

I don't know your costs, your margins, or who your clients are. If most of them are tiny seed-stage startups rather than funded companies, the ceiling may be lower. Even so, the evidence says you're well under it.
