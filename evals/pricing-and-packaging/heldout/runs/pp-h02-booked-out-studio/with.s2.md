**Raise the price on new quotes. Your partner is right, and the test costs you very little.**

A three-month waitlist, almost every quote closing, and no pushback all point the same way. You have more demand than capacity, and the price isn't filtering anyone out. A near-100% close rate also tells you nothing about the right price, because you've never seen where buyers hesitate.

**Why the downside is small**
- The waitlist covers you. At a higher price you can lose buyers and still keep the calendar full.
- At $2,500, you'd need to close only 72% of what you close now to match revenue ($1,800 ÷ $2,500). At $3,000 it's 60%.
- Capacity is fixed at three people, so the number to watch is revenue per slot, not close rate.

**How to do it**
1. **Protect people already in line.** Anyone already quoted or booked keeps $1,800. Everyone quoted after a date you pick pays the new price.
2. **Step 1:** quote $2,500 for the next 8 to 10 quotes. That's about +39%, and you'd know within a few weeks.
3. **Read the result.**
   - If nearly all still close, you were well under the market. Go to roughly $3,000–3,500 for the next batch.
   - If a few push back, ask why. Those answers are the first real price information you've had.
   - Keep stepping up until close rate falls to roughly 60–75% and the waitlist is still there. Revenue and profit usually peak around there, not at 100%.
4. **Stop and reverse** if the waitlist drains below about a month, or if close rate drops below the break-even figures above.

**For your partner:** you aren't finding out whether you're good enough. You're finding out how much slack there is, and the waitlist means a wrong guess costs you a few quotes, not the business.

**Assumed:** the $1,800 covers a comparable scope each time (similar length, revisions and complexity). Your three-month waitlist reflects real capacity limits rather than slow scheduling. I haven't assumed any competitor prices. Check what comparable studios list publicly, and ask a couple of recent clients what they'd budgeted before they got your quote.

**Would change if:** a few clients account for most of your repeat or referral work and would be upset by an increase (give them a stated grace period instead). Or your waitlist comes from one bottleneck, like a single animator, that more money wouldn't fix.
