You're almost certainly undercharging, probably by a lot. A three-month waitlist, a near-100% close rate and zero pushback are the three classic signs of underpricing, and you have all of them. A healthy close rate for custom creative work is more like 40 to 60%. Studios making 60 to 90 second explainers for software companies commonly charge several thousand dollars, and often $5k to $15k or more. Your buyers are funded companies spending someone else's budget, so $1,800 may even make you look less credible.

Your partner is right, and her fear is understandable. But the test is cheap and easy to undo.

**What I'd do**

1. **Raise the price for new quotes only.** Jump to $3,000 to $3,500 now. Don't nudge it by $100. A tiny increase teaches you nothing, because nobody will notice it.
2. **Keep the waitlist as your safety net.** If the higher price slows demand, you lose nothing you were relying on. You're just trading an overbooked calendar for a fuller price. Existing waitlist clients can keep their quoted price, or you can give them a short notice window.
3. **Treat it as a ratchet.** Every 5 to 10 quotes, check your close rate. If you're still closing more than about 70%, raise the price another 20 to 30%. Stop when the rate settles around 50 to 60% and the waitlist is down to 3 to 4 weeks.
4. **Quote the number plainly.** Don't justify it, discount it or apologize for it. Put the scope and revision limits in writing so a higher price doesn't come with unlimited changes.
5. **Consider tiers once you have data.** For example, a base package, one with extra revisions and faster turnaround, and one with a script and storyboard. The top tier makes the middle one feel reasonable, and some clients will choose it.

**For your partner**

The worst case is that a few quotes get declined or negotiated. You'd learn the ceiling and could drop back down. Staying at $1,800 has a certain cost: every project you book is probably leaving thousands of dollars on the table. With three people and a year of full demand, that adds up to a lot.
