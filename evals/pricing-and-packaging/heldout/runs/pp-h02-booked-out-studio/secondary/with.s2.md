**Recommendation: raise the price on new quotes, starting around $2,400 (+33%), and keep booked projects at $1,800.** Your waitlist and near-100% close rate are behavioral evidence that demand exceeds your capacity. With three people, you can't take more work, so price is how you choose which work to take.

**Why the downside is small.** Because you're capacity-limited, a lost deal frees a slot for someone already waiting. The break-even test is how many closes you can lose before the rise stops paying:

| New price | Profit per video vs. $1,800 today | Closes you can lose before profit falls back |
|---|---|---|
| $2,200 (+22%) | +$400 | about 18% of quotes |
| $2,400 (+33%) | +$600 | about 25% of quotes |
| $2,500 (+39%) | +$700 | about 28% of quotes |

These assume your cost per video is about $900 (**assumed**; replace it with your real figure, including freelancers, voice, music, and fees). The formula is (current profit ÷ new profit) = (1,800 − cost) ÷ (new price − cost). At a $1,200 cost the margin of safety is smaller, so check your real number before you commit.

**How to test it without the fear:**
- Quote the new price only to new inquiries. Don't reprice anyone already booked.
- Before you start, write the reversal rule: for example, if the close rate drops below the break-even figure for the price you're testing, go back to the old price. Set the window now, such as the next 10 quotes or six weeks.
- Ask each prospect who passes what else they considered and what it cost. Your "nobody pushes back" may partly reflect who finds you, so this will tell you whether price is really invisible to them.

**Assumed:** your cost per video is about $900, you produce roughly the same number of videos a year as you have capacity for, and your goal is profit rather than growth.

**Would change if:** your real cost per video is much higher than $900 (the margin shrinks), you want to grow headcount and need the waitlist to fill new capacity (then raise less and hire more), or the waitlist is mostly poor-fit leads who would never have bought at any price.
