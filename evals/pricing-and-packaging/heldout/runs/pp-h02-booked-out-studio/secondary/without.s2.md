**Raise the price, and do it on the next quote.** A year-long waitlist, a near-100% close rate, and no pushback are what underpricing looks like. You're capacity-constrained, so price is the only lever that adds revenue without adding hours. Your partner is probably right.

**Why the fear is smaller than it feels:** Finding out costs almost nothing. You'll learn from the next few quotes, and the downside is small because you're turning away work anyway.

**A concrete plan:**

1. **Move new quotes to $2,400** (about 33% up). It's large enough to be a real test and small enough to stay in a sensible range for software-company buyers.
2. **Track three numbers for the next 8 to 10 quotes:** close rate, how many people ask about price, and whether anyone walks. Keep a simple log.
3. **Read the results.** If you still close most quotes, go to about $2,800 next. Keep stepping up until the close rate starts falling noticeably. Stop one step before the drop.
4. **Make the change visible.** Raise the price in writing, with a line like "our rates have increased." Don't apologize for it or explain it at length.

**Why a higher close rate threshold is fine:** At full capacity, revenue per project slot is what matters, not revenue per quote. Even if your close rate drops from 95% to 70%, $2,400 at 70% beats $1,800 at 95% for the same three-person schedule.

**Before you change anything, check these:**

- **Your real hourly rate.** Divide $1,800 by the total hours per video, including revisions, calls, and admin, across all three people. If it's well under what you'd pay a senior freelancer, that's the number to show your partner.
- **Whether your sample is biased.** "Nobody pushes back" can mean the people reaching you already accept your price. Ask a few recent clients what other studios quoted them. You'll learn a lot.
- **Scope and revisions.** Flat pricing gets expensive when revision rounds are unlimited. A tighter scope or a paid revision tier can raise your effective price without changing the headline number.

**Other levers, once the base price is set:**

- A 50% deposit to reduce no-shows and cash-flow stress.
- Rush fees for short timelines.
- A retainer or package for clients who want several videos a year. Those are often better for both sides.

Your partner's instinct is probably right, and the test is cheap. Start with the next quote, then track what happens.
