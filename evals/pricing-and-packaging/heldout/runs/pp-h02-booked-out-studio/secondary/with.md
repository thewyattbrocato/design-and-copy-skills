**Recommendation: raise the price on new quotes, starting at $2,400 (+33%), and keep already-booked projects at $1,800.** A three-month waitlist means you're capacity-constrained, so revenue depends on what each production slot earns, not on how many quotes you win. Your partner is probably right.

The arithmetic: at $1,800 with about a 95% close rate, each quote is worth about $1,710 in expected revenue. Here is the close rate you'd need at each new price to match that:

| Price | Close rate needed to match today |
|---|---|
| $2,000 | 86% |
| $2,200 | 78% |
| $2,400 | 71% |
| $2,800 | 61% |

Since you're booked out, losing some quotes mostly shortens the queue. It doesn't leave slots empty. The risk you're afraid of is a drop in close rate, and that's a risk you can measure cheaply.

**Test plan:**
- Quote $2,400 to all new prospects. Keep current clients at $1,800.
- Log every quote with its outcome and the reason for any loss. Lost quotes are your most useful data, since you've never seen one priced high enough to lose.
- After 10 to 15 quotes, if the close rate stays around 75% or higher and the queue persists, go to $2,600 or $2,800 and repeat.
- If the close rate falls below about 70%, you've found the ceiling. Stay there.

**Also check:** whether the waitlist comes from one referrer or a narrow niche, and whether "nobody pushes back" happens because you only quote prospects who already see $1,800 as normal. Asking a few lost prospects why they went elsewhere will show you that.

Assumed: the waitlist reflects production capacity, not one-off referrals. Close rate is roughly 90–95% on quotes sent. Your cost per video (labor hours, freelancers, licenses, revisions) is well below $1,800, so the margin is comfortable at any of these prices.

Would change if: a video takes so many hours that $1,800 barely covers costs (then the floor moves up and the test is urgent); lost quotes turn out to be mostly price objections; or revisions are unlimited, in which case the price should be tied to scope, such as tiers by length or revision rounds, not one flat fee.
