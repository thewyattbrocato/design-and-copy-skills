Your partner is probably right. A three-month waitlist, near-perfect close rates, and no pushback all point to a price below what the market will bear. When you have more demand than capacity, the price is the lever that matches the two.

**What I'd do:**

1. **Raise the price for new quotes to about $2,500, starting now.** That's roughly 40% higher, which is big enough to learn something from. A smaller bump like $2,000 would tell you very little, because your close rate would hardly move.

2. **Honor the existing waitlist at $1,800.** It's a good-faith move, and it keeps the people who already committed from feeling punished.

3. **Track three numbers for 8 to 10 weeks:** close rate, how long the waitlist is, and hours per project. Pay attention to the waitlist first. Your capacity is the real constraint, so what you want to know is whether the queue shrinks toward a few weeks.

4. **Set a rule in advance.** For example: if the waitlist is still above 4 to 6 weeks and close rate stays above about 70%, raise again. If close rate drops below about 50% and the waitlist disappears, you've found your ceiling. Settle on a number before you see the results so the decision doesn't hinge on a bad week.

**Why the fear is smaller than it feels:** Suppose close rate drops from 90% to 70% after the raise. Your expected revenue per quote goes from $1,620 to $1,750, and you spend the same hours on fewer projects. Since you're capacity-limited, fewer projects at a higher price is a win even with lower conversion. The downside is mostly that you lose a few marginal clients, and you can live with that.

**Two things to do before the conversation with your partner:**

- **Figure out your real floor.** Take what you need to pay each person, add overhead and taxes, and divide by the number of billable hours you can actually produce per year. Then compare that to hours per video. If the number is well above $1,800 divided by your hours, the case is already made.
- **Ask your clients why they picked you.** Price is probably not what they're comparing you on. If they say your style, speed, or the way you handle the process, you can charge more without it feeling like a stretch.

I'd also suggest a tiered structure later, such as a standard package and a premium one with revisions, motion design depth, or rush turnaround. For now, a single price increase is the cleanest test.
