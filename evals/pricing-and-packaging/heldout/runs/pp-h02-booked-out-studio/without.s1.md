Raise your prices. Your own numbers already answer the question: a three-month waitlist, a near-100% close rate, and no pushback in a year. If demand exceeds what three people can make and nobody flinches at the number, the price is too low. A healthy close rate for custom creative work is more like 40-60%. Software companies routinely pay several times $1,800 for a 60-90 second explainer, so you're probably well under market, not slightly under.

**Why your partner's fear is smaller than it feels**
- The test is cheap and reversible. A new quote only applies to new clients, and you can lower it later.
- You have a buffer. With a three-month waitlist, losing a few prospects costs you almost nothing, because your calendar is full anyway.
- The downside of staying put is real. You're working at capacity for far less than the work is worth, and a low price can make buyers doubt your quality.

**What I'd do**
1. **Honor existing quotes and the current waitlist at $1,800.** That keeps goodwill and removes the "what if we burn bridges" worry.
2. **Quote new inquiries at about $3,500.** If that feels too scary, $3,000 is fine as a first step. Don't raise by 10%. Small bumps teach you nothing because nobody notices them.
3. **Track every quote.** Record the price, whether it closed, and the reason for any no. Give it 8-10 quotes.
4. **Read the result.**
   - Closing above 80%: still too cheap, so go up again (to $4,500-5,000).
   - Closing around 50-70%: you've found the range. Stay there.
   - Closing below 30%: pull back a bit, but that would surprise me.
5. **Add structure over time.** Use tiers (for example, a base video, one with custom illustration or voiceover, and a rush option) and charge for extra revision rounds. These raise your average project value without a single scary headline number.

**What a higher price gets you**
Even if you lose some deals, you could earn the same revenue with half the projects. That would shorten the waitlist and free time for better work, a retainer product, or a fourth hire.

If your partner is nervous, frame it as an experiment with a fixed endpoint ("we try $3,500 for the next 10 quotes, then review") instead of a permanent change. That's an easier yes, and you'll finally have real data on what the work is worth.
