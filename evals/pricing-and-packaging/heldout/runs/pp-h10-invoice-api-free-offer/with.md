**Recommendation: don't do the forever-free tier. Combine the other two options: give a one-time free allowance of about 100 pages with no time limit (or a generous 30 days), then move people to a paid starter plan. Size the starter plan so it earns a margin.**

**Why not the other options**
- **500 free pages forever:** At your 2¢ per page, a user who uses all 500 pages costs you $10 a month. That's the entire starter price, so every heavy free user is a full customer you don't get paid for. Free users also pile up month after month, and the cost never ends. A developer testing an extraction API doesn't need 500 pages a month to judge it.
- **14-day trial with no page cap:** The cost depends on how many pages people run, not on days, and a script can process thousands of pages in an afternoon. The calendar limit also doesn't fit developers, who often sign up now and test a week later.
- **Nothing free, $10 plan:** This is the safest for cash, but developers need to run their own messy invoices before they trust the accuracy. Charging up front will probably cut conversions a lot. I'd skip it unless you can't afford any free usage.

**Arithmetic (2,000 signups a month, 2¢ per page)**

| Option | Worst-case cost per signup | Monthly ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| 500 pages/month forever | $10, every month | $20,000 a month, and it grows as users accumulate |
| 100-page one-time allowance | $2 | $4,000 |
| 50-page one-time allowance | $1 | $2,000 |

Most people try it for a day, so real cost should be a fraction of the ceiling. If the average signup runs about 25 pages (assumed), that's about $0.50 each, or roughly $1,000 a month.

**Starter plan:** $10 for 500 pages is break-even at best, because the pages alone cost $10 before payment fees and support. Instead, try $10 for about 250 pages, which costs about $5 to serve and leaves roughly $5 before fees. Add a per-page overage price of about 4–5¢ (assumed) for heavier users. Treat $10 as a starting test price, not a proven one. I don't know what your buyers would pay, and the real alternative to compare against is their manual data entry.

**Payback check:** With about $5 margin a month and a customer staying about 6 months (assumed), each paying customer earns about $30. At $0.50 of free cost per signup, you need roughly 1.7% of signups to convert to break even on free usage. That's about 33 customers out of 2,000. At $2 per signup, you'd need about 6.7%, which is risky. So start with the lower cap, and raise it only if conversion is healthy.

**Assumed:** about 25 pages of typical free use, about 6 months of retention, about $5 margin per starter customer, and payment fees and support not yet counted.

**Would change if:**
- Your current conversion from signup to paid is already above about 5%. Then a bigger allowance is affordable.
- Real free usage averages well under 10 pages. Then a bigger allowance costs almost nothing.
- Users need more than about 100 pages to judge accuracy. Then use a paid pilot instead.
- Most of your revenue will come from teams rather than solo developers. Then the $10 plan matters less than a higher tier, and I'd look at that next.
