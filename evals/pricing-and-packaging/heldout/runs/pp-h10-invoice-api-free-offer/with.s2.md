**Recommendation: a 14-day trial, capped at 100 pages, no card required, with the $10 Starter plan as the paid step after it.** Don't do the forever-free tier. Don't launch with nothing free either.

**Cost of each option** (2,000 signups a month, 2¢ per page)

| Option | Worst case per month | Likely case per month (assumed) |
|---|---|---|
| Free 500 pages/month forever | 2,000 × 500 × $0.02 = **$20,000** | If 10% of signups keep using it fully, that is $2,000 of new cost each month, and it stacks. After 12 months you carry about $24,000 a month in free usage. |
| 14-day trial, 100-page cap | 2,000 × 100 × $0.02 = **$4,000** | If the average signup uses 20 pages, about **$800** |
| Nothing free, $10 plan | $0 | $0, but see below |

- **Forever-free:** Its cost never ends and grows every month, and the limit doesn't stop it covering what paid users need. A developer building a small integration can stay under 500 pages a month indefinitely. For a company with tight runway, this is the riskiest of the three.
- **Nothing free:** Developers need to try the API on their own invoices before they will pay. You would lose most of your 2,000 signups before they could judge accuracy. That is a lot of lost demand to save a few hundred dollars.
- **Capped trial:** The cap is what bounds your spend. Without it, 14 days of unlimited use could cost far more than the table shows. One hundred pages is enough to test real invoices and build a prototype. If most people poke around for a day, the average will sit well below the cap.

**Starter plan at $10:** At 2¢ a page, $10 covers only 500 pages at cost, so a plan that included 500 pages would have no margin. I'd include about **250 pages**:
- Page cost: $5.00.
- Card fees: roughly $0.60.
- Margin: about $4.40 per subscriber.

Charge overage at a rate above cost, for example 4¢ a page, so heavier users don't lose you money. Price against what the invoices cost to key in by hand, which I don't know. If a page takes someone a minute or two to key in, 250 pages is hours of work for $10, and you have room to charge more.

**Assumed:**
- A 3% + 30¢ card fee.
- An average trial use of 20 pages.
- Your 2¢ figure covers all per-page cost, with no support or storage on top.
- Abuse is limited by email verification, so people can't sign up repeatedly.

**Would change if:**
- You can pull real data on how many pages signups process in their first day or two. If the median is under 10, drop the cap to 50 pages. If many hit 100 and then ask for more, the cap is too low.
- Trial-to-paid conversion comes in under about 2%. Then test the $10 price and page allowance before widening the free offer.
- Users say 14 days is too short to integrate. Then extend the time, but keep the 100-page cap.
