**I'd run a trial, but cap it by pages rather than days: about 50 free pages, then the $10 starter plan.**

**Why not the other two**
- **Free tier, 500 pages a month forever:** Each user can cost you up to $10 a month, so a signup cohort costs you every month. If only 10% of your 2,000 monthly signups use their full allowance, that's about $2,000 a month. It also stacks as cohorts accumulate, and people will make multiple accounts. That's too much for tight runway.
- **No free access, $10 plan only:** This costs nothing, but developers can't tell whether your extraction works on their invoices without trying it. Most won't pay $10 to find out, so you'd lose conversions you'd otherwise get.
- **14-day trial, as stated:** A time limit alone doesn't cap your spend. One user can push 10,000 pages through in a day. The limit has to be on pages.

**The numbers for 50 free pages**
- The worst case is $1 per signup, or $2,000 a month if all 2,000 max it out.
- Realistically most people run a handful of pages and leave, so I'd expect $100 to $300 a month.
- At 100 pages the worst case doubles to $4,000 a month. I'd start at 50 and raise it only if conversion looks page-starved.

**Guardrails**
- Require a verified email, and ideally a card or GitHub login, to cut down on duplicate accounts.
- Set a global monthly spend cap on free usage so a surge can't hurt you.
- Let the credit expire after 14 to 30 days so it doesn't linger.
- Size the $10 plan for margin. At 2¢ a page, 500 included pages would be break-even before payment fees. About 250 pages, with overage priced above cost, is safer.

If you want a quick sanity check, track the median pages used per trial in the first month. That tells you whether 50 is too stingy or too generous.
