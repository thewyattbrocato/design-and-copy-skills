**I'd skip all three as written and offer a small one-time credit (about 50 pages, no card) with the $10 starter plan behind it.**

**Why not the others**

- **500 pages/month free, forever:** A full user costs you $10 a month (500 × $0.02), and nothing pushes them to pay. Your cost grows with signups, not revenue. If 2,000 people sign up each month and even a few hundred use it heavily, you could be spending thousands a month. Multiple accounts per person make it worse. This is the riskiest option when runway is tight.
- **14-day trial:** Time isn't what drives your cost, pages are. Without a page cap, one developer can run thousands of pages in a day. With a cap, you've built the credit option anyway, just with an expiry that does nothing useful.
- **Nothing free, $10 plan:** This is the safest on cost, but developers want to see output on their own messy invoices before paying. Most would leave without trying it, and your signup funnel would mostly disappear.

**What I'd do**

- **Credit:** Give 50 pages as a one-time grant. The most it can cost is $1 per signup.
- **Abuse limits:** Require a verified email or GitHub login. Cap the free tier by account and block disposable email domains.
- **Paid plan:** When the credit runs out, show the $10 plan. Make it include fewer than 500 pages, say 250. At 500 pages the plan costs you the full $10 in processing, so you'd make nothing on it. Add per-page overage pricing above that.

**Rough cost**

These are estimates. I don't have your actual usage numbers.

- If the typical "poke around for a day" user runs 10–20 pages, the credit costs about $0.20–$0.40 per signup. That's $400–$800 a month across 2,000 signups.
- The worst case, where everyone uses all 50 pages, is $2,000 a month.
- If you want it tighter, drop the credit to 25 pages. That caps the worst case at $1,000 and is still enough to test a handful of invoices.

**Check this first**

Look at how many pages your current signups actually process in their first day. If the median is under 10, a 25-page credit is enough. If a meaningful share hit 100 or more, those are your real buyers, and the credit should be enough for them to see it work. You can also offer a bigger credit on request, such as a short form for people who say what they're building.

Track credit-to-paid conversion for a month or two. If it's under about 1%, the problem is probably the product or the pricing, not the free offer.
