**Recommendation: option B, a 14-day trial with a hard cap of 50 pages, no card required, one trial per account.** Don't run a free tier forever, and don't skip free entirely.

**Why not A:** 500 free pages is $10 of your cost per account, which is the same as the whole $10 starter. Every free account keeps costing money every month, so the cost stacks with sign-ups.

| Option | Max cost/mo (all 2,000 sign-ups) | Likely cost/mo (assumed) | Revenue/mo | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: 500 pages forever | $20,000 added each month, cumulative | ~$800 new each month, growing with cohorts | ~$0 from free users | Reject |
| B: 14 days, 50-page cap | $2,000 (a cohort's ceiling, not a stacking cost) | ~$400 (avg 10 pages used) | 2% convert × $10 ≈ $200 | Break-even at ~43 paying starters (~2.1%) |
| C: no free tier | ~$0 for non-payers | ~$0 | 1% convert × $10 ≈ $200 | Cash-safest, but evaluators can't test on real invoices |

Each cost is 2,000 sign-ups × pages × $0.02. Option B's cost ends with the trial, so it doesn't stack like A.

**Why B over C:** Most sign-ups are poke-arounders, and they won't pay $10 without testing on their own invoices. A trial lets them do that for a bounded $1 at most per account. Option C is the cheapest in cash, though, and if the trial doesn't convert above about 2%, C is the fallback.

**Set the cap from your budget:** the cap times $0.02 times monthly trials is your ceiling. For a $500/month ceiling at 2,000 trials, the cap is about 12 pages, which is too small for a real integration test. If you can only fund a smaller ceiling, lower the cap or the sign-up volume. Don't cut the trial length.

**Fix the starter price too:** if the $10 starter includes 500 pages, it has zero margin at $0.02 a page. Include about 250–300 pages so it covers cost plus payment fees.

Assumed: 80% of trials use under 20 pages, average 10 pages; the starter includes ≤300 pages; about 2% of trials convert to starter; payment fees are about $0.60 per $10 charge; a 50-page trial is enough for a real integration test.

Would change if: trial conversion comes in under about 2%, in which case go to C; if trial users use the full cap on average, the trial costs roughly $2,000 a month, which needs a smaller cap or a card-required trial to filter sign-ups.
