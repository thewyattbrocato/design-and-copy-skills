**Recommendation: take the 14-day trial (B), but cap it by pages, not just time, and drop the forever free tier.** Also fix the $10 starter before launch.

**Why not the forever tier:** its cost keeps growing because every month's active free users stay on the bill. Assumed: 5% of signups stay active and use the full 500 pages a month. That's 100 users × 500 × $0.02 = $1,000/month after month one, and about $6,000/month after six months as the pool builds. The 1,900 casual users add roughly $700–800/month on top.

**Why the trial:** cost is bounded because the trial ends. Assumed: average trial use of 15 pages.

| Option | Monthly page cost (assumed) | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| A. 500 free/month forever | ~$1,800 in month one, rising each month | none that holds |
| B. 14-day trial, 50-page cap | 2,000 × 15 × $0.02 = **$600** | 2,000 × 50 × $0.02 = **$2,000** |
| C. No free tier | $0 | none, but few devs will try a paid-only API |

**Why not C:** with no way to try it, few of the 2,000 monthly signups will ever run a page, so you lose the people who would have converted.

**The $10 starter has no margin.** At $0.02 per page, 500 pages costs you $10, which is all of the revenue before card fees and support. Either cap the starter at about 200 pages ($4 cost), or price 500 pages at about $25.

**Cheap levers to pair with B:**
- Require a card for the trial. This cuts the casual signups that burn pages without intent.
- Add a sandbox that returns canned output for a few sample invoices at zero cost. Most "poke around" use doesn't need real processing.

**Assumed:** 2,000 signups/month, 5% stay active on free, 15-page average trial use, and the starter's buyer value is above $10. I don't know your buyers' alternative, such as manual entry or another OCR service, so the $25 figure is a test price, not a value-based one.

**Would change if:** you can measure trial-to-paid conversion and the free tier converts much better than the trial, if buyers compare you to a much cheaper OCR service, or if your per-page cost drops well below $0.02.
