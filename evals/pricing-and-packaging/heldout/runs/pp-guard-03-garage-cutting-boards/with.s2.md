**No to all three.** Your constraint is your own hours, not a lack of ways to sell.

**Why not:**
- **Good/better/best:** You already have three sizes, and they differ only in quantity. Renaming them wouldn't add a tier built for a different buyer.
- **Subscription box:** A board is a durable, one-off purchase, and few people want a new one every month. Each box would also be hand-made time from you, so you'd be committing hours in advance for a customer who can cancel.
- **Free sample:** A free board costs you the hours, the wood and the shipping, and you'd be giving away $38 or more of revenue per giveaway. That only makes sense if you have idle hours and nobody finds you, and 25 sales a month suggests the problem isn't that.

**The real issue is revenue per hour:**

| Size | Price | Hours | $/hour (before materials and fees) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | $38 | 1.5 | $25.33 |
| Medium | $62 | 2.5 | $24.80 |
| Large | $95 | 4 | $23.75 |

- The biggest board pays you the least per hour, which is backwards, since large boards are usually bought as gifts and statement pieces.
- Assuming an even mix, 25 boards is about 67 hours and about $1,625 a month. That's roughly $24 an hour before wood, finish, packaging, payment fees and shipping, so your real take is lower.

**What I'd do instead (two changes):**
1. **Raise large to about $110–120.** At $115, the large board earns about $28.75 an hour, in line with or above the others. If you sell about 8 a month, that adds about $160 a month. Test it for a month or two. If large sales hold, try $125.
2. **Add paid personalization**, such as engraving a name or date, for about $10–20. Gift buyers value it, and it adds little time. This is the only segment split I'd add, and it fits how people actually buy boards.

Keep small and medium as they are for now. Raise them only if you're booked solid or sales stay steady after the large increase.

**Assumed:** an even mix of sizes, since you didn't give your split. Materials, fees and shipping costs are unknown. I also assumed you have no spare capacity you're trying to fill.

**Would change if:**
- You have idle hours and traffic is the problem. Then a small giveaway, like a coaster or spoon made from scraps, could make sense.
- Repeat buyers are asking for regular deliveries. Then a seasonal or quarterly offer might work.
- Your real materials and fees cost more than I've assumed. Then all three prices need to go up, not only the large.
