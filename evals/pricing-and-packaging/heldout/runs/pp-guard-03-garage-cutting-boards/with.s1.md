**No to all three. If you change one thing, raise the large board's price.**

**Why not the three ideas**
- **Good/better/best:** You already have three sizes, and they differ only in quantity. Renaming them doesn't give a different buyer a reason to pay more. A real tier needs a buyer group that values something extra, like personalization for gift buyers or end-grain walnut for serious cooks. Try that later as an add-on if buyers ask for it.
- **Subscription box:** A cutting board is a buy-once item, and nobody needs a new one every month. A box would only work with small consumables like board oil or spoons. That means new products and more hours, and you're already the bottleneck.
- **Free samples:** A free board costs materials plus 1.5 or more hours of your time. At your prices, one free small board costs about $38 or more in lost sales. You'd need to know it reliably turns into paying customers, and a board lasts years, so nobody needs a sample.

**What your numbers do show: revenue per hour falls as boards get bigger**

| Size | Price | Hours | $ per hour (before materials and fees) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | $38 | 1.5 | $25.33 |
| Medium | $62 | 2.5 | $24.80 |
| Large | $95 | 4 | $23.75 |

The large board is your worst use of garage time. To match the small board's rate, it would need to be about $101. Wood cost also rises with size, so the real gap is probably wider.

**Test:** Move large to **$105–110** (the range is a guess; $101 is just the break-even for equal hourly revenue). Leave small and medium alone. The move is worth about $10–15 per large board. If you sell 5 large a month, that's roughly $50–75 more per month. Even if you lose 1 of those 5 sales, you come out about even and get 4 hours back, which you can use to make more small or medium boards. Existing orders keep the old price. The new price applies to new orders from the day you change it.

**Assumed:** I don't know your materials cost, your size mix, or whether you're capped by hours or by demand. I also assumed fees on your website are small.

**Would change if:**
- You're short on demand rather than hours. Then the priority is traffic, not packaging.
- Customers ask for engraving or premium woods. Then a priced add-on makes sense.
- Large boards sell out within days. Then raise the price further.
- Large sales drop by more than about a third after the increase. Then go back.
