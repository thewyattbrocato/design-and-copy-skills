**Recommendation: don't add any of the three yet. Keep the three sizes at $38, $62 and $95, and check your materials cost before changing anything.**

**Good/better/best:** Your sizes already form a ladder, so a good/better/best split would mostly repeat them with different labels. Tiers only help when different buyers value different things, such as a gift buyer who wants engraving or a cook who wants a specific wood. If customers start asking for those options, add one premium option for that group. Don't create tiers by quantity.

**Subscription box:** I'd skip it. You're one person already at roughly 67 hours a month, and a subscription commits you to a fixed monthly output and churns when people skip a month. You haven't shown that anyone wants recurring boards.

**Free sample:** A free board costs you about $38 to $95 in labor plus materials, and it's a gift to people who may never buy. If you want a marketing sample, use offcut coasters or wood-finish swatches that cost you almost nothing, and cap how many you give away.

**The arithmetic:**
- At about 8 boards per size (25 a month, split evenly): roughly $1,625 gross, about 67 labor hours.
- That's about $24 per labor hour before materials, shipping and payment fees.
- The per-hour rate barely changes by size: $25.30 small, $24.80 medium, $23.75 large. So the sizes are priced consistently with each other.

**Assumed:** An even mix across the three sizes. Your hour figures are all-in labor, including sanding and finishing. Materials, shipping and payment fees aren't in the $24 figure. Current prices are selling as expected.

**Would change if:** Materials plus fees plus shipping run more than about $8 to $10 per board, which would push your real hourly rate toward $15 and make a price rise the first move. Or if boards sell out quickly at these prices, which would suggest room to raise prices. Or if customers keep asking for a specific option, which would justify a premium tier.
