**Short answer: no to all three for now. Your three sizes already work as your price ladder, and the bigger question is whether you're charging enough.**

**Why not the tiers:** Good/better/best only helps when different buyers value different things. Your tiers differ only in size, so calling them good, better and best would be labels on the same product. If you later find a group that wants something extra, such as a gift set or a custom engraving, add that as an option, not a rank.

**Why not the subscription box:** Cutting boards are a one-off purchase. People rarely need a new one every month, so you'd be signing up for a recurring obligation for buyers who may not want it. Each box also takes real hours from your production time.

**Why not the free sample:** A free small board costs about 1.5 hours plus materials, and nothing guarantees it leads to a sale. If you want to test samples, make minis from offcuts, which cost you close to nothing, and track how many become paying customers.

**The arithmetic (assumed even mix of 25 boards, about 8 of each):**

| Size | Price | Hours | Price per hour (before materials and fees) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | $38 | 1.5 | $25.33 |
| Medium | $62 | 2.5 | $24.80 |
| Large | $95 | 4 | $23.75 |

- Revenue is about $1,625/month, from about 67 hours of production. That's roughly 40% of a 170-hour month, so **you have capacity to spare. Demand is probably your constraint, not tiers.**
- Your hourly rate is low and falls as boards get bigger. Materials, fees, packaging and shipping come out of that before you pay yourself.
- Test a rise on large first. Going from $95 to $105 breaks even on revenue if large sales fall by less than about 9.5%. You'd earn more per hour, and you'd see the response quickly.

**Assumed:** an even size mix, materials and fees are not yet subtracted, and the hour estimates are accurate. I have no evidence of what buyers will pay, so the $105 figure is a test, not a forecast.

**Would change if:** your actual size mix is very different, your materials cost is high (which would make the small board's hourly rate much worse), or customers start asking for a recurring or gift option. In that case, look at the specific request before building a plan around it.
