**Short answer: not the subscription or the free samples, not yet. Fix the pricing first.** Tiers are reasonable, but you may already have them in the form of three sizes.

**Your pricing has a problem.** Gross revenue per hour falls as the boards get bigger:

- Small: $38 ÷ 1.5 h = **$25.33/hr**
- Medium: $62 ÷ 2.5 h = **$24.80/hr**
- Large: $95 ÷ 4 h = **$23.75/hr**

Those are before wood, finish, packaging, shipping, card fees, and taxes. So each board is earning roughly $24/hour before those costs. Your large board is your least efficient product, and it's the one that makes the most noise in a "premium" price.

If you want about $30/hour before costs, the prices would be roughly:

- Small: ~$45
- Medium: ~$75
- Large: ~$120

I'd test a smaller step first, such as large at $110 or a 10–15% increase across the board, and watch whether orders slow down. At 25 a month you'll learn quickly. Check your actual material and shipping costs per size before you pick a number.

**Tiers:** If you add them, make the upgrades more valuable, not just bigger. Engraving, a premium wood, a special finish, or a matching set can add $10–25 for very little extra labor. Those are better margins than a fourth size.

**Subscription box:** I'd skip this for now. A subscriber is a promise to ship a board every month. If you had 20 subscribers buying small boards, that's about 30 hours of work a month, which is most of your current output for a product that may not sell at all. A subscription makes sense when you already have steady demand and a repeatable product. To test demand cheaply, try a waitlist or a "founding member" pre-order for a few months and see whether people sign up.

**Free samples:** These cost you time and material. Use offcuts for something like coasters or a small cutting board, and send it with a discount code for a future order. Keep it small enough that it doesn't take hours from your main production.

**Order of operations:**
1. Calculate your true cost per board (wood, finish, packaging, shipping, fees).
2. Raise prices, starting with the large.
3. Add one or two paid upgrades.
4. Only then consider a subscription, and test it with a waitlist first.
