**Probably not yet, at least not the subscription or the free samples. Your pricing is the bigger lever.**

**Your prices are low for the time involved.** Your per-hour gross is about $25 for the small, $25 for the medium, and $24 for the large. That's before materials, finish, packaging, shipping, card fees, and the time spent on sanding, sales, and packing. If you pay yourself even $25/hour after materials, the math looks like this (assuming about $10 in wood and finish per board):

- Small: 1.5 h × $25 + $10 ≈ $48 before fees
- Medium: 2.5 h × $25 + $10 ≈ $73
- Large: 4 h × $25 + $10 ≈ $110

Those are rough numbers, so plug in your real material costs. But unless your materials are very cheap, $38/$62/$95 probably leaves you working for well under minimum wage. Raising prices 15–25% is a lower-risk change than any of your friend's ideas. With 25 sales a month, you'll learn quickly whether demand drops.

**On your friend's ideas:**

- **Good/better/best tiers:** These work when the higher tier adds something customers value, like a different wood, an edge profile, engraving, or a finish, and it costs you less time per dollar than the base. Sizing alone is already a ladder. If you try one, make a single premium version and see if it sells before building a whole lineup.
- **Monthly subscription box:** I'd skip this. At $60 a month, 25 subscribers would consume all your capacity, and recurring commitments are hard to keep up with alone. It also locks you into a schedule when your output is lumpy. A waitlist or a periodic "drop" of limited boards gets most of the benefit with less risk.
- **Free samples:** Only worth it if they convert. A free offcut coaster costs you some wood and time, so think of it as marketing spend. A cheaper option is a discount code for newsletter signups or a small thank-you add-on with orders.

**What I'd do:** Work out your true cost per board, including materials, packaging, shipping, and fees, then set your prices from a target hourly rate. Raise prices and watch sales for a couple of months. Then test one premium option.
