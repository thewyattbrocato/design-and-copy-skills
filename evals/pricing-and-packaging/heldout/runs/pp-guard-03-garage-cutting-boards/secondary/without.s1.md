**Not yet, and I'd skip the subscription box. Look at pricing first.**

Your current prices work out to:

- Small: $38 / 1.5 hr = **$25.33/hr**
- Medium: $62 / 2.5 hr = **$24.80/hr**
- Large: $95 / 4 hr = **$23.75/hr**

Those numbers are before wood, finish, packaging, shipping, card and platform fees, and taxes. Once you subtract materials, you may be netting well under $20/hr. At 25 boards a month, you're probably spending 60 to 75 hours on boards alone. If that's close, a better tier structure won't fix the problem. Price will.

**Good/better/best:** You already have tiers, by size. A three-level structure works when the upgrade gives people a reason to pay more and costs you little time. For example, "better" could add a juice groove or laser engraving, and "best" could add a premium species or a custom design. Don't add upgrades that add hours per board.

**Subscription box:** I'd pass for now. Your boards are one-time purchases, and a subscription means committing to a fixed monthly output, handling churn, and building a product around a schedule that a one-person shop may not sustain. If you want predictability, try a monthly limited drop with a waitlist or email list. You get most of the benefit without the commitment.

**Free sample:** Giving away a full board costs you about 2.5 hours and materials, so that's expensive. Cheaper options:
- Send a small offcut board or coaster to a few woodworking or cooking creators in exchange for honest posts or reviews.
- Include a free offcut coaster with orders over a set amount. Offcuts are nearly free to you.

**What I'd do:**
1. Work out your true cost per board, including materials, packaging, shipping, fees, and your target hourly rate. $30 to $40/hr is a reasonable starting target for skilled handwork.
2. Raise the large first. It's the lowest per hour at $23.75. A 15 to 25% increase is a reasonable test.
3. Watch sales for 4 to 6 weeks. If they hold, raise the others too. If they drop a lot, you've learned where your price ceiling is.
4. Add one upgrade option, such as engraving or a juice groove, as your "better" tier.
5. Use offcuts for a low-cost free gift or creator sample.
