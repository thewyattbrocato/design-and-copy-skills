**Short answer: don't do any of the three as described. Raise prices first, and test the other ideas before building them.**

**Good/better/best:** Your three sizes already form a ladder, but they differ only in size. Adding labels like "good/better/best" on top gives you no new buyer to aim at. A tier only earns its place if a different group pays for something the others lack, such as a personalized or engraved option for gift buyers, or a premium wood line. If that's a real product, make it a separate offer, not a rank.

**Subscription box:** Not yet. People rarely need a new cutting board every month, so a recurring box sells against a weak need. It would also commit your garage to a fixed monthly output and shipping schedule. If you want recurring revenue, test a wood-care kit (oil, wax) or a waitlist for a seasonal release first, and only build it if people pay up front.

**Free samples:** Each free board costs you about 1.5 to 4 hours plus materials and shipping. Two free small boards a month is roughly 3 hours of your time, about two small boards' worth of making. Treat any giveaway as a marketing budget with a cap, for example a few boards for reviewers or wholesale prospects, not an open offer.

**Price:** Your gross revenue per hour of making is roughly:
- Small: $38 ÷ 1.5 h = $25.33/h
- Medium: $62 ÷ 2.5 h = $24.80/h
- Large: $95 ÷ 4 h = $23.75/h

That's before materials, card fees, packaging and shipping, so your real take is lower. The large board earns the least per hour, which suggests it's the first to change. A 10% rise across all sizes adds about $160/month at 25 boards (25 × $65 × 10%). Revenue only falls if volume drops more than about 9%, to roughly 23 boards a month. Test a rise on the large size first, then the others.

**Assumed:** An even mix of sizes (about $65 average, 25 boards/month), the hours you gave are your making time only, and materials, fees and shipping are not yet subtracted.

**Would change if:** Materials, fees or shipping take a large share of each sale (that would push the price rise from "should" to "must"), or customers keep asking for a recurring product (that would justify testing the box).
