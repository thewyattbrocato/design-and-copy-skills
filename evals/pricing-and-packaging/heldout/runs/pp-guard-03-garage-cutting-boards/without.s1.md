No to the subscription box and the free samples. The tiers you already have, and the real problem is price.

**The math**

| Board | Price | Hours | Revenue per hour (before materials) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | $38 | 1.5 | ~$25 |
| Medium | $62 | 2.5 | ~$25 |
| Large | $95 | 4 | ~$24 |

After wood, glue, finish, packaging, and payment fees, you're probably netting $15 to $20 an hour. That's low for skilled work. The large board is your worst deal per hour, not your best.

**Your friend's three ideas**

- **Good/better/best:** Small, medium, and large already are your tiers. What's missing is a premium option. Offer end-grain construction, walnut or other pricier wood, or engraving at a real markup (+$30 to $60). That adds margin without adding hours in proportion.
- **Subscription box:** This fits poorly. A cutting board is a one-time purchase, and nobody needs a new one every month. Each box would also cost you hours you don't have. Subscriptions work for consumables like board butter or oil, not for boards.
- **Free samples:** Each sample costs you real hours and wood, and your products are too labor-heavy to give away. If you want a low-risk entry point, make cheap items from offcuts, like spoon rests, coasters, or a small board with a lower price, and sell those. An email signup discount code costs you much less than a free sample.

**What I'd do instead**

1. Raise prices about 15 to 20% (roughly $45 / $72 / $115) and watch whether sales drop. At 25 sales a month with just you in a garage, you're likely capacity-limited. If demand holds, you're underpriced.
2. Price the large board higher than the straight line suggests, since it eats the most time.
3. Add the premium option and an engraving or personalization add-on.
4. Sell board oil or butter as an add-on at checkout. It's the one repeat-purchase product that fits.
