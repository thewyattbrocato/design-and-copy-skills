**No to all three, at least for now.** Your bigger issue is price.

**Your pricing:** $38 / 1.5h, $62 / 2.5h and $95 / 4h each work out to about $24-25 per hour of your time. That's before wood, glue, finish, sandpaper, shipping supplies, payment fees and your time on photos and packing. Once you subtract those, you're probably clearing $12-18 an hour. At 25 boards a month you're making roughly $1,500 in revenue. Raising prices 15-25% would add more profit than any of your friend's ideas, and if sales hold, it costs you nothing. Large boards are your worst deal per hour, so raise those most.

**Good/better/best:** You already have this with small, medium and large. What would help is making the middle option look like the obvious pick. You can do that with a "most popular" label, or by adding a feature to medium like a juice groove or a nicer wood. A fourth premium option, such as end-grain, walnut or custom engraving at $140-200, is worth testing. It's a low-risk way to find out whether some customers will pay much more.

**Subscription box:** This is a poor fit. A board is a one-time, durable purchase, and almost nobody wants a new one every month. A subscription also commits you to making a set amount every month, and you're a one-person shop with no slack. If you want repeat revenue, sell add-ons like board butter, a gift set or a refinishing kit. Those are cheap to make and customers can reorder them.

**Free sample:** Giving away a board costs you 1.5 or more hours plus materials and shipping, about $30-40 in value, and you'd have no way to know whether it leads to a sale. If you want a low-cost hook, use one of these:
- A small free item made from offcuts, like a coaster or spoon rest, with orders over a set amount.
- A discount code for people who join your email list.
- A small sample of board butter in each order.

**What I'd do, in order:**
1. Raise prices and watch sales for 1-2 months.
2. Track materials and shipping cost per board so you know your real margin.
3. Add a premium option and a board butter add-on.
4. Revisit the other ideas once you're consistently selling more than you can comfortably make.
