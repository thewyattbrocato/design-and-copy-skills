Mostly no. Your friend's ideas are standard advice for businesses that don't match yours.

**Good/better/best tiers: you already have them.** Small, medium and large are the tiers. Adding more would mean more designs to make and more choices for customers, and you'd have no spare time to handle either.

**Subscription box: skip it.** A cutting board is a one-time purchase that lasts for years, so nobody needs a new one each month. A box would also add packing, shipping and customer-service work for a one-person shop. The one version that could work is a small recurring consumable like board butter or conditioner, but that's a side item, not a subscription business.

**Free samples: skip it.** Your "sample" is 1.5 or more hours of labor plus wood, so it's expensive to give away. If you want a low-cost way in, offer a small item made from offcuts, like coasters or a trivet, or give a discount code for joining your email list.

**Your pricing is the bigger issue.** Before wood, finish, shipping, payment fees and your tools, you earn about:
- Small: $25/hr
- Medium: $25/hr
- Large: $24/hr

After materials and fees, your real hourly pay is probably $15–18. Your large boards pay the least per hour, even though they take the most time. At 25 boards a month you're working roughly 60 hours, so you have room to grow, but each extra hour is badly paid.

**What I'd do instead:**
1. **Raise prices 15–20%**, especially on the large boards. Handmade boards at your prices are usually underpriced, and with 25 sales a month a small price rise is unlikely to hurt volume. Try something like $45 / $72 / $120.
2. **Add paid personalization**, such as engraved names or dates, for $10–20. It takes little time and has high margin, and it sells well as gifts.
3. **Sell add-ons and gift sets**, like a board plus conditioner, at checkout.
4. **Take advantage of the holidays.** It's late October, so gift demand is about to peak. Set a cap on orders and a shipping cutoff date so you don't burn out.
