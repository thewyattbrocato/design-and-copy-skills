**No to all three. Your bottleneck is your hours, not a lack of options, and each idea adds work without adding much money.**

**What your numbers say (revenue per hour, before materials and fees)**

| Size | Price | Hours | $/hour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | $38 | 1.5 | $25.33 |
| Medium | $62 | 2.5 | $24.80 |
| Large | $95 | 4 | $23.75 |

At 25 boards a month, an even mix is about 67 hours and about $1,600 in revenue. That's roughly $24/hour before wood, finish, payment fees and shipping. Your biggest board pays the least per hour, so the pricing problem is the large size, not a lack of tiers.

**Your friend's three ideas**
- **Good/better/best:** You already have three sizes, and tiers that differ only in size are just quantity. Tiers only help if they split buyers who value different things. A real version would be a premium line, such as end-grain, exotic wood or engraving, aimed at gift buyers who'll pay more for a standout piece. Only do that if some customers are already asking for it.
- **Monthly subscription box:** A cutting board lasts for years, so nobody needs a new one every month. You'd have to invent a recurring product, like conditioner or small items, and that means more sourcing, packing and shipping hours for you. It would also pull your time away from the boards, which are your best seller.
- **Free sample:** A free board costs you its materials plus 1.5 hours, which is about $38 of revenue given up. One free board is as expensive as selling at zero for a day and a half of your month. A small free item, like a coaster or a pack of board butter in each order, gets you most of the goodwill for much less.

**The one change I'd make**
Raise the large board and nudge the medium. To match the small board's hourly rate, the large would be about $101 and the medium about $63. I'd test $105–115 on the large and leave the rest alone. If the large board's sales stay roughly flat, the extra is nearly pure profit. At about 6 large boards a month, $10 more is about $60 a month. If the large drops off sharply, move back toward $100.

Assumed: the 25 sales are split somewhat evenly across sizes, and your materials cost per board is unknown. Wood is likely higher on the large board, which makes its real hourly rate worse than shown.

Would change if: you have waitlists or repeat gift buyers (then a premium line makes sense), you have idle hours you want to fill (then a small add-on item is worth testing), or your monthly mix is mostly small boards (then fix the small price first).
