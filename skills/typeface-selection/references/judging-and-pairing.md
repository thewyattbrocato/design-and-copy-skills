# Judging and pairing

Load when choosing among candidates. Judge on real text, not a name: set one paragraph at the smallest size used, one headline, a column of figures, and a dark-ground sample.

## Structure checklist

| Trait | Look for | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| x-height | medium to large for screen text | Bigger counters read clearer small; the face looks larger at the same size, so compare at matched color, not matched points |
| Apertures | open on c, e, a, s | Closed shapes fill in when small or on dark grounds |
| Contrast | moderate for text; hairlines only at display size | Thin strokes break up in low-resolution rendering |
| Terminals and serifs | sturdy, blunt or bracketed | Survive small sizes and coarse output |
| Distinct glyphs | I, l, 1 and O, 0 differ; a slashed or dotted zero in data faces | Codes, IDs and amounts are misread otherwise |
| Figures | tabular lining available; old-style only for prose | Columns align; prose does not shout |
| Italic | drawn, narrower, cursive in structure, not a slanted roman | Emphasis that reads as a different voice, not a smear |
| Weights | at least regular, medium or semibold, bold; no hairline for text | Hierarchy without adding a family |
| Width | real width variants, not stretched glyphs | Stretching reverses thick-thin stress |
| Coverage | the scripts, diacritics, symbols and currencies you ship | A missing glyph silently swaps to another face |

Two files with one name can be different drawings (a stripped web cut, a lighter family member). Judge the file that ships.

## Reading versus interface versus display

- **Long reading:** text serif or humanist stack; drawn italic; medium weight. Test paragraphs, not specimens.
- **Interface:** sturdy sans, distinct glyphs, tabular figures, a weight range. Avoid condensed, short-x-height or ultra-light faces for small labels.
- **Display:** anything with character, at large size and short length. Set it lighter as it grows, not heavier, unless the ground demands it.
- **Code and data:** monospace with distinct 0/O and 1/l; or the interface sans with tabular figures when alignment alone is the need.

## Pairing

1. **Start with one family.** Use its weights and italic first. Add a second only for a role the first cannot play.
2. **Share a skeleton or differ clearly.** A calligraphic serif beside a humanist sans, or a geometric sans beside a geometric-leaning serif, share construction. A display serif over a plain grotesque differs clearly. A grotesque beside another grotesque is a near miss.
3. **Fix roles before choosing.** Primary for the text people read most; secondary for headings, labels or code. Do not swap roles across the page.
4. **Check proportion.** Matched x-height, or sized to look matched; if the second face needs a size correction at every use, it is the wrong partner.
5. **Keep emphasis inside the owner.** Italic and bold come from the family the text is in. A script beside an italic of the same name clashes.
6. **One idea per page.** One expressive face per view. If every heading is loud, none is.

## Common wrong pairs

- Two near-identical neo-grotesques (indistinguishable except in small flaws).
- A rounded sans with a high-contrast fat-face serif and no shared trait.
- Monospace beside a geometric sans for headings "to look technical".
- Several historically unrelated classics from a menu: pick one and leave the rest out.

## When voice leads

For a brand, poster or campaign the face may be the message. Use a distinctive display face for the few large lines and a plain, sturdy partner for everything small. For product interfaces the voice yields to readability: calm in transactional screens, expressive at hero moments.
