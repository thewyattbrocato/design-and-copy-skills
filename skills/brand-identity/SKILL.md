# Brand identity

A mark is judged where it lives: 16 pixels wide, in one color, on a dark photo, on a sign, beside a rival. Unguided, a logo illustrates the category (a coin for finance, a leaf for anything green), stacks three ideas, leans on gradient and shine, sets the name in an untouched stock font, locks a tagline inside the mark, ships one full-color horizontal version, replaces a known mark to look modern, and promises it is unique.

## When to use

Drawing or critiquing a logo, wordmark, monogram, icon or favicon; deciding whether an existing mark should be redrawn or replaced; building or outlining an identity system and its guidelines; preparing mark files; presenting directions. A one-line request ("a logo for my bakery") is not a small one: the same tests apply, at sketch speed.

## When not to use

- Interface palettes and token plumbing: color-palette or design-system-builder if installed. Choosing a typeface in depth: typeface-selection if installed. Critique of a whole product screen: design-critique if installed.
- Names, taglines, trademark clearance: give the craft view only and send legal questions to a lawyer.
- A request to put a specific literal picture in the mark (a key for a locksmith) is a legitimate brief. Draw it plainly, bold and legible at sign distance, with the name easy to read; do not argue it into an abstraction.
- Temporary campaign art is not the identity. A time-boxed playful variant is fine when the core mark stays recognizable and the standard returns afterward; say so instead of reciting "never alter the logo".
- A mark that already works. If it reads small, in one color and reversed, say so and change at most one detail.

## Rules that change the output

- **Brief first, in one paragraph.** What the organization does, for whom, three adjectives it should be known for, what rivals already use (to leave it), where the mark must live (smallest, largest, dark grounds), who decides. For a quick job write these as stated assumptions and proceed.
- **One idea.** Say the mark in one spoken sentence. If it needs "and", it is two ideas; keep the strongest. Relevance can be a quiet hint rather than a picture of the product; a plain picture is fine when recognition at a glance is the job.
- **Black, then small, then color.** Judge the form solid black on white first, at the smallest real size (16 to 32 pixels for icons), then reversed on dark. Hairlines and fine detail die small: draw a heavier simplified small version. Choose color last, with a reason, and keep a shortlist in one palette so color does not decide the vote.
- **Craft the wordmark.** Readable in a second, checked aloud and upside down for unintended words, spacing tuned by eye, one custom adjustment (a cut, ligature or reshaped letter) so it is not an untouched stock font. Symbol and lettering share weight and temperament.
- **Lockups.** Horizontal, stacked and symbol-only, with clear space and a minimum size. A tagline is a separate optional line, never inside the mark.
- **Gradients, glass, shine and 3D are not ideas.** The core mark is flat and solid; effects belong to specific applications.
- **Refine or replace on evidence.** A widely recognized mark whose meaning still fits is redrawn and adapted (sizes, weight, one outdated detail) and keeps its known color; novelty is not a reason to replace. Replace when it misleads, fails at needed sizes, is generic or confusable with another, or blocks the strategy. Recognition evidence means asking customers, so propose that rather than asserting a number. Details in [existing marks and systems](references/existing-marks-and-systems.md).
- **A system is more than the logo.** Cover the mark: the organization should still be recognizable from color roles, type, a graphic device, image style and layout constants, with rules that let others extend it. Details in the same reference.
- **Present few, strong directions in context.** Two or three you would ship (or one, with its reasoning, when the strategy is settled), each with a sentence of reasoning, shown on real surfaces (app icon, site header, card, avatar, sign). No decoy options. Treat feedback naming a hex value or a font as a problem to solve, and when you disagree make both versions and compare them to the brief.
- **Claim only what is true.** Say a mark is distinctive, appropriate and adaptable. Never guarantee it is unique or forecast sales, recognition or ROI; unique needs a trademark search by a lawyer. Check symbol, color and direction meanings in the markets served, and ask before using a community's sacred or traditional sign.
- **Files.** A mark delivered as SVG or a page follows [mark production](references/mark-production.md).

## Missing facts

If the name, trade, audience or existing mark is not given, assume, say so in one line and still deliver. Ask once, with a default, only when interactive and the job is large; never reply with only questions. Never invent the client's current logo, brand guide, recognition data, customers or results. When proof is needed (a survey, a trademark search), name it as a next step.

## Do not produce

- The trade's stock icon as the whole idea, or three ideas in one mark.
- A gradient or shadow logo that is mud in one color, or a tagline inside the mark.
- One full-color horizontal version as the only deliverable, or a favicon that is the full logo shrunk.
- A replacement of a recognized mark because "modern" was the brief.
- "100% unique", "increases trust", "boosts sales by...", or invented survey, award or customer figures.
- Decoy options, or a fourth direction to look thorough.
- A critique that invents faults in a mark that works.

## Output discipline

Deliver the requested thing first (the mark, the versions, the verdict, the outline), no preamble and no history of logo design, because the reader came for the work. When a count is asked for, give exactly that many directions or points. Honor any length cap by dropping the lowest-value point, and count instead of asserting. A verdict names the evidence it rests on and the next test. After the work, at most two one-line notes (an assumption, the one trade-off), unless more is asked.

## Quick checks

- The idea fits one sentence without "and".
- Solid black reads; reversed reads; the smallest size reads without hairlines.
- Name read aloud and upside down says nothing else.
- Cover the mark: is the system still recognizable?
- Existing mark kept, redrawn or replaced on stated evidence.
- No guarantee, forecast or invented proof; direction count and length cap met.
