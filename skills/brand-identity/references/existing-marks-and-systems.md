# Existing marks and identity systems

## Refine, replace or leave

1. Name the trigger: a new organization, a tired look, a mark that fails at some size, a merger, a strategy shift. The trigger sizes the work; a tired look is rarely a reason to start over.
2. Find the equity: what people already use to recognize it (a shape, a color, a letterform, a signature). List those cues before touching anything.
3. Decide on evidence. Recognition high and meaning still right: redraw the same idea cleaner, fix the sizes and weights, retire the dated detail, keep the known color. Misleading, generic, confusable with a rival or parent, or blocking the strategy: replace, and say which evidence points that way.
4. When the evidence is not in hand, say what would settle it (ask a sample of customers to identify the mark with the name covered) and give the safer default: refine.
5. Change the one signal that carries the new meaning and leave the rest, so loyal users still find the product by sight.
6. Mergers: reduce each old mark to its idea and decide which ideas survive before combining any shapes. Two crests jammed together keep the shapes and lose the meaning.
7. A rename or redesign alone does not change the organization. Say what else must change (story, behavior, touchpoints) and list every place the old mark lives (site, app icons, email, signatures, store listings, signage, uniforms).

## Identity beyond the mark

Cover the mark: if the organization is still recognizable, the system works. Components and what to fix for each:

- **Color roles.** One lead, a few supporting, an action color, neutrals; named roles with screen and print values (see color-palette if installed).
- **Type.** One family or pair that complements the wordmark, with a licensed fallback for documents and email (see typeface-selection if installed).
- **A graphic device.** A pattern, shape, crop or frame drawn from the mark's own geometry, used consistently.
- **Image and icon style.** One treatment, one icon grid and line weight; icons for navigation and steps, not decoration.
- **Layout constants.** Margins, alignment, a recurring structure, so a new format needs no new decisions.
- **Voice.** One voice across headlines, buttons, support lines; vary tone by audience, not voice.
- **Architecture.** One master brand, endorsed sub-brands, or separate brands; decide before naming sub-brands, and write how a new branch, program or format derives from the fixed parts.
- **Expressive versus transactional.** Let marketing and hero moments carry the expressive layer; keep transactional screens calm and conventional.
- **Guidelines.** Short, visual, with the reason behind each rule, example uses and one misuse, an owner to ask, living online rather than a long static file. Rules should enable others to build correctly, not police them.

A flexible identity still has fixed parts: name them, and state the rule of variation.

## Evidence for later

Recognition and preference can be measured over time (do people notice, recognize, prefer, choose). Propose the measures; never state results nobody has collected, and never credit a logo alone for a sales change.
