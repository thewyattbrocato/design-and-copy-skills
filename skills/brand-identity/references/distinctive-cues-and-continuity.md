# Distinctive cues and continuity

Load this when a known look must survive a campaign, refresh, sub-brand or rebrand, when the question is whether a look is recognized without the name or confused with a rival's, or when a new organization has no cues yet. Skip it for one mark judged on craft alone.

## Two questions about any cue

A cue is whatever lets people say "that one": color, shape, letterform, character, sound, recurring line, image style. This skill draws the visual ones; list the rest and say who owns them. Ask separately: do the people who matter link the cue to us, and do they also link it to someone else? A well-known cue a rival shares is a liability; a cue only we use is worth little until many people have met it. A description answers neither. Ask for survey results; without them write "unknown" where the cue is used and name the sample that would settle it (the identification test in the existing-marks reference). Never attach a percentage.

## Judgment calls

The evidence behind the first three comes mostly from frequent, quick purchases among similar brands and is thinner for rare, costly or locked-in choices, so each says where it loosens.

- **Recognition, not meaning.** Default: judge a cue by whether people link it to the brand, not by what its color or shape is said to symbolize. Change when the cue misleads, or when buyers study the offer and read the look for signals.
- **Continuity.** Default: name the two or three cues present in every campaign, pack and page, and let idea, picture and wording vary around them; a new look each season restarts recognition. Change when the cues are generic or confusable (stage a replacement), or the purchase is too rare for anyone to see the brand twice. With no cues yet, choose two or three to repeat from the first piece and call them candidates.
- **One cue per release.** Default: order requested changes from least to most recognized, ship one, and rerun the identification test, same wording and sample, before the next. Change when a merger or legal change forces several: leave the most recognized cue alone and say what is at risk.
- **Exchange check.** Default: put a rival's name on your page, pack or ad, then yours on theirs. If neither looks wrong, the look is generic; change the shared cues (the category's usual color, the stock icon), not everything. A quiet look used identically everywhere can pass; a loud look rivals share fails. Change when the brief fixes the look.
- **Borrow-or-build check.** For a new product, tier or sub-brand: does it add weight to what people already link to the name, or borrow the name's credit for something it does not stand for? Default: keep it under the name only when a customer would guess correctly what it is; otherwise an endorsed or separate look.
- **Promise check.** Name one moment (unboxing, support reply, cancellation) where what customers receive could contradict what the look promises, and what to fix there before redrawing.

## Asking people about directions

For choosing among two or three marks, names or lines. Default: one direction per person, shown to people who resemble the real audience, not colleagues. First ask what they think it is, who is behind it and what they would expect from that sender; only then show the options together and ask which they would trust with a real purchase, and why. Never open with "which do you like". About ten people surface misreadings but cannot measure shares or crown a winner, so report patterns and say what stayed untested. Change when no audience is reachable: name the untested assumption and the cheapest way to reach a few real people.

Report these checks inside the recommendation, not as a separate section, unless asked. Never claim a recognition, trust, premium or sales effect without data.
