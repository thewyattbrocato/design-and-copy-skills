# Pacing long pages

Load this for landing pages, long reads, and any page with more than three or four sections. A long page is read as a sequence, so its rhythm matters as much as any one section.

## Order versus feel

Decide two things separately.

- **Order of content:** what comes first, second, last. This follows the reader's questions: what is this, why should I care, how does it work, is it real, what does it cost, what do I do next.
- **Feel of the sequence:** how dense, how quiet, how loud each stretch is. This is what keeps the page from reading as one long slab.

Sketch the sequence abstractly (boxes labeled open, woven, full, loud) before choosing colors or fonts.

## Density states

- **Open:** a little content, lots of space, large type. Good for a claim or a transition.
- **Woven:** text and images interleaved, moderate density. Good for explanations.
- **Full:** a lot of content in a tidy structure. Good for specifications, comparisons, and proof.

Alternate them. Two full sections in a row tire the reader; three open ones in a row feel thin.

## One loud section

- Choose one section to be the loudest: the biggest type, the strongest color field, or a full-bleed image.
- Keep the others calmer so the loud one registers.
- If everything is loud, nothing is; if nothing is, the page feels flat.

## Section patterns beyond a row of cards

Pick the pattern from what the section says.

| Content | Structure |
| --- | --- |
| A process with steps | A numbered sequence, vertical or horizontal, with connecting lines |
| Two or three options compared | Side by side with shared row labels |
| One strong claim | A single large statement with a short supporting line |
| Proof (customers, numbers) | A band of figures, quotes, or logos in a simple row |
| A feature with a visual | Text beside a large image, then flip it for the next feature |
| Many small parallel items | A grid of equal cells (cards are fine here) |
| A question the reader will have | A short list of questions with answers |
| The next step | A closing band with one action |

Cards in rows are right when items are parallel and equal. They are wrong as the default for everything.

## Shared edge

All sections share one content edge and one outer margin so the page reads as one object. Variation lives inside the sections: image side, background field, density. Background fields can run edge to edge while content stays on the shared edge.

## Named breaks

A break from the structure needs a job, named in a phrase:

- the hero image runs full bleed because it is the page;
- a pull quote hangs into the margin to mark the article's turn;
- one card in a set is larger because it is the recommended plan.

A break with no job is decoration. If the same break appears a third time, it is a rule now: fold it into the structure or remove it.

## Checks

- List the sections with their patterns; adjacent sections should differ unless their content is parallel.
- Name the loud section.
- Check that the first and last sections each carry one clear action.
- Check the sequence at narrow width: does the story still read in order?
