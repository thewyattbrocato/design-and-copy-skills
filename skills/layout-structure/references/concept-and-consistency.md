# Concept, consistency and breaking out

Load this when a page or set of pages is meant to feel like something (a studio, a story, a launch, an event), when several pieces must read as one family, or when an image wants to leave the grid. Skip it for tools, forms, settings, tables and documentation: there the conventional structure is the right direction, and a plain, evenly weighted page can be the whole answer.

## Concept before composition

Write the concept as one phrase before placing anything: what the content is like or what the reader should feel, and how strongly. "Calm, handmade, unhurried." "One subject, tense, no distractions." "A tidy shelf of equals." Keep it as working notes; the answer needs no section for it unless you assumed something the user should correct, and then one line.

If the brief gives no concept, derive one from the audience and the content, not from the genre of site. Then let the structure carry it:

| The content is... | The layout says it with... |
| --- | --- |
| Peers of equal rank | Equal cells, symmetric placement, even weights |
| One lead item and its support | An unequal column pair (the lead roughly twice the support), one scale jump, supporting facts and captions in the narrow column |
| Two different kinds of material | Separate bands with different inner grids or a change of ground, not one grid stretched over both |

Intensity works the same way. Calm reading wants shallow steps between sizes, even weights and generous margins; drama wants steep steps, uneven weights and a tighter crop. Take the actual ratios from the typesetting skill when it is installed.

- **Where to push.** Default: conventional structure at moments of trust or routine work (payment, sign-in, settings, errors, safety and legal notices), and expressive structure where the reader is being persuaded or moved (a landing page, a feature story, first run, a confirmation after a purchase). Change when the user asks for a bold payment page or a plain story; follow them.
- **Escaping the template.** Default: before adding effects, change the proportions. Collapse twelve equal columns into six wider ones, use one wide and one narrow column, or leave a column empty on purpose. Change when the design system fixes the grid; then vary spans within it.
- **Irregular placement.** Default: offset blocks by whole columns so they still relate. Change for deliberate scatter only when the subject is loose and playful; pair a scattered image group with a strict text column, and never scatter a meticulous or orderly subject.
- **Moves per page.** Default: one or two structural moves (unequal pair, scale jump, edge bleed, stacked grids), each with a named job. Four moves on one screen is a collage with no entry point.

### Worked outline (an open-day page for a bicycle co-op)

- Concept: "welcoming, a bit scruffy, hands-on", medium intensity. Not a polished shop, not a protest poster.
- Content relationship: one lead event with a supporting list of workshops and a practical block (time, address, how to get there).
- Structure: a wide column for the event story and one large photo that bleeds off the leading edge; a narrow column for the workshop list and the practical block, which stays in plain type and on the grid. The sign-up action sits in the narrow column at the top.
- One scale jump: the date in large numerals. No tilted blocks, no second bleed.
- Phone: the photo is re-cropped tighter on the mechanic's hands, the date leads, and the practical block follows the sign-up action.

## Consistency across a set

A set is the pages of a site, the cards of a series, the posters of a campaign. Fix the frame and vary the content.

- **Hold constant:** grid and margins, type roles, image ratio and color treatment, and the positions of title, date and mark. **Let vary:** the picture, the headline length, one accent per piece.
- **Template plus override.** Default: most pieces use the shared template. Change for a flagship piece (a launch, the lead story) that gets an override limited to color, scale and layout weight, built from the same parts rather than new components. When every piece overrides, the override has become the template; fold it in.
- **Rhythm and emphasis.** Equal-size images give rhythm; one larger image says "this one matters". Enlarge only the piece that ranks first, and do not repeat the same rhythm of sizes page after page.
- **Constant quirks.** A fixed device (a small tilt, an oversized portrait above the text) identifies a set only when it is identical every time and reading is unharmed. A quirk that varies is noise.

Check it by covering the headlines and logos and lining the pieces up: they should still look related, and the one that looks foreign should be there on purpose.

## Breaking out for an image

- **When to break.** Default: the image sits on the grid. Change when the image is the point of the page, or when the scene continues beyond the frame (a crowd, a landscape, a process). Bleeding off one edge suggests more beyond it; a picture bleeding on every side is just full width.
- **Eyeline.** Put the subject's gaze toward the headline or text block, so the text sits on the side the subject faces. A portrait looking off the page edge drags the eye away. If flipping the photo breaks nothing in it (no lettering, no logo), flip it rather than move the text.
- **Magnet.** A strong image pulls the eye into itself. Do not put the one fact the reader must see (a price, a deadline, a warning) directly above or against it; set it beside the image or on a calmer ground.
- **Frequency.** A dramatic full-bleed on every page teaches readers to skip it. Spend it on the pieces that earn it and keep the pages between calm and on the grid.
- **Phone.** Recompose rather than shrink. Use a different crop in a taller or tighter frame around the subject, keep the eyeline toward the text, drop decorative overlap, and keep source order equal to reading order. A wide hero squeezed to a thin strip with the headline across a face is the failure to look for.
- **Guardrails for any break.** Text over the image passes contrast at its worst point, the entry point stays obvious, key facts stay findable, and rotation never touches body text. If the break would appear a third time, it belongs in the structure (see the pacing reference).

## Checks

- The concept fits one phrase, and you can point to two layout choices that express it and one thing it rules out.
- Routine and trust surfaces (forms, checkout, settings, tables) kept their conventional structure.
- Each structural move has a named job; no screen has more than two.
- Covering headlines and logos, a set still reads as one family, and constant elements sit in the same place in every piece.
- The subject's gaze points toward the content, and no key fact sits directly above a strong image.
- The phone crop differs from the desktop crop and is not the same image scaled down.
