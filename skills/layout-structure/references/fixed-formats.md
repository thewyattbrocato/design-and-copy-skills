# Fixed formats

Load this for slides, posters, social graphics, and print pages. A reflowing screen can bend; a fixed format cannot. The structure has to add up exactly, and what the format crops or hides is part of the brief.

## Unit arithmetic

- Choose a base unit from the smallest text that must line up (for example the leading of the body text). Build margins, gutters, and cell heights as whole multiples of it.
- Columns, gutters, and margins must sum to the format width with no fractional leftover. If a design leaves 3 units over, change a gutter or the column count, not the text.
- Work from the copy: count the lines and figures that must fit, then choose the grid. Do not pick a grid and hope the copy fits.
- Check the remainder: if the sums do not close to zero, adjust before styling.

## Margins

- Outer margins are larger than gutters.
- For printed pages, use unequal margins: the foot deeper than the head, the outer side larger than the spine side. For a spread, consider the two pages as one shape.
- For screens and slides, equal margins are fine; keep them generous and consistent.
- Do not let text sit closer to the trim than the margin, and keep key content out of the area that gets cut or hidden.

## Safe areas

- Slides: keep text inside a margin of at least about 5 percent of the width on every side; projectors and thumbnails crop.
- Social cards and stories: keep text and faces inside the central area. Platforms overlay interface chrome on the top and bottom of vertical formats (leave roughly the top and bottom 12 to 15 percent clear), and link previews are cropped differently by each app.
- Print: keep key content inside the margin; extend backgrounds past the trim edge (bleed, commonly 3mm or 0.125in) so a small cutting error does not show a white edge.

## Recompose, do not crop

One message in several formats needs one design per format. Cropping a wide hero into a tall story cuts the text and leaves an empty remainder.

For each ratio (about 1.91:1 link preview, 1:1 post, 4:5 portrait post, 9:16 story, 16:9 slide):

1. List what must appear: the headline, the key fact, the mark.
2. Place those in the new frame from scratch; use more vertical stacking in tall frames and side-by-side in wide ones.
3. Hold constant: type family and weights, the color relationships, the position logic of the mark (for example, always bottom leading). Let sizes and positions change.
4. Check each at its smallest display size.

## Sheet proportions

Standard paper sheets (the A series and similar) share one proportion of roughly 1 to 1.414, which keeps its shape when folded in half. Use that for page structures rather than a golden-ratio rectangle. Do not use a golden ratio as a default for margins, columns, or sidebars; choose them from the content and the measure.

## Slides specifically

- One idea per slide; the headline states the point.
- Use a small number of structures (title, statement, two-up, figure, quote) and repeat them so the deck feels consistent.
- Text large enough to read from the back of a room; if it does not fit, split the slide.
- Charts and images get the width they need; do not crop them to fit a box.

## Posters specifically

- One entry point visible from a distance: the largest or strongest element.
- Secondary information (dates, place, details) stays findable at the second level.
- Expressive, off-grid compositions are fine, if the entry point and the key facts remain findable and the relationships look intended.

## Checks

- Do margins, gutters, and columns sum exactly to the format?
- Is key text inside the safe area at each ratio?
- Was each ratio composed, not cropped from another?
- At the smallest display size, can the headline still be read?
