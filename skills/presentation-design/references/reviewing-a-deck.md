# Reviewing a deck

Load this when asked to review, critique, improve or rescue an existing deck or talk: "why is this boring", "my slides are walls of text", "will this land with the committee", "give me feedback". Fix the most expensive problems first; polish questions come last. Scored, severity-ranked critiques of a visual design are design-critique's format when installed; this file says what to look at in a deck and in what order.

## Pick the reader

Judge clarity as the least informed person in the room would, and accuracy as the most expert one would. A deck that the newcomer can follow and the specialist can trust passes both. Say who you assumed.

## Review order

1. **Titles only.** Read the slide titles in sequence with nothing else. Do they give the argument and the ask? If not, the deck fails at the story level, and visual notes are premature. Report which titles are labels and write a claim for each.
2. **Takeaway and action.** Is there one sentence the deck is about, and a verb the audience should do? Say what the deck seems to want, and whether that is stated early.
3. **Order.** Does it run need, takeaway, support, recap, ask, or does it follow the source or the calendar? Is there an agenda, a greeting slide or a "thank you" ending to remove?
4. **Fit to time.** Count content slides against the minutes. State the arithmetic and what to drop first.
5. **Slide by slide.** For each slide: one message? Words (count them) under the cap? Anything the speaker will not say? Does it stand alone? Is detail that belongs in a handout on the slide?
6. **Data slides.** Conclusion in the title, one emphasis, no table beside the chart, fixed axes across runs.
7. **Consistency.** Same roles in the same places, one image family, no decoration with no job. Hand off specifics to visual-hierarchy and the other styling skills.
8. **Close.** The ask has an owner and a date; the last slide is not a "Thank you".

## A light look stays light

When the user asks for a quick look or a once-over, or says the deck is nearly final: one line of verdict first, then at most three changes ranked by cost. Keep the slide count and order unless the story fails, the time overruns or the data misleads, and leave alone titles that already state a claim. Judge a deck meant to be read alone as a document: fuller sentences and context are right there.

## What a good review produces

- The three to five problems that cost the most, in order, each with the slide number, what is there, and the specific fix.
- The rewritten titles, as a list.
- A word count for the worst slides and where their text should go (notes, handout, deleted).
- What to keep. A deck that has strong claim titles or a clear ask deserves to hear it, and should not be rewritten wholesale.
- What was not checked (the delivery, the room, the source data).

When the user asks to fix the deck, rebuild the slide list, not just the critique. When the deck is already good, say so, name what makes it work, and change little.

## Review phrasing

Comment on the slide, not the author. Point to the problem and a fix; phrase suggestions as "this slide would work harder if..." or "the audience will have to...". Separate must-fix (the story fails, the time overruns, the data misleads) from suggested (a nicer picture). Do not rewrite everything to make a point about one title.

## Patterns and fixes

| What you see | What it usually means | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every title is one or two words | The deck is organized by topic | Claim titles, then reorder by the argument |
| 60+ words on a slide | Script or document on the screen | Split, or move to notes or a handout |
| The deck mirrors a report's sections | Structure by inheritance | Rebuild around the takeaway; sections become appendix |
| An agenda and an overview slide before any content | The need is missing | Replace with the need and the takeaway |
| Four charts with four different axes in a row | Shifting baselines | Fix ranges and colors, or separate the comparisons |
| The last slide says "Questions?" | No ask | Takeaway and ask with owner and date |
| Looks fine, nobody remembers it | No single message | Write the takeaway, drop slides that do not serve it |
