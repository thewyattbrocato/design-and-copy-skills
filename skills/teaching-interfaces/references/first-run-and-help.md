# First run and help

Load for first-run flows, tours, sample data, empty states, what's-new notes, and help articles. Starting values; change them when the product says otherwise.

## First run

- Walk your own path and count the screens, fields and clicks before the first real success. Cut or postpone anything not needed for it. Ask only what the first task needs.
- Open in the product, or one step from it. One sentence of concept, one primary action, an example or sample visible, a skip.
- If the product has several main reasons to arrive, let a first question pick a short path for each; with one dominant reason, do not branch.
- Prefer a guided first task over a tour. If you keep a tour, three to five steps that move toward the first success; the rest as hints at the point of use, ignorable and dismissible. Block only where skipping causes real harm.
- Show progress and start with part of it done. Do not pad a checklist with trivial steps to inflate completion.
- Welcome briefly and restate what the person came for. Confirm the first win in a line that says what now works and offers the next step; no ceremony that delays it.
- Keep a way back: a help entry, a replay link, and the tip reappearing when the person reaches the feature for real.
- Prompts the person set themselves, or that vary, last longer than identical ones.

## Sample data

- Use sample content when a blank screen has no visible shape. Mark it as sample, make it removable in one action, and keep numbers obviously illustrative.
- Do not seed realistic-looking records into an account where they could be mistaken for the person's own work or counted in totals.

## Empty states

- Purpose in a line, the one action that starts it, an optional example or template, and the controls that do nothing yet hidden.
- The illustration, if any, is small and shows what the area will hold. The action button is the loudest element.
- Different reasons for emptiness get different messages: nothing yet, nothing matches, cleared, or failed to load.

## What's-new notes

- Lead with what the person can now do, in their words, one line each; link to the place it lives. Mention only changes they will notice. Show it once, where it applies, and keep it findable.

## Help articles

- Title the task ("Export your data"), not the feature. One line on when and why, and what you need first.
- Numbered steps, one action each, each with what the person will see afterwards. Name the control exactly as the interface does; if the label is not known, use a visible placeholder.
- Crop screenshots to the relevant area and mark the target; do not rely on color alone. Alt text says what the step shows.
- Put problems and their fixes last, in the person's symptoms ("The file is empty"). Link related tasks. Keep reference tables out of tutorials and tutorials out of reference.
- Short enough to follow with the product open beside it; split by task rather than by menu.
