# Discounts and negotiated prices

Load when a customer asks for a better price, when discount rules, approval limits or exceptions are being set or checked, when a bid or tender is in play, or when someone asks whether the discounts given are justified. Examples are invented; use the user's figures and mark any other figure "assumed". The words said in the room belong to sales-conversations, the wording of a concession to offers-and-value-propositions.

## Is it an ordinary, fair trade?

A small discount traded for something real (payment up front, a longer term, a reference) inside the stated floor needs a yes, the arithmetic in one line and a note of what was received. Do not add policy, audits or warnings to it. Everything below applies when the ask is large, repeated, or outside the rules.

## An exception is a rule in waiting

Default: ask whether the same terms could go to every customer in the same position and still earn. If yes, write them as a rule open to all; if no, decline or trade for something. A one-off becomes the standing price once it is granted, so say what the customer gives for it and when it ends. Change to a true one-off only when the circumstance cannot recur (a launch partner, a one-time clearance).

## A volume ask

Default: price only the added units, as a rebate paid after they are bought, never a cut on everything. An account buying 300 seats at 80 asks for 8% off if it grows to 360. Across the whole order that gives up 2,304 for 60 extra seats, so the added seats sell at about 41.60 each, roughly half price. A 15% rebate on the 60 added seats gives up 720 and keeps the base intact. Change to a lower price on the whole order only when the bigger order truly lowers your cost to serve, and price that saving, not the size of the order.

Where a discount applies to every unit once a threshold is crossed, a buyer just under it pays more in total than one just over. Use steps that apply only to the units inside each band, or check the boundary.

## A discount asked on a rise

An account already on a discounted price that asks for a discount on the rise is asking to be discounted twice. Default: keep the rate and give time (a later start, or steps inside the contract term). Offer the same concession to every account in the same position; never reverse a rise only for the customers who object, since the quiet ones then pay more for the same thing.

## Making up for your own failure

Default: a one-off credit or lump sum sized to the harm, with an end date. A permanent price cut becomes the new base and the customer never sees it as an apology.

## Habits that teach waiting

A quarter-end deal or the same sale every year trains buyers to hold out, and makes full-price demand look weaker than it is. If a promotion is needed, make it rare, dated and open to a defined group. Count only the lift from new buyers or new use, and watch the dip after it ends. On a subscription, cutting the first months' price makes renewal feel like a rise; prefer a one-time credit or extra that leaves the recurring price alone.

## Approval limits

Default: tie each approval level to what is received (term, prepayment, volume, reference), not to who is asking or how hard. A ladder of rising authority teaches buyers to climb it. State the floor and the named approver in advance; if most quotes need the top rung, the list price or the limits are wrong.

## Standing discounts that outlived their reason

List every standing discount with its reason and date. Remove or re-trade those that pay for work the buyer no longer does (an old pilot, a reference never given, a volume no longer bought).

## Checking whether discounts are justified

Default: compare what each account actually pays after every discount, rebate, free extra and payment term with what similar accounts pay for the same thing. Name the data needed (invoice lines and contract terms), the grouping of similar accounts, and the accounts furthest below their peers as the first to look at. Watch for discounts bunched on round steps and for small accounts with deeper discounts than large ones. To move from case-by-case to rules, start with the worst outliers, bring them up in steps over the contract term, and accept losing buyers who only buy on price.

## Tenders and formal bids

Default: bid when the specification is defined and the buyer will award on it. A vague spec, a choice that seems already made and no commitment to award suggest a price check for the incumbent; decline or bid the plain version. If bidding on price, offer the plain scope with a priced list of extras, and fix the walk-away price before bidding starts and keep it.
