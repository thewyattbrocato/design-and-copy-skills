# Multi-step and long forms

Load this when a form spans steps or sittings, or when you design checkout, review and confirmation.

## When to split

Default: keep a short single-topic form on one page. Split when at least one of these is true:

- The form is long, so errors and scrolling pile up.
- Later questions depend on earlier answers.
- Payment happens on a phone, where one decision per screen is easier to complete.
- People will be interrupted and need to save or resume.
- One step needs room for a complex widget, such as a seat map or a slot picker.

Splitting costs page loads and orientation. Keep steps fast, and make the next question obvious. Group two questions only when the person sees them as one decision.

## Order

Order by when a question feels reasonable. A sensible checkout order: email, optional phone with its reason, delivery address, delivery options, notes, payment, review, confirmation. Ask for payment after the person has committed to the order, not before.

## Naming steps

Put the step name and count in the page heading ("Delivery, step 2 of 4"). Assistive technology reads the same words that sighted people see. Keep one heading level for the step title on every step.

A visual progress bar costs a phone screen and misleads when a path skips steps (collecting in store skips delivery). Add one only when the form is long or runs over several sessions, or when testing shows people get lost. If you do add one, do not announce the same information twice. Never use unnamed dots.

## Back and keeping answers

Back always works and keeps what was typed. Editing an earlier answer does not wipe later ones unless they depend on it, and then say what will be asked again.

## Checkout specifics

- Offer guest checkout first. Invite an account after the order succeeds, with a stated benefit (faster reorder).
- Give a reason beside the email field (receipt, delivery updates).
- Remove site-wide navigation and search during the task. Keep the logo, a trust cue and the accepted payment types.
- Show the order summary beside or above the step so people can verify what they are paying for.
- When picking among options with a price or time (delivery slots, tickets), show the comparing detail on each option: window and fee, together.
- The step's primary button names what comes next ("Continue to payment").

## Review step

Before anything irreversible, show every answer in a readable list with a "Change" link next to each. The review's button says the committing act and the amount ("Place order - $48.20"). A review is not needed for a reversible, low-stakes form.

## Confirmation

The confirmation page says:

- That it worked, with a reference the person can quote.
- What happens next and when ("Delivery Thursday, 2 to 4 pm").
- What to do if it goes wrong, with a contact.
- A single offer to create an account if the person checked out as a guest.

Send the same details by email when the person might not return to the page.

## Long jobs that take days

Many applications and claims cannot be done in one sitting. Plan for hours or days, several people and steps that happen offline.

1. **Readiness page.** Before the first question, say what the person needs: eligibility, documents, dates, who else must be involved, and how long it takes. This saves dead ends.
2. **Task list.** Break the work into tasks short enough for one sitting. Name each with a verb ("Add your policy details"). Show status (not started, in progress, done) and a time estimate. Keep tasks of roughly equal size at the same level. Say who owns a task when several people share the job.
3. **Documents inside tasks.** Ask for each document in the task that uses it, not in a separate upload drawer at the end.
4. **Save and resume.** Save as the person goes. Offer a way back with a link or a sign-in, and say how long drafts are kept.
5. **Branching.** Use one topic per page for branches. Ask "Add another?" after each repeated item instead of cloning a big block of fields.
6. **Finish.** Show the completed list, let people reopen any task, run a review of all answers, and give one clear way to submit.

## Repeating items

For short, non-branching repeats (adding collaborators), list what has been added above the form with a remove link on each row, and keep the form in place. When each item needs different questions, give each item its own page and ask whether to add another.
