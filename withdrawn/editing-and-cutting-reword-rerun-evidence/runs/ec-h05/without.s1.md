Overall it's solid. The tone is human, you give real numbers, and you offer an escape hatch. But it's built in the wrong order, and a few things will draw pushback.

**Check first: the date.** Today is Oct 14, so "starting June 3" is in the past. If you mean next June, say the year. If the date is a leftover from an earlier draft, fix it before Friday.

**What to fix**

1. **The news is in paragraph 4.** Customers get history and an apology before they learn their delivery window is doubling. They'll read that as stalling. Lead with the change in one or two sentences, then explain why.

2. **The statistic invites a skeptical reading.** A four-hour window makes "missed" easier to avoid by definition. A sharp customer will think you moved the goalposts. Say so yourself: "Yes, a wider window makes it easier for us to hit. That's partly the point, because it gives dispatch room to absorb traffic and gate delays. But the pilot also showed X." Also define "missed delivery" (outside the window? late by more than an hour?). If you have any other pilot result, like fewer reschedules or better ETA accuracy, add it.

3. **The apology is vague.** "Some of that we deserved" and "our dispatchers worked overtime" read as defensive. Either name what went wrong ("we told you two hours and didn't hit it often enough") or cut it. The line about dispatchers and drivers is a non sequitur in an apology paragraph. Thank them elsewhere.

4. **The appointment option is buried and unclear.** For customers with a tight dock, it's the most important sentence in the post. Move it up next to the change. Also clarify "at no extra charge for existing accounts": what do new accounts pay, and is there a cap on appointments?

5. **The 30-minute text may not be enough.** With a four-hour window, a dock manager may need more than 30 minutes of notice to staff a door. Say whether you're considering an earlier heads-up, or acknowledge the gap.

6. **The ending is boilerplate.** "Continue" and "grow" both appear twice in the last line and the intro. End on something actionable, like a phone number or email for questions.

**Smaller points**
- The founder and box-truck opening is nice, but it should be a sentence, not a paragraph, in a post like this.
- The title is flat. "Our delivery windows are changing to four hours on [date]" tells people what's coming.

**Suggested structure:** change and date, why, the pilot numbers with the tradeoff acknowledged, what you're doing about customers it hurts (appointments, notifications), and a contact. Keep the short apology, but make it specific.
