Honest take: the substance is good, but the structure works against you.

**What's wrong**

1. **The news is in paragraph 5.** Most readers are dispatchers and dock managers, and what they need to know is that the window is doubling on June 3. They have to get through the founder story and the apology first, and they'll feel like you're stalling. Lead with the change and the reason.
2. **Paragraphs 1-3 are mostly about you.** The 2009 history, "we're proud of that," and "our drivers deserve a lot of credit" read as self-congratulation. That's a bad mood to set for a post that is bad news for some customers. Keep one plain sentence acknowledging last winter ("many of you felt it, and some of that we deserved") and cut the rest.
3. **The statistic may backfire.** A skeptical reader will notice that a missed delivery is easier to avoid when the window is twice as wide. If "missed" means outside the window, 11 to 4 doesn't show you got more reliable. It shows you moved the goalposts. Say how you measured it. If the pilot counted misses against the original two-hour window, say so, because that's a much stronger claim. Also, "three routes" out of how many?
4. **The drivers-idling benefit is yours, not the customer's.** It's fine to mention, but don't offer it as a reason customers should be happy.
5. **The best part is buried.** The fixed-appointment option is what lets a customer with a tight dock keep their current service. Move it up, and answer these questions:
   - What does "existing accounts" mean for new ones?
   - How do they ask for an appointment: a phone number, an email, or their rep?
6. **The close is boilerplate.** "Continue" appears twice, and "as we continue to grow" circles back to the opening. End on the action: call or email here if four hours won't work.

**Check before Friday:** the post says June 3 with no year. If that's next June, say so. Today is Oct 15, so a reader could take it as already past.

**Suggested order:** the change and date, then why (reliability, plus the pilot numbers with a definition), then the 30-minute text, then the fixed-appointment option with contact info, then a short line owning last winter. Cut the founder story, or trim it to one sentence.
