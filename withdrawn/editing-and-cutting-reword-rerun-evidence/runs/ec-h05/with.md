The reasoning is solid and the tone is mostly human, but I wouldn't post it as is.

**What works**
- The pilot numbers are specific, which is rare in company posts.
- The fixed-appointment offer is a real escape hatch.
- You acknowledge that a longer window sounds like a step backward.

**What I'd fix**

1. **The news is in paragraph 4.** Readers want to know what's changing and when. Open with something like "Starting June 3, delivery windows go from two hours to four. Here's why." Then give the reasons.

2. **The first two paragraphs are throat-clearing.** The 2009 origin story and "we're proud of that" delay the point. If you want the history, cut it to one sentence.

3. **The apology is grudging.** "Some of that we deserved" and "many of you felt it" read as defensive. The weather, retirements and migration are also excuses, and the line about drivers deserving credit is a non sequitur in an apology. Either apologize plainly ("last winter we let you down") and say what you fixed, or drop the section.

4. **The stat invites an obvious objection.** A sharp customer will think, "Of course you miss less often when the window is twice as long." Say how you defined a miss in the pilot. If it was measured against the old two-hour window, say so. If it wasn't, you're comparing different things. Also give the dates and how many stops the pilot covered. "Three routes" sounds thin.

5. **The link from reliability to wider windows is implied, not stated.** Say it directly: "A wider window gives drivers room to absorb traffic, weather and slow docks, so we can keep the promise we make."

6. **"Less time idling in lots" doesn't help the customer.** Either tie it to something they care about, like fewer delays, or cut it.

7. **A 30-minute text is thin for a four-hour window.** Docks that have to staff a delivery will wonder if that's enough notice. If you can offer an earlier heads-up or a live ETA, say so. If you can't, expect that question.

8. **The appointment offer needs a way to act on it.** Add a phone number or link. Moving it higher would also help, since the customers most upset by this change are the ones who need it.

9. **The closing is boilerplate.** "Continue" appears twice, and "thank you for your trust" is filler. End on the call-to-action instead.

**Check the dates before Friday.** I'm seeing today as October 15, 2026. A post saying "starting June 3," "this spring" and "last winter" would be off by months. If this was drafted earlier, update the timeline. If June 3 is next year, a seven-month lead time changes how the whole post should read.
