Honest take: the facts are fine, but the structure works against you. Readers will see this as a downgrade dressed up as good news.

**What's working**
- The fixed-appointment option is the best part. It gives customers with tight docks a way out.
- The 30-minute text heads-up is useful.
- Using real numbers (11 to 4 per 100) is better than vague claims.

**Problems**
1. **The change is in paragraph 4.** Customers who care about this will skim past the history and the "we've grown" material and feel ambushed when they find it. Put the change, the date, and what it means for them in the first two sentences.
2. **The headline stat is easy to poke at.** If a "miss" means arriving outside the window, doubling the window will lower misses almost automatically. A sharp customer will say so. Either say how you defined a miss, or add something that isn't a side effect of the window size, such as on-time rate against the old two-hour window, or the idling-time improvement with a number attached. Also, three routes in one spring is a small sample, so don't oversell it.
3. **"Reliability" is doing a lot of work.** You tell customers they value reliability and then give them less precision. That's defensible, but say plainly that you're trading precision for dependability. Acknowledge that some docks will be hurt, rather than just saying a longer window "sounds like a step backward."
4. **The apology is vague.** "Some of that we deserved" and the dispatchers/drivers line read as a half-apology that pivots to praise. Either own the specific failures in a sentence or cut the paragraph. Right now it's the weakest section.
5. **"At no extra charge for existing accounts"** raises the question of what new accounts pay. Clarify, or expect calls about it.
6. **The ending is boilerplate.** "Continue" and "continue to grow" repeat, and the closing says nothing. End on the action: call this number to set up an appointment.
7. **Check the date.** It says "starting June 3." If you're posting Friday, make sure that's the right month and year. As of today (Oct 14, 2026), June 3 has already passed.

**Missing details customers will want**
- Is it a morning/afternoon window, or does it float?
- Who do they call, and by when, to lock in an appointment before the change?
- Does the 30-minute text replace anything, or is it new?

**Suggested order**
1. What's changing and when (two-hour to four-hour windows, date).
2. Why: the reliability problem, with the pilot numbers and how you measured them.
3. What you're doing about the downside: texts, fixed appointments, how to request one.
4. A short, specific acknowledgment of last winter.
5. Contact info.
