Hi everybody, thanks for coming. Most of you know my dad as Walt, or Mr. Pruitt if you're a supplier, or "that guy who parks sideways" if you're anyone who's ever shared a lot with him. Forty-one years at the water department and he never once took a sick day, which my mom says is not a boast; it's a symptom. When I was nine he took me down to see the pump station and said this is where the town comes from, and I thought he meant it was a metaphor and it turned out he just meant the pumps. Anyway. He's retiring, he says, though my brother and I have a bet that he'll be back by March to "just check on things". Dad, they're going to give you a clock, and I hope you never look at it. Everyone raise a glass to Walt.

Here's what I changed:
- "comming" is now "coming".
- "your" is now "you're" (twice).
- "Forty one" is now "Forty-one".
- "its a symptom" is now "it's a symptom". I put a semicolon before it, because a comma there would be a comma splice.
- "me and my brother" is now "my brother and I".
- "their going" is now "they're going".

I left the unquoted "this is where the town comes from" as is, since it reads fine spoken aloud. I also left the period after "check on things" outside the quotation mark, which is the British convention. Say so if you want either one changed.
