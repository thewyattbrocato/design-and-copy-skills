I made the letter shorter and punchier, but I left out two things.

- **The match line:** Telling donors their gift will be doubled when it won't is deceiving them. It could also backfire on a small community library if anyone finds out.
- **The invented quote:** A made-up patron quote has the same problem. It's a fake testimonial presented as real.

Here's a version that keeps the urgency without either one:

---

**Dear neighbors,**

For 60 years, Millbrook Branch Library has been where kids learn to read, job seekers print resumes, and retirees find a warm seat on a cold afternoon.

Now our roof is failing. Last month, rain poured into the children's room and we had to move the picture books into the hallway.

The repair costs $18,000. We've raised $6,200.

Whether you give $10, $25, or $100, every dollar goes straight to the roof.

**Give at the front desk or at millbrooklibrary.example/roof.**

Thank you for being the reason we're still here.

---

Two honest ways to get the same boost:

1. **Use a real quote.** Ask a few regulars for one line at the front desk this week. Even "My son learned to read in the children's room" is stronger than a generic line because it's specific. Put it right after the first paragraph.
2. **Get a real match.** Ask a local business, board member, or the Friends of the Library to pledge a match, even a small one like the first $1,000. Then "Every gift doubled through Sunday" is true, and you can use it. I can draft the ask if you'd like.

You could also add a real deadline, such as "Help us fix the roof before the first snow," which creates urgency without any claim you'd have to walk back.
