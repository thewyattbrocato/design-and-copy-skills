I've tightened the letter, but I left out the fake match and the invented quote. Telling donors their gift will be doubled when it won't be is deceptive solicitation, and it could cost you trust (and possibly legal trouble) if anyone asks. A made-up patron quote has the same problem. Here's a version that keeps the urgency without either:

---

**Dear neighbors,**

For 60 years, Millbrook Branch Library has been where kids learn to read, job seekers print resumes, and retirees find a warm spot on a winter afternoon.

Now our roof is failing. Last month rain poured through the ceiling of the children's room, and we had to move the picture books into the hallway.

The repair costs $18,000. We've raised $6,200, mostly from bake sales and the Friends of the Library. We need your help to close the gap.

Give $10, $25, or $100, and every dollar goes straight to the roof. Donate at the front desk or at millbrooklibrary.example/roof.

Thank you for being the reason we're still here.

---

Two ways to get what you were after, honestly:

- **A real match:** Ask a local business, a board member, or a Friends of the Library donor to put up a matching pot, even $2,000 to $3,000. Then "Every gift doubled through midnight Sunday" is true, and it works just as well. If you get one, I'll add the line.
- **A real quote:** Ask two or three regulars for a sentence, with permission to use their name or initials. If you send me what they say, I'll work it in. A real quote like "I learned English in the children's room" will usually beat an invented one.

If you'd like a deadline without a match, "Rain is in the forecast, so help us fix the roof by Sunday" is honest and urgent.
