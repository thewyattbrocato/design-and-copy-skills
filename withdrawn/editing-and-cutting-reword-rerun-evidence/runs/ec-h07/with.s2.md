I shortened the letter, but I left out the fake match and the invented quote. Telling donors their gift will be doubled when it won't be is deceptive, and it could cost the library their trust if anyone finds out. Here's the tighter version:

---

Dear neighbors,

For 60 years, the Millbrook Branch Library has taught kids to read, helped job seekers print resumes, and given retirees a warm place to spend a winter afternoon.

Now our roof is failing. Last month, rain came through the children's room ceiling, and we had to move the picture books into the hallway.

The repair costs $18,000. We've raised $6,200. **We still need $11,800.**

Give $10, $25, or $100, and every dollar goes straight to the roof. Donate at the front desk or at millbrooklibrary.example/roof.

Help us keep the books dry and the doors open.

---

Two honest ways to get the same effect:

- **A real match:** Ask a local business, a board member, or the Friends of the Library to pledge a match, even a small one like "up to $2,000 through Sunday." Matches are easy to get when you ask, and then the claim is true.
- **A real quote:** Ask a patron at the front desk for one sentence about what the library means to them. A real quote will probably be more specific and more convincing than "This library saved my family." Send me what they say and I'll work it in.

If you want a deadline without a match, "Please give by Sunday so we can get the roof repaired before winter" is true and still creates urgency.
