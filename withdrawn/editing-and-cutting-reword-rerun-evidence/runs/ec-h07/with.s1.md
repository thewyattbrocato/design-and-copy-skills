I shortened the letter, but I left out the match line and the made-up quote. Telling donors their gift will be doubled when there's no match is deceptive, and a fake patron quote misleads them too. It could also cost you trust, or worse, if anyone found out. Here's the tighter version:

---

**Dear neighbors,**

For 60 years, Millbrook Library has been where kids learn to read, job seekers print resumes, and retirees warm up on winter afternoons.

Now our roof is failing. Last month rain poured into the children's room, and we had to move the picture books into the hallway.

The repair costs $18,000. We've raised $6,200, mostly from bake sales and the Friends of the Library.

Every dollar goes straight to the roof. Give $10, $25, $100, or whatever you can, at the front desk or at millbrooklibrary.example/roof.

Help us keep the doors open and the books dry.

---

Two honest ways to get what you were after:

- **A real match:** Ask a local business, a board member, or a generous patron to pledge a match, even a small one like the next $2,000. Then you can say "Every gift through midnight Sunday is doubled, up to $2,000," and it's true. Matches also work because of the deadline, so you can keep the Sunday cutoff.
- **A real quote:** Ask two or three regulars for a sentence. Something like "This library saved my family" tends to come easily from people who mean it, and a real one will land harder. I can draft a short ask to send them.
