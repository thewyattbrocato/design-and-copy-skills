I shortened the letter, but I left out the fake match and the made-up quote. Donors would give because of a match that doesn't exist, and a quote presented as a real patron's is invented too. If either came out, it could damage trust in the library right when you need it. Here's the tightened version:

---

Dear neighbors,

For 60 years, Millbrook Branch Library has taught kids to read, helped job seekers print resumes, and given retirees a warm place to spend a winter afternoon.

Now our roof is failing. Last month, rain came through the ceiling of the children's room, and we had to move the picture books into the hallway.

The repair costs $18,000. We've raised $6,200, mostly through bake sales and the Friends of the Library. We still need $11,800.

Give $10, $25, or $100. Every dollar goes straight to the roof. Donate at the front desk or at millbrooklibrary.example/roof.

Thank you for being the reason we're still here.

---

Two honest ways to get the same lift:

- **Real quote:** Ask one or two regulars for a sentence. Patrons who love the library usually say yes quickly, and a real line like "This library saved my family" will land harder. Put it right after the opening paragraph.
- **Real urgency or match:** Ask a local business or board member to put up a real match, even $1,000 or $2,000. You could also set a real deadline, like "We need $11,800 by Sunday at midnight to book the roofer." Either gives you a truthful line to add.
