# Marrow & Co voice and tone guide

**The idea:** we sound like a former restaurant manager explaining the numbers to an owner they respect. I inferred this from what you told me, so correct it if it's off.

## Four habits

**1. Use the words owners use.** We say food cost, labor, covers, slow Tuesday. We don't swap in accountant vocabulary, and we don't pile on slang to seem friendly.
- We say: "Food cost hit 34% in March because beef went up."
- We don't say: "COGS variance was driven by protein procurement."

**2. Number first, then what it means.** We give the figure, then one sentence on why and what to do about it. We stick to one number per point.
- We say: "Labor was 36% of sales, up from 31%. Two extra closers on slow nights explain most of it."
- We don't say: "Labor metrics trended unfavorably."

**3. Never make them feel dumb.** We explain a term the first time it shows up. We never write "simply" or "as you know". Owners are smart and busy, so we don't over-explain either.
- We say: "Prime cost is food plus labor, your two biggest bills."
- We don't say: "It's simple, just check your P&L."

**4. Show we've been there, don't announce it.** Our experience shows up as specifics. Any story gets one sentence at most.
- We say: "Nobody wants to chase receipts after a double."
- We don't say: "We understand the unique challenges of hospitality."

## How tone shifts

- **Website and intros:** warm, confident, short.
- **Monthly reports:** calm and specific. Good news and bad news get the same even voice.
- **Bad news** (cash shortfall, tax bill, missed payment): the plainest version. Lead with the fact, then the next step. No cheer, no cushioning.
- **Requests for missing documents:** friendly, with one clear ask and a date.

The more stressed the reader, the more the voice recedes.

## Mechanics

- Write "we" and "you", in short sentences.
- Say "restaurant" unless the client is a bar, café or hotel.
- Write numbers as digits, with % for percentages and dates like "March 3".
- Retire these: best-in-class, solutions, tailored, unique needs, dedicated team of experts, exceptional results, next level.

## The test

Before publishing, ask two questions:
1. Would one of us say this out loud to an owner at the end of a shift?
2. Could another bookkeeping firm put its name on it? If yes, add something only we could say.

## The two rewrites

**Before:** "At Marrow & Co, we provide best-in-class financial solutions tailored to the unique needs of your hospitality business. Our dedicated team of experts is committed to delivering exceptional results."

**After:** "Marrow & Co does the bookkeeping and monthly reporting for restaurants. All five of us used to manage restaurants, so we know what a slow Tuesday does to your labor number. We'll explain every figure in plain English."

**Before:** "Contact us today to learn how we can help take your restaurant to the next level!"

**After:** "Have a question about your numbers? Email us at [add: address or booking link]. Someone who's run a restaurant will answer."

The old copy could sit on any bookkeeper's site. The new copy rests on the two facts only you have: you're five ex-managers, and you explain numbers plainly.
