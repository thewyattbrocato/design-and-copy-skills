## Marrow & Co Voice and Tone Guide

**Who we are:** Bookkeeping from restaurant people. We're ex-managers who understand your cash flow, seasonal swings, and the specific stress of running a restaurant. We explain your numbers in plain language so you can actually use them.

**The four habits**

**1. Plain before impressive.** We name the number, say what it means, and skip the jargon. We never use accounting terminology as shorthand—it's not shorter for the person reading it.
- Say: "Your labor cost went from 28% to 31% last month. Here's what changed and three ways to bring it back down."
- Don't say: "Your compensation expense ratio has experienced an unfavorable variance requiring mitigation strategies."
- Why: You didn't hire us for vocabulary. You hired us to understand what the numbers mean.

**2. Peer-level, never superior.** We remember what it felt like to be drowning in spreadsheets. We're on your team, not evaluating it from above.
- Say: "I see this all the time. Let me walk you through it."
- Don't say: "As a seasoned professional, I would suggest..." or "You should have caught this sooner."
- Why: Most people avoid their numbers because accountants made them feel dumb. We won't.

**3. Restaurant-real.** We reference the business you actually run—cash float timing, January slowdowns, December gift card loops, vendor payment schedules—not theoretical scenarios.
- Say: "December looks great on paper, but most of it comes in January because of gift card redemptions."
- Don't say: "Fourth-quarter fiscal metrics indicate material seasonal fluctuations."
- Why: You need to make decisions about your restaurant next week, not understand accounting concepts.

**4. Actionable over complete.** We focus on the numbers that change how you run the place. We don't dump everything; we highlight what matters this month.
- Say: "Three things to watch: food waste jumped, your new vendor terms shift payables by two weeks, and payroll dipped because of turnover."
- Don't say: "Your financial statements reflect several material line-item movements."
- Why: Overload kills decisions.

**Tone shifts by moment.** In monthly reports: warm and direct. When you're stressed: plain and reassuring ("this is normal"). When explaining an error: quick and solution-focused. In a first call: curious and conversational—prove we're not like other accountants.

**What we never do:** Hide bad news. Assume you know jargon. Make you feel behind. Pretend your restaurant is generic. Apologize for your own data.

**Words we use:** "Let me show you..." (you're not alone). "This is normal for..." (context). "Here's what changed" (clarity). "You have options" (empowerment).

**Words we avoid:** "Variance," "optimization," "reconciliation," "GL," "cost of goods sold" (unless translated). "Best practice" (yours beats generic rules). "Unfortunately" (it's data, not misfortune). "As suspected" (peer, not teacher).
