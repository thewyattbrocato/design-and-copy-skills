# Marrow & Co voice and tone guide

## Who we sound like
A restaurant manager explaining the numbers to an owner after close. We've run the floor, so we know the owner is tired, busy, and smart about everything except accounting. We respect all three. Our clients like that we explain numbers in plain language and never make them feel dumb. Every habit below protects that.

## Four habits

**1. Use the owner's words.** We say food cost, labor, covers and prime cost, not "performance metrics." When we need an accounting term, we use it once and explain it in the same sentence. Plain doesn't mean cute: no kitchen puns.
- We say: "Prime cost, your food and labor combined, went up this month. Here's why."
- We don't say: "Prime cost variance exceeded the target threshold."

**2. Number first, then meaning, then next step.** Lead with the figure, say what caused it, say what we'd do. Don't bury it in caveats.
- We say: "Labor ran three points higher than February. Two private events explain most of it. If that's one-off, no action needed."
- We don't say: "Labor costs experienced upward pressure during the period."

**3. Never make them feel dumb.** Assume they're smart and short on time. Accounting names things badly, and that's not their fault. Skip "simply," "obviously" and "as you know." Don't over-explain either; a clear answer is respectful enough.
- We say: "That line is confusing. It means money you've earned but haven't been paid yet."
- We don't say: "Simply reconcile your accruals."

**4. Say what we'd do.** We've sat in their chair, so we give an opinion and leave the decision to them. Advice, not orders.
- We say: "I'd fix the pricing on the two dishes before touching portions."
- We don't say: "We recommend evaluating your pricing strategy."

## Tone changes, voice doesn't
- **Bad news** (a rough month, a tax bill, our own mistake): the plainest version. Figure, cause, next step. No jokes, no cheerfulness. If the mistake is ours, the first sentence says so and the second says what we've fixed.
- **Monthly reports:** calm and short. They'll read them between tasks.
- **Invoices and deadlines:** exact. Amount, date, what happens next.
- **Client questions:** answer first, then context. Never make anyone justify asking.
- **Website and first hellos:** warmer, with a little dry humor allowed.

## Word choices
- Use: "we," "you," "your restaurant," real names.
- Ban: solutions, best-in-class, tailored, leverage, next level, "reach out," exclamation marks.
- Short sentences, one idea each. Contractions are good.
- Write numbers the way owners read them: $4,200, 31%, "by the 10th."
- Never claim what we can't show. "Exceptional results" is a claim; a specific fact a client can check is better.
