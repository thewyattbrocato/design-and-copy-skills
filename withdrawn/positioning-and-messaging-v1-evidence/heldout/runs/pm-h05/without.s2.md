# Marrow & Co Voice and Tone Guide

## Who we are
We're five ex-restaurant managers who do the books. We've closed out a night, counted the drawer, and stared at a P&L at 11pm. We know what it's like because we've done it. That's the voice: a colleague who's been in your shoes and happens to be good with numbers.

## Our voice in three traits

**1. Plain.** We say the thing the way we'd say it across the bar. If a word wouldn't come up in a staff meeting, we find a different one. When a term is unavoidable (COGS, prime cost), we explain it once, in a sentence, and move on.

**2. Respectful.** No one has to apologize for not being an accountant. We never imply a question is basic, and we never use jargon to look smart. Assume the reader is sharp and busy, because they run a restaurant.

**3. Specific.** We name the real thing: food cost, labor, the walk-in, a slow Tuesday. Specifics prove we've worked the floor. Vague promises prove nothing.

## How we sound, and how we don't

| We say | We don't say |
|---|---|
| "Here's what your food cost did last month." | "Leverage actionable insights." |
| "You spent more on labor than you planned. Here's why." | "Labor variance was unfavorable." |
| "Good question." | "As previously stated..." |
| "We'll send your report by the 10th." | "Timely deliverables." |

## Words to retire
best-in-class, solutions, tailored, unique needs, dedicated team, experts, committed to, exceptional results, next level, synergy, leverage. If a phrase could appear on any company's website, cut it.

## Tone by situation

**Marketing and website:** Warm, direct, a little dry. Short sentences. Lead with what the client gets.

**Monthly reports:** Calm and clear. Start with the headline ("You made money in March, but less than February"), then the reason, then what to consider doing.

**Bad news:** Straight and kind. Say it early, don't pad it, and pair it with a next step. Never alarmist, never breezy.

**Quick emails and texts:** Casual. Contractions are fine. Answer first, context after.

## A quick test
Before you send or publish anything, ask:
1. Could a competitor say this word for word? If yes, rewrite it.
2. Would a first-time owner understand it without Googling?
3. Does it say what we actually do?
4. Would we say it out loud to a client at the bar? If not, change it.

## Rewrites

**Original:**
"At Marrow & Co, we provide best-in-class financial solutions tailored to the unique needs of your hospitality business. Our dedicated team of experts is committed to delivering exceptional results."

**Rewrite:**
"We do the bookkeeping and monthly reporting for restaurants. All five of us used to manage them, so we know a bad week when we see one. Every month you get your numbers in plain language, with no jargon and no feeling like you should've already known."

**Original:**
"Contact us today to learn how we can help take your restaurant to the next level!"

**Rewrite:**
"Want to know where your money's going? Send us a note and we'll talk through your numbers. No pressure, no jargon."

**Why these work:** They say what we do (bookkeeping and monthly reporting), who we do it for (restaurants), and why we're credible (we've managed them). They also drop the hype and speak the way our clients like us to speak.
