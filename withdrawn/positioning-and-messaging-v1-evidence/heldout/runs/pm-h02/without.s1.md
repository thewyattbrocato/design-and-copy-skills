People nod and then ask what it is because "a thing that helps" doesn't say what the thing does. Listeners need a concrete picture: who uses it, what they do with it, and what gets better.

**Say-out-loud options**

- "We help independent bookstores run their author events, so a reading or a book club takes an hour to set up instead of a whole weekend."
- "If you've ever been to a signing at an indie bookstore, we make the behind-the-scenes part easier: the sign-ups, the reminders, the author logistics."
- "Indie bookstores run events to bring people in, but they're doing it with spreadsheets and email threads. We replace that."

The third works well in conversation because it names a pain people can picture and invites the question "oh, like what?"

**Site copy (short)**

> **Events for independent bookstores, without the busywork.**
> Readings, book clubs, and signings, from the first invite to the last thank-you. [Product name] handles [sign-ups / reminders / author coordination] so your team can focus on the books and the people.

**Caveat:** I filled in the specifics (sign-ups, reminders, author coordination, the "hour instead of a weekend" claim) as guesses. They'll only land if they're true. Two questions would let me tighten this:

1. What does a bookstore actually *do* in your product? For example, sell tickets, send invites, manage RSVPs, coordinate with publishers, or promote events.
2. What's the before and after? What does a store do today that your thing replaces, and what's the result?

Tell me those and I'll cut it down to one sentence that's specific enough that nobody has to ask what it is.
