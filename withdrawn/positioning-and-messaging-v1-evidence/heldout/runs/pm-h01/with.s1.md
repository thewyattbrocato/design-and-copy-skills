# Tidewell message brief

**One-liner**
Tidewell is laundry management software for small commercial laundries. It tracks every bag and linen load from drop-off to delivery and prices each job the way you charge for it.

**Controlling idea**
Every bag accounted for, every job priced right.

Use it as a test. If a headline or feature request doesn't help a shop know where a load is or charge correctly for it, it isn't a launch message.

**Who it's for**
Owners and managers of wash-and-fold shops and hotel and spa linen contractors with 3 to 25 employees. They run today on paper tickets, a spreadsheet, or a point-of-sale system built for retail. [assumed: the best fit is a shop that charges different customers different rates and answers "is it ready?" calls all day. Confirm with the pilot shops.]

**Not for**
- Shops that want payroll in the same tool.
- Shops that need live accounting-software sync today.
- [assumed] Operations well above 25 employees.

**What we're up against**
- **Paper tickets:** free, familiar, and they work anywhere.
- **A spreadsheet:** flexible, and the owner already knows it.
- **A retail point-of-sale system:** handles the counter sale well, but it was built for retail, not for loads of laundry.

We name these plainly and don't run them down. The people we're talking to chose them for reasons.

**What's different**
1. **Built by people who ran a laundry.** The two founders ran one for six years. *Proof:* a fact anyone can check. *It lets the owner* talk to people who know the work.
2. **Barcode tracking with a pickup-ready text.** Each customer's bags and linen loads carry a tag from drop-off to delivery, and the customer gets a text when the order is ready. *Proof:* demoable in minutes. *It lets staff* answer "where is my order?" by scanning a tag. [unproven: that it cuts calls or lost loads. Measure it in the pilot.]
3. **Pricing by weight or by piece, with a rate card per customer.** *Proof:* demoable. *It lets one shop* charge a hotel and a walk-in differently without recalculating.
4. **Offline-capable.** It runs on a tablet at the counter and a phone for drivers, and syncs later. *Proof:* demoable by turning off the wifi. *It lets* the counter and the van keep working when the connection drops.

**Objections, answered straight**
- **"Does it do payroll?"** No. Keep the payroll tool you have.
- **"Does it connect to our accounting software?"** Not yet. Today it exports to CSV. We don't give a date until we have one.
- **"Is anyone using it?"** Nine shops are in our pilot. We don't have results to publish yet.

**Proof on hand**
- Nine shops in pilot, as of October 2026.
- Founders ran a laundry for six years.
- Placeholders, to fill only with real material: [add: a pilot customer's own sentence], [add: time saved, measured in the pilot], [add: first case study].

Until those exist, don't write "saves hours", "trusted by", or "shops like yours report". We have no basis for any of them.

**Words**
- **Use:** bags, loads, drop-off, pickup-ready, delivery, rate card, tag. Use "tag" every time for the barcode label.
- **Avoid:** solution, platform, streamline, end-to-end, seamless, "all-in-one". Those words describe nothing a shop owner does.

**Variants**
The claim stays the same. The emphasis changes.
- **Owner:** rate cards, CSV export, what it doesn't do.
- **Counter staff:** the tablet, the tag, the pickup-ready text.
- **Driver:** the phone and offline use.
- **Hotel and spa linen contractors:** per-customer rate cards and load tracking by account.

**Voice** *(inferred from how you described the product)*
- **Plain about limits.** We say what it doesn't do before anyone asks. Not self-deprecating.
  - We say: "No payroll. Accounting export is CSV."
  - We don't say: "Integrations coming soon!"
- **Shop-floor specific.** We name the bag, the tag, the van.
  - We say: "Text your customer when the bags are ready."
  - We don't say: "Delight customers with real-time notifications."
- **Founder-honest.** We say "we ran a laundry" and let that carry the credibility. Don't oversell it as expertise in everything.
- **Tone:** Plain in pricing, errors, and anything about a customer's money. Lighter only in low-stakes moments.

**Note:** The brief has no numbers because none exist yet. The most useful thing to do before launch is to ask the nine pilot shops for one measured result and one quote. Those would fill the three biggest gaps.
