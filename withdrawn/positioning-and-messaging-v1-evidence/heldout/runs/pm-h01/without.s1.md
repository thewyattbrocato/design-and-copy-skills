# Tidewell message brief

## What we are
Tidewell is software for small commercial laundries. It follows every customer's bags and linen loads from drop-off to delivery with a barcode tag. It prices each job the way that customer is billed, and it texts the customer when their order is ready for pickup. It runs on a tablet at the counter and a phone for drivers, and it keeps working offline and syncs later.

## Who it's for
Owners and managers of commercial laundries with 3 to 25 employees, in two main groups:

- **Wash-and-fold shops** that take in customer bags at the counter and need to know where each one is.
- **Hotel and spa linen contractors** that run recurring loads and bill by weight or by piece.

These are hands-on operators who run the business from the floor. They don't want an enterprise system. They want fewer lost bags, fewer "is my order ready?" calls, and invoices that match the work.

## What we're up against
Our real competitor is the way shops already work. Most use one of three things:

- **Paper tickets.** They're cheap and familiar, but a ticket can get lost or separated from the bag, and nothing tells the customer their order is ready.
- **A spreadsheet.** It's flexible, but someone has to keep it current, and it can't follow a bag from the counter to the van.
- **A retail point-of-sale system.** It rings up sales, but it was built for items on a shelf, not for a load that moves through stages.

None of these is a bad choice on its face. They work until the shop gets busy. Our job is to show the owner what the current method costs them in lost, late, or mispriced orders. We shouldn't run down the tools they already use.

## Why we're different
1. **We've stood at this counter.** Tidewell was founded by two people who ran a laundry for six years. They built it around problems they had themselves.
2. **It's built around the job, not the sale.** A barcode tag follows each order from drop-off to delivery, so the shop and the customer always know where it stands.
3. **Pricing matches how laundries bill.** Jobs are priced by weight or by piece, with a rate card for each customer.
4. **It works where laundries work.** It runs on a counter tablet and a driver's phone, and offline use is built in, so a bad connection doesn't stop the day.

## What we say about ourselves
Use these four lines consistently in the website, sales calls, and outreach:

1. **"Built by people who ran a laundry for six years."**
2. **"Every bag, tracked from drop-off to delivery."** Add "and your customer gets a text when it's ready" where there's room.
3. **"Price by the pound or by the piece, with a rate card for every customer."**
4. **"Keeps working when the Wi-Fi doesn't."**

**Tone:** plain, practical, and specific. Write the way an owner would talk to another owner. Avoid "revolutionize," "next-generation," and "all-in-one."

## What we don't say
We're early, and our credibility depends on staying inside what's true today.

- **No results claims.** We have no time-saved or revenue numbers, so we don't say "save hours a week" or anything like it.
- **No social proof we don't have.** We have no case studies or customer quotes. The accurate statement is "9 shops are using Tidewell in a pilot." We don't say "trusted by" or "loved by."
- **Payroll.** Tidewell doesn't do payroll. If asked, say so directly.
- **Accounting.** There's no accounting integration yet. Say "exports to CSV." Never say "integrates with" or "syncs with" accounting software.
- **No "all-in-one" or "complete solution."** We cover tracking, pricing, and customer notification, and nothing beyond that.

When a prospect asks about something on this list, an honest "not yet" works better than a stretch. The pilot shops are our best source of proof, so collecting quotes and before-and-after numbers from them is the highest-value next step.
