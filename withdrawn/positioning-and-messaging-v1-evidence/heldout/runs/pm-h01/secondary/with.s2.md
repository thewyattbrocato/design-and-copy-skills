**Tidewell message brief**

**What we are and who it's for**

Tidewell is job-tracking software for small commercial laundries—shops with three to twenty-five employees that wash, fold, and deliver linens for hotels, spas, restaurants, and direct customers. It replaces paper tickets and spreadsheets by tracking each bag and load from drop-off to delivery with barcode tags, sending automatic text updates to customers when their order is ready for pickup, and pricing each job by weight or individual pieces with per-customer rate cards. The software runs on a tablet at the counter and a phone for delivery drivers, works when the network goes down, and syncs when connectivity returns. Tidewell was built by two people who ran a laundry for six years.

**What they use today**

Right now, small commercial laundries manage jobs the way they did twenty years ago. Most use paper tickets—handwritten notes that track an order through the shop, stuck to a bag or a board, easy to lose and impossible to search. Some have moved to spreadsheets: a Google Sheet or Excel file that staff update throughout the day, prone to typos and merge conflicts, with no way to tell a customer their order is ready without calling them. A few have bought a point-of-sale system—software built for retail shops that rang up sales, managed inventory, and tracked employees. None of these were designed for a laundry's actual workflow: loose bags arriving at random times, loads that live in washers and dryers for hours, deliveries that need to happen at specific windows, and customers who need to know when to pick up.

**Why that doesn't work**

Paper tickets break down as a laundry grows. A five-person shop can keep everything in someone's head. At fifteen employees, losing a ticket means losing a job and a customer's trust. Spreadsheets are better but still require a person to manually update every status change, type in the same pricing information for the same customer over and over, and remember to call or text when something is ready. Retail POS systems charge by the seat, include point-of-sale features a laundry does not need, and assume work happens in one location—not between a counter, a washing floor, and a delivery van. None of these options were built by someone who had to live with them at three in the morning when a hotel called asking where their sheets were.

**Why Tidewell is different**

Tidewell was built by people who actually ran a commercial laundry. That means every feature solves a real problem, not an imagined one. The barcode system is fast enough for a busy counter and reliable enough for drivers in a van with spotty service. The text notifications mean customers stop calling to ask if their order is ready, and they get reminded automatically when they need to pick up. The per-customer rate cards mean a hotel customer with different pricing than a walk-in pays the right amount every time, with no manual calculation. The tablet and phone apps split the workflow between front-of-house and field, so a counter person and a driver never have to use the same interface. The offline capability is not a nice-to-have—it is essential, because laundries move fast and do not wait for wifi.

Tidewell does one thing: track jobs and customers through a laundry's operation. It does not do payroll, it does not connect to accounting software yet, and it does not pretend to be a general business tool. That focus means the feature set stays lean and the software stays fast.

**How we talk about ourselves**

We are a tool for people who run laundries, not software generalists selling to everyone. We speak in the language of the work: bags, loads, pickup-ready, per-piece pricing, driver routes. We do not call anything "innovative" or "revolutionary" because we are solving a problem people have been managing with paper for decades. We show the work, not the platform. We say what Tidewell does, not what it could do if you added a module. We stay specific: it is for commercial laundries with three to twenty-five employees, not laundromats or industrial operations, and not retail businesses pretending laundry is the same as merchandise. When we talk about ourselves, we mention that our founders ran a laundry, because that is why we built something that works for laundries instead of forcing laundries into retail software.

We launch next month. The message is: Tidewell is built by laundry operators for laundry operators.
