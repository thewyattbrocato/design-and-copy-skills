No, a full rewrite isn't necessary. The statement does the main jobs of a positioning statement:

- It names a specific audience.
- It names a real problem.
- It defines the product and says what's in it.
- It gives a differentiator you can defend: one kit built around a night-shift schedule, with a checklist, versus pieces or apps.
- It's honest about what you can and can't claim.

It also tested well with the nurses you interviewed and matches what you sell. A rewrite from scratch would throw away that validation, so I'd make targeted edits only.

**What I'd fix**

1. **"turn any bedroom into a place you can sleep at noon" overclaims.** You can't promise that for any bedroom, such as one with a large uncovered window or a noisy street. The checklist implies you adapt to the room, and that is the more credible story. I changed it to "dark and quiet enough to sleep in."
2. **"kits" becomes "kit."** The opening says "kits" and the differentiator says "one kit." Pick one.
3. **The disclaimer is in the wrong place.** "We have not run clinical studies" is honest, but it's a claims guardrail, not positioning. If the statement is internal, it weakens the pitch. If it goes into public copy, it belongs as a footnote or FAQ line. I've kept it, but separated it.

**Revised**

> For night-shift nurses who struggle to sleep during the day, Dayrest is a blackout-and-white-noise kit (curtain panels, a door seal, and a small sound machine) that makes your bedroom dark and quiet enough to sleep in at noon. Unlike generic blackout curtains or sleep apps, Dayrest is one kit designed around a night-shift schedule, with a setup checklist for your own room.

**Claims guardrail (keep alongside, not inside):** We have not run clinical studies. Our claim is practical, not medical.

If your marketing lead has specific objections, such as tone, the audience being too narrow, or the competitive frame, I'm happy to look at those. Those would be reasons to change the statement. "Start from scratch" isn't one.
