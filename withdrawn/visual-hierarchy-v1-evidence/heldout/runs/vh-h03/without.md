**Ship B.**

**Why**

- **It answers the visitor's one question immediately.** Someone landing on a same-day courier site wants to know "can you get this there today, and for how much?" B lets them start a quote right there, with no click-through.
- **The headline is your differentiator.** "Delivered by 6pm or free" is a concrete promise that competitors with vague "fast delivery" copy can't match. It builds trust and gives people a reason to choose you.
- **One dominant action.** A form with a single button gives a clear path, and it's easy to measure (quote starts and completions).
- **A and C each fail on something important:**
  - **A** is mostly brand mood. The one conversion action is a tiny text link, and a big rider photo says nothing about speed, price, or reliability.
  - **C** has no headline, so a new visitor never learns why Parcelo is different. Equal tiles also mean no priority. Track and Careers are for existing customers and job seekers, and they compete with the quote for attention. These belong in the nav.

**What I'd tweak**

1. **Make the guarantee credible.** "Or free" invites the question "what's the catch?" Add a short line under the headline or button, like "Order by 2pm, within [zone]. Terms apply," linking to the conditions. Check the offer is operationally and financially sustainable before you promote it this hard.
2. **Cut the form to the minimum.** Pickup and drop-off postcodes, then a button like "Get my price." Ask for package size, time, and contact details on the next step. Fewer fields at the start means more people begin.
3. **Show a price or a delivery window early.** If you can, show an instant estimate after the two postcodes. People abandon quote forms that end in "we'll email you."
4. **Make the photo work harder.** Muted is fine for contrast, but use a real rider and parcel, not stock. Keep it from competing with the form, and check that it doesn't push the form below the fold on mobile.
5. **Move the other paths to the nav and a thin strip.** Put Track in the header (existing customers use it a lot), and add a small "For business" link near the form. That keeps what's good about C without the clutter.
6. **Add one line of proof under the form.** Something like an on-time rate, a delivery count, or a review score. Use real numbers only.
7. **Test it.** Run B against a variant with a different guarantee wording, or with the proof line moved. Track quote-start rate and quote completion rate.

One caveat: this recommendation rests on general conversion principles and what you've described, not your data. If you have traffic mix numbers (for example, mostly returning customers tracking parcels), C's logic gets stronger, but I'd still solve that with a Track link in the header.
