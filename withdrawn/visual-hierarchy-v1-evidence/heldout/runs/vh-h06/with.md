**What's going on:** the page has no hierarchy. Font and palette are surface choices. What makes a page feel designed is visible steps between levels, and this page has almost none.

- **The hero is one level.** Name, job title and bio are all 18px regular, so nothing is the entry point. A visitor has to read to learn who this is.
- **The cards are one level.** Six identical cards with same-size gray thumbnails and same-size text give the eye no reason to go to one first. Gray placeholders are the biggest area on the page, and they carry no information. This is the classic template look.
- **The page has no focal point and no next step.** Squint at it and you see a uniform grid. There's also no clear primary action, such as "See my work" or "Contact".
- **Pastels lower the contrast further.** A soft palette with uniform type leaves no strong value difference to anchor the eye.

**What to change first: the hero.** It's the entry point and the cheapest fix.

1. Make the name the clear top level, with a big jump in size and a heavier weight. Aim for roughly 3 times the body size or more, not 20px against 18px.
2. Make the job title the second level. Use a visibly smaller size than the name, or a medium weight, so it reads as a separate step.
3. Keep the bio at body size and regular weight, as the quiet third level. Limit its line length and leave generous space around the hero.
4. Add one primary button, and make it the only loud element in that area. Anything else gets a quieter text or outline style.

Change one variable per step: position and space first, then weight, then size, then color. Make each step big enough to look chosen. A size ratio of at least 1.25 to 1.5 between neighboring levels is a good floor, as is a regular-to-semibold weight jump.

**Second: the project grid.**
- Replace the gray thumbnails with real screenshots or covers. That's the biggest single upgrade.
- Make the project title semibold and the description smaller and regular, so each card has two levels.
- Consider making one or two projects larger, such as a featured project spanning two columns, so the grid has a ranking. Then use six equal cards only if you truly can't rank the work.
- Skip shadows, borders and icons unless they do a job. Space between cards usually works better than boxes.

**Check afterward:** blur the page or convert it to grayscale. The name should be the first thing you see, then the button, then the featured work. If you have to look twice to tell two levels apart, widen the gap.
