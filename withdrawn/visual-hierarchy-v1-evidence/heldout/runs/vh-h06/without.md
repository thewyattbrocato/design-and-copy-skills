**What's going on:** the page has no visual hierarchy. Font and color are surface styling, but "template-like and flat" comes from structure and contrast, and everything here has the same weight.

- **The hero is one level.** Name, title and bio are all 18px regular, so nothing tells the eye where to start. Visitors can't tell who this is, what they do, and the supporting detail apart.
- **The cards are identical.** Six same-size cards with gray thumbnails and the same text sizes read as a placeholder grid. Gray thumbnails say "content goes here," and with nothing distinctive in them, the work itself isn't visible.
- **A pastel palette has low contrast by nature.** If sizes and weights are also uniform, there's no tonal range, so the page looks flat.
- **Sameness is what reads as "template."** Distinctive sites vary scale, weight, space and emphasis. Default sites give every element equal treatment.

**What to change first: the hero typography.** It's the cheapest fix with the biggest effect.

1. Make the name much larger, around 48-72px on desktop, with a heavier weight and tight line-height (about 1.1).
2. Make the job title a clear second level, around 20-24px, in a medium weight or a muted/accent color.
3. Keep the bio at 16-18px regular, but limit it to about 60 characters per line and put more space above it.
4. Add generous whitespace around the hero. Give it more vertical padding than feels natural.
5. Use at least one color with real contrast, such as a deep tone for the name or a saturated accent for a link or button. Pastels can stay as backgrounds, but text needs strong contrast.

**Second: the project cards.**

- Replace the gray thumbnails with real screenshots or imagery, even if rough. This matters most.
- Make the title bold and about 20px, and the description smaller and muted.
- Break the grid. Make one or two featured projects larger (span two columns), or alternate layouts, so there's a lead project.
- Add a hover state, such as a lift, a color shift or an arrow.

If they do only the hero and the real thumbnails, the "flat template" feel will mostly go away. Then they can refine spacing and the nav.
