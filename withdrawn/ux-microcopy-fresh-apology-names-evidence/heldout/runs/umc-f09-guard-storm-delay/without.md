Both pieces are below. They say plainly what happened and what it means for the customer, and they don't apologize for something that wasn't your fault.

## Homepage banner

**Short version (for the banner itself):**
> **Weather delay:** A storm has closed our regional courier hub, so some orders will arrive 2-4 days after their estimated date. [See details]

**Longer version (for the page the link goes to, or a wider banner):**
> **Delivery update:** A storm has temporarily closed our regional courier hub, and some orders will arrive 2-4 days later than the date shown at checkout. Your plants are safe with us and the courier, and we've asked for our orders to be moved first when the hub reopens. We'll email anyone affected. [Track your order]

## Notes

- **The plant-care line:** Keep it only if it's true for your products. If some items, like delicate or cold-sensitive ones, are riskier with a delay, say so or tailor the line. A customer who finds a damaged plant after you told them it would be fine will be more upset than one who got no reassurance.
- **"Packed and with the courier":** Change this if some orders haven't shipped yet.
- **Compensation:** I didn't offer a discount or refund because I don't know your policy. If you'd like to add a goodwill gesture, one line after the care tips would work, such as "As a thank-you for your patience, here's [X] off your next order."
- **Tone:** I left out "we apologize," which can read as taking the blame. "We know you were looking forward to your plants" shows you care without admitting fault. If you want a light apology, "Sorry for the wait" is enough.
