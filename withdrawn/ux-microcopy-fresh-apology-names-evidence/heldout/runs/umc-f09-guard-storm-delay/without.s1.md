## Customer email

**Subject:** A weather delay on your Fernhill Plants order

Hi [First name],

A storm has closed the regional courier hub that handles your area, so your order [#12345] will arrive 2–4 days later than the date we showed at checkout. The new estimate is [date range].

We've asked the courier to move our packages first when the hub reopens. We'll email you tracking updates as soon as they're moving again.

Your plants are packed to handle extra time in transit. When your box arrives, please open it right away, give the plants a little water if the soil is dry, and set them somewhere bright but out of direct sun.

If you have questions, or the delay is a problem for you, reply to this email or write to [support address]. We'll help however we can.

Thank you for your patience,
The Fernhill Plants team

## Notes
- **The plant-care paragraph:** only keep "packed to handle extra time" if it's true for your packaging. If you're not sure, cut that sentence and keep the unboxing tips.
- **Compensation:** I didn't offer any, since you didn't mention it. If you want to (a discount code, or free cancellation for anyone who no longer wants the order), a line like that usually goes over well with delays of this size. I can add one.
- **Placeholders:** fill in the order number, the new date range and the support address. If you can merge the date range in per customer, do. It's more useful than a generic "2–4 days."
- **Tone:** neither message apologizes at length or blames anyone. The cause is stated once and plainly, followed by what you're doing about it. That keeps it from sounding defensive or like you're hiding something.
