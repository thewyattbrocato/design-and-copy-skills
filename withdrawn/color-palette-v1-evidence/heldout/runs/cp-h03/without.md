**Pick the teal, #2a9d8f.**

**Why teal over the others**
- **Orange-red (#ff5a36):** It's energetic and kid-friendly, but it's the most common "kids' program" color. It also reads as warning or urgency, and it's the hardest to match across screen, flyer and thread. White text on it is only about 3:1 contrast, which is too low for body text.
- **Indigo (#3a2e8c):** It has great contrast and looks strong in embroidery. But it's the most serious and "corporate/tech" of the three, and it goes muddy on cheap flyer printing. That's a poor fit for 8–12 year olds.
- **Teal:** It's friendly without being babyish, and it's gender-neutral and distinctive. It prints reliably in CMYK and stitches well in a standard thread. A bird silhouette looks good in it. The one caveat is contrast: white text on #2a9d8f is about 3.3:1. Use it for large headings, buttons with bold large text, and the logo, not for small text.

**What goes alongside it**

| Role | Color | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Teal `#2a9d8f` | Logo, headings, buttons, patch |
| Dark neutral | Deep navy/ink `#1f2a44` | Body text, small text, flyer text (high contrast) |
| Accent | Orange `#ff5a36` | Sparingly: calls to action, highlights, stickers. Your runner-up becomes the pop color. |
| Light neutral | Warm off-white `#fdf8f0` | Backgrounds. It's softer than pure white and saves ink. |

**Practical rules**
- **Text:** Use the dark navy for anything small. Don't put small white text on teal. If you need text on teal, make it large and bold, or use navy text on a lighter teal tint.
- **Accent orange:** Keep it to about 10% of any layout so it stays a highlight and doesn't compete with the teal. Don't put orange text on teal, because the two clash.
- **Patches:** Embroidery handles 2–3 colors best. Do a teal bird on a navy or off-white twill, and ask the embroiderer for the nearest thread match to teal. Don't try to embroider tints or gradients.
- **Flyers:** Specify the teal as a Pantone or CMYK value with your printer, and ask for a proof. Teal shifts the most between screens and paper. School photocopies will turn it into mid-gray, so make sure the logo also works in one-color black.
- **Logo variants:** Make four versions: full-color, all-navy, all-white (for use on teal), and black.
- **Website:** Check button and link colors for contrast. Use navy for links on light backgrounds, or darken the teal to about `#1f7a70` for text-sized uses.

If you want to test it before committing, print one flyer and order one sample patch in teal. Those two will show any problems quickly.
