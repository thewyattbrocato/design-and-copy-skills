The layout is mostly sound. It has one comparison per number, one accent, one amber, and direct labels instead of a legend. These are the changes I'd make, most important first.

1. **Check the sales comparison before sending.** The data runs to Friday 5pm. If last week's $16,280 is a full week and this week is missing Friday evening and the weekend, the +13% understates the real gain. If it's the other way around, it overstates it. Compare the same cutoff on both weeks, or say "through Fri 5pm" on the card. Mark Friday's bar as partial, for example with a lighter fill and a note.

2. **The headline contradicts the page.** It says the repair queue is the only thing needing attention, but two items are out of stock. Pick one:
   - If stock-outs matter, say so: "Sales are $2,140 above last week; 9 bikes in repair (oldest 1 day from our promise) and 2 stock-outs."
   - If they don't, keep the headline and make the stock-out card quiet.

   I'd lean toward the first.

3. **Make the amber say what it means.** Amber with no words relies on color alone. Use an icon and wording like "Oldest 6 days, promise 7: 1 day left". Nothing has breached yet, so "watch" is more honest than "problem". Keep it the only amber.

4. **Connect the stock-outs to the reorder table.** If 700c tubes and chain lube are among the 5 reorder items, mark them in the table with a text tag like "Out now", so the owner sees what's already empty. If they aren't in the table, that's a gap to fix. Sorting by lead time is fine. Put the unit in the header: "Lead time (days)".

5. **Move the online-orders caveat into the label.** Change "Sales this week" to "In-store sales this week". A footnote is easy to miss, and the owner could read $18,420 as total revenue. Keep the footer for "Data as of Friday 5pm".

6. **Title the chart with its answer.** For example, "Sales ahead of last week on 4 of 5 days". Use whatever is actually true. Keep this week in blue and last week in gray, with the same colors in the end-of-pair label. Start the bars at zero. If 10 or more value labels feel crowded, label only this week's bars and let last week's go unlabeled.

7. **Show the change in words on the sales card.** Use "+$2,140 (+13%) vs last week". The headline repeats the dollar figure, so the card can lead with the percentage. Round the main figure to $18.4k if the owner doesn't need exact dollars.

I'd leave the rest as is: the three-number row, the footer's small size, and the restrained palette.
