The layout mostly works already: a clear headline, one comparison per number, one accent, one amber, direct labels, and a sorted table. I checked the arithmetic and it's right ($2,140 and +13%). I'd change these things, in order of importance.

1. **Check whether the sales comparison is like for like.** The footer says data is as of Friday 5pm, so Friday is partial and Saturday and Sunday are missing. If last week's $16,280 is a full week, the +13% compares unequal periods.
   - Either compare to last week through the same Friday 5pm, or label it "this week so far vs. full last week".
   - Mark Friday's bar as partial, for example hatched, and show no bars for days that haven't happened.

2. **Fix the headline.** It says the repair queue is the only thing needing attention, but you also list two stock-outs. 700c tubes are a repair consumable, so that may be tied to the backlog.
   - If the stock-outs matter, change the sentence to something like "Repairs are 1 day from missing our 7-day promise; 2 items are out of stock."
   - If they don't, say why they're only informational.
   - Check that both stock-outs appear in the reorder table, and mark them there.

3. **Make the amber say what it means.** Right now it signals "attention" while the oldest bike (6 days) is still inside the 7-day promise.
   - Use an icon and a word: "⚠ Oldest 6 days, promise 7 (1 day left)".
   - Amber then means "about to breach" rather than "breached". Keep it as the only amber, and don't add a second alert level.

4. **Put "in-store" in the labels, not only the footer.** The owner will read "Sales this week $18,420" as total sales. Label the card and chart "In-store sales" and keep the footer for the date. Online is the part that's missing, so it shouldn't be the smallest text on the page.

5. **Retitle the chart with its answer.** For example, "Sales ahead of last week every day except Wed", or whatever the data shows. That's better than "Daily sales".
   - Start the bars at zero and keep this week in blue and last week in gray.
   - Labels on all 14 bars can get crowded, so use short values ($2.6k) and keep the series label on the first pair.

6. **Tidy the table.**
   - Give it a title such as "Reorder, longest lead time first".
   - Right-align quantity and lead time, with units in the headers (Qty, Lead time (days)).
   - Add a supplier column if it isn't already there.

I'd leave the three-number row, the colors, and the footer wording as they are.
