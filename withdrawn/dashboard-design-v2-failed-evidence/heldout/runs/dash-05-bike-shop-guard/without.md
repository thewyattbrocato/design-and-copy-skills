I'd change four things, most important first.

1. **The headline contradicts the stock-outs.** It says the repair queue is the only thing needing attention, but two stock-outs sit right beside it. Tubes and chain lube are likely fast sellers, and the owner may read this as you dismissing them. Pick one fix:
   - Reword to "Sales are $2,140 above last week; the repair queue is close to our 7-day promise, and 2 items are out of stock."
   - Or, if the stock-outs really are fine, say why, for example "reorders are already placed."

   Also check whether the stock-outs appear in the reorder table. If they do, mark them there, so the owner sees the link without hunting for it.

2. **The week-over-week comparison may not be like for like.** The data is as of Friday 5pm. If last week's $16,280 includes Saturday and Sunday and this week's $18,420 doesn't, the +13% is misleading. The same problem affects the chart's weekend bars. Either compare the same cutoff for both weeks or label the partial days. State the cutoff in the KPI sub-label, not only in the footer.

3. **"Sales" should say "in-store sales".** Online orders are excluded, so the headline and the KPI make a claim bigger than the data supports. Put "in-store" in the label. Keep the footer note as well, because it's good.

4. **The chart is a bit heavy.**
   - Fourteen value labels is the upper limit. They work if they're small and gray, with this week's bars in blue and last week's in gray.
   - Drop the y-axis and gridlines, since the bars are labeled, and keep a zero baseline.
   - Put the "This week" and "Last week" labels at the first pair, as you planned. Make them the same colors as the bars so no legend is needed.

Smaller polish:
- **Amber:** keep it as the only amber. Don't use amber for text on white, because the contrast is poor. Use it as a fill or marker, with dark gray text.
- **Backlog tile:** add how many bikes are at risk of missing the promise, such as "3 will hit 7 days by Monday". That gives the owner a decision to make, not only a number.
- **Reorder table:** put the unit in the header ("Lead time (days)") and right-align the numeric columns. Use tabular numerals so the digits line up.
- **Visual hierarchy:** make the three KPI numbers the biggest thing on the page after the headline. Keep the "last week $16,280, +13%" line small and gray.

What you have right: the arithmetic checks out ($2,140 and 13%), and the single accent, the single amber, and the direct labeling are the right choices.
