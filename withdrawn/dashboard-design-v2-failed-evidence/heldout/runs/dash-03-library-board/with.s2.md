**Put these 6 on the page:**

| Measure | Compare it to | Why the board cares |
|---|---|---|
| **Visits** (door counts) | Same two months last year, shown as a % change | Whether the town is showing up. |
| **Items checked out** | Same two months last year | Whether people are using what you own. |
| **Program attendance** | Same two months last year | Whether the programs are reaching people. |
| **Cardholders as % of town population** | Last year's %, plus a town-wide goal if the board has one | Whether you reach the town. This is better than a raw count. |
| **Budget spent vs plan, year to date** | The plan for this point in the year, shown as a dollar and % gap | Whether the money is okay. |
| **Holds waiting more than 14 days** | A target you and the board set, plus last period | The one measure of service quality. A rising number means patrons are waiting. |

**Leave these off:**
- **New cards issued:** it's folded into the cardholder share. Cardholder counts also include people who stopped using the library years ago, so the % of the town is more honest than a total.
- **Cost per visit:** it's just budget divided by visits, so it adds no new information. It works well as one annual line in a budget report.
- **Computer/wifi sessions, volunteer hours, items per capita:** these are good for the annual report, but they move slowly and don't prompt a decision at a bimonthly meeting.
- **Overdue items:** this is a staff operations number, not a board one.
- **Social media followers:** it's a vanity number and doesn't show whether you serve the town.
- **Donations:** leave it off unless it's a big share of your revenue. If it is, swap it in as a "vs. same period last year" line next to the budget.

**Comparison choices:**
- **Same period last year, not last period.** Library use is seasonal (summer reading, winter lulls). Comparing March–April to January–February would make normal seasonality look like good or bad news.
- **Plan to date, not the annual budget.** "62% of the budget spent" means nothing without knowing you should be at 58% by now. Show something like "$14,200 under plan (3%)."
- **Holds needs a target.** I haven't invented one, so write "target: set by staff and board" until you pick a number.

**Layout for volunteers:**
- Put the budget and holds on the top row, because they are the "is anything wrong" items. If either is off plan or target, say so in a plain sentence with the gap and when it started.
- Below that, show the four use and reach measures as cards. Each card has the value, the change in words ("up 6% from last year"), and a small 12-month line.
- Add one plain-English sentence at the top, such as "Use is up, spending is on plan, holds are slipping."
- Keep healthy items in a neutral color. Use one alert color, always with an icon and a word, so the exception is the loudest thing on the page.
- If one branch is driving a big swing, name it in a single line. Don't show four branches of every measure.

**Two caveats:**
- Note any change in hours, closures, or construction during the period. Otherwise a dip in visits will be misread.
- I assumed the page is for the board's meeting and is read cold, with no staff presenting. If you'll present it, I'd keep the same measures but trim the sentences.
