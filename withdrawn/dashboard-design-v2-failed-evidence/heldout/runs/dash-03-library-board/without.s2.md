# Recommended dashboard: 7 items

| Board question | Metric | Compare against |
|---|---|---|
| Are people using us? | **1. Visits** | Same period last year |
| | **2. Items checked out** | Same period last year |
| | **5. Program attendance** | Same period last year |
| Do we reach the town? | **4. Cardholders**, shown as *active* cardholders as a % of town population | Last year's %, and a target the board sets |
| Are we doing the job well? | **7. Holds waiting over 14 days** | A target (e.g. "under 5% of holds") and last period |
| Is the money okay? | **8. Budget spent vs plan YTD** | The plan prorated to today's date, not a straight line |
| Is it well spent? | **10. Cost per visit** | Same period last year, and similar-sized libraries once a year |

## Why these comparisons

- **Use the same period last year, not the previous meeting.** Library use is seasonal. Summer reading and winter slumps would make every other meeting look like a crisis or a triumph. Last year's same two months removes that.
- **Use targets only where a target means something.** Visits and checkouts are best judged against last year. Holds and cardholder reach need a goal. Set those goals once with the board so the page isn't arguing about them each meeting.
- **Prorate the budget by when money actually goes out.** Some bills land in lumps, like insurance, database subscriptions, and annual contracts. A straight-line comparison will flag false overspending. Add one line of plain-English status: "on track," "watch," or "off."
- **Calculate cost per visit over a rolling 12 months.** A single two-month slice swings too much. It's also the one number that connects "serving the town" to "is the money okay."
- **Use active cardholders, not total registered.** Total registered counts everyone who ever signed up, including people who moved away, so it only goes up. Count cards used in the last 12 to 24 months. Dividing by the town's population gives the board a number they can judge.

## Leave off

- **3. New cards:** Fold it into a note under cardholders if it's interesting. On its own it's a detail.
- **6. Computer/wifi sessions:** This is the first one I'd swap in if your board cares about digital access. Otherwise it's a detail.
- **9. Social followers:** It's a vanity metric and says little about service or money.
- **11. Volunteer hours:** Save it for the annual report.
- **12. Items per capita:** It changes slowly and is an annual state-report number.
- **13. Overdue items:** This is an operational number for staff, not a board decision.
- **14. Donations:** If donations are a meaningful share of revenue, show them as a revenue line inside the budget item. Otherwise leave them off.

## Layout

Put the money item and the use items side by side, so the page reads as "here's what we delivered, here's what it cost." For each item, show the current number, the comparison number, and a simple up, down, or flat indicator. Add one sentence from you at the top saying what the board should notice this time. Volunteers will read that sentence before anything else.
