# Harbor Hill board dashboard

## Put on the page (6 items)

| Item | Compare it to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Visits** (door counts) | Same two months last year | Library use is seasonal. Comparing to the previous period would make every summer and December look like a crisis or a triumph. |
| **2. Items checked out** | Same two months last year | This is your core "are people using us" number, and it covers more than visits do. |
| **3. Program attendance** | Same two months last year | It shows you're serving the community beyond lending. Break it out only if the board cares about one audience, such as kids or seniors. |
| **4. Registered cardholders** | Town population, as a % with a card | A raw count of 11,400 means nothing to a volunteer, but "62% of residents have a card" does. Count only cards used in the last 1–3 years, since old accounts inflate the total. |
| **5. Holds waiting more than 14 days** | A target you set (for example, under 5%), plus last period | It's the one early warning on service quality. Rising numbers usually mean you need more copies or more staff time. |
| **6. Budget spent vs plan, year to date** | Where the plan says you should be on this date | Compare to the planned spend at this point, not a straight 1/12 per month. Insurance, databases, and other lumpy costs make a straight line misleading. Mark it green, yellow, or red. |

Visits, checkouts, and program attendance are the "serving the town" story. Cardholders show reach. Holds show quality. Budget is the money question. That covers both of the board's questions with no filler.

## Leave off

- **New cards issued.** It's noisy month to month, and cardholders already covers reach.
- **Computer/wifi sessions.** This is a real service, but staff use it more than the board does. Swap it in for holds if digital access is a town priority.
- **Social media followers.** It's a vanity metric and says nothing about service or money.
- **Cost per visit.** It just divides two numbers already on the page. It also penalizes you for digital use and programs, which don't show up in door counts. Save it for the annual report.
- **Volunteer hours.** It's good for the annual report and for thanking people, but it doesn't change in a way the board would act on.
- **Items per capita.** The collection barely moves over two months, and state reports already use it.
- **Overdue items out.** This is operational, so staff should manage it.
- **Donations.** Leave it off unless donations are a budgeted revenue line. If they are, show them as "donations vs plan" next to the budget item.

## Design tips

- **One comparison per number.** Show a single arrow or "+4% vs last year", not three baselines.
- **Normalize for hours.** If branch hours or closures changed, note it or show visits per open hour. Otherwise a closed week looks like falling demand.
- **Add a one-line plain-English takeaway** at the top, such as "Use is up, holds are slowing down, spending is on plan."
- **Keep the same six every time.** The board learns to read the page, and trends become obvious.

Is there a specific concern on the board right now, like a levy vote, a hours cut, or a new branch? If so, I'd swap one of these for a metric that speaks to it.
