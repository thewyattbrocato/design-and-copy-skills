# Recommended dashboard: 7 items

The page should answer two questions: are we serving the town, and is the money okay.

## Serving the town

| Measure | Compare against |
|---|---|
| **1. Visits (door counts)** | Same two months last year. Libraries are seasonal, so comparing to the previous period misleads. |
| **2. Items checked out** | Same two months last year. |
| **5. Program attendance** | Same two months last year. |
| **4. Registered cardholders** | Share of town population (e.g., "58% of residents"). Count only cards used in the last 2–3 years, or the number will be inflated by dead cards. |
| **7. Holds waiting more than 14 days** | A target the board sets, such as "under 5% of holds". This is your one service-quality measure. It tells them whether patrons are getting what they asked for. |

## Money

| Measure | Compare against |
|---|---|
| **8. Budget spent vs. plan YTD** | The plan phased by month, not a straight-line share of the year. Annual subscriptions and other lumpy costs will otherwise look like overspending. Show it as "% of budget spent" next to "% of year elapsed". |
| **10. Cost per visit** | Last year, and peer libraries of similar size. Your state library's annual statistics usually give you the peer figure. This ties spending to use, so it tells the board whether the money is well spent. |

## Leave off

- **3. New cards:** it's noisy month to month. The cardholder figure covers it.
- **6. Computer/wifi sessions:** useful, but it's a detail. If your town has a notable digital-access gap, swap it in for #2 or #5, or keep it for the annual report.
- **9. Social followers:** a vanity metric. Board members can't act on it.
- **11. Volunteer hours:** good for the annual report and for thanking volunteers, but it doesn't answer either question.
- **12. Items per capita:** it changes slowly, so once a year is enough.
- **13. Overdue items:** an operational number for staff. Holds waiting covers the patron-experience side.
- **14. Donations:** put this in the treasurer's report. If gifts are a large share of revenue, add a one-line note under the budget tile instead of giving it its own tile.

## Design suggestions

- **One number, one comparison, one plain-language cue per tile.** For example: "Visits: 14,200, up 6% vs. last year." Add a green, yellow or red status mark based on thresholds the board agrees on in advance.
- **Branch breakdown:** for visits and circulation, show the 4 branches in small print under the total, not as separate charts.
- **Short note:** add 2–3 sentences at the bottom on what changed and why. Volunteers will read those before the numbers.
- **Consistency:** keep the same layout and the same 7 measures every meeting, so the board learns to read it at a glance.
