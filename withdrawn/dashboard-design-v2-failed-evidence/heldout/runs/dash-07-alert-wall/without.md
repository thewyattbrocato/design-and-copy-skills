# Why "brighter red" won't fix it, and what will

## The core point
The outage wasn't missed because the red wasn't bright enough. It was missed because it looked the same as 11 other things that were also red or amber that day. Making red brighter makes all 12 louder, so the real problem stays just as hard to spot.

## Analogies
- **Car dashboard:** Imagine 40 warning lights, and on the day your engine overheats, 11 others are lit too. Making them all brighter doesn't tell you which one means "pull over now."
- **Car alarm:** When one goes off every day, people stop hearing it. Turning up the volume trains them to tune it out faster.

## What I'd say to him
> "On the day of the outage, 12 of our 40 tiles were red or amber. Only one of them was a real customer-impacting problem, and it sat there for 25 minutes. If we make red brighter, we make all 12 brighter, and the one that matters is still lost in the crowd. People also learn to ignore things that are always lit. The fix is to make fewer things loud, and to make sure the loud ones always mean 'act now.'"

## What to do instead
1. **Rank the tiles.** Pick the 3 to 5 that mean customers can't pay, such as payment success rate, error rate, and latency. Put them at the top, large. Everything else goes into a smaller "diagnostic" section.
2. **Clean up the noise.** Review the 11 tiles that were red or amber. Fix the thresholds, or remove tiles that don't need action. Every tile that's red should have a clear "what do I do" answer.
3. **Don't depend on someone watching.** A dashboard only works if someone is looking at it. For the critical few, send an automatic alert (page or phone call) to the on-call person. That is the real fix for the 25-minute delay.
4. **Assign an owner.** Each critical signal should have a named person or team who responds.

## How to measure success
Set a target such as "a real payment outage is noticed within 2 to 5 minutes," down from 25. Then test it with a practice drill. He can understand that goal, and it turns the discussion from colors to results.

## Tips for the conversation
- Acknowledge his instinct first: "You're right that people didn't notice. The question is why."
- Avoid claiming the other 11 tiles were meaningless. Say they weren't outage-level, which is easier to defend.
- If you can, mention how many of the 40 tiles are red or amber on a typical day. If it's often 5 or more, that is your strongest evidence.
