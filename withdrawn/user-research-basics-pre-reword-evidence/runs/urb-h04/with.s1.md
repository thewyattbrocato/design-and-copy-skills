# Screener: clinic front desk interviews

**Study:** 30-minute remote interviews, about 6 people. **Target:** people who staff the front desk at a rural clinic. **Incentive:** [to set].

## Intro (shown at the top of the form)

> We're talking with people who work at medical offices and clinics about their day-to-day work. It's a 30-minute video or phone conversation, and you'll receive [incentive] as thanks. This short form takes about 3 minutes. Not everyone who fills it out will be invited.

## Questions

**1. Which of these have you done at work in the past month? (Select all that apply)**
- Checked patients in at a front desk or reception
- Answered phones for a business
- Handled cash or card payments
- Scheduled or rescheduled appointments
- Worked in a retail store or restaurant
- Ordered or managed supplies
- Supervised or trained other staff
- None of these

*Qualify:* "Checked patients in" **and** "Scheduled or rescheduled appointments."

**2. Which best describes where you work most of the time?**
- Medical or dental clinic
- Hospital
- Pharmacy
- Veterinary office
- School or university
- Government office
- Other: ____

*Qualify:* Medical clinic. Review "Other" by hand.

**3. What is the setting around your workplace?**
- Large city
- Suburb
- Small city or town
- Rural area, with the nearest large town 30 or more minutes away
- Not sure

*Qualify:* Rural, or small town if the clinic mainly serves a rural area. Review by hand.

**4. In a typical week, about how many patients does your front desk see in person?**
- Fewer than 25
- 25–75
- 76–200
- More than 200

*Quota:* Aim for a spread across ranges. This is a target, not a guarantee.

**5. How do patients usually reach you on a typical day? (Select all that apply)**
- Walk in without an appointment
- Arrive for a scheduled appointment
- Phone calls
- Online or portal messages
- Referrals from other offices

*Qualify:* "Walk in" **and** "Scheduled appointment."

**6. Which do you use at work at least weekly? (Select all that apply)**
- Paper sign-in sheet or appointment book
- Whiteboard or sticky notes
- Spreadsheet
- Electronic health record or practice management software
- Texting or messaging apps
- Shared calendar
- None of these

*No one is disqualified here.* Use it to get a mix of paper, spreadsheet and software setups.

**7. Do you have a computer, tablet or phone with a camera and a stable internet connection for a video call during work hours or at a time of your choosing?**
- Yes, video call works
- Phone call only
- Neither

*Qualify:* Either of the first two. Video is preferred.

**8. Open question (2–3 sentences):** *Tell me about the last time your front desk had a very busy stretch. What was going on, and what did you do?*

*Review by hand.* Keep people who describe a specific recent event. Drop empty, copied or vague answers.

**9. Do you or a close family member work for any of these?**
- A company that makes healthcare software
- A market research or design firm
- None of these

*Disqualify:* The first two.

**10. Have you taken part in a paid research study in the past 3 months?**
- Yes / No

*Disqualify:* Yes.

**11. Contact:** first name, email or phone, best days and times, time zone.

## Selection notes

- Don't name the product, the company or "no-shows" or "queues" anywhere in the screener or invitation. Q1 and Q5 bury the target behavior among decoys.
- Aim for a mix. Target at least 2 clinics on paper or spreadsheets, at least 2 on software, and a spread of patient volume in Q4. Treat these as targets.
- Pay people who show up even if they turn out to be a poor match. Note it and set their data aside.
- The sample leaves out urban and suburban clinics, patients, and clinicians or managers who don't work the desk. Say so beside every conclusion.
- Add this to the session intro: "Please don't share patient names or anything identifying."
