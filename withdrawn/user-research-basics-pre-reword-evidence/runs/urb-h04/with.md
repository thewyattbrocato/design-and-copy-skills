# Screener: Interview about everyday front-desk work (30 min, remote)

**Intro (shown at top):** We're talking with people who work in healthcare offices about how their day-to-day work goes. This is a 30-minute video call. Participants get **[incentive: to set]**. This form takes about 3 minutes. Please don't include any patient names or details in your answers.

**Q1. Which of these have you done at work in the last month? (Select all that apply.)**
- Answered phones or greeted people at a front desk or reception
- Scheduled or rescheduled appointments
- Handled walk-in arrivals
- Called or texted people about missed appointments
- Processed insurance or billing paperwork
- Ordered supplies
- Managed a staff work schedule
- None of these

*Qualify: Q1 includes "Handled walk-in arrivals" and "Scheduled or rescheduled appointments." The other options are decoys.*

**Q2. Where do you do this work?**
- Medical, dental, or behavioral health clinic
- Hospital department
- Pharmacy
- Non-health office
- Other: ____

*Qualify: clinic. Hospital departments and the others are out.*

**Q3. Which best describes the area around your workplace?**
- Rural or remote (small town, long drives to other services)
- Small city
- Suburban
- Large city

*Qualify: rural. Treat small-city as a backup tier (see Fallback). Self-reported, so check it against the clinic's town on the call.*

**Q4. About how many staff work at your location, and how many people are at the front desk on a typical day?** (Two short number fields)

*Target: small sites, roughly 1–3 at the desk. Use this to get a mix, not as a hard cutoff.*

**Q5. In the last week, how did you keep track of who was waiting and who was scheduled? (Select all that apply.)**
- Paper schedule or sign-in sheet
- Whiteboard or sticky notes
- Spreadsheet
- Practice management or EHR software
- Separate scheduling or reminder software
- Memory or verbal handoff
- Other: ____

*Not a pass/fail question. Use it to make sure the six people cover different setups, not six paper-only or six EHR-only desks.*

**Q6. Which of these will you have on the call?**
- A computer or tablet with a camera and working audio
- Phone only
- Neither

*Qualify: first option.*

**Q7. (Open text, 2–3 sentences.)** Think about the last time the front desk got unexpectedly busy. Walk me through what was happening and what you did.

*Check for: a specific, recent story in their own words. Screen out one-word answers, generic statements ("we just handle it"), or answers that don't describe real events.*

**Q8. Do you or an immediate family member work for any of these?**
- A company that makes healthcare software
- A market research or design firm
- None of these

*Qualify: none.*

**Q9. Are you able to talk for 30 minutes on a video call during weekday business hours, including during a lunch break or slow period?** Pick all windows that work: [calendar slots to set]. Please also confirm you're not driving during the call.

**Contact:** first name, email, best phone number, time zone. Don't ask for the clinic's name until scheduling.

## Selection notes
- **Target of 6:** at least 4 from clinics with 1–3 people at the desk, and a mix of Q5 setups (not all paper, not all software). Both are targets, not guarantees.
- **Don't reveal what's being built.** The screener never says "queue," "no-show," or "app." Don't name the product or sponsor until after consent on the call.
- **Pay mismatches anyway** if they join the call, and set their data aside.
- **Who this leaves out:** providers and office managers who don't staff the desk, and clinics with no internet during the day. It also leaves out anyone who doesn't see the post.

## Fallback
- If you have fewer than 6 qualified people by mid-week, relax one criterion at a time: small-city clinics first, then clinics with more desk staff. Don't relax the walk-in and scheduling behaviors.
- If you still fall short, run 4 interviews on time and book 2 more the following week. Say that on the research plan so nobody reads 4 as the full study.

  - Your own customers or waitlist, if any
  - State rural health associations and Primary Care Association newsletters

  - Referrals from clinic contacts you already have
