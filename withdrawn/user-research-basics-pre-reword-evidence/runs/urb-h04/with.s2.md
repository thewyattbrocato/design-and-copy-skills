# Screener: front desk walk-in and no-show study

**Study label (shown to candidates):** "A 30-minute remote interview about how clinic front desks run the day." No product or sponsor is named. Incentive: `[amount to set]`, paid even if the person doesn't qualify for the interview.

## Questions

**Q1. Which of these did you do at work in the last 4 weeks? (Select all that apply.)**
- Checked patients in at a front desk or reception ← *must select*
- Answered phones or scheduling calls ← *must select, or Q2 = a scheduling or walk-in task*
- Ordered or stocked supplies
- Handled insurance billing or claims
- Worked as a nurse, provider or medical assistant with patients
- Worked in IT or software support
- None of these → **end**

**Q2. In a typical week, how often do you personally do each of these?** (Never / A few times a week / Daily)
- Check in patients who walked in without an appointment
- Reschedule or fill appointments after a patient didn't show up
- Reconcile the day's schedule at close
- Handle patient complaints about wait times

*Qualify:* "Daily" or "A few times a week" on at least one of the first two rows.

**Q3. Where do you work?**
- Independent or small-group clinic (1–10 providers)
- Federally qualified health center or community health center
- Rural health clinic
- Hospital outpatient department
- Large health system clinic
- Other: ____

**Q4. What is the nearest town or city with more than 50,000 people, and roughly how far is it from your clinic?** (Free text. A person on the research team judges "rural" from this, not the candidate.)

**Q5. Which tools do you use at the front desk in a normal week?** (Select all that apply.)
- Paper sign-in sheet or paper schedule
- Whiteboard or sticky notes
- Practice management or EHR scheduling module
- Spreadsheet
- Text or reminder service
- Phone only
- Other: ____

*Qualify:* any answer, but record it. This is a quota variable, not a filter.

**Q6. (Open question.)** *Think of the last time a patient didn't show up for an appointment. In a few sentences, tell me what happened at the desk afterward.*
*Pass:* a specific, concrete account. *Decline:* "I don't know" or a generic policy statement with no actual event.

**Q7. Do any of these describe you or your household?** *(Exclusion)*
- I work for a company that makes healthcare scheduling or practice management software
- I work in UX design, user research or marketing
- I took part in a paid research study in the last 3 months

*Any yes → end, thank them.*

**Q8. Can you do a 30-minute video call on a computer or tablet between `[dates]`, and which windows work?** (Pick from slots.) Also ask for name, email and clinic role. No clinic name is needed yet.

## Selection targets for about 6 people

These are targets, not guarantees:
- At least 4 of 6 in clinics that fit the rural definition in Q4.
- A mix of tools in Q5, with at least 1 mostly paper or whiteboard and at least 1 mostly EHR-based.
- A mix of clinic types in Q3, so the six aren't all one type.
- Ideally 1–2 people who work alone at the desk and 1–2 who work with a team.

**Who this leaves out:** anyone who can't take a video call during work hours, which skews toward busier, understaffed desks. It also leaves out clinic managers who don't work the desk, and desks that rely on phone-only workflows with no internet access.

## Notes for the session
- Tell participants up front not to share patient names or identifiers. Ask about what happened, not who it happened to.
- Get recording consent before recording.
- Pay anyone who attends, even if the session shows they're a poor fit, and note the mismatch.

## What a human has to do

I can't find or contact people from here. I have no way to post, message or look anyone up, and I wouldn't contact people without you choosing the channels. Someone on your team needs to:
1. Pick channels. Likely options are rural health associations, state primary care associations, community health center networks, rural-clinic groups on LinkedIn or Facebook, and personal referrals. Customer contacts from your own product are the best source if you have any.
2. Put the screener into a form tool and send the invitation.
3. Schedule sessions and collect consent.
