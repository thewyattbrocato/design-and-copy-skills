A usability test watches a handful of real people try to use your design, so you learn *why* something is confusing, where they hesitate, and what they expect. It's qualitative and works well early, even with sketches or prototypes. An A/B test ships two versions to many live users and measures which one performs better on a specific metric, like signups or clicks. It tells you *which* version wins, but not why. Roughly: usability tests help you find problems and form ideas, while A/B tests help you confirm which solution actually moves the numbers.
