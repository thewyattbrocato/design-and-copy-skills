A usability test watches a handful of real people try to complete tasks with your design, so you learn *why* they get confused, hesitate, or succeed. It's qualitative and works with early prototypes. An A/B test shows two live versions to large numbers of users at random and measures which one performs better on a specific metric, like sign-ups or clicks. It tells you *what* won, but not why. Usability tests help you find problems and shape ideas, while A/B tests help you confirm which option moves the numbers. Most product teams use both together.
