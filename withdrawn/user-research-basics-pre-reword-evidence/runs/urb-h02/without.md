A usability test watches a handful of real people try to use your design while you observe, so you learn *why* they get confused, hesitate, or succeed. It's qualitative and works well early, even with sketches or prototypes. An A/B test ships two versions to lots of live users and measures which one performs better on a specific metric, like sign-ups or clicks. It tells you *which* version wins, but not why. Think of usability tests as finding problems and generating ideas, and A/B tests as confirming, with numbers, which solution actually works.
