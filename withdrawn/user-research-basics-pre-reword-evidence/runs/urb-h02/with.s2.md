A usability test watches a handful of real people try to complete tasks with your design, so you learn *why* they get confused, hesitate, or succeed. It's qualitative and works with early prototypes. An A/B test ships two versions to many live users at once and measures which one performs better on a specific number, like sign-ups or clicks. It's quantitative and tells you *which* version wins, but not why. Usability tests help you find and fix problems; A/B tests help you confirm that a change actually moves a metric. Most teams use the first to shape ideas and the second to validate them.
