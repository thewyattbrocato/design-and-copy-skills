A usability test is about *why*: you watch a handful of people try to use your design and notice where they get confused, stall, or misread something. It's qualitative and works with a small group, even five or so. An A/B test is about *which*: you show two versions to large numbers of real users and measure which one drives more of a specific behavior, like sign-ups or clicks. Usability testing helps you find problems and understand them, while A/B testing tells you, with statistical confidence, which option performs better. Designers often use the first to shape ideas and the second to validate them.
