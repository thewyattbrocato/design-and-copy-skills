A usability test watches a handful of real people try to complete tasks with your design, so you learn *why* something confuses them. It's qualitative and works even on a rough prototype. An A/B test ships two versions to thousands of live users and measures which one performs better on a specific metric, like sign-ups or clicks. It's quantitative, so it tells you *which* version wins but not why. Think of usability testing as finding problems and generating ideas, and A/B testing as settling a decision with data once you have options worth comparing.
