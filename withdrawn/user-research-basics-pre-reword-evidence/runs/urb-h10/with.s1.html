Paddlepoint: Kayak Renter Interview Guide

40 min · one person at a time · Participant code: ______   Date: ________
Goal (for you, not read aloud): Describe how people find, book, and show up for a kayak rental from a small outfitter, and where it goes wrong. Listen for stories about the last real time, not opinions about a tool.

1. Intro and consent 0–4 min

  • “Thanks for making time. I'm learning how people rent kayaks and boats for the day. There are no right answers, and I'm not selling anything.”
  • “Can I record audio so I can focus on listening? Only I and my team will hear it, and we'll delete it after [date]. You can skip any question or stop anytime.”
  • Got a clear yes to recording before pressing record   Anyone else listening? Say so.
  • Don't name Paddlepoint or pitch it. If they ask, “I'll tell you at the end.”

2. Warm-up 4–8 min

  1. Tell me a bit about how paddling fits into your life. How often do you get out, and with whom?
  2. Do you own any gear, or do you usually rent? Roughly how many rentals in the past year?

3. Core stories 8–32 min

  1. Last rental. Tell me about the last time you rented a kayak. Start from when you first thought about going.
    Probes: What happened next? Who else was involved? What day and time did you pick, and why?
  2. Finding an outfitter. How did you end up choosing that place? What else did you look at?
    Probes: What did you search or ask? What made you rule something out?
  3. Booking. Walk me through how you actually reserved it: phone, website, walk-up, message. What did you do, step by step?
    Probes: Anything you had to wait on? Anything you couldn't find out (price, availability, gear, tides/weather)?
  4. Group and gear. How did you sort out how many boats, what types, and who was in each?
    Probes: Who decided? How did you pay or split cost? Did the plan change?
  5. Day of. Tell me about arriving and getting on the water. What went smoothly? What didn't?
    Probes: Waivers, check-in, lines, late arrivals, parking, shuttles?
  6. When plans changed. Tell me about a time a rental got cancelled, rescheduled, or didn't work out (weather, sold out, running late).
    Probes: What did you do? How did you hear? What happened to your money?
  7. Other options. What did you do when the outfitter you wanted wasn't an option? What do you use instead of renting?
  8. Hardest part. Across all the rentals you've done, which step was most annoying or took the most effort? Tell me about that time.
If time is short, protect #3, #5, #6, #8 (last rental, booking, day of, when plans changed).

4. Close 32–40 min

  • “Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?”
  • “Could I follow up by email if I have a quick question?”
  • Now you can say what Paddlepoint is. Thank them, confirm how the incentive will be sent. Pay even if they weren't a good fit.

Do

  • Ask for the last time, not “usually.”
  • Say “tell me more” or “what happened next?” and then wait. Silence is fine.
  • Use their words back; don't supply yours.
  • Treat the guide as a checklist. Let them wander, then return.

Avoid

  • “Would you use…?” / “Do you like…?” / “Would you pay…?”
  • Asking what features they want.
  • Nodding hard, agreeing, or sharing your own stories.
  • Yes/no questions. Turn them into “what happened?”

Notes: quick capture (jot facts; mark what they did vs. what you think it means)

After each session: save recording and notes right away, named paddlepoint_P#_date. Use participant codes, not names. Write down what you saw before you interpret it. Report counts as “3 of 6,” not percentages. If sessions run over, cut #7 first.
