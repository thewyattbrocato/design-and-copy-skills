Paddlepoint · Kayak Renter Interview Guide

40 min · one person at a time · people who rent from small outfitters
Goal: Learn how people actually find, book, and show up for kayak rentals today, and where it goes wrong. You're here to listen, not to sell. Ask about the past, not the future.

1. Intro 3 min

“Thanks for doing this. I'm researching how people book kayak rentals. There are no right answers, and you can't hurt my feelings. I'm not selling anything. Is it OK if I take notes [and record audio]? Only I'll see it, and you can skip any question.”
  • Get a clear yes before recording. Then start.

2. Warm-up 5 min

  1. How did you get into paddling? How often do you rent versus own?
  2. Who do you usually go with? (solo, partner, kids, group)
  3. Roughly how many times did you rent in the past year? Where?

3. Core: the most recent rental 15 min

  1. “Take me back to the last time you rented a kayak. What made you decide to go?”
  2. How did you find that outfitter? What else did you look at?
  3. How did you find out if they had boats on the day you wanted?
  4. How did you book: phone, website, email, walk-up? Show me / what did you do next?
  5. What did you need to know before committing? price, boat types, group size, time slots, weather, gear, parking
  6. How did payment, deposit, or waiver work?
  7. What happened between booking and showing up? reminders, changes, weather calls
  8. How did the day itself go at check-in?

4. Pain & workarounds 8 min

  1. What was the most frustrating or confusing part of that rental?
  2. Has a rental ever fallen through or changed on you? What did you do?
  3. Have you ever given up on a place or picked a different one? Why?
  4. Any tricks you use? calling ahead, checking Instagram, asking friends

5. Idea reaction 5 min · only now

“We're exploring an online booking tool that small outfitters would use. Imagine you could see live availability and book in one place.”
  1. What's your first reaction? What would you want to check first?
  2. What would make you trust it, or not?
  3. How is that different from how you do it today?

6. Wrap-up 4 min

  • “Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?”
  • “Do you know anyone else who rents kayaks I could talk to?”
  • Thank them. Confirm any thank-you or incentive. Stop recording.

Do

  • Ask for stories: “Tell me about the last time…”
  • Stay quiet after they answer. Count to 5.
  • Follow the energy and let them wander briefly.
  • Write exact words in quotes.
  • Ask “why?” gently and in different ways.

Don't

  • Pitch Paddlepoint before Section 5.
  • Ask “Would you use…?” People guess badly about the future.
  • Lead: “Wasn't that annoying?”
  • Fill silences or finish their sentences.
  • Argue or defend the idea if they dislike it.

Probes when stuck

  • “Tell me more about that.”
  • “What happened next?”
  • “Can you give me an example?”
  • “What did you do about it?”
  • “How did that make you feel?”
  • “What did you expect to happen?”

If things go sideways

  • Short answers: go back to “walk me through it.”
  • Hypotheticals: “When did this last actually happen?”
  • Running late: skip Section 2, protect Sections 3 and 4.
  • Rambling: “That's helpful. Can I jump back to…?”

Listen for

  • Phone calls and DMs used instead of a site
  • Uncertainty about availability
  • Weather and cancellation anxiety
  • Group coordination and deposits
  • Moments of giving up or switching
