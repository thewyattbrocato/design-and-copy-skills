Interview Guide: People Who Rent Kayaks from Small Outfitters

40 min, one person at a time  |  Participant code: ______   Date: ________   Recording OK? ☐ yes ☐ no
Goal (for you, not read aloud): Describe how renters actually find, book, change and show up for a kayak rental today, and identify where it goes wrong. Ask about real recent events, never opinions about tools or features. Don't say "Paddlepoint" unless asked at the end.

Run of session

0–3 min
Intro and consent "Thanks for doing this. I'm researching how people book outdoor rentals. There are no right answers; I'm interested in what really happened, including the messy parts. I'd like to record audio so I can focus on you. Only I and my notes will use it, and I'll keep it [how long]. You can skip any question or stop anytime. Okay to record?" Start recording only after a yes.
3–8
Warm-up "Tell me a bit about how kayaking fits into your life. How often do you rent, and who do you usually go with?"
8–15
1. Last rental, from the start "Think of the last time you rented a kayak. Take me back to when you first decided to go. What happened?"
"How did you end up with that outfitter? What else did you look at?"
15–23
2. Booking "Walk me through what you did to actually get the kayak(s) reserved, step by step."
"What did you need to know before you could commit? Where did you find it, or not?"
"Was there a point where you got stuck or had to wait for something?"
23–30
3. Changes and the day itself "Tell me about a time a rental plan changed (weather, group size, timing). What did you do?"
"What happened when you arrived: check-in, paperwork, payment, getting on the water?"
30–36
4. Rough spots "Tell me about the roughest rental or booking experience you've had. What happened, and what did you do?"
"Is there a time you gave up on a place or booked somewhere else? What led to that?"
36–40
Close "Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?" Ask permission to follow up. Thank them; confirm the incentive. Stop recording, save the file as study_P#_date right away.
Running late? Cut warm-up detail first, then section 4. Protect sections 1 and 2.

Probes (use these a lot)

  • "Say more about that."
  • "What happened next?"
  • "Why was that?" / "What were you hoping for?"
  • "Who else was involved?"
  • "What did you use instead?"
  • Wait 5 seconds in silence. They'll fill it.
  • Reuse their words; don't supply yours (like "portal" or "slots").

Avoid

  • "Would you use…?", "Do you like…?", "Would you pay…?"
  • "Which features do you want?" (Ask what they did instead.)
  • "Usually…" questions. Ask about the last time.
  • Yes/no questions. Rephrase as "what happened".
  • Nodding hard, agreeing, or telling your own stories.
  • Pitching or defending any product.

If they drift

Let a story run a minute, then: "That's helpful. Going back to the time you booked…" Revisit skipped topics at the end. It's a checklist, not a script.

Stop the session if

They're driving, distressed, or ask to stop. End kindly; pay the incentive anyway.

Notes: what to catch

  • Exact quotes (mark with " ")
  • Channel used: phone, walk-in, web, text, email
  • Waits, workarounds, double-checking
  • Money, group size, weather moments

Before you start

  • Recording tested; notes template open
  • Participant rents from a small outfitter and did so recently
  • Incentive ready to send
  • Name hidden in notes: use P1, P2…
  • Water nearby; phone silenced
  • After: 5 minutes to write top 3 surprises
Reminder: after each session, record what happened vs. what you think it means in separate columns. Report findings as "3 of 6", never percentages. Stop once new interviews stop surprising you; plan roughly 5–8 people to start.
