Kayak renter interview guide

40 min · one person at a time · Participant code: ______   Date: ________
Goal (for you, not read aloud): Describe how people actually found, booked, and showed up for their last kayak rental, and where it went wrong or got awkward. Listen for what they did, not what they'd like. Don't mention Paddlepoint. Call it "an interview about renting outdoor gear."

1. Intro and consent0:00 – 0:04

  • "Thanks for your time. I'm learning how people rent kayaks. There are no right answers, and I didn't build anything you need to be nice about. About 40 minutes."
  • "May I record audio so I can focus on listening? Only our small team hears it, and it's deleted after [__ to set]. You can skip any question or stop anytime."
  • Got a clear yes to recording before pressing record   Not driving or on a busy dock

2. Warm-up0:04 – 0:08

  • How often do you paddle, and where did you last go? Who was with you?
  • Do you own any gear, or do you usually rent?

3. Core: the last rental, as a story0:08 – 0:32

  • Tell me about the last time you rented a kayak. Start from when you first thought about going. Then let them talk.
  • How did you pick that outfitter? What else did you look at? What made you rule others out?
  • Walk me through how you booked. Phone, walk-up, site, message? What did you need to know first? What did you do when you couldn't find it?
  • What happened between booking and showing up? Confirmation, payment, waivers, questions, weather checks.
  • Tell me about the day itself, from arriving to paddling out. Waiting, paperwork, gear mix-ups, group size.
  • Has a rental ever changed or fallen through (weather, no availability, a late group)? Tell me about the last time. What did you do next? Who did you contact?
  • Tell me about a rental that went badly or annoyed you. What happened next?
  • If you organized for others: how did you collect headcount, money, and sizes?

4. Close0:32 – 0:40

  • Return to any uncovered topic above (use 0:32 – 0:37).
  • Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?
  • "Can I follow up if I have a question?" Thank them, give the incentive [__ to set].
  • Right after: save the recording and notes as study_participantcode_date in the shared folder. Write 3 lines of takeaways.

Do

  • Ask for the last time, not "usually."
  • Probe with: "Say more." "What happened next?" "Why was that?"
  • Wait through silence. Count to 5 before talking.
  • Reuse their words, not yours (their "reservation" vs. your "booking").
  • Treat the guide as a checklist. Follow good tangents, then return.
  • Write quotes verbatim and mark what they did vs. what they think.

Don't

  • Ask "Would you use...?", "Do you like...?", "What features do you want?", or "Would you pay?"
  • Ask yes/no questions about the topic. Turn them into "What happened?"
  • Pitch, defend, or explain the tool, or share your own rental stories.
  • Nod hard or say "great" or "exactly." Use "Thanks, that helps."
  • Finish their sentences or suggest answers.
  • Trust predictions. Ask about the last real decision instead.
Running short? Keep sections 1, 3 (first three prompts and "went badly"), and 4. Drop 2 and the group question.   After each session: note the sample (who they rent from, rough trip frequency) and report counts as "3 of 7," not percentages.   Limit: these are renters only. Outfitter staff are not covered.
