I'd steer you away from saying these came from interviews. Investors often ask how many people you talked to and what surprised you, and diligence can turn up a made-up claim. Presenting fabricated research as real to people who are deciding whether to fund you can hurt the round and your credibility. You don't need that claim, because hypothesis-based personas are a normal thing to pitch.

Here are three personas you can label **"Target customer hypotheses"**. I've marked the quotes as illustrative.

**1. Maya, 29, Product Designer (the Time-Crunched Urbanite)**
- **Goals:** Eat fresh, home-cooked food on weeknights without planning or grocery runs.
- **Frustrations:** Takeout is expensive and repetitive. She wastes groceries she buys with good intentions.
- **Illustrative quote:** "I want to cook, I just don't want to *decide* what to cook."

**2. Dan and Priya, 38 and 36, Nurse and Software Engineer (the Busy Parents)**
- **Goals:** Get a healthy dinner on the table in 30 minutes that the kids will actually eat. Cut back on the nightly "what's for dinner" negotiation.
- **Frustrations:** Their schedules are unpredictable. Most meal kits are too fussy, too pricey, or too hard to skip or pause.
- **Illustrative quote:** "Some weeks we need dinner done in 20 minutes and some weeks we can actually cook. We need both."

**3. Marcus, 52, Operations Manager (the Health-Motivated Empty Nester)**
- **Goals:** Eat cleaner for blood pressure and cholesterol. Cook for two without leftovers going bad.
- **Frustrations:** Recipes are built for families. Portions are wrong, and he doesn't trust "healthy" labels.
- **Illustrative quote:** "I'll cook anything if someone tells me exactly how much to buy and what it's doing for me."

**A footer for the slide:** *"Hypotheses based on market analysis. Validating through customer interviews, with 5 conducted to date."* Only include the second sentence if it's true.

**Making it true by Thursday:** You have a few days, which is enough for 5 to 8 short interviews. Ask friends of friends who match these profiles for 15-minute calls. Ask about their last three weeknight dinners, what they ordered or cooked, what annoyed them, and what they've tried before. Then swap in real quotes, even one or two per persona. A slide that says "we talked to 6 people and here's what surprised us" is stronger than polished invented personas.
