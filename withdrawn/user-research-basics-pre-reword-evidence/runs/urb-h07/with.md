**The numbers**

- A standard two-proportion test gives p ≈ 0.4. If the label made no difference at all, a gap this big or bigger would appear about 4 times in 10 from chance alone.
- The 95% confidence interval on the lift runs from about -0.7 to +1.7 percentage points. B could be worse than A, or much better.

**Other problems**
- **Two days isn't a full cycle.** Ticketing traffic swings with weekends, on-sale announcements and event dates. A test should cover whole weeks, and Monday to Wednesday doesn't.
- **Peeking inflates false positives.** If you stop the test because B is ahead, the odds of a false win go up.
- **Check that the metric was chosen beforehand.** If purchase rate was the pre-agreed metric, good. If it was picked after seeing the results, discount it further.

1. **Ship B anyway if you like it.** A label change is cheap to reverse and B isn't visibly worse. Call it "shipping B, results inconclusive so far," not "B won."
