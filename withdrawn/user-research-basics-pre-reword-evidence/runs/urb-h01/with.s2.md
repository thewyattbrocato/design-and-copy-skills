# Plan: pet-insurance add-on, two weeks, no budget

**Reframe for the VP.** In two weeks you can't prove people will pay. Asking "would you buy this?" is the one thing that won't work, because people say yes and then don't. You can answer these questions instead:

1. Do bad events happen during bookings often and expensively enough that owners worry about them?
2. What do owners do today when one happens?
3. Is checkout the moment they'd care?

Your recommendation at the planning meeting would be one of three: pursue, reshape, or drop.

*Assumptions: you have booking and support data, and you can email or message past owners and sitters in-app.*

## Week 1: existing evidence, then conversations

**Days 1–3: mine what you have (about 0 designer hours).**
- Support tickets, claims, and disputes mentioning injury, illness, vet bills, damage, or "who pays." Count them as n of N tickets over a stated period. Note that tickets only come from people who write in.
- Bookings: how many involve puppies, seniors, or multi-night stays? Those are likely the higher-risk segments.
- Any existing sitter-protection or guarantee policy. Insurance may overlap with it.
- Reviews and sitter chat for the same themes.

**Days 2–7: 6–8 owner conversations, 30 minutes each, plus 2–3 sitters.**
- **Owners:** recruit past bookers from the last 3 months. Mix people with a recent overnight booking, people with a puppy or senior dog, and at least 2 who booked once and left. Offer a thank-you credit from the marketing or promo budget if you can, and ask the VP for that small exception.
- **Sitters:** they see what goes wrong, and they'd bear the friction of claims.
- **Core questions:** ask about the last real booking.
  - "Tell me about the last time you left your dog with someone."
  - "Did anything go wrong, or almost go wrong? What happened next?"
  - "Who paid, and how did that feel?"
  - "What do you do today for vet costs?" (Their own pet insurance is a key signal.)
  - Never mention the add-on or ask about wants. Show it at the very end, if at all.
- **Designer (3h):** sit in as note-taker on 3–4 sessions. Don't have them facilitate.

## Week 2: a behavioral check, then the memo

**Optional fake-door test (designer 3h, plus an engineer for a few hours).** Add a checkout line like "Add pet protection, learn more." Clicking opens a short "not available yet, tell us what worries you" page. Check these first:
- You have enough checkout traffic to see something. A few hundred sessions is a lead, not a result.
- Legal is fine with it. Insurance is regulated, so don't display a price or imply real coverage you can't deliver.
- Fix the metric before you start (click-through from checkout). Run it for at least a full week.

If traffic or legal rules this out, skip it and say so.

**Memo, one page, for the planning meeting.**
- Evidence by source, all as n of N, with each source's bias.
- What owners do today when something goes wrong.
- Which segments, if any, show real pain.
- What's still unknown.
- A recommendation tied to what you found.

## Say this before the meeting

- **Not a pricing test.** Willingness to pay and the right price are unanswered after this. Answering them needs a prototype with real price points, or a partner pilot.
- **Regulation and a partner.** You need a licensed insurer or MGA, and likely licensing review. That may kill or delay the idea regardless of demand. Ask the VP now who owns that question, because it can run in parallel.
- **Small sample.** 8–11 conversations are directional. Report "5 of 8 described X," never percentages.

## What would change the plan

- **Pursue:** incidents are frequent in your data, owners describe real out-of-pocket pain, and few have coverage.
- **Reshape:** the pain is real but sits with sitters or sits in a trust guarantee, not owner-paid insurance.
- **Drop or defer:** incidents are rare, and owners say your platform's existing protection or their own pet insurance covers it.

**What a human must do:** pull the ticket and booking data, recruit and schedule the people, get recording consent, run the sessions, and clear the fake-door test with legal.
