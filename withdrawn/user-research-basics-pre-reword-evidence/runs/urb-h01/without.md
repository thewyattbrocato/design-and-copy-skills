Two weeks is enough to find out whether the idea has a fatal flaw. It isn't enough to prove demand, so I'd tell your VP that now. Frame the meeting as "here's what we learned and what we'd test next," not "validated."

**Day 1: pin down the decision**
- Ask your VP what the planning meeting needs to decide. Is it fund a pilot, or build it?
- Write down what result would make you say go, and what would make you say stop. For example, "fake-door take rate above 5% and at least 4 of 10 interviewees describe a real past incident." Set these before you see any data.
- Clarify what "pet insurance" means here. Vet-cost coverage, sitter-caused damage or incident protection, and cancellation protection are three very different products.

**Week 1: free evidence you already have**
- **Incident base rate:** Search support tickets and sitter reports for vet visits, injuries, escapes, and disputes over who pays. If incidents are rare, the value is mostly peace of mind, and that's a weaker pitch. If they're common and messy, that's a strong signal.
- **Checkout data:** Look at the average booking value and your current checkout drop-off. An add-on that costs conversion could lose more than it earns.
- **Customer interviews:** Do 8–10 calls of 20 minutes each with recent bookers. Offer a small booking credit, which is cheap even if it isn't a research budget. Add 3–4 sitters, since they see the incidents and carry the liability worry.
  - Ask about past behavior, not hypotheticals. Examples: "Tell me about the last time something went wrong during a stay." "Who paid?" "Do you already have pet insurance?" "What did you do when you booked, to feel safe?"
  - Don't ask "would you buy this?" People say yes and don't follow through.

**Week 2: a behavioral test**
- Spend about 3 of the designer's 6 hours on a checkout mock showing the add-on with a real price and a clear coverage summary. Spend about 1 hour on the interview guide and the rest on revisions.
- Ask engineering for a small fake door. Show it to a slice of real checkouts: "Add coverage for $X. Learn more." A click leads to "Not available yet, join the waitlist." Be upfront that it isn't live. Track the click rate and any change in checkout completion.
- If engineering can't spare the time, send a survey to recent bookers that shows the mock and asks them to pick from several prices. It's weaker evidence, but it still tells you something.

**Run in parallel: feasibility**
- You can't sell insurance without a licensed carrier or an embedded-insurance partner. Contact two or three of them this week and ask about the following:
  - Their commission or margin.
  - Regulatory requirements.
  - Integration effort.
  - Minimum volumes.
- Feasibility is often the bigger risk, and it costs you only a few emails.

**What to bring to the meeting**
- Incident frequency and what customers do today when something goes wrong.
- Fake-door take rate and its effect on checkout conversion, compared against your thresholds.
- Partner economics, estimated as price × take rate × bookings versus the cost of the integration.
- Your recommendation: kill it, run a small pilot, or do more testing. Name the one thing you're still unsure about.

A caveat on the evidence: interviews tell you why people might want it, and the fake door tells you whether they'll act. Lean on the fake door when the two disagree.
