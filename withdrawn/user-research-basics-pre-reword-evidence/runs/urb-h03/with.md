## Revised guide (about 45 min)

**Goal (for the team, not read aloud):** Describe how freelancers handle irregular income and taxes today, and where that breaks down.

**1. Intro and consent (3 min)**
- "Thanks for joining. This is about 45 minutes, a conversation about how you manage money as a freelancer. There are no right answers."
- Say what's recorded, who will see it, and how long it's kept. Say whether anyone else is listening. Ask permission to record. They can skip any question or stop at any time.
- Don't name Ledgerleaf yet.

**2. Warm-up (5 min)**
- "Tell me about the work you do and how long you've been freelancing."
- "Walk me through how money came in over the last month."

**3. Irregular income (10 min)**
- "Tell me about the last time income was lower than you expected. What happened next?"
- "How did you decide what to pay first?"
- Probe: "Say more about that." / "What happened next?"

**4. Taxes (10 min)**
- "Tell me about the last time you paid taxes, or set money aside for them. Walk me through it."
- "How did you work out how much?"
- "What do you use for this now: spreadsheet, accountant, app, nothing?"
- "Tell me about a time that went badly or surprised you."

**5. Tools and spending (8 min)**
- "What have you tried for tracking money? What made you start, and what made you stop?"
- "What do you pay for today to manage your finances or business?" Ask about actual spend, not hypothetical prices.

**6. Invoicing (5 min)**
- "Tell me about the last invoice you sent. What did you do, step by step?"
- "What happened when you waited on a payment?"

**7. Close (3 min)**
- "Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?" Ask permission to follow up.

## What I changed and why

| # | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Leading: "Don't you find it stressful…" supplies the answer. | Ask for the last low-income month as a story (section 3). |
| 3 | "Would you use…" asks for a prediction, and people say yes to be polite. | Ask how they handled taxes last time (section 4). |
| 4 | Asks for a price prediction and anchors on $5/$10/$15. | Ask what they pay for now (section 5). If pricing matters, test it separately. |
| 5 | "Perfect app" gets a wish list, not needs. | Ask what they tried and why they stopped. |

## Notes for tomorrow
- Keep Ledgerleaf out of it until the end, and probably entirely. Naming the sponsor makes people soften what they say.
- Don't fill silences. Follow up using their words, not yours ("tax bucket", "set-aside").
- If you're recruiting friends or other freelancers you know, treat the findings as leads. Write down now what answer would change your plan, so you don't just hear what you hoped for.
- I assumed these are one-on-one conversations with working freelancers. I didn't change your 45-minute length, but this guide is tight for it. If you run over, drop section 6 first.
- Only you can get recording consent, run the sessions, and store the notes. Label participants A, B, C in the notes rather than by name.
