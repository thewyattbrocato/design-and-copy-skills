**Short version:** the guide is friendly, but questions 2–6 mostly ask for opinions and predictions. Those get polite "yes"es that don't tell you what people do. Here is a revised guide.

## Revised guide (about 45 min)

**Goal (for you, not read aloud):** Describe how freelancers handle irregular income, taxes, and invoicing today, and where it goes wrong. That tells you which problem is worth building for first.

**1. Intro and consent (3 min)**
- "Thanks for joining. This is a conversation about how freelancers manage their work and money. There are no right answers, and I'm not selling anything."
- Cover these points: what you'll record, who will see it, how long you'll keep it, and that they can skip any question or stop at any time. Get a clear "yes" to recording before you start.
- Don't mention Ledgerleaf yet.

**2. Warm-up (5 min)**
- "Tell me about your work. What do you do, and how long have you freelanced?"
- "Roughly how many clients did you have in the last few months?"

**3. Irregular income (10 min)**
- "Tell me about the last time money was tight or arrived later than you expected. What happened?" Follow with "What did you do next?"
- "Walk me through how you decided what to spend or save last month."
- "What do you use to keep track of what's coming in and going out?" Then ask what they do when it doesn't match reality.

**4. Taxes (10 min)**
- "Tell me about the last time you paid taxes, or set money aside for them. Walk me through it."
- "How did you work out the amount?" Then "Was there ever a surprise?" and "What happened?"
- "Who or what helped you, such as an accountant, a spreadsheet, or a friend?"

**5. Invoicing and getting paid (10 min)**
- "Tell me about the last invoice you sent. What did you do from start to finish?"
- "Tell me about a time a client paid late. What did you do?"
- "What tools did you use, and what did you do by hand?"

**6. Things they've tried (4 min)**
- "Have you tried any app, spreadsheet, or system for this and then stopped? Tell me about that." Ask what led them to stop.

**7. Close (3 min)**
- "Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?"
- "Can I follow up if I have a question?"

## What I changed and why

- **Q2 ("Don't you find it stressful…?")** is leading, because it supplies the feeling. It's now a request for the last time money was tight. If stress is real, they'll bring it up themselves.
- **Q3 ("Would you use…?")** asks for a prediction. People overstate what they'd do. The tax story in section 4 shows whether they already have this problem and how they handle it.
- **Q4 (price: $5, $10, $15)** has two problems. Hypothetical willingness to pay is unreliable, and your options anchor the answer. Ask what they pay now, or have paid, for tools like this (section 6). Test pricing later with a real offer.
- **Q5 ("perfect budgeting app")** gets a wish list of features, not needs. Their stories in sections 3–5 show the needs. The "tried and stopped" question replaces it.
- **Q6 ("Our app makes invoicing really easy…")** names the product and tells them what to feel, so they'll want to agree. It's now a story about their last invoice. If you need reactions to the real app, run a separate session where they do tasks in it.
- **Q7** is fine, and it's now part of the close.
- **Timing:** the original had no room for stories. Seven questions in 45 minutes works if each is a story. Treat the guide as a checklist, let people wander, and come back to anything you missed.

## Before tomorrow

- **Sample bias:** if tomorrow's participants are friends, colleagues, or people already interested in Ledgerleaf, they'll be kinder than strangers. Note that next to anything you conclude.
- **Screening:** confirm each person freelances, has had irregular income recently, and has dealt with taxes, so you aren't talking to someone who doesn't fit.
- **Notetaker:** if possible, have a second person take notes, and keep them quiet during the session.
- **Write down in advance** what would change your plan. For example: "If most people already have a working tax routine, tax set-asides drop in priority." Then you can't talk yourself out of a result you don't like.
- **Counting:** report results as "3 of 7", not percentages.

This assumes interviews with freelancers, not a live demo. You'll need to do the recruiting, consent, recording, and note storage yourself.
