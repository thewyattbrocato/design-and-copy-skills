Here's a light edit. It keeps your voice and adds a reason to act:

> Last Saturday the rhubarb jam sold out by 10:40, which has never happened before. So I'm making double this week, about 120 jars, though the rhubarb is at the mercy of the weather, so don't hold me to it. If you want one, come early.
>
> The raspberry is still on the shelf if you'd like to try it. I think it's better than the rhubarb, but Jen on the next stall disagrees. Come and settle it.

What I changed:

- **Split the first sentence.** The sellout is your best line, so I ended the sentence on it. The plan for this week now gets its own sentence.
- **Added "come early."** The email had no ask, and the sellout story is what makes that ask believable.
- **Added "Come and settle it."** The Jen line is the most you-sounding part, and this turns it into an invitation. Cut it if it feels too pushy.
- **Kept the weather caveat.** It's honest and it sounds like you. I moved it so it doesn't undercut the sellout news.

If you're happy to hold jars, you could change the ask to "reply and I'll put one aside". That would sell more, but only if you'd actually do it. I didn't add it, since I'd be inventing an offer on your behalf.
