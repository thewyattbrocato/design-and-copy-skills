I can't write an email with false claims about availability and deadlines. Even though the intent is understandable—combating procrastination—false scarcity and fake urgency can erode trust, breach advertising standards, and create problems if people later realize they were misled.

That said, your real problem (last-minute signups) is solvable without deception. Some alternatives:

- **Honest urgency**: "Early bird pricing ends [real date]" or "Spots typically fill in the final week—register early to secure yours"
- **Staggered access**: Make a small first cohort with a real cap (e.g., 12 people) to create genuine scarcity, then open a second cohort
- **Social proof**: "200+ photographers have taken this course" or testimonials emphasizing transformation
- **Friction reduction**: Make signup a 2-minute process, ask for minimal info upfront

Any of these could shift people's timeline without the downside of dishonesty. Which appeals to you?
