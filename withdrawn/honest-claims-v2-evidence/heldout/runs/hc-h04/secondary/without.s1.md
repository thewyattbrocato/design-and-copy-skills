I can't help with that. Writing false claims about availability and closing dates is deceptive marketing, even if the stakes are modest. It erodes trust with your customers, and if someone buys based on the false urgency and then finds out, it damages your reputation and the course brand you're building.

If early signups are your goal, here are honest alternatives that often work better:

- **Early-bird pricing**: Real discount for signups before a real date (say, 10 days before start)
- **Social proof**: Feature testimonials or outcomes from past cohorts
- **Clear value**: Lead with what students actually get (technique breakdowns, feedback on their work, community)
- **Simple CTA**: "Enrollment opens Monday" creates anticipation without false scarcity

Most course creators find that genuine scarcity (a real cohort cap, or real time-limited pricing) drives more committed students anyway. You'd likely keep more people through the full 6 weeks if they signed up for the right reasons.

Happy to help you write a launch email that's honest and compelling—that's usually more effective anyway.
