# Revised interview guide (45 min)

**Goal (for the team, not read aloud):** Learn how freelancers handle irregular income, taxes, and invoicing today, and where it breaks down.

**1. Intro and consent (3 min)**
"Thanks for joining. This is a conversation about how freelancers manage money. It runs about 45 minutes. There are no right answers, and I didn't build anything you need to be nice about. With your permission I'd like to record it for my notes. Only our small team will see it, and we'll keep it [X months]. You can skip any question or stop at any time. Is recording okay?"

**2. Warm-up (5 min)**
- What kind of work do you do, and how long have you been freelancing?
- Roughly how many clients or projects do you have in a typical month?

**3. Topic prompts (30 min)**
1. Tell me about the last time you got paid for a job. What happened to that money over the next few days?
2. Tell me about your most recent slow or uneven month. What did you do? *(Replaces "Don't you find it stressful…". Let them bring up feelings themselves.)*
3. Tell me about the last time you dealt with taxes, whether setting money aside or making a payment. How did you work out the amount? What happened? *(Replaces "Would you use an app that sets aside money for taxes?")*
4. What do you use now to keep track of money? Can you show me, or walk me through the last time you opened it? *(Replaces "What features would you want…". Current behavior and workarounds show the real needs.)*
5. Tell me about the last time you sent an invoice. What happened from sending it to getting paid? *(Replaces "Our app makes invoicing really easy…". It drops the product name and the leading claim.)*
6. Tell me about the last time you paid for, or decided not to pay for, a tool for your business or finances. What led to that, what else did you consider, and what did you end up paying? *(Replaces "$5, $10, $15?". See the note below.)*
7. What's the most recent money surprise or mistake you've had? What happened next?

**4. Close (5 min)**
"Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?" Ask permission to follow up. Thank them and confirm the incentive.

**Probes:** "Say more about that." "What happened next?" "Why was that?" Then pause and don't fill the silence.

## What I changed and why
- **Q2, Q3, Q4, Q5** asked for opinions, predictions, or wants. Stated willingness to use or pay is unreliable, so these became "last time" stories.
- **Q4** also anchored people on $5–$15. A price range needs a different method later, such as a pricing test once you have a concept. Interviews can show what people already pay for.
- **Q6** names your product and tells people it's easy, which invites politeness. Keep Ledgerleaf out of the session unless you're deliberately showing a prototype, and then make that a separate, task-based section.
- **Q1** had no consent or recording ask, and no warm-up.
- **Q7** was fine. I kept it as the close.

- **Assumption:** these are exploratory interviews with current freelancers, not a concept test.
- **For you to do:** confirm each participant did freelance paid work in the last month (not just that they say they're a freelancer). Get recording consent before you start. Take notes under participant codes, not names.
