Your guide has a few problems that will skew what you learn. Most of them come from questions that lead the witness or ask about hypotheticals.

**Main problems**

- **Q2 ("Don't you find it stressful...?")** is leading. It tells people what answer you expect, and most will agree to be polite. Try: *"Walk me through the last time your income changed month to month. What did you do?"*
- **Q3 ("Would you use an app that...?")** is a hypothetical yes/no. People say yes to things they'll never use. Ask about past behavior instead: *"How do you handle setting aside money for taxes today? Tell me about the last quarterly payment."* If they've built a spreadsheet or hacked something together, that's strong evidence of real need.
- **Q4 (pricing)** anchors them on $5–$15 and assumes they'd pay at all. Stated willingness to pay is unreliable at best. Ask what they pay for now (tools, accountant, software) and what they've tried and abandoned. If you do want a price signal, ask open-ended: *"At what price would this feel too expensive? So cheap you'd doubt it?"*
- **Q5 ("perfect budgeting app")** gets you a wishlist of features people won't use. Ground it in current workflow: *"What's the most annoying part of managing your money right now?"*
- **Q6 ("Our app makes invoicing really easy. How do you feel about that?")** is a pitch, not a question, and it fishes for compliments. Ask about their current invoicing experience first: *"Tell me about the last time you sent an invoice or chased a payment."* Save any concept reactions for a separate, clearly framed section.

**Structural gaps**

- **No warm-up or context.** Start with who they are: what they do, how long they've freelanced, and how their income arrives (clients, retainers, platforms). You'll want this to segment your answers later.
- **No current-tools question.** "What do you use today, and what do you like or hate about it?" is often the most valuable question in the whole interview.
- **No consent or recording note** in the intro. Ask permission to record, and say there are no wrong answers and that you're not selling anything.
- **Not enough "tell me about the last time..."** prompts. Concrete stories beat opinions.
- **No planned follow-ups.** Add probes like "Why?", "Can you give an example?", and "What happened next?"

**Suggested flow for 45 minutes**

1. Intro and consent (3 min)
2. Background and income pattern (7 min)
3. Current money management: tools, taxes, pain points, with last-time stories (15 min)
4. Invoicing and getting paid (7 min)
5. Past attempts and switching: what they've tried, why they stopped (5 min)
6. Optional concept reaction to Ledgerleaf, after the unbiased material (5 min)
7. Wrap-up: "Anything I should have asked?" and "Who else should I talk to?" (3 min)

**Smaller notes**

- Q7 is fine. "Is there anything I didn't ask that I should have?" tends to get slightly better answers.
- Q1 covers logistics only. Fold it into a proper intro.
- Seven questions in 45 minutes is thin, which is fine, since good interviews are mostly follow-ups. Just make sure you have probes ready.
