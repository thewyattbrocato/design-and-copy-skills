Most of the guide needs rewriting. Questions 2–6 each lead the participant, ask them to predict, or both.

**What's wrong**
- **Q2** ("Don't you find it stressful…") is leading. It tells them the answer.
- **Q3** ("Would you use…") and **Q4** ("How much would you pay… $5, $10, $15") ask for predictions. People are poor at forecasting their own behavior, and the price options anchor them.
- **Q5** ("perfect budgeting app") asks what they want. You'd get a wish list, not evidence of a problem.
- **Q6** names your product and tells them it's "really easy". That's the most leading question in the guide, and it's a pitch.
- **Q1** has no consent. You need recording permission, who is listening, and that they can skip or stop.
- There's no warm-up. The guide also asks for no stories about real past behavior, and that's where the useful data is.

**Revised guide (45 min)**

*Goal (for you, not read aloud): learn how freelancers handle irregular income and taxes today, and where it breaks.*

1. **Intro and consent.** "Thanks for joining. This is about 45 minutes. I'm trying to learn how freelancers manage their money. There are no right answers, and I'm not selling anything. With your permission I'd like to record it for my notes. Only our team will see it, and I'll delete it after [retention period]. You can skip any question or stop anytime. Is recording okay?"
2. **Warm-up.** "Tell me about your work. What do you do, and how long have you freelanced?"
3. **Income.** "Tell me about the last time money came in a lot different from what you expected. What happened? What did you do next?"
4. **Day to day.** "Walk me through how you decided what you could spend last month." Probes: What did you look at? What tools or notes? What was hard?
5. **Taxes.** "Tell me about the last time you dealt with taxes, whether a quarterly payment or filing. How did you know how much to set aside? What happened?" Probe: "Was there a time you were short or surprised?"
6. **Current tools.** "What do you use now to track money, invoices, or taxes? How did you pick it? What have you tried and dropped?"
7. **Invoicing.** "Tell me about the last time you invoiced a client. Walk me through it from start to getting paid." Probes: What took the longest? What went wrong?
8. **Spending already.** "What do you currently pay for to manage your work or money (software, an accountant, anything)? How did you decide to pay for it?"
9. **Close.** "Is there anything I should have asked and didn't? Can I follow up if I have a question?"

**Mapping to your original questions**
- Q2 (stress) is covered by Q3 and Q4 above. If stress is real, it will come up in their stories, in their words.
- Q3 (auto tax set-aside) is covered by the tax story. If people improvise ways to do this today, that's your evidence.
- Q4 (price) is covered by Q8. Past spending is a far better signal than a stated price. Willingness to pay is hard to learn from interviews at all, so don't treat any answer as a price point.
- Q5 (features) is dropped. Derive features from the problems you hear.
- Q6 (invoicing) now asks for a story instead of a reaction.

**Running it tomorrow**
- Don't name Ledgerleaf until the end, if at all. Use the person's own words when you probe, not "budgeting" or "tax set-aside" unless they say it first.
- Wait through silences. "Say more about that" and "what happened next" are your main probes.
- Treat counts as "3 of 7", not percentages.
- If participants are friends or came from your own network, expect them to be kinder than strangers would be, and note that in your findings.
