The guide's main problem is that most of the questions are leading or hypothetical. People will mostly say "yes, that sounds great," and you won't learn much from that.

## Question by question

**1. Opener.** Add a few things:
- Ask permission to record.
- Say there are no right answers.
- Say you're not selling anything and honest criticism helps.
- Start with a warm-up, such as "Tell me about your work and how long you've freelanced."

**2. "Don't you find it stressful...?"** This is leading and a yes/no question. It assumes the pain exists. Ask for a story instead: *"Walk me through the last time your income was uneven. What happened, and what did you do?"*

**3. "Would you use an app that automatically sets aside money for taxes?"** This is hypothetical and leading, and people overpredict their own future behavior. Ask about what they do now: *"How do you handle taxes today? Tell me about the last time you had to make a payment."* Then follow up on what's annoying, what they've tried, and what they've abandoned.

**4. Pricing ($5, $10, $15).** This anchors them on your numbers, and stated willingness to pay is unreliable. Ask what they spend now:
- *"What do you currently pay for tools, an accountant, or a bookkeeper?"*
- *"Have you ever paid for something like this and stopped? Why?"*

If you still want price sensitivity, ask it open-ended late in the session: *"At what price would this feel too expensive? So cheap you'd doubt it?"*

**5. "Perfect budgeting app" features.** This invites a wish list, and users are bad at designing solutions. Ask about workarounds instead: *"What do you use today (spreadsheets, apps, notebooks)? What do you like and hate about it?"*

**6. "Our app makes invoicing really easy. How do you feel?"** This is a pitch, and the answer will be polite. Ask about their current behavior: *"How do you invoice now? Tell me about the last time a client paid late."* If you want feedback on your invoicing feature, save it for the end and show a prototype.

**7. "Anything else?"** This is fine. Better: *"What should I have asked that I didn't?"* Also ask whether they know other freelancers you could talk to.

## What's missing
- **Screener and context:** type of work, income variability, how long they've freelanced, whether they pay quarterly taxes. You want a mix of people, not only enthusiasts.
- **Trigger moments:** *"When did you last go looking for a better way to manage money? What prompted it?"*
- **Probes:** write down "Tell me more," "Why?" and "Can you give me an example?" so you use them. Seven questions for 45 minutes is fine if you probe.
- **A rough time plan:** about 5 minutes of intro, 10 on background, 20 on past behavior and pain, 5 on a concept or price reaction, and 5 to wrap up.

## Suggested revised flow
1. Intro, consent, and expectations
2. Background and warm-up
3. Walk me through your last month of income and expenses
4. How do you handle taxes today? Last payment story
5. Current tools and workarounds, including what you've abandoned
6. How do you invoice and get paid? Last late payment story
7. What do you pay now for help with money?
8. (Optional) Show a concept and ask for reactions and price expectations
9. What should I have asked? Referrals?
