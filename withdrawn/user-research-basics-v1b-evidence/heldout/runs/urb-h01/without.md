The first move is to reframe "validate" with your VP. In two weeks with no budget you can't prove the add-on will make money. You can find out whether there's a credible reason to pursue it, and what the cheapest next step is. Agree on that with the VP up front, along with what evidence would make them say go, no-go, or pilot.

**Day 1–2: Pin down the question**
- **Which product is it?** Insurance for the dog's vet bills (a Trupanion-style policy) and coverage for injury or damage during a stay are very different. They differ in who the buyer is, the price, and the regulatory path. Ask the VP what they picture, and check what protection you already offer.
- **Who underwrites it?** Selling insurance usually means a licensed partner, not something you build. Find out early whether partners exist and what the rev share looks like. This is often the real feasibility question, and it costs nothing to look into.

**Week 1: Use what you already have**
- **Mine your data (free, fast).**
  - Support tickets and incident reports about injuries, vet visits, and damage.
  - Booking value and frequency.
  - Checkout funnel drop-off.
  - Any existing refund or guarantee costs.
  
  This shows how often the problem happens and how much it costs.
- **Do 6–8 customer interviews, 20 minutes each.** Recruit recent bookers by email, and offer a booking credit as the incentive. Add 3–4 sitters, since they'll tell you about incidents and whether it would affect how they're booked. You run these, not the designer. Ask about past behavior ("Tell me about the last time your dog got hurt or sick while you were away. What happened? What did it cost?"). Don't ask "would you buy this?" People say yes to that and don't follow through.
- **Take 1–2 calls with embedded-insurance partners or competitors' offerings.** You want terms, pricing ranges, and typical attach rates.

**Week 2: One behavioral test**
- Spend the designer's ~6 hours on a single, simple add-on mock with a realistic price. Don't build high-fidelity variants.
- Run it as a **fake door**: show the add-on to a small slice of traffic, and when someone opts in, show "coming soon, want to be notified?" Put it on the booking confirmation page or in a post-booking email rather than in the middle of checkout, so you don't hurt conversion or trust on real transactions. If you do test at checkout, keep it to a small percentage.
- Measure opt-in rate and compare it to attach rates from your partner calls.

**What you hand the VP: a 1–2 page memo**
1. What you learned: incident frequency and cost, interview themes, fake-door numbers, partner terms.
2. What you still don't know: real willingness to pay, claims experience, and effect on conversion.
3. A recommendation, probably "run a small partner-backed pilot" or "don't, because of X."

**Be upfront about the limits.** Interest isn't purchase, and a fake door overstates demand. Eight interviews won't give statistical confidence. Don't present this as proof. Present it as enough evidence to decide whether a pilot is worth running.
