**Short version:** two weeks and no budget won't prove demand. You can find out whether the problem is real and frequent enough to justify a test, and you can bring the meeting a recommendation instead of an opinion poll.

I'm assuming you have booking, support and checkout data, and that nobody has done research on this yet.

## The decision and the result that would change it
The meeting decides whether to spend months building and licensing an insurance add-on. A wrong guess is expensive because insurance usually needs a licensed underwriting partner and legal review, not just a checkbox.

Reframe "validate it" as three questions:
1. **Is there a real, recurring problem?** Do pets get hurt or sick during stays, and who pays when they do?
2. **Is checkout the right moment, and are we the right seller?** Do owners already have pet insurance? Does our existing guarantee or policy already cover this?
3. **Will people pay at checkout?** This is the only one you can't answer in two weeks by asking.

If the evidence shows few incidents and most owners already insured, recommend not building. If incidents are frequent and costly, recommend a fake-door test.

## Week 1: use what you already have (about 2 days of your time, no designer)
- **Support tickets and sitter reports:** Count incidents involving injury, illness, vet visits, or disputes over who pays. Report them as "X of Y tickets in period Z", and note the bias that tickets only come from people who write in.
- **Current coverage:** What does Kestrel already offer or promise (guarantee, host protection)? A gap or overlap here shapes the whole idea.
- **Booking data:** Average booking value, repeat rate, and how many bookings are multi-day or involve older or special-needs dogs. Those are the likely buyers.
- **Checkout analytics:** Where people drop off. Every add-on competes with conversion, so know your baseline.
- **Legal and partner feasibility:** Spend an hour with whoever handles legal or partnerships. Which insurers sell embedded cover to marketplaces, and what is the revenue share? If it's infeasible, the rest doesn't matter.

## Weeks 1–2: 6 to 8 short conversations (about 30 minutes each)
**Who:** Owners who booked a sitter in the last 60 days, mixed across multi-day vs. short bookings and older vs. younger dogs. Include 2 or 3 who had a health scare or incident. Recruit from your own booking list by email, offering a small credit if you can, since a credit may not count as research budget. Also talk to 2 or 3 sitters about what happens when a dog gets hurt.

**Ask about the last time, not the future:**
- "Tell me about the last time your dog was sick or hurt. What happened, and who paid?"
- "How do you pay for vet costs today? What do you have set up?"
- "Think about the last booking. Was anything about the dog being away from you a worry? What did you do about it?"
- For incident cases: "What happened with the sitter and the platform?"

**Don't ask:** "Would you buy insurance?" or "Would you like this at checkout?" People say yes and don't pay. Don't mention the idea until the end, if at all.

**Your designer's 6 hours:** Have them help run or co-note 2 or 3 sessions (a designer shouldn't be the sole facilitator on a design they'd later build), and spend the rest on a simple mock of the checkout add-on for the fake-door test.

## The only honest demand test: a fake door
If week 1 shows a real problem, propose a live test to the VP: a checkout line like "Pet cost protection: coming soon, join the waitlist" with clear, honest wording, with no charge and no claim that it exists. Pick one metric before you start (click or waitlist rate), run it for whole weeks, and treat low traffic as a lead rather than a result. You won't finish it in two weeks, but you can have it designed and approved for the meeting.

## What to bring to the planning meeting
A one-page summary:
- Incident counts from tickets, with the sample bias.
- "N of 8 owners" and "N of 3 sitters" findings, never percentages.
- Existing coverage and the feasibility answer.
- A recommendation: **stop**, **run the fake-door test**, or **proceed to a partner conversation**, plus what's still unknown.
