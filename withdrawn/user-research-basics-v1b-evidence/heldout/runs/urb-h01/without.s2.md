Two weeks and no budget won't get you full validation, but it's enough to learn whether customers care, and that's what the planning meeting needs. Reframe it with your VP now: "I'll bring evidence on demand and willingness to pay, plus a list of what's still unknown (partner, pricing, regulation)."

## Week 1: mine what you already have (about 3 days)
- **Support tickets and incident data.** Search for vet visits, injuries, illness, and "who pays" disputes. Count how often they happen per 1,000 bookings and what they cost. This tells you whether the problem is real and how big it is.
- **Checkout funnel.** Look at conversion, average booking value, and repeat rate. An add-on at 8–15% of booking value is a very different ask on a $40 stay than on a $400 one.
- **Check what you already offer.** If Kestrel has a guarantee or sitter-liability coverage, customers may think they're already protected. Your product might then be a gap-filler, or redundant.

## Week 1–2: talk to 6–8 people (about 5 hours total)
- Recruit from recent bookers by email, with a $0 incentive like early access or a thank-you credit. Include 2–3 owners who had an incident, and 1–2 sitters. Sitters will tell you how an incident actually plays out.
- Run 20-minute calls, with you leading and the designer taking notes. Ask about behavior, not the idea:
  - "Tell me about the last time your dog was hurt or sick while you were away."
  - "What did you do and what did it cost?"
  - "Do you have pet insurance now? Why or why not?"
  - Show the concept only at the end.
- Stated intent ("sure, I'd buy that") is weak evidence. Past behavior is strong evidence.

## Week 2: fake-door test (designer's main 3–6 hours)
- Add a checkout line like "Add pet protection for $X" for a small slice of traffic. Clicking leads to a "Coming soon, want early access?" screen with an email capture.
- Test two price points if you have the volume.
- **Be honest on the follow-up screen.** Don't take payment or imply coverage exists. Insurance is regulated, so check with legal before launching even a fake door.
- If traffic is thin, send a one-question email or in-app survey to recent bookers instead.

## Set thresholds before you look
For example: "If more than 5% click and more than 3 of 8 interviewees describe a real cost incident, we proceed to partner exploration. If under 2%, we don't." Without this, any result will get read as a yes.

## What to bring to the meeting
1. Incident frequency and cost, from your data.
2. Interview themes, with quotes.
3. Fake-door click rate by price.
4. **Open risks you didn't validate:** underwriting partner (you'll almost certainly need a licensed insurer or MGA), margin, claims handling, and regulatory requirements.
5. A recommendation: proceed to a partner conversation, run a bigger test, or drop it.

Insurance is hard to run well. The expensive part is the partner and claims operation, not the checkout button. The strongest result you can honestly bring in two weeks is "customers do or don't want this, at roughly this price." The VP then decides whether to fund the harder questions.
