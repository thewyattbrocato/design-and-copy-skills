# Screener: Front desk work in small clinics

**Format:** Online form, about 3 minutes. Describe it to people as "a 30-minute video chat about how front desks run day to day." Don't mention an app, queues or no-shows in the invitation.

**Invitation text**
> We're talking with people who work at clinic front desks about how their day runs. It's a 30-minute video call, and you'll receive [incentive] as thanks. A few quick questions first to see if it's a fit.

**Questions**

1. **What best describes your main work setting?** *(single choice)*
   - Clinic or medical practice in a town or rural area → continue
   - Clinic in a mid-size or large city → *end*
   - Hospital department → *end*
   - Pharmacy, dental office or veterinary office → *end*
   - Other → *end*

2. **In the past month, which of these did you personally do at work?** *(select all)*
   - Check patients in at the front desk
   - Answer the phone and book appointments
   - Handle billing or insurance questions
   - Order office supplies
   - Call patients to confirm or reschedule appointments
   - Manage the schedule for a provider
   - Train new staff
   - Take clinical notes or do procedures
   - *Must select "check patients in," "answer the phone and book appointments" and "manage the schedule." Otherwise, end.*

3. **How many days a week do you usually work the front desk?** *(single choice: 1–2 / 3–4 / 5 or more)*
   - *Target is 3 or more. End if 1–2.*

4. **Think about the last week you worked. Did patients ever come in without an appointment?** *(single choice)*
   - Yes, most days → *qualifies; quota A*
   - Yes, a few times → *qualifies; quota B*
   - No → *end*

5. **In the last month, did a patient miss an appointment without calling?** *(single choice: Yes, several / Yes, once or twice / No)*
   - *Must be "Yes." Otherwise, end.*

6. **How do you keep track of who is waiting and who is booked today?** *(select all)*
   - Paper schedule or sign-in sheet
   - Whiteboard or sticky notes
   - Scheduling or records software
   - Spreadsheet
   - Memory or verbal handoffs
   - Other: ____
   - *Record this for sampling. It isn't a pass/fail question.*

7. **Open response (required):** *"Tell me about the last time the front desk got really busy. What was going on, and what did you do?"* (2–4 sentences)
   - *Screen out one-word or vague answers and answers that don't describe a real event.*

8. **Do you, or does anyone in your household, work in any of the following?** *(select all)*
   - Software or app design/development
   - Healthcare software sales or support
   - Market or user research
   - Marketing or advertising
   - None of these
   - *End if any are selected except "None."*

9. **Have you taken part in a paid research study or interview in the past 6 months?** *(Yes / No)*
   - *End if Yes.*

10. **Can you join a 30-minute video call with working audio between [date range] on a computer or tablet?** *(Yes / No)*
    - *End if No.*

11. **Contact details:** first name, email, phone, best days and times, time zone.

**Quotas (targets, not guarantees)**
- 6 participants in total.
- At least 2 from quota A (walk-ins most days) and 2 from quota B.
- At least 2 who use paper or whiteboard methods and at least 2 who use software.
- Spread across at least 4 different clinics. No more than 1 person per clinic.

**Who this leaves out:** urban clinics, hospital departments, clinicians who don't run the desk, and desks that never get walk-ins or no-shows. It also leaves out people who can't make a video call. Rural desks with poor connectivity are therefore likely underrepresented, so offer a phone-call option if you can. State these limits alongside any conclusions.

**Housekeeping**
- Anyone who completes the call gets the incentive, even if you later set their data aside.
- At the start of each call, get explicit consent before recording. Say who is watching, how long files are kept, and that they can skip any question or stop.
- Ask people not to share patient names or details. Code participants P1–P6 in notes.

---
- **I can't recruit anyone.** I can't contact people or post to groups, and I won't invent participants. A person on your team has to send this out. Good places to start are your own contacts at clinics, state or regional rural health associations, medical office manager networks, and any existing pilot or waitlist contacts. Confirm with those groups first whether they allow recruiting posts.
- **Timeline risk:** Rural front-desk staff are hard to reach and the busy-season schedule is tight. If you're behind by mid-week, relax one quota (for example, the software versus paper mix) before cutting the sample, and book the rest of the sessions a week later.

I can also draft the outreach message, the interview guide or the consent text if you want them.
