# Screener: front desk staff at rural clinics

**Study framing (shown to candidates):** "A 30-minute remote conversation about how front desks run their day. You'll get [incentive: to set]. No preparation is needed."

**Recruiting note (not shown):** Don't name the product or say "queue" or "no-show" in the invitation.

## Questions

**1. Which of these did you do at work in the past two weeks? (Select all.)**
- Answered the phone or checked in people at a front desk or reception → *must select*
- Ordered or restocked supplies
- Handled billing or insurance claims
- Did clinical care (exam, procedure, medication) → *if this is the only role, end*
- Scheduled or rescheduled appointments → *must select*
- Managed staff schedules or payroll
- None of these → *end*

**2. Where do you work?** (Select one.)
- Clinic or practice in a town or area I'd describe as rural or small-town → *qualify*
- Clinic in a mid-size city or suburb → *end*
- Clinic in a large city → *end*
- Hospital front desk or ER → *end*
- Other → *end*

**3. In a typical week, about how many patients are at your desk without an appointment?**
- None → *end*
- A few → *qualify*
- Several each day → *qualify*
- Most of our patients → *qualify*

**4. In the last month, how many times did a scheduled patient not show up?**
- Never → *end*
- Once or twice → *qualify*
- Several times → *qualify*
- Most days → *qualify*

**5. What do you use today to keep track of who is waiting and who is booked?** (Select all.)
- Paper list or sign-in sheet
- Whiteboard or wall calendar
- Practice management or EHR software
- Spreadsheet
- Memory or sticky notes
- Something else (please describe)
- *Record the answer. Don't screen on it, but aim for a mix.*

**6. How many hours a week are you at the front desk?**
- Under 10 → *end*
- 10 or more → *qualify*

**7. Do you, or does a close family member, work for a company that makes scheduling, patient-intake, or clinic software, or work in design, market research, or marketing?**
- Yes → *end*
- No → *qualify*

**8. Have you taken part in a paid research study in the past 3 months?**
- Yes → *end*
- No → *qualify*

**9. (Open, 2–3 sentences.) Think of the last time the desk got busy or things went off-plan. What happened?**
- *Screen out one-word, vague, or copy-paste answers. Advance people who describe a specific event.*

**10. Contact details**
- First name, email or phone, best days and times, and time zone.
- Confirm they can join by video or phone from somewhere private, not while driving.

## Targets (not guarantees)

- 6 participants. Soft quotas:
  - At least 2 who use paper, a whiteboard, or a spreadsheet.
  - At least 2 who use practice management or EHR software.
  - Mixed clinic sizes.
- Recruit 8–10 qualified candidates to allow for dropouts.
- Anyone who joins and turns out not to fit still gets the incentive. Set their data aside.

## Who this leaves out

- Clinic managers who don't staff the desk.
- Hospital and urban staff.
- Anyone who won't answer a screener, which probably favors people with more time and more comfort with technology.
- If you recruit mainly through one channel, such as a single association, your conclusions will reflect that channel's members.

## What you or a teammate must do

I can't recruit people. I have no way to contact anyone, and sending outreach in your name should be your decision. These steps need a human:

1. **Pick channels.** Good options are:
   - Your own pilot or contact clinics.
   - State rural health associations or Primary Care Associations.
   - Medical Group Management Association chapters.
   - Rural health centers' office managers, who can pass the invitation to desk staff.
   - Rural-health Facebook or LinkedIn groups, with the moderator's permission.
   - A paid panel with healthcare-role filters, if you have budget.
2. **Post the invitation** and link the screener (a form tool works).
3. **Set the incentive**, then schedule, send reminders, and obtain recording consent.
4. **Tell participants not to share patient names or other identifying details** during sessions. Staff should describe situations generically.
5. **Check the date.** Six sessions in about a week and a half is tight for this audience, because clinic staff are hard to reach during business hours. Fallbacks are to book 4 sessions by Friday and 2 the following week, or to relax the "10+ hours at the desk" criterion. Tell me which you prefer.

I can also draft the invitation text, the consent script, or the interview guide next. Say which one you'd like.
