## Screener: Rural Clinic Front Desk Study

**Intro (shown at top)**
> We're researching how front desk staff at rural clinics handle walk-ins and missed appointments. We're looking for people to join a 30-minute video call. You'll receive [$X incentive] as thanks. This form takes about 3 minutes. Your answers are used only to decide eligibility for this study.

**Questions**

1. **Which best describes your current role?** *(single select)*
   - Front desk / reception / patient registration at a clinic → continue
   - Office or practice manager who also works the front desk → continue
   - Clinical staff (nurse, MA, provider) → screen out
   - Other / not currently working in a clinic → screen out

2. **How would you describe the area your clinic serves?** *(single select)*
   - Rural or small town (e.g., fewer than ~50,000 people, long drives to other care) → continue
   - Suburban or urban → screen out
   - Not sure → follow up on city/county (see Q10)

3. **How long have you worked at a clinic front desk?**
   - Under 6 months → screen out
   - 6–12 months / 1–3 years / 3+ years → continue (aim for a mix)

4. **How does your clinic handle walk-ins?** *(single select)*
   - We regularly accept walk-ins → continue (prioritize)
   - Occasionally → continue
   - Never → screen out

5. **In a typical week, roughly how many appointments are missed (no-shows)?**
   - 0–2 / 3–10 / 11+ → continue (aim for a mix; deprioritize 0–2)

6. **What do you currently use to track the walk-in line and no-shows?** *(multi-select)*
   - Paper list or whiteboard / EHR scheduling module / Spreadsheet / Separate queue or text-reminder software / Nothing formal / Other: ___
   - *(Used for variety, not screening out.)*

7. **Do you personally handle check-in, scheduling, and/or calling patients about missed appointments?**
   - Yes, daily → continue
   - Sometimes → continue
   - No → screen out

8. **Can you join a 30-minute video call on a laptop, tablet, or phone with a stable connection?** *(Yes / Yes, but connection is unreliable / No)*
   - "No" → screen out. "Unreliable" → offer a phone-call option.

9. **Are you able to participate during work hours, or would you need evenings or early mornings?** *(open text, used for scheduling)*

10. **Open-ended:** Tell us about the last time a walk-in or a no-show caused a problem at your desk. *(2–3 sentences)*
    - Used to filter out low-effort or bot responses. Look for specific, concrete detail.

11. **Contact:** First name, email, phone (optional), clinic city/state, and best times.

**Disqualifiers and conflicts (add as a hidden check or a Q12):**
- Anyone who works for a health-tech or scheduling-software company
- Anyone who has done a paid research study in the last 3 months
- Your own clinic contacts, to avoid biased answers

**Target mix for 6 participants:** 2–3 high-walk-in clinics, a range of tenure, and at least 2 different tools or paper-based workflows. Over-recruit to 8–9 to cover no-shows, which is fitting for this study.

## Where to find them by Nov 13

1. **Your own network first.** Ask colleagues and advisors for warm intros to rural clinic managers. This is the fastest route.
2. **State rural health associations and Offices of Rural Health.** Many have member newsletters or contact lists, and a short email to the program coordinator often works.
3. **Community health center networks and Primary Care Associations.** These are good for front desk staff in particular.
4. **Medical Group Management Association (MGMA)** and regional chapters. Members include practice managers who can forward to their front desk staff.
5. **LinkedIn and Facebook groups** for medical receptionists, patient access staff, and medical office administration. Check each group's rules before posting.
6. **A recruiting panel** (User Interviews, Respondent). This costs more, but it's fast, and you can filter on role and location.
