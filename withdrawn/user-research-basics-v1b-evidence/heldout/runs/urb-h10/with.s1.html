Kayak Renter Interview Guide

Goal (for you, not read aloud): Learn how people actually find, book, and show up for a kayak rental, and where it goes wrong. Ask about the last real time, not opinions. Don't pitch or name the booking tool. Say "a study about renting outdoor gear."
0-4 Intro
4-8 Warm-up
8-22 Last rental
22-30 Rough spots
30-35 Choosing
35-40 Close

1. Intro + consent 0-4 min

"Thanks for doing this. I'm learning how people rent kayaks and gear. There are no right answers, and I'm not testing you. You can skip anything or stop any time."

  • Ask: "Is it OK if I record audio so I can focus on listening?" Say who will hear it and how long you'll keep it.
  • Say if anyone else is listening in.
  • Start recording only after a clear yes. Notes use the participant code, not their name.
  • Driving or distracted? Offer to reschedule.

2. Warm-up 4-8 min

"Tell me about the paddling you've done in the last few months."

  • Where, with whom, what kind of water?
  • Own a boat, rent, or both? (Note what they rent from whom.)
  • Say more about that.

Facts here are context. Don't dig yet.

3. The last rental, start to finish 8-22 min  |  the core, protect this time

"Tell me about the last time you rented a kayak. Start from when you first thought about going."

Then follow their story with: "What happened next?" and "Why was that?" Cover these only if they don't come up on their own:

  • How did you find that outfitter?
  • What did you need to know before you committed? Where did you look?
  • How did you reserve? (phone, text, walk-up, site, other)
  • Who else was involved? Who handled the booking and payment?
  • What did you pick: day, time, boat type, number of boats? How?
  • What happened between booking and arriving?
  • What happened when you got there? (waiver, payment, wait, gear)
  • What did you do when you wanted to change or cancel?

4. Rough spots 22-30 min

"Tell me about a time renting a kayak didn't go the way you planned."

  • What happened? What did you do then?
  • Weather, sold out, running late, group size changed: what happened last time?
  • How did you hear back? Who did you contact?
  • What did you do instead?

"Anything else I should understand about renting?" (then wait)

5. Choosing between places 30-35 min

"Tell me about the last time you picked one place to rent from over another."

  • What did you compare? What did you check first?
  • What made you walk away from one?
  • Did you ever give up and skip a trip? What happened?

No answer? Try "the last time you looked at more than one option for anything outdoors."

6. Close 35-40 min

  • "What should I have asked that I didn't?"
  • Return to anything uncovered above.
  • "May I follow up if I have a small question?"
  • Thank them. Give the incentive as promised.
  • Stop the recording. Save the file as study_participant_date.

Don't when you hear yourself doing these

  • Ask "would you use / pay for / like..." or "which features do you want." Ask for the last time instead.
  • Say "usually." It invites a tidy summary. Ask about one real occasion.
  • Fill silence. Count to five.
  • Correct, agree, praise, or explain the product. Ask "how did you come to understand it that way?"
  • Use your own labels ("booking flow," "availability"). Use their words.
  • Ask two questions at once. One clause, then stop.

Rescue lines

  • Short answers: "Can you walk me through one specific time?"
  • Asks about your tool: "I'll tell you after, I'd like to hear your side first."
  • Wants to chat about features: "What did you do the last time you needed that?"
  • Rambles: let them finish, then "Back to that last rental, what happened next?"
  • Not a fit (didn't rent recently): finish politely, pay them, set the notes aside.
Notes while listening: write what they did and said in one column, what you think it means in another. Mark exact quotes. Note every question they ask you.
