1 · Intro & consent 0:00–0:03

Say: Thanks for your time. I'm learning how people rent kayaks. I'm not selling anything and there are no wrong answers. Your honest, even critical, take helps most. May I record for my notes only? You can skip any question or stop anytime.

2 · Warm-up: who they are 0:03–0:08

  • Tell me about the last time you paddled, and who was with you?
  • How often do you rent versus bring your own?
Probe: Where? Group size? Beginner or experienced? Local or traveling?

3 · Last rental, start to finish 0:08–0:23

  • Walk me through the last time you rented a kayak, from the first thought of going to returning the boat.
  • How did you find that outfitter? (Google, friend, walk-up, other)
  • How did you decide to book with them and not another?
  • How did you actually reserve? Phone, site, email, show up?
  • What happened on the day: check-in, gear, waiting, payment, return?
Probe: “What happened next?” · “What did you do then?” · “Why that one?” · “Who else was involved?” · “What did you check first?”

4 · Friction & workarounds 0:23–0:31

  • What was the most annoying or confusing part?
  • Tell me about a time a booking went wrong: sold out, weather, change, cancel.
  • Did you ever give up on or skip a rental? What happened?
  • How did you handle changing plans or group size?
Probe: “What did you do about it?” · “How much did it matter?” · “Did you pay or tolerate anything extra?”

5 · Tools & expectations 0:31–0:36

  • What apps or sites do you use to book outdoor activities? What do you like or dislike?
  • With a small local outfitter, what would you expect to be able to do online?
Careful: only show or describe Paddlepoint now, if at all, and ask for reactions, not approval.

6 · Wrap-up 0:36–0:40

  • Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?
  • Do you know anyone else who rents kayaks I could talk to?
Thank them, confirm any incentive, stop the recording.

Before you start

  • Recorder tested, phone silenced
  • Clock visible
  • Guide + blank notes ready
  • Incentive on hand, if any

Do

  • Ask about real past events, not “would you…”
  • Stay quiet 5 seconds after answers; they often add more
  • Ask “why?” and “tell me more”
  • Repeat their words back to check you understood
  • Write down exact quotes
  • Let it run over on stage 3 and trim stage 5 instead

Don't

  • Pitch or defend Paddlepoint
  • Ask leading questions: Wouldn't online booking be easier?
  • Ask for feature wishes; ask what they did
  • Finish their sentences or fill silences
  • Judge answers or react with surprise

Listen for

  • How they found and chose the outfitter
  • Phone calls, DMs, waiting for replies
  • Availability, price, and weather uncertainty
  • Group changes, cancels, no-shows
  • Workarounds and “I just…” habits
  • Emotion: frustration, relief, surprise
