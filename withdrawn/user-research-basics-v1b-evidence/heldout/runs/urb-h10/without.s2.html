1 · Open & consent 0–4 min

“Thanks for making time. I’m researching how people book kayak rentals from small outfitters. There are no right answers and I’m not selling anything. Your honest, even negative, experience helps most. Can I take notes (and record, just for me)? You can skip any question or stop any time.”

2 · Warm-up: who they are 4–9 min

  • How did you get into paddling? How often do you go?
  • Where do you usually go, and who with?
  • Do you own gear or rent? What makes you choose renting?

3 · The last rental, step by step 9–24 min · the heart of it

“Think of the last time you rented a kayak. Walk me through it from the very start.”

  • What made you decide to go that day? How far ahead?
  • How did you find this outfitter? Who else did you consider?
  • How did you book: phone, website, message, walk-up? Why that way?
  • What did you need to know first (price, availability, skill level, weather)? Where did you get it?
  • Payment, deposit, waiver: how did that go?
  • Did anything change (weather, group size, time)? What happened?
  • The day itself: arrival, check-in, pickup, return. Any friction?

Keep digging: “What happened next?” · “Why?” · “Tell me more about that.” · “How did you feel then?” · “Can you show me?” (e.g. the email or site)

4 · Tools & workarounds 24–32 min

  • Was there a time booking was a hassle, or you gave up? What did you do?
  • How did you check a time was really open? Did you call or text to confirm?
  • With a group: who organized, who paid, how did you share details?
  • Ever had to cancel or reschedule? How did that go?

5 · Wishes 32–37 min

  • If you could change one thing about renting a kayak, what would it be?
  • What would make you choose one outfitter over another?

Don't pitch or describe Paddlepoint. If they ask, say: “I'd love to hear what you'd expect first.”

6 · Close 37–40 min

  • “What should I have asked that I didn’t?”
  • “Who else rents from small outfitters that I could talk to?”
  • Thank them, stop recording, mention any thank-you or incentive.

Do

  • Ask about real past events, not “would you…”
  • Stay quiet 5 sec after answers. They'll often add more.
  • Ask “why?” or “how come?”
  • Write down exact words and quotes
  • Skip questions if time runs short, but protect section 3

Don’t

  • Ask leading questions (“Isn't booking by phone annoying?”)
  • Ask about the future (“Would you use an app?”)
  • Finish their sentences or fill silences
  • Defend or explain Paddlepoint
  • Ask two questions at once

Listen for

  • Workarounds (calls, texts, screenshots)
  • Emotion: frustration, worry, delight
  • Weather and last-minute changes
  • Things they did vs. what they say they'd do

Right after (5 min)

  • Top 3 surprises
  • 2–3 best quotes
  • Biggest pain point
  • Follow-ups or people to contact
Quick notes / reminders:
If you're running behind at minute 24, move to section 5, then close. A good story beats finishing every question.
