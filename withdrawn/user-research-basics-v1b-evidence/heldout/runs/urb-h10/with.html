Kayak renter interview guide

40 min · one person at a time · participant code: ______ · date: ______

Goal (not read aloud): learn how people actually find, book, and pick up a kayak from a small outfitter, and where it gets awkward, from their last real rental. Do not mention or pitch the booking tool. Ask for stories, not opinions or wishes.

1 · Intro and consent 0–4 min

  • “I’m learning how people rent kayaks from local outfitters. There are no right or wrong answers, and I’m not testing you. You can skip any question or stop whenever you like.”
  • “Can I record audio so I can focus on listening? Only I will hear it, and I’ll keep your name off the notes. Is that okay?”
  • Wait for a clear “yes” before hitting record. Confirm they are not driving.

2 · Warm-up 4–8 min

  • “Tell me about how you got into paddling.” · “What have you rented lately, kayaks or anything else outdoors?”
  • “When was the last time you rented a kayak? Where was it, and who came with you?” (Pick that rental for the next section.)

3 · The last rental, start to booking 8–22 min · core

  • “Take me back to the start. What made you decide to go paddling that day?”
  • “How did you find that outfitter? What else did you look at?”
  • “Walk me through what you did to get it booked. What happened first? Then what?”
  • “Was there anything you needed to know that you couldn’t find out? What did you do then?”
  • “Who else was involved in the plan? How did you all settle on the day and time?”
  • “How did you pay, and when?”

Midpoint (~15 min): “Is there anything I should understand about renting that I haven’t asked about?” Then wait.

4 · The day itself 22–29 min

  • “Tell me about getting to the outfitter and getting on the water. What happened when you arrived?”
  • “What did you have to sign, fill in, or explain on the spot?”
  • “What happened when you returned the gear?”

5 · When plans changed or went wrong 29–35 min

  • “Tell me about a time a rental didn’t go as planned: weather, a late arrival, a group size change, a cancellation.”
  • “What did you do? Who did you contact, and how? What happened next?”
  • “Tell me about a time you wanted to rent and ended up not doing it. What happened?”

6 · Close 35–40 min

  • “Is there anything I should have asked and didn’t?” Wait. Return to any topic you skipped.
  • “May I contact you if I have a quick follow-up question?” Thank them and give the incentive as promised.

Probes (use their words)

  • “Say more about that.” · “What happened next?”
  • “Why was that?” · “Who else was there?”
  • A statement (“Sounds like a scramble”), then silence. Count to five.
  • They say “usually”? → “Tell me about the last time.”

Swap these out

“Would you use an app?”→“What did you do last time?”
“Is booking online easier?”→“How did you book it?”
“What features do you want?”→“When did you last need that? What did you do instead?”
“Would you pay for…?”→“What did you pay for last time?”

Rules for a first-timer

  • Talk less than 20% of the time. One short question at a time.
  • Don’t agree, praise, correct, or explain. Don’t sell.
  • Asked about your product? “Happy to talk after, I want to hear your side first.”
  • It’s a checklist, not a script. Let them wander, then come back.

If it gets awkward

  • Short answers: “Tell me about one specific time.”
  • Goes off-topic: steer back, and jot the detour.
  • Vents: let them finish, then return to the question.
  • Hasn’t rented recently: thank them, pay, set the notes aside.
