1 · Open 0–3 min

“Thanks for your time. I’m learning how people rent kayaks from small local outfitters. I’m not selling anything, and there are no right answers. Honest and negative is the most useful. It’s about 40 minutes. Can I take notes (and record)? Skip anything you like.”

2 · Warm-up 3–8 min

  • Tell me about your paddling: where, how often, who with?
  • Roughly how many times did you rent in the last year? From whom?

Listen for: own vs. rent, tourist vs. local, group size, how planned the trips are.

3 · The last rental, step by step 8–23 min · core

“Think of the last time you rented a kayak. Walk me through it, starting from when you first thought about going.”

Then follow the timeline. Let them talk; only ask:

  • Choosing: Where did you look? Why that outfitter? Who else did you compare?
  • Availability: How did you find out if boats were free for your date and group?
  • Booking: How did you actually book (call, text, web, walk-up)? How long did it take?
  • Paying: Deposit? Waiver? Anything unclear on price or rules?
  • Day-of: Check-in, waiting, gear, getting on the water. What went smoothly or not?
  • After: Anything you had to follow up on?

4 · Pain and workarounds 23–33 min

  • Tell me about a time booking a rental was a hassle, or you gave up or went elsewhere.
  • What happened the last time weather or plans changed? How did you cancel or reschedule?
  • For groups: how did you decide how many boats and which types (single, tandem)?
  • What did you do about it? Did you try anything to make it easier?

Listen for: workarounds, money or time lost, strong feelings, repeated complaints.

5 · Tools and trust 33–37 min

  • What apps or sites do you use to plan outings or book activities? What do you like or dislike?
  • What makes you trust a small outfitter enough to pay in advance?

6 · Close 37–40 min

  • Is there anything I should have asked but didn’t?
  • Who else rents kayaks that I should talk to?
  • OK to follow up? Thank them, and send the incentive if promised.

Do

  • Ask about the past: “last time…”, “tell me about when…”.
  • Follow up with “Tell me more”, “Why?”, “What happened next?”
  • After they answer, count to 5 in silence. They often add the real thing.
  • Write down their exact words.

Don’t

  • Pitch Paddlepoint or describe features.
  • Ask “Would you use…?” or “Do you like the idea of…?”
  • Suggest answers or defend the idea. Opinions about the future are unreliable.

Within 10 minutes after the call

  • Top 3 surprises
  • One strong quote, word for word
  • Workarounds they use today
  • What it cost them in time or money
  • What I assumed that turned out wrong
  • Follow-up contacts and promises
If you run behind, shorten section 5 first. Never cut section 3.
