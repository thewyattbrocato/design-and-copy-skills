Kayak renter interview guide

40 min  |  Participant code: ______   Date: ________   Recording OK: ☐ yes ☐ no
Goal (for you, not read aloud): learn how people who rent from small outfitters actually find, book, change and show up for a rental, and where it goes wrong. Stories about the last real time, not opinions or guesses about the future.
0–4 Intro
4–9 Warm-up
9–32 Stories (prompts 1–6, check-in at ~20)
32–37 Gaps
37–40 Close

1. Intro & consent (4 min)

“Thanks for doing this. I’m talking with people who rent kayaks to learn how it goes for them. There are no right or wrong answers, and I’m not testing you. I’m interested in what really happened. You can skip any question or stop whenever you like.”

“With your OK, I’d like to record so I can focus on listening. Only [me / my team] will hear it, it’s kept until [date], and in notes you’ll be a code, not a name. Is that OK?”

☐ Said who else is listening (or no one)   ☐ Got a clear yes to record before pressing record

2. Warm-up (5 min)

  • Tell me a bit about yourself and when you get out on the water.
  • What other outdoor things do you do, and how do you usually get gear for them?

3. Stories (23 min, a checklist, not a script)

  • 1Tell me about the last time you rented a kayak. Start from when you first thought of going.
  • 2How did you end up choosing that outfitter? What else did you look at?
  • 3Walk me through how you booked it, step by step. What did you do first? Then what? Listen for: device, phone call, website, message, walk-in, payment, waiver.
  • 4What happened between booking and showing up? Listen for: confirmations, reminders, weather, changes, group updates.
  • 5Tell me about a time a rental didn’t go smoothly. What happened? What did you do? Listen for: cancellations, no reply, wrong time, double booking, sold out.
  • 6Who else was involved in that trip? Who organized it, who paid, who got told what?
~Minute 20: “Is there anything else about renting that I should understand?” Then wait a full 5 seconds.

4. Fill the gaps (5 min)

  • 7Tell me about the last time you picked between places to rent, or decided not to go. What happened?
  • 8What do you use to keep track of a trip (texts, calendar, email, notes)? Show me or describe the last one.

Return to anything on the checklist you didn’t cover:

☐ Finding   ☐ Booking   ☐ Paying   ☐ Changes/cancel   ☐ Weather   ☐ Groups   ☐ Day-of   ☐ A bad time

5. Close (3 min)

  • “What should I have asked that I didn’t?”
  • “Can I follow up if I think of something?”
  • Thank them. Give the incentive as promised.

Follow-up probes (say little, then wait)

Say more about that.What happened next?Why was that?What did you do then?Who else?What did you use instead?…(silence)

Reuse their words for things. Short answer? Ask for one specific recent time.

Avoid

  • Pitching or naming Paddlepoint. If asked: “I’ll tell you after.”
  • “Would you use…”, “Do you like…”, “Which features do you want?” Instead ask: “When did you last need that? What did you do?”
  • “Usually” questions. Ask for the last time.
  • Agreeing, praising, correcting, or sharing your own stories.
  • Long, multi-part questions. One clause, then stop.

Notes habit

Raw (what they did and said, in their words) on one side; what I think it means on a separate line. Count as “3 of 7,” never percentages.

Before you start: ☐ Recording works   ☐ Participant booked a kayak in the past few months (not an outfitter employee)   ☐ You’re not in a rush, so leave 10 min between sessions   ☐ Stop the session if they’re driving or distressed. Right after: ☐ Save recording and notes with the participant code   ☐ Write a 3-line summary while it’s fresh. Since you’re the one building this, stay with the scripted prompts and keep your reactions out of the notes.
