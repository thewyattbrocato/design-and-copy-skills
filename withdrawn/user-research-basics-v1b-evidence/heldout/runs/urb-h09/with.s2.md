# Suggested layout for Monday

Lead with the decision you need, not the chronology. Product teams act when each item has a severity, an owner and a clear ask.

## 1. Opening slide: the headline
Something like: "Seat selection is the one thing to fix before anything else. 4 of 5 couldn't tell which seats were available, 2 bought the wrong deck, and our screen reader participant couldn't complete it at all."

Add one line on the sample: "5 participants, one at a time, real tasks. These are counts, not percentages."

## 2. One-page ranked summary
Sort by severity and how central the task is, not by the order you ran the tasks.

| # | Task | Hit it | What happened | Severity | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Seat selection | 4 of 5 | Couldn't tell which seats were available. 2 bought the wrong deck. | Blocks, costly error | Strong |
| 2 | Seat selection (screen reader) | 1 of 1 | Couldn't complete the task | Blocks | Single user, but a total failure on a core task |
| 3 | Card payment | 1 of 5 | Error on card entry, abandoned | Blocks | Lead. Check error logs. |
| 4 | Terminal map link | 2 of 5 | Missed it. Both were first-time visitors to the port. | Slows | Moderate |
| 5 | Promo code field | 3 of 5 | Didn't notice it. 1 said they'd have used one. | Slows | Weak on impact |
| 6 | Date picker | 1 of 5 | Picked the wrong month, noticed and fixed it | Annoys | Watch only |

Put the two seat-selection rows next to each other. They're one story, and it's the biggest.

## 3. One slide each for the top 3–4 items
Use the same template every time:
- **Task and count:** "Seat selection, 4 of 5."
- **What happened:** observed behavior, with a 20–30 second clip or a screenshot. Clips persuade better than quotes.
- **Severity and why:** for example, "2 people paid for the wrong deck."
- **Decision needed:** who decides, and by when.

Keep proposed solutions out of the findings themselves. Put them in the decision line or a separate "options" box, so the team debates the problem before the fix.

## 4. What's working
Confirmation page: 5 of 5 found their ticket without trouble. It's short, and it tells the team what not to touch.

## 5. Parked, not findings
- **Home screen colors ("a bit dull," 2 of 5):** this is preference, not a usability problem. Mention it in one line, or leave it for a design review.
- **Date picker:** 1 person self-corrected. Note it and see if it recurs.

## 6. Unknowns and next checks
- **Payment error:** one person is a lead, not a pattern. The cheapest check is your payment error logs or analytics for card-entry failures, which could tell you if it's widespread.
- **Terminal map link:** all misses were first-time visitors. Say so, but don't generalize to returning visitors from this sample.
- **Screen reader:** one participant is enough to prove the barrier exists. It doesn't tell you how many other barriers there are, so consider an accessibility audit of the seat picker.
- **Retest:** after fixes, run the seat task again with new people.

## Tips for the room
- Say "4 of 5," never "80%". Five people can't support percentages.
- Be upfront about who the sample leaves out, such as how they were recruited and whether they're typical riders.
- End on a slide that lists the asks, each with an owner and a date.

Two things only you can do: attach the session clips and confirm who recruited the participants. I assumed the 5 were a mix of first-time and returning visitors, so correct me if not.
