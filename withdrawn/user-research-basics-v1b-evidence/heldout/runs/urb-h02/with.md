A usability test watches a handful of real people try to complete tasks with your design, so you see where they get confused and, crucially, why. It's qualitative and works even on a rough prototype. An A/B test, by contrast, ships two versions to many live users and measures which one performs better on a specific metric, like sign-ups or clicks. It tells you which version wins, but not why. Think of usability testing as finding problems and A/B testing as settling a choice between options you already have.
