A usability test watches a handful of real people try to complete tasks with your design, so you learn *why* something confuses them. It's qualitative: you observe, listen, and spot problems. An A/B test shows two versions to large numbers of live users and measures which one performs better on a specific metric, like sign-ups or clicks. It's quantitative: it tells you *which* version wins, but not why. Designers often use usability tests to find problems and shape ideas, then A/B tests to confirm which solution actually moves the numbers.
