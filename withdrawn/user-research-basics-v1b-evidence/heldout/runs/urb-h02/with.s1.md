A usability test watches a handful of real people try to complete tasks with your design, so you see where they get confused and, crucially, why. An A/B test shows two versions to large numbers of live users and measures which one performs better on a specific metric, like sign-ups or clicks. In short, usability testing is qualitative and explains problems, while A/B testing is quantitative and settles which option wins. Designers often use the first to find issues and shape ideas, then the second to validate that a change actually moves the numbers.
