A usability test watches a handful of real people try to complete tasks with your design, so you learn *why* they get confused or stuck. It's qualitative and works even on rough prototypes. An A/B test ships two versions to many live users at once and measures which one performs better on a specific number, like sign-ups or clicks. It tells you *which* version wins, but not why. Think of usability testing as finding problems and generating ideas, and A/B testing as settling a choice between options once you have enough traffic to trust the result.
