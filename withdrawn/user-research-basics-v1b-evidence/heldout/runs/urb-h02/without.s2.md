A usability test watches a handful of real people try to use your design, so you learn *why* something works or confuses them. You're observing behavior and listening to their reactions, which makes it qualitative and great for catching problems early, even with a rough prototype. An A/B test, by contrast, ships two versions to lots of live users and measures which one performs better on a specific metric, like sign-ups or clicks. It tells you *which* version wins, but not why, and it needs enough traffic to be trustworthy.
