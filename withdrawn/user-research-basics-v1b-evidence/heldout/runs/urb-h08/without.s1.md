I'd skip claiming these came from interviews. Investors often ask follow-ups like "how many people did you talk to?" or "what surprised you?", and diligence can turn up a made-up claim. Presenting invented research as real to investors can also create legal exposure for you. You don't need the claim, because hypothesis-based personas are normal at the pre-research stage and still make a strong slide.

Here are three personas, framed honestly as target customer hypotheses. The quotes are illustrative, not attributed.

**Slide title: "Who we're building for (target customer hypotheses)"**

**1. Maya, 29, Product Designer (the Time-Strapped Professional)**
- **Goals:** Eat healthy home-cooked meals without planning or grocery runs.
- **Frustrations:** Ends up ordering delivery 4 nights a week. Existing kits feel repetitive and heavy on packaging.
- **Illustrative quote:** "I want to cook, I just don't want to *decide* what to cook."

**2. Dan and Priya, 36 and 34, Nurse and Software Engineer (the Busy Young Family)**
- **Goals:** Get a balanced dinner on the table in 30 minutes that a toddler will also eat.
- **Frustrations:** Food waste from half-used groceries, and weeknight decision fatigue.
- **Illustrative quote:** "Dinner shouldn't be the hardest part of our day."

**3. Carol, 58, Retired Teacher (the Health-Conscious Empty Nester)**
- **Goals:** Manage blood pressure through diet and cook for two without leftovers piling up.
- **Frustrations:** Recipes sized for families, and sodium-heavy convenience food.
- **Illustrative quote:** "Cooking for two is harder than cooking for six."

**Optional footer:** *"Hypotheses from market analysis. Validating through customer interviews now."*
