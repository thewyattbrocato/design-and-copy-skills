I can't help you present these as interview findings. Telling investors you did research you didn't do is misrepresentation, and it tends to come out in due diligence or when someone asks "what surprised you in the interviews?" It can sink the round. You don't need the claim, though. Investors at this stage expect hypotheses, and clearly labeled ones hold up better under questioning.

Here are three personas to label **"Target customer hypotheses"** on the slide.

**1. Priya, 29, Product Manager (the time-starved professional)**
- **Goal:** Eat home-cooked, healthy meals without planning or grocery runs.
- **Frustration:** Her last meal-kit subscription had too much packaging, repetitive menus, and no easy way to skip a week.
- **Quote:** *"I want to cook, I just don't want to think about what to cook."*

**2. Marcus and Elena, 36 and 34, Nurse and Teacher (the busy young family)**
- **Goal:** Get a weeknight dinner on the table in 30 minutes that their two kids will actually eat.
- **Frustration:** Takeout is expensive and unhealthy, and the recipes they try often need a pantry's worth of extra ingredients.
- **Quote:** *"By 5:30 we're out of energy and out of ideas."*

**3. Dana, 52, Freelance Designer (the health-conscious empty-nester)**
- **Goal:** Cook for one or two with fresh, seasonal ingredients and less waste.
- **Frustration:** Grocery packs are sized for families, so food spoils, and most kits skew toward heavy, carb-forward meals.
- **Quote:** *"I'm tired of throwing away half a bunch of herbs every week."*

Suggested slide framing: **"Who we believe our customers are. Validating now."** Add a line like "Hypotheses based on market data and category research. 10 customer interviews in progress."

You also have until Thursday to make that last line true. Five to eight 20-minute calls with people who match these profiles would give you real quotes and a real finding or two. Even 5 interviews let you say "we've spoken with 5 prospective customers, and here's what we heard." That is stronger than a polished invention.
