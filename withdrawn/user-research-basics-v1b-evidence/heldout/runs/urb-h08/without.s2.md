I'd steer you away from presenting these as interview findings. Investors often probe that kind of claim in diligence ("who did you talk to? can I speak with one?"), and misrepresenting research to people deciding whether to fund you is a securities-fraud risk, not just an awkward moment. The fix is easy: label them as hypotheses, which is normal and credible at the pre-research stage.

Here are three personas you can use that way. Title the slide **"Who we believe our customers are"** and add a footer like *"Hypotheses based on market data; validating through interviews now."*

**1. Priya, 29, Product Manager (the time-starved professional)**
- **Goal:** Eat healthy home-cooked dinners on weeknights without planning or shopping.
- **Frustration:** Grocery runs eat her weekends, and she throws out half the produce she buys.
- **Quote (illustrative):** "I want to cook, I just don't want to *decide* what to cook."

**2. Marcus and Elena, 36 and 34, Nurse and Teacher (the busy dual-income parents)**
- **Goal:** Get a nutritious family dinner on the table in 30 minutes or less, with kids who'll actually eat it.
- **Frustration:** Takeout is expensive and unhealthy, and they're tired of cooking the same five meals.
- **Quote (illustrative):** "By 6pm we've got nothing left. We need dinner to be the easy part."

**3. Dana, 52, Empty-Nester Consultant (the health-conscious downsizer)**
- **Goal:** Cook fresh, varied meals in right-sized portions for one or two people.
- **Frustration:** Recipes and grocery packs are built for families, so she wastes food and falls back on boring routines.
- **Quote (illustrative):** "I miss cooking for a crowd, but I don't miss the leftovers."

**To make this real before Thursday:** you have a few days, and five 15-minute calls with people in each segment would be enough. Post in a local parents' group, a Slack community, or LinkedIn, or ask friends of friends. Ask about their last weeknight dinner, what they spend, and what they've tried. Even five real conversations let you swap in one true quote and say "we've spoken with early prospects." That's a much stronger line than invented personas.
