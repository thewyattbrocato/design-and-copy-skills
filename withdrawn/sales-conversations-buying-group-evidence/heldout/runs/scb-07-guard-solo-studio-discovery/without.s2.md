# Discovery call prep: Ines Varga (20 min)

## What we know
- Solo brand designer for small businesses.
- Found us through a newsletter and booked the call herself, so she has some intent.
- She's the only decision-maker, with no partners or accountant. You can aim for a decision on the call or a clear next step.
- The price is $19/month, a low-risk purchase. The real cost to her is switching effort and attention, not money.

**Unknowns to confirm:** what she uses today, what triggered the booking, and which part of the tool interested her (invoicing, time tracking, or both).

## Goal for the call
Find out whether she has a real, current problem with invoicing or tracking time. If she does, get her to start a trial or sign up, with a specific next step booked.

## Agenda (20 min)
| Time | Section |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Open and set the frame |
| 2–12 | Discovery |
| 12–17 | Tailored demo |
| 17–20 | Next step |

**Opening (script):** "Thanks for booking. I'd like to spend most of the time understanding how you run things now, then show you only what's relevant. Does that work? Is there anything specific you hoped to cover?"

## Discovery questions (in priority order)
1. **Trigger:** "What made you book this call? What was going on when you read the newsletter?"
2. **Current setup:** "Walk me through what happens from finishing a project to getting paid. What tools do you use?"
3. **Pain:** "Where does that process get annoying or break down?" Follow up with "How often are invoices late? How long does invoicing take you?"
4. **Time tracking:** "Do you bill hourly, per project, or on retainer? Do you track time at all?" Brand work is often project-priced, so time tracking may matter less to her than it seems. This is the biggest assumption to test.
5. **Cost of the problem:** "What does that cost you in time or in money you chase?"
6. **Past attempts:** "Have you tried other tools? What did you like or drop?"
7. **Decision:** "If this did what you want, what would you need to see to switch? Is there anything that would stop you?" She decides alone, so this is a direct question about her criteria.

Listen for her exact words and reuse them in the demo.

## Demo (5 min, only what she cares about)
Show 1–2 features tied to the pain she described, such as sending a branded invoice or logging time against a project. Designers tend to care about how invoices look to their clients, so be ready to show that.

## Likely objections
| Objection | Response direction |
|---|---|
| "I already use [Wave/FreshBooks/spreadsheets/etc.]" | Ask what's missing. Don't attack the incumbent. Offer an easy trial or import. |
| "Is it worth $19/mo for me?" | Tie it to hours saved or faster payment. Ask what an hour of her time is worth. |
| "I don't track hours" | Fall back to invoicing only. Ask if project-level tracking would help with scoping and pricing. |
| "Migration sounds like a hassle" | Say exactly what moving over involves. Offer to help set up. |
| "Let me think about it" | Ask what specifically she'd want to think through, and book a follow-up date. |

Check beforehand what your tool does for data import, client-facing invoice customization, payment processing, and multi-currency or tax handling. She's likely to ask.

## Close (last 3 min)
- Summarize her problem in her words and say whether the tool fits.
- Propose one concrete next step: "Want to start a trial today and send your first real invoice this week?"
- If she isn't ready, book a short follow-up and send a recap email the same day.
