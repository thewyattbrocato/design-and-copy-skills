**Goal:** Learn whether Ines has a real, sizeable invoicing/time-tracking problem that our tool fixes. If she does, she gets a small, reversible first step on a dated call.
**End on:** a next step with who, what and when. Say it aloud, put it on both calendars and confirm it in writing today.

**Facts**
- Supplied: she runs a one-person brand design studio for small businesses. She found us through a newsletter and booked the call herself. She decides alone, with no partners and no accountant involved. Our price is $19/month.
- Unknown: how she invoices and tracks time now, what that costs her, and why she booked now.
- Assumed: nothing. Everything else is `[to learn]`.

**Opening (about 1 min)**
"Thanks for booking. You found us through [newsletter name]. Is that right? I have 20 minutes. I'd like to hear how invoicing and time tracking work in your studio today. Then we'll decide together whether it's worth a next step. Does that work?"

**Questions (about 12 min)**
Skip "who decides" and "who else is involved". You already know she decides alone. Ask only about timing.

1. "What made you book this now, and what did you see in the newsletter?" *(the trigger and the date driving it)*
2. "Walk me through the last invoice you sent, from finishing the work to getting paid." *(steps, tools, minutes per invoice)*
   - If she's vague: "Roughly how many invoices a month, and how long does each take?" *(count, hours per month)*
3. "How do you track time or scope on a project today? Do you bill hourly, by project, or on retainer?" *(billing model, whether time tracking matters to her at all)*
   - If she says "it's a hassle": "How often, and how long each time?" *(hours per week)*
4. "How late are payments, and what was the last one that slipped?" *(days late, dollars outstanding)*
5. "What have you tried so far, and what did you use?" *(current tool and its cost, so we can compare to $19)*
   - If she says "it's too expensive" or "it's fine": ask what she pays now, in dollars and in hours.
6. "If this worked perfectly, what would be different in three months? Why that bar?" *(her target and her motive)*
7. "What happens if you change nothing?" Ask this only if she has named a problem. Do not argue the cost of waiting for her.
8. "What should I have asked that I didn't?"

**Gate check (during the call, before any demo or trial)**
- Is there a problem she feels and can size, in her own units? If not, don't demo. The next step is for her to get the number, for example "pull last month's invoices and note the time spent".
- Signer: already met, since she is the signer.
- What she'll put in: ask "If you tried it, what would you need to see in the first two weeks?" and "Would you move a real client's invoices over?"
- If the problem is small, say so. For example: "At that volume, a spreadsheet may be enough. Here's where it would start to hurt." Don't enlarge it.

**Price (only if she asks, or after value is clear)**
Give one figure and what it covers, then stop talking: "It's $19 a month for [what's included, from your plan]." Don't discount on a first reaction. If she hesitates, ask where $19 sits against the hours or late payments she just described.

**Close (last 3 min)**
- **Problem real, wants to change:** recommend one small step with a reason and a condition that would change it. For example: "I'd suggest a [trial length] trial with your next two real invoices. If the setup takes more than [her limit], it's not for you." Then fix it aloud: "I'll send the trial link and a one-line recap today. Can we speak [day, time] for 10 minutes to see how it went?"
- **Problem unclear:** the next step is her getting the number, with a dated check-in.
- **No:** confirm it, note her reason in her own words, and stop.

Don't end on "let me know".
