## Questions (20 min)

**0:00–2:00 Open and confirm**
- "Thanks for booking. I'll keep this to 20 minutes. I'd like to understand how you handle invoicing and time today, and by the end we'll both know whether it's worth a next step. Does that work?"

**2:00–5:00 Trigger**
1. "What made you book a call now, as opposed to a month ago?" *(yields: the trigger event and the date driving it)*
   - If vague, ask: "Was there a specific invoice or week that went wrong?"

**5:00–12:00 Current process and size of the problem**
2. "Walk me through the last project, from finishing the work to the money arriving. What tools did you use, and what did you do by hand?" *(yields: tools, steps, hours)*
3. "How many invoices did you send last month, and how many were paid late?" *(yields: count, and the share paid late)*
   - If she doesn't know, write `[to learn]`. Don't suggest a number for her to agree with.
4. "How long do you spend each week on invoicing, tracking time, and chasing payment?" *(yields: hours per week)*
5. "Do you track time at all? Do you bill hourly, by project, or on retainer?" *(yields: billing model, which decides whether time tracking matters to her)*
6. "Think of the last time you under-billed, forgot to invoice, or waited too long for payment. What did it cost you?" *(yields: dollars or days, or a non-money cost she names herself)*
   - If she says "it's a hassle", ask: "How often, and how long each time?"

**12:00–16:00 Cause, target, alternatives**
7. "What have you tried, and why isn't it enough?" *(yields: current tool, its price, why she's dissatisfied)*
   - If she is already paying for something, ask what she pays now.
8. "What would need to change for this to feel solved? Why that bar?" *(yields: her target and her motive)*
9. "If you changed nothing, what happens over the next six months?" *(yields: consequence in her words)*

**16:00–18:00 Purchase route** (she decides alone, so ask only about timing and what she'd put in)
10. "Is there a date you want this sorted by? If it looked right, how soon could you move your invoices over, and what would that take?" *(yields: a date, and her effort and data-migration burden)*
11. "What should I have asked that I didn't?"

**18:00–20:00 Next step.** See the gate check below.

## Gate check (before any demo)
Check these three before you offer a walkthrough or trial:
1. **A felt, sizable problem.** She has given you a number (hours, late invoices, or money) or a clear outcome she cares about.
2. **A signer.** Already met: it's her.
3. **What she'll put in.** She has agreed to bring real data, such as her last 3 invoices or a week of time entries, and to give about 15 minutes.

- **If all three are met:** offer a short walkthrough on her own data. Say a date aloud, for example "Thursday 10:00".
- **If the problem is missing:** the next step is for her to gather the figure, such as last month's invoice count and payment dates, and you book 15 minutes to go through it.
- **If the problem is small:** say so. One or two invoices a month in a spreadsheet may not be worth $19/month. Point her to something lighter, or leave the door open for when she has more clients. Don't enlarge the problem for her.

## Price
Don't bring it up. If she asks, answer directly: "$19 a month, and it covers [what's included, which you need to confirm]." Then stop talking. Don't discount in response to her first reaction. Ask where $19 sits against what she told you the problem costs.
