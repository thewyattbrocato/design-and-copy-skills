# Discovery call prep: Ines Varga (20 min)

## What you know
- Solo brand designer for small businesses.
- Found you via a newsletter and booked the call herself, so she has some intent.
- Sole decision-maker, so there's no committee and no accountant. You can close or set a clear next step on this call or shortly after.
- $19/month is an easy spend for her. The real obstacle is switching effort and whether she trusts the tool with her billing, not price.

## Goal for the call
Find out how she invoices and tracks time today, what hurts, and whether that pain is big enough to make her switch. Leave with a concrete next step, such as a trial started or a follow-up booked.

## Agenda
| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0-2 min | Open. Thank her, confirm the 20 minutes, and state your goal: "I'd like to understand how you run things, then show you whether we can help." |
| 2-5 min | Context. Ask what prompted her to book and what she hopes to get out of the call. |
| 5-13 min | Current workflow and pain (the core of the call). |
| 13-17 min | Brief, tailored demo or fit check. Show only what ties to the pain she described. |
| 17-20 min | Next step, and price and timing only if she raises them. |

## Questions to ask
**Why now**
- What made you book this after reading the newsletter?
- Was something going wrong with how you handle invoicing or time?

**Current workflow**
- Walk me through what happens from finishing a project to getting paid.
- What do you use today (spreadsheets, Wave, FreshBooks, Notion, nothing)?
- Do you bill by project, hourly, retainer, or a mix? Brand work is often project-based, so time tracking may matter less to her than invoicing. Find out which one she's really after.

**Pain and impact**
- How often are invoices late, and how do you chase them?
- How much time a week goes to admin?
- Have you ever under-billed because you didn't track time?
- What have you tried before, and why didn't it stick?

**Decision**
- What would make this a clear yes for you?
- What would stop you from switching?
- When do you want something in place? (Year-end and tax season can create urgency.)

## Hypotheses to test
Treat these as guesses, not facts.
1. Invoicing is her main need and time tracking is secondary. Project pricing is common in brand work.
2. She's wary of tool sprawl and wants something light.
3. Her main costs are late payments and the mental load of chasing clients.
4. Switching costs may matter: existing client records, past invoices, and branded invoice templates. As a designer, she may care a lot about how invoices look.

## Things to have ready
- A 2-3 minute demo flow: create a branded invoice, send it, and track payment status. Add time tracking only if she asks.
- A short answer on migration and import, since you can't assume she'll start from zero.
- Answers on payment processing, fees, and whether clients can pay online.
- A one-line answer to "why $19 and not Wave or FreshBooks?" Find your real differentiator beforehand rather than improvising it.
- A trial or free-start link to send during the call.

## Likely objections
- **"I'm fine with spreadsheets."** Ask what it costs her in late payments or missed billable hours.
- **"I'm not sure I need time tracking."** Position invoicing first and treat time tracking as optional.
- **"Can I import my data?"** Know your answer before the call.
- **"Let me think about it."** Ask what specifically she'd want to think through, then set a date to follow up.

## Close
Aim for a specific commitment, such as "Want to start a trial today and send your first real invoice this week? I'll check in Friday." If she isn't ready, agree on the next step and the date.

- Note anything in your CRM or booking form, especially any answers she gave when booking.
