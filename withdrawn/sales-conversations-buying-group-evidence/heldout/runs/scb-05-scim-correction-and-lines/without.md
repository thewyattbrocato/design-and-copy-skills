Two notes:

- If you want to say "we've logged this as a feature request," make sure you do log it first.

**Lines for discovery calls**

Admitting you don't know isn't what makes you look clueless. A guess that turns out wrong does. These lines keep you credible:

- "Good question. I don't want to guess on that one. Let me get you an accurate answer from our engineers by end of day tomorrow."
- "I know we support [X]. I'm not certain about the specifics of [Y], so let me confirm rather than give you something half-right."
- "Can you tell me more about what you're trying to accomplish? That'll help me get you the right answer, not just a fast one."
- "That's the kind of detail I'd like to bring a solutions engineer in for. Can I loop one into a follow-up?"
- For yes/no feature questions: "Here's what I can say confidently: [what you know]. Whether it covers your exact case, I'll confirm in writing."

Then actually send the follow-up within the promised time. That's what turns "I don't know" into a credibility boost.

To stop this from recurring, keep a one-page cheat sheet of integrations, auth and provisioning, security and compliance, and API limits. Mark each item as supported, not supported, or roadmap (committed vs. not). Ask an engineer to review it. The questions you keep getting are probably the same ten, so you can cover most of them in an afternoon.
