**Lines for when you don't know the answer**

Use these on discovery calls when an engineer asks something you can't answer.

- "I don't want to guess on that. Let me get you the exact answer from our engineers. Can I have it to you by [day]?"
- "I know it works with [thing you're sure of]. I'm not certain about [specific part], so I'll confirm rather than say something wrong."
- "What's behind the question? Is that a requirement for you or a nice-to-have?" This tells you how much the answer matters. Sometimes it also gives you the answer.
- "Good question. Let me bring in someone from our technical team. Would it help to have them on the next call?"
- To log it live: "I'm writing that down as an open item, and you'll have an answer by [date]."

Each of these ends in a dated follow-up you then deliver, and that's what makes you look credible. Prospects forgive "I don't know, I'll find out." They don't forgive "I believe so" when the answer turns out to be no.

To stop this from recurring, keep a one-page cheat sheet of yes, no and partial for the questions you hear most: SSO, SCIM, audit logs, encryption, uptime, API limits. Ask an engineer to check it once. Then you only need the "I'll confirm" lines for the edge cases.
