Keep it short, confirm, and make it concrete. Something like:

**"Yes. Any report exports to CSV in one click, using the export button in the top right."**

Then add one of these, depending on what they seem to care about:

- **If they're evaluating workflow fit:** "Most teams pull it straight into Excel or Sheets. What do you want to do with the data once it's out?" That turns a yes/no into a discovery question and tells you what they actually need.
- **If they're skeptical or comparing vendors:** Offer to show it live. "Happy to do it on screen right now. It takes about five seconds." Seeing it beats claiming it.
- **If they're a bigger or more technical buyer:** Ask whether they need a one-off export or something recurring. If it's recurring, they may want scheduled exports or API access, so only mention those if you offer them.

**Tips:**
- **Don't oversell.** Say what it does and stop. If they ask about limits (row caps, formatting, scheduled exports), answer honestly. If you don't know, say "let me confirm and get back to you today" and then do it.
- **Don't act surprised they asked again.** Repeat questions usually mean it matters to them, or someone new is on the follow-up who didn't hear the first answer. Answer as if it's the first time.
- **Send a follow-up artifact.** A 15-second screen recording or a screenshot with the button circled lets them forward the answer to whoever else is involved.
