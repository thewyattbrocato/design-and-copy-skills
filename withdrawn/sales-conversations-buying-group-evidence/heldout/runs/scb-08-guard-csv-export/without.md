Lead with the direct answer, then make it concrete. Something like:

**"Yes. Any report can be exported to CSV with one click, using the export button in the top right. Want me to show you on a real report?"**

A few tips for the follow-up:

1. **Answer first, then show.** Since they've asked twice, it's likely a real requirement (finance, ops, or a downstream tool). A 10-second live demo or a screenshot of the button lands better than a longer explanation.

2. **Ask what they'll do with it.** For example: "What are you planning to do with the data once it's exported?" This tells you whether CSV is enough or whether they also need scheduled exports, an API, or a BI connector. It also keeps you from overselling if the real need goes beyond a manual export.

3. **Don't overpromise.** "Any report, one click" is a strong claim. Before the call, double-check it holds for the specific reports they care about, and know the limits (row caps, which columns are included, whether filters carry over, how large exports behave). If there's a limit, mention it briefly up front instead of letting them find it later.

4. **Acknowledge the repeat.** If they raise it again, a light "Yes, that's definitely supported, and happy to show you" avoids sounding like you're repeating yourself, and confirms you took their need seriously.

5. **Send it in writing too.** Put a one-liner in the follow-up email, plus a short GIF or help-doc link, so they can forward it to whoever is making the decision.
