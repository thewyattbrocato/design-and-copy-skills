**Say yes, show it, then ask what the export is for.**

**The answer (about 10 seconds):**
"Yes. Any report exports to CSV in one click, top right. I'll show you." Then do it live on one of their own reports if you have one. Seeing the click is stronger than hearing about it.

**Then ask the follow-up:**
"What would the CSV feed into: a spreadsheet, a BI tool, something your finance team needs?"

People rarely ask about CSV because they want a CSV. The usual reasons are:
- They have a report they build by hand today.
- They're worried about being locked in.
- Someone else, such as finance, an exec, or an auditor, needs the data in a different tool.

Their answer tells you what they're trying to do. It may also show a bigger need, like a recurring report, a scheduled delivery, or an API. If you do have those, you can bring them up then, but don't mention them first.

**On the follow-up call:**
- If they ask again, don't just repeat the answer. Say "Yes, and last time you mentioned [their use case]. Here's what that looks like." That shows you remembered and moves things forward.
- If they never gave a use case, ask it now. If you can, put an actual export in the same-day recap, with a short screen capture or a sample file from a report they care about, so you aren't relying on them remembering what you said.

**Things to avoid:**
- Don't oversell it. "One click, any report" is accurate, so stick to that. Don't add claims about format options, row limits, or scheduling unless you've checked them. If they ask about those, say "let me confirm" and send the answer by a date you name.
- Don't treat it as a feature to pitch. A repeated question usually means a concern is sitting behind it, so find out which one.

**One thing to fill in:** if they asked a second time because they didn't believe the first answer, the fix is to show it on screen, not to say it more firmly.
