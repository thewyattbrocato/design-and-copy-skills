**Goal for this week: find out whether the deal is real. Don't build the proposal yet. Do (b), but as a specific ask rather than "pushing."**

**Why not (a)**
- Two weeks of custom work goes into a deal where you don't know who signs, what the security team needs, or whether they're blocked.
- A proposal doesn't unblock security. At best it sits in the queue next to the review.
- You'd also be spending your best selling time on the part of the process you can influence least.

**Why not a pure "push on security"**
Nagging Elena or the security team about status gets you "still reviewing." What you need is information.

**What I'd do (c): a one-week test, then decide**
1. **Ask Elena for a named contact and a dated step.** For example: "Who on the security team owns this, and can you get me 20 minutes with them this week? I want to answer their questions directly instead of you relaying them." Her reaction tells you a lot (see below).
2. **Ask the security team directly:** what they have received from you, what's open, which questionnaire or evidence is outstanding, and what their typical turnaround is. Six weeks with no questions back is a red flag. A real review usually generates requests.
3. **Make sure your side isn't the blocker.** Check whether anything is sitting unanswered on your end: a SOC 2 report, a pen test summary, a DPA, a completed questionnaire. Have it ready.
4. **Find the signer and the budget route.** For a 210k data platform, a marketing VP may not be the signer. Security and probably IT or data leadership have a say too. Ask Elena: "Who else has to say yes, and have they seen this?" `[to learn]`
5. **Ship a light version of the proposal if the AE needs momentum.** One page, or a scoped outline with the problem in Kintaro's numbers `[to learn]` and an implementation sketch. That's a day or two, not two weeks. Build the full custom version once security has a named owner and a date.

**Decision rule at the end of the week**
- Named security contact, a dated next step, and a list of open items: build the proposal in parallel.
- Elena can't or won't connect you, or security returns nothing: the deal is stalled, not slow. Re-qualify with her directly and don't invest in the proposal.

**Is Elena a champion or just friendly?**
Taking every call and sharing internal gossip is friendliness and access. A champion drives change. Test it with what she does, not how she sounds:

| Test | Champion | Friendly |
|---|---|---|
| **Does she push back?** | Challenges your points, asks hard questions, corrects your assumptions | Agrees with everything, "sounds great" |
| **Does she talk about the group's needs?** | Describes what security, IT and finance need | Talks only about her own team's wish list |
| **Will she do real work?** | Pulls a figure, names the reviewer, forwards your security packet | Promises to "check on it" |
| **Can she convene people?** | Gets the right people on a call | Relays messages one way |
| **Has she told you bad news?** | Yes, including internal resistance | Only reassurance |

"They're just slow" is the red flag in your description. A champion at a six-week stall would name the blocker and the next step. She's offering reassurance with no specifics. She may be a good ally who lacks the pull to move security, or she may not know the real status. Either way, that is what the one-week test finds out.

A senior sponsor who is warm but does none of the above is a route to a signature at best. Treat her that way until she shows otherwise.

Two things to check:
- **AE reasoning:** "She takes every call" is activity, not progress. Progress is something the buyer did: a security contact named, a questionnaire returned, a meeting booked.
- **Missing facts:** I don't know what you've already sent security, whether Elena has budget authority, or the close date. Those could change the call.
