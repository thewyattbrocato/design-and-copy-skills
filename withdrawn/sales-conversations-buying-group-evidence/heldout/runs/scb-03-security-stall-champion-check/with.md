**Recommendation: (b), with a small piece of (c). Don't start the two-week custom proposal yet.**

**Why**

- Six weeks of "reviewing" with no named reviewer, no questions, and no date is a stall. It isn't proof that they're slow.
- Elena is VP marketing. A data platform at 210k ARR almost certainly needs security, IT or data, and probably procurement or finance to sign off. She can't unblock any of them. You don't yet know who signs, so a custom proposal is aimed at someone you haven't identified.
- The proposal is the one thing you can't take back. It costs two weeks of effort and it signals to the buyer that you're already committed. A security review that fails or stalls makes that work worthless.
- Your AE's evidence that Elena is a champion is that she takes calls and shares information. That shows she's friendly and accessible. It doesn't show she has influence.

**What I'd do this week**

1. **Ask Elena for a specific piece of work.** For example: "Can you introduce me to whoever owns the security review, or tell me their name and what they've asked for so far?" Her response is your first test (see below).
2. **Go to the security reviewer, or get the facts about the review.** You need to know who owns it, what stage it's at, what they've received from you (questionnaire, SOC 2 report, DPA, pen-test summary) and what is still open. Offer a 30-minute call to answer their questions directly. Send nothing new beforehand.
3. **Get the buying path on the table.** Ask Elena: "Who signs a deal this size, and what are the steps between now and a signature?" Write the answer down by name.
4. **Build only a short scoping document in parallel.** Use one page: the problem as Kintaro describes it, the figures behind it (`[to learn]` wherever you don't have them), and the scope you'd propose. That's about a day of work, not two weeks. It gives Elena something to forward, and it sets up the full proposal once the gate is met.

**Condition for starting the full proposal:** all three of these are true.

- A named signer or a confirmed route to one.
- The security review has a named owner and a date, and nothing in it is a known blocker.
- A problem Kintaro has sized in their own numbers (`[to learn]` today).

**What would change my recommendation:** if Elena comes back with the reviewer's name and a date within a few days, and the signer is clear, the AE's instinct to build is fine. Start then.

**Is Elena a champion or just friendly?**

A champion drives change inside the company. She doesn't just talk to you. Test her with real requests and watch what she does:

| Test | Champion | Friendly |
|---|---|---|
| Ask her to name the security reviewer and the signer | Does it, often with context | Stays vague ("they're just slow") |
| Ask her to get the right people into one meeting | Gets it scheduled, or tells you what blocks it | Offers to "see what she can do" |
| Ask her to pull a figure (current cost, volume, time lost) | Brings it by the date she gave | Doesn't, or gives a ballpark |
| Push back on her ("what if security rejects us?") | Engages and gives you real information | Reassures you ("don't worry") |
| Does she talk about the company's need or only her own team's? | Talks about what the group needs | Talks about her own wishes |
| Do her predictions turn out right? | Yes, over weeks | "Next week" keeps moving |

If she does none of these after two or three real asks, treat her as a friendly contact and a route into the company, not as an ally. Then find the actual driver: the data or IT lead, or whoever is hurt most by the problem you solve.

**One caution for the AE conversation:** "She tells us what's going on internally" is only useful if what she says turns out to be true. Check her last few predictions against what happened. If she said "security will be done by X" and it wasn't, her information isn't reliable.

**Next step to book:** a 20-minute internal call with your AE, today or tomorrow, to agree on this order. Then the call with Elena, ideally this week, ending with a named reviewer and a dated next step.

**Gaps to fill:** I don't know what's been sent to security, whether Kintaro has a stated deadline or a competing vendor, or who else you've spoken to. Any of these could change the order of steps.
