**Recommendation: (c), push on the security review first, and don't commit two weeks to a full custom proposal yet.**

**Why not (a)**
- Six weeks of "reviewing" with no questionnaire, no follow-up questions and no named reviewer is a stall, not a slow queue. A security team that's actually engaged asks you things.
- The custom proposal is expensive for you and low-risk for them. If security blocks the deal, the work is wasted.
- Elena is VP of marketing. She can want this, but she likely doesn't control the security gate, and she may not control the budget either.

**Why not purely (b)**
Pushing through Elena won't work, because she's the one telling you not to worry. You need a direct line to security, and you need to learn what the review is waiting on.

**What I'd do this week**
1. **Ask Elena for an intro to the security reviewer.** Frame it as "help me unblock this for you." Ask who owns it, what stage it's at, and what artifacts they have (SOC 2 report, pen test summary, DPA, security questionnaire, architecture doc). If she hesitates or can't name anyone, that tells you something.
2. **Send the security package proactively.** Don't wait for a request. Include the SOC 2, a completed CAIQ or SIG, the DPA and a data flow diagram.
3. **Find out who else is involved:** IT/data owner, procurement, legal, finance. A data platform at $210k almost certainly needs the CIO/CTO or data team, not just marketing.
4. **Build a lightweight proposal in 2–3 days:** scope, pricing, timeline, and a one-page mutual action plan with dates for security sign-off, legal and procurement. Don't do the full custom version until security has a named owner and a date.
5. **Set a gate.** If you have no security contact and no review stage by about 10 days from now, treat the deal as at risk and downgrade the forecast.

**Champion or just friendly?**
Friendly people give you information. Champions spend their own capital to move the deal. Test for these:

| Signal | Champion | Friendly |
|---|---|---|
| Access | Introduces you to the economic buyer, security and IT | Takes your calls but keeps you in marketing |
| Power | Has budget or real influence, and you can name the buyer | Can't say who signs |
| Information | Gives bad news and internal politics, including who opposes you | Reassures ("don't worry, they're slow") |
| Action | Does things between calls: lobbies, escalates, forwards your materials | Waits for your next email |
| Stakes | Has a business problem with a deadline they own | No urgency or consequence for delay |
| Coaching | Tells you how to win, including what to change | Agrees with everything you say |

Elena's "just slow" is reassurance, not information. Her taking every call is friendliness, which is the cheapest signal there is. Your AE is treating access as advocacy.

**A test that settles it quickly:** ask Elena for something that costs her something. For example: "Can you get me 30 minutes with the security lead, and with whoever owns the budget?" A champion makes it happen, or tells you honestly why she can't. A friendly contact stalls or deflects.

**With your AE:** this isn't about doubting the relationship. Say that you'll build the proposal once the deal has the structure to justify it, and that the intro request is how you find out in a week instead of after the two weeks.
