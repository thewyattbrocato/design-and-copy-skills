Your manager is half right. Priya is your only path in, but more of the same emails won't work. Two follow-ups with no reply tells you the next "checking in" won't either. She's probably not rejecting you. She's stuck: she loves it, but she now has to sell it to three people who don't care about her coordinators' week, and that's work she hasn't done. So make that work easier for her, and stop depending on her alone.

**1. Give her something to forward**

Build three short blurbs she can paste into an email, one per approver:
- **Finance:** the math. Say she has 10 coordinators, each saving a day a week. At roughly $35/hr loaded, that's about $146k/yr in time, against your $38k. Use her real headcount and rates, and ask her to confirm them. Add the overtime and agency-staffing reduction if you have numbers.
- **IT:** security and compliance docs (SOC 2, HIPAA/BAA, SSO, EHR/HRIS integration), pre-answered so she doesn't need to ask you.
- **Head of nursing:** what changes for nurses, such as self-scheduling, shift swaps, fairness and fatigue rules, and a reference from a comparable hospital group.

**2. Change the ask**

Your recent emails probably asked "any update?", which is easy to ignore. Ask something small and specific:

> Priya, I know finance, IT and nursing all need to weigh in, and I'd rather make that easy than leave it on your plate. I put together a one-paragraph summary for each of them that you can forward as is. Would it help if I joined a 20-minute call with whoever you think is the toughest to convince? Or if it's easier, who in IT should I send our security docs to directly?

**3. Get a second thread**

Ask her outright: "Who in finance and IT would normally evaluate something like this?" Champions often say yes to an intro request even when they ignore a status check. Also try a different channel, like a short phone call or voicemail, since email has already failed twice.

**4. Find out what's really going on**

Check for a budget cycle, since it's November and she may be waiting on 2027 planning. Check whether something else is consuming her time, such as an EHR rollout or a staffing crisis. Ask directly whether this is a priority this year. If it isn't, a nurturing plan makes more sense than chasing.

**5. If there's still silence after about 10 days, send a breakup email**

> Priya, I haven't heard back, so I'll assume the timing isn't right and close this out on my end. If things change, I'm happy to pick it back up. Would it be useful if I checked in again in January when budgets reset?

This often gets replies because it's low-pressure and gives her an easy out. Either answer is useful to you.

**With your manager**

Tell them you'll still work Priya, but you're also adding a second path in and giving her materials. Then in your forecast, treat this deal as at-risk, not committed. A single contact with no access to the signers, three weeks of silence, and no scheduled next step is a weak position. You shouldn't count it until you have a meeting on the calendar with someone other than Priya.
