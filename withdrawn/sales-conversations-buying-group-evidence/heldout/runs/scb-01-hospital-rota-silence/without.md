Your manager's advice is the problem. Priya liked the product, but she can't sign, and you've sent three emails to someone who has to sell it internally to three other people. Silence usually means one of these:

- She's busy and it's not urgent.
- She's trying to get finance, IT and nursing aligned and is stuck.
- She doesn't have what she needs to make the case.

More "checking in" emails don't help with any of those.

**1. Change what you're asking for.**
Stop asking "any update?" Ask about the process, which is easier for her to answer:

> Hi Priya, I don't want to keep pinging you. Quick question so I can be useful: what does approval at Westmoor actually look like? Who needs to sign off, in what order, and is there anything finance, IT or nursing will want to see? I can put together whatever makes that easier, like a one-pager for finance, an IT security packet, or a short note for the head of nursing.

This shows you understand she has homework and offers to do it for her.

**2. Build the internal selling kit.**
She said it would save her coordinators a day a week. Turn that into something she can forward:
- **Finance:** a one-page ROI. Coordinators × hours saved × loaded cost, set against $38k. A day a week across four hospitals probably pays back well within the year, but use her real headcount.
- **IT:** security and compliance docs (SSO, data handling, HIPAA/BAA, integrations), ready before they ask.
- **Nursing:** how it affects shift fairness, self-scheduling, and nurse satisfaction and retention. That's likely their main concern.

**3. Ask Priya to introduce you to the other three.**
You're single-threaded, which is the biggest risk. Offer a 20-minute call with IT or finance, framed as saving her the explaining: "Happy to answer their questions directly so it's not all on you." If she won't connect you to anyone, that tells you something too.

**4. Find out whether it's a real priority.**
Ask directly: "Is this something Westmoor wants to solve this quarter, or is it more of a next-year thing?" Also ask whether there's a budget cycle or fiscal year-end that sets the timing. It's early November, so budget planning may be the reason for the silence. If so, you want to be in the next cycle's plan.

**5. Vary the channel.**
Three emails with no reply means try a short call or voicemail, or LinkedIn. Keep it to something like "30 seconds, no pitch, just want to make sure I'm not leaving you with homework."

**6. Send a break-up email in about a week if it's still quiet.**
> Priya, I'll assume the timing isn't right and stop reaching out. If it comes back up, I'm easy to find. If it's useful, I'm happy to leave you a short summary you can share internally.

This often gets replies because it takes the pressure off and makes the loss feel real. If you still hear nothing, move her to a long-term nurture list and re-engage around budget season.

**Tell your manager:**
"Priya's engaged but isn't the buyer, and three follow-ups haven't worked. I'm switching to enabling her internal sell and getting access to the other signers." If you have a sponsor on your side, such as a VP or a customer reference at a similar health system, ask them to reach out to someone senior at Westmoor.
