Send the form today.

The buyer has told you he wants it, has the authority to sign, and gave you a start date. A mutual action plan, a business case, and stakeholder alignment exist to manage risk when there's a committee, an unclear champion, or a big spend. None of that applies here. It's a $4,200/yr purchase, and the owner of an 11-van shop signs it himself. Holding the form back adds friction, gives the deal time to cool, and risks making a decisive buyer feel like he's being put through a process he didn't ask for. Deals like this tend to die from delay, not from missing documentation.

**What I'd do instead**
- Send the order form now with a short note confirming the Nov 1 start. Note that today is Nov 4, so "the 1st" probably means Dec 1. Confirm the date in your note rather than assuming.
- Fold the sales manager's goals into onboarding, not into the sale. Offer a kickoff call and say you'd like his office manager and the two senior techs on it so rollout goes smoothly. That's an easy ask after he's signed, and it protects retention, which is probably what your manager is really worried about.
- If you want a light MAP, make it a three-line onboarding timeline in the same email: signed form, kickoff, go-live. Don't make it a gate.

**One caveat**
If you have a real reason to think the office manager or techs could sink adoption, such as dispatch being her turf or techs resisting a new app, that's a good reason to involve them early. Even then, do it as a pre-go-live step, not before he signs.

**For your manager**
You could say: "He's the sole decision maker, he asked for the form, and it's a small deal. I'll run the stakeholder work in onboarding so we don't risk the close." That respects their instinct without slowing the deal.
