**Send the form today.** Gus is the signer, he said what he wants, and he gave you a start date. That's a felt problem, a signer, and a route to purchase. A mutual action plan, a business case, and two extra stakeholder meetings would be selling to people who aren't buying, and they'd put a $4,200 deal at risk. The usual cost of delay applies here: a buyer who has already said yes and then gets handed homework often cools off or goes back to "let me think about it."

Your manager's instincts are sound for a bigger, committee-bought deal. They don't fit a small purchase that one owner signs. If you explain it that way, it's a difference in deal type, not a rejection of their process.

**What to do:**
1. **Send the order form today**, with a short note confirming the 1st as the start date.
2. **Add a light onboarding plan** to the same email: three or four lines covering who needs to be set up, what you need from him (van and tech list, current dispatch process), and when you'll run training. This is the useful part of a mutual action plan, and it doesn't slow his signature.
3. **Bring in the office manager and techs at onboarding, not before signing.** Ask Gus: "Who should be on the kickoff call so the office manager and your senior techs are set up on day one?" Adoption is the real risk with a dispatch app, and this addresses it without gating the sale.
4. **Fix a dated next step on the call or in the email.** For example: "Form back by [date], kickoff call [date/time], go-live the 1st." Put it on both calendars.

**When I'd change this advice:** if Gus said anything suggesting he needs buy-in from the office manager or techs before committing (a hesitation, "I'll run it by them"), or if the office manager will be the daily owner and has shown resistance. Neither came up in what you described, so I'd go with his word.

One thing to verify: whether your company has a rule requiring a business case or MAP above a certain deal size. If it doesn't, your manager's request is a preference, so tell them you're sending the form because the buyer asked for it. You can offer to use the onboarding plan as the MAP for their records.
