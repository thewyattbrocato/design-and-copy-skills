**Send the form today.** Gus has said yes. He's the signer, the price is $4,200/yr, and he gave you a start date. A mutual action plan, a business case and two extra stakeholders are meant to find a buyer's problem, signer and route to purchase, and you already have all three. Making a ready buyer sit through that process gives his enthusiasm time to cool, and it's the most common way a deal like this goes stale.

**Why your manager's instinct is reasonable but doesn't fit here:**
- A MAP and a business case are built for buying groups, where the person who liked the demo can't sign. Gus signs everything himself.
- A $4,200 purchase for an 11-van shop is small enough that he can decide it alone.
- Asking him to write up an ROI case for something he's already chosen can make it sound like a bigger decision than he thinks it is.

**What to take from your manager's view:**
- **Send the form with a short dated plan inside it, not a separate process.** For example: "Here's the order form. Once it's signed, we'll kick off on the 1st. Can we book 45 minutes on [date] to get your dispatchers set up?" That's a lightweight MAP, and it's the one Gus will read.
- **Ask for the office manager and the senior techs after he signs, as onboarding.** They decide whether the app gets used, so bring them in for setup and training. Don't make them approval gates before the order.
- **Put "who does dispatch day to day?" on the kickoff call.** You didn't mention who that is, and adoption is where a deal like this can go wrong later.

**When I'd change this advice:**
- Gus says he needs to check with his office manager or techs before signing.
- The office manager controls the budget or the paperwork.
- The order form involves a data migration or an integration with their existing systems.

If any of those is true, loop that person in first. Nothing you've told me says it is.

**Tell your manager:** "He's the signer, he asked for the form, and the start date is his. I'm sending it now with a kickoff date and pulling in the office manager and techs for onboarding. If he stalls, we'll do the fuller process." That keeps their concern in play without putting a hurdle in front of a yes.
