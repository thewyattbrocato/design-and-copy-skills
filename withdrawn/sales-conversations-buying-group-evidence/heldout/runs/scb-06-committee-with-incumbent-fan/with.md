**Before Thursday (this does most of the work)**
1. **Call Rachel one-on-one.** Don't ambush her in the room. Ask: "What would a switch have to look like for it not to be a hassle?" Ask what has gone wrong in past vendor changes at Calderon. Her answers become your migration plan and her criteria, which gives her authorship over the risk instead of a position to defend.
2. **Brief the two ops managers.** Ask each to present their own problem in their own numbers: how often, how long, how many people, what a miss cost last time. Their numbers should carry the case, not your slides. Ask their permission to say that the group may weigh renewing against switching. Don't name anyone's private worry in front of the others.
3. **Find the signer.** If you don't know who signs, find out before Thursday. Ask the ops managers.

**Running the room**
1. **Open (5 min):** Name the decision plainly: "Today is a choice between renewing DocuVault and switching. Both are legitimate, and I'd like us to judge them on the same criteria." Saying this out loud takes the sting out of Rachel's position.
2. **Ops managers state the problem (10–15 min):** Each gives the figure, with a specific example. Yields: frequency, hours or dollars per month, `[to learn]`. Ask the CFO and legal one question each, so every role speaks early. The CFO: "What number would make this worth 85k?" Legal: "What must be true on terms, data and exit?"
3. **Renewal on the table (5 min):** Ask Rachel, or state it yourself, what renewing costs and what it leaves unfixed. Don't dismiss "fine". Ask: "Fine against what you need, or fine against what you have?" Then stop talking.
4. **Switching plan (10 min):** Show only what addresses her hassle. That means a named migration owner on your side, a timeline, what Calderon staff must do and for how many hours, how contracts and data move, and when DocuVault can be switched off. Be honest about what is hard.
5. **Recommend (3 min):** Give one recommendation with a reason and a flip condition: "Given [the ops figures], I'd recommend switching. If the problem is smaller than [threshold], renewing is the better call, and I'd tell you so." If you have a real safety net, such as a scoped first phase, a short first term or an exit clause, offer it as a lower-risk way to start. Don't invent one for the meeting.
6. **Close (5 min):** Agree the criteria, name one owner per open item, set the decision date, and say the next step aloud.

**Where this goes wrong**
- **Don't argue with Rachel or pile up reasons to switch.** Pressure raises her fear of choosing wrong.
- **Don't use the cost of waiting, a deadline or "the price goes up".** The committee already knows the stakes.
- **Don't discount if the price gets a first reaction.** Offer a smaller scope or a trade (term, payment) instead.
- **Don't make up savings figures.** Use only the ops managers' numbers and your own verified pricing.

**Gate check:** Before presenting the plan in full, you need a problem someone feels and can size (the ops managers' numbers), a signer or a route to one, and what Calderon will put in (staff time, data, a reviewer).
