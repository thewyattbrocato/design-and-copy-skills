**Treat Rachel as a risk owner, not an opponent.** Her objection is "switching is a hassle" and "DocuVault's been fine." That's a risk and effort objection, not a loyalty one. If you spend the meeting trying to win her over, you'll look like you're fighting the status quo. If you answer her concerns head-on, you can win the committee, and maybe her.

**Before Thursday**
- **Talk to the ops managers first.** Ask them for specific, concrete pain with DocuVault: workarounds, delays, missed renewals, time lost. "Fine" loses to a named example with a number attached. Ask them to speak to it in the meeting, because it lands better from them than from you.
- **Consider a short call with Rachel beforehand.** Ask what a smooth switch would have to look like for her. Her answer tells you what to put in the deck, and she won't be hearing your plan for the first time in front of her peers.
- **Know the other three.** The CFO cares about total cost and risk, and legal cares about contract terms, data handling and migration of existing agreements. Both are likely to ask about the same things Rachel will.

**Structure of the meeting**
1. **Open with the ops problem, in their words.** Have the ops managers describe it, or quote them. This frames the meeting as "here's a problem the business has" rather than "vendor A vs. vendor B."
2. **Name the switching cost yourself, early.** Say something like: "The honest tradeoff is that switching takes effort, so I want to show exactly what that effort is and how we'd carry most of it." Raising it first takes the air out of her main point and shows you aren't hiding it.
3. **Make the switch concrete.** Show a migration plan with a timeline, what your team does vs. what Calderon does, who their point person is, and how you'd handle the cutover so nothing lapses. Include a reference customer who switched from DocuVault, if you have one. This is the part that actually answers Rachel.
4. **Give the cost comparison honestly.** Include the renewal price, your ~85k, and the cost of the pain they currently live with. Don't ignore that DocuVault renewal is the zero-effort path. Show why it isn't free.
5. **Offer ways to lower the risk.** Options include a phased rollout, a pilot with one region, a parallel run before DocuVault's term ends, or implementation support included in the price. Check the DocuVault renewal date too. If the deadline is close, a pilot-then-decide path may be easier for her to accept than a full cutover.

**In the room with Rachel**
- Ask her questions instead of rebutting her: "What would worry you most about a migration?" "What would we need to show you to make this feel low-risk?" Then address the answer.
- Concede what's true. If DocuVault is adequate for basic needs, say so, then show where it falls short for what the ops managers do.
- Don't let it become ops vs. procurement. Position her as the person who makes the switch succeed, for example by reviewing the migration plan and approval criteria.
- Don't call out her emails or her preference, and don't try to flip her in front of the group. If she pushes back, thank her for the question and answer it fully. Her peers will be watching how you handle it.

**End with a clear, small next step.** Skip "decide today." Ask for something like a pilot, a reference call, a legal review of the contract, or a follow-up with Rachel on the migration plan. It's easier for a reluctant stakeholder to say yes to that than to a full replacement.
