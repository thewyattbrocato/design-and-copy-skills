## What to say

**0–5, frame**
"Thanks for making time. I've talked with Marco and Dana separately and heard the problem described a few different ways. That's normal across five sites. Today is not a demo. I want to hear how each of you sees it, find where those views connect, and leave with one shared description and a list of numbers to check. If the problem turns out to be something we can't help with, I'll say so."

**5–10, confirm**
"Before I ask questions: I understand you run five Midwest warehouses, Joliet is one, and overtime, forecasting and attendance have all come up. Is that still right? What have I missed?"

**10–35, the round**
Go shift leads first, so they aren't anchored by their managers. You don't know them, so don't put words in their mouths.

*Shift leads:*
- "Walk me through the last time a shift was short. What happened, who was involved, and how long did it take to cover?" (count, minutes)
- "How often does that happen in a typical week?" (shortfalls per week)
- "When it happens, what do you do: call people in, hold people over, run short?" (the overtime link)
- "What would have to be true for this to stop being a problem? Why that level?" (target, motive)

If they say scheduling is fine, don't argue. Ask: "When you get the schedule, how close is it to who actually shows up?" (planned vs. actual headcount)

*Marco:*
- "You mentioned overtime. How many hours last month at Joliet, and against what you'd budgeted or expected?" (hours, % over)
- "What drives it most: absences, volume spikes, or something else?" (cause)

*Dana:*
- "You've talked about forecasting accuracy. What's the miss today, and what miss would be acceptable? Why that target?" (%, motive)
- "When the forecast is off, what does it cost you or what do you have to explain, and to whom?" (money or consequence, who cares)

*Everyone:*
- "What's been tried already?" and "What happens if nothing changes?"

**35–48, reconcile**
Write on screen, then edit live. Draft, with brackets for what you don't know yet:

"Volume is forecast at [accuracy %]. Shifts are staffed against that forecast, but [no-show rate] means actual headcount comes in under plan. The gap is covered with overtime ([hours/cost]). Joliet [and other sites] feel it as [X]."

Then ask: "Does that match what you see? Where is it wrong?"

- **If the chain holds**, ask each role: "Which of these numbers are you held to?"
- **If it doesn't**, don't force it. The shift leads may be right that scheduling is fine. If attendance is the root cause and nothing you sell touches it, say so: "That part is outside what our software does. Here's the part that might be relevant, and here's the part that isn't."

**48–55, Kevin and Ola**

Don't pitch to either of them.
