# Thursday call plan: Halvorsen Freight

## Goal
Don't pitch or demo. Leave with (1) a shared problem statement the room accepts and (2) one concrete next step, such as a data pull or a pilot at Joliet. A demo would force you to pick whose problem you're solving, and you'd lose the other three groups.

## Working hypothesis
The three views may be one chain:

**No-shows** (shift leads) → **short-staffed shifts, covered with overtime** (Marco) → **labour cost variance and forecasts that miss** (Dana)

Treat this as a hypothesis, not a pitch. If you walk in saying "you're all describing the same problem," it will sound like you're dodging. Instead, ask questions that let them find the connection, then reflect it back. It may turn out to be wrong. For example, overtime might be driven by volume spikes rather than absence. Be ready to say "that doesn't fit what I expected."

## Before the call (15 minutes)
- **Email Marco and Dana separately.** Ask each for one number: Marco, Joliet overtime hours or % over the last quarter. Dana, forecast-vs-actual labour hours or cost variance. Ask if anyone tracks unplanned absence rate. Even rough numbers let you test the chain.
- **Ask Marco** to tell the shift leads what the call is for and that you want their view. Cold, they may assume this is a tool being imposed on them.
- **Send a 3-line agenda** the day before so nobody feels ambushed.

## Agenda (60 min)

| Time | Segment | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Intro and framing | Set the "I'm here to listen first" tone |
| 5–30 | Round-robin discovery | Each person describes the problem from their seat |
| 30–42 | Reflect back and test the chain | Surface the connection, get corrections |
| 42–50 | What "fixed" looks like, and how a decision gets made | Bring in Kevin and Ola properly |
| 50–60 | Next step and owners | Leave with something specific |

## What to say

**Opening (0–5)**
> "Thanks everyone. I've talked with Marco and Dana separately, and what I took from those is that you each see this from a different angle. That's useful, because the problem looks different from the finance office, the Joliet floor, and the shift lead's desk. I'm not going to demo anything today. I'd like to spend most of the hour hearing how it looks from where you sit, tell you what I'm seeing, and then check whether I've got it right. We'll finish by agreeing on one next step, or deciding there isn't one."

**Discovery (5–30).** Go to the shift leads *first*. They're the people you haven't met, and they're least likely to speak if the managers go first.
- Shift leads: "Walk me through a bad shift. What happened, and what did you do about it?" Then: "When someone doesn't show, what's the first thing you do?"
- Marco: "When overtime goes up, what's usually behind it?"
- Dana: "When the forecast is off, where do you see it first, and what does it cost?"
- Don't argue with anyone's framing. Write down their exact words, because you'll use them later.

**Reflect back (30–42)**
> "Let me say back what I heard, and tell me where I'm wrong. [Shift lead] described no-shows as the main disruption. Marco, you described covering those gaps with overtime. Dana, you're seeing the result as labour cost that doesn't match the forecast. It sounds like these could be three views of one chain rather than three problems. Does that match your experience, or does one of them stand alone?"

If the shift leads say scheduling is fine, don't contest it:
> "That's useful. It sounds like the schedule isn't the issue and what happens after the schedule is published is. Is that fair?"

Their pushback is data. If they reject the chain, find out which link is wrong.

**Fixed, and who decides (42–50)**
- To the room: "If this were solved in six months, what would you notice?"
- To Kevin: "What would you need to see before you'd be comfortable with anything touching your WMS or HR data?" Ask about integrations and security now. Don't wait for them to become objections.
- To Ola: "How does a decision like this normally move on your side? Who's involved, and what's the usual timeline?"

**Close (50–60)**
> "Here's what I'd propose as a next step: [pick one: we pull three months of Joliet attendance, overtime, and forecast data and I bring back what the chain looks like in your numbers / a scoped pilot at Joliet]. Marco, would you own the data pull? Dana, could you sanity-check the cost side? I'll send a summary tomorrow so we're all working from the same version."

## Things to avoid
- **Don't rank the problems.** Saying any one is "the real issue" costs you the other three people.
- **Don't talk features.** If asked "can your tool do X?", answer briefly and bring it back to "is that the problem you want solved first?"
- **Don't let the call become Marco vs. Dana.** If they start debating, say "Both of those can be true. Let's get the numbers on each."
- **Don't neglect Kevin and Ola.** Silent attendees often hold a veto. Make sure each of them speaks at least once.

## Things I can't know from here

- Who really holds the budget. Dana may, but ask Ola in the 42–50 slot.
- How the shift leads feel about software that tracks attendance. They may read it as surveillance, so be ready to frame it as helping them with coverage rather than monitoring people.
