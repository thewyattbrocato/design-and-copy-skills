# Board deck: 6 slides, about 11 minutes, 4 for discussion

**Takeaway:** We're out of kitchen capacity, and Dunmore is the only option that gets us the 30% more output we need by next fall, so please approve it Thursday.

I planned six slides for the 15 minutes. That's about 11 minutes of talking and 4 for questions. A board will usually spend its time on the ask, so I front-loaded it.

## Slides and what to say

**1. Title: "Approving Dunmore on Thursday keeps our three new cafes supplied by next fall"**
*On screen:* "Today's ask: approve the Dunmore lease ($9,400/month) and the $610,000 build-out."
*Say (~1 min):* "Hollis & Rye runs 11 cafes, all supplied by two central kitchens, which we call commissaries. Eastside and Mill Street make the food, and the cafes sell it. We plan to open three more cafes, and our kitchens can't supply them. I'm asking you to approve a third kitchen at Dunmore. The next five slides show why, and why now."

**2. Title: "Our kitchens are nearly full, and it's already costing us sales"**
*On screen:* two big numbers: **94%** (Eastside weekday utilization) and **$210,000** (17 wholesale orders declined in two quarters).
*Say (~2 min):* "Utilization is the share of our kitchen's capacity we use on a typical weekday. Eastside runs at 94%, which leaves almost no room for a surge, a breakdown or a new customer. Mill Street is at 81%. The cost is already showing up. In the last two quarters we turned down 17 wholesale orders, about $210,000 in revenue. That's business we had to refuse, and it was with the cafes as they are today."

**3. Title: "Three new cafes means about 30% more output by next fall"**
*On screen:* "11 cafes → 14 cafes in 14 months. Required: ~+30% output by next fall."
*Say (~1.5 min):* "Over the next 14 months we plan to open three cafes. Supplying them takes about 30% more output than we make today, and we need it by next fall. We're already full, so this isn't a stretch goal. It's the gap we have to close."

**4. Title: "A second shift stops at +26%. Dunmore is the option that gets past 30%"**
*On screen:* a simple two-column comparison.

| | Option A: Dunmore | Option B: Eastside second shift |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | $610K build-out + $9,400/mo rent | ~$180K/yr added labor |
| Output | [Dunmore added output: ___%] | Tops out at ~+26% |
| Ready | ~9 months | [fill in] |

*Say (~3 min):* "There are two ways to get more output. Option B adds a second shift at Eastside. It's cheaper up front, about $180,000 a year in labor, but it tops out around 26%. That's short of the 30% we need, and Eastside is already our tightest kitchen. Option A is a new kitchen at Dunmore. It costs more up front, a $610,000 build-out and $9,400 a month in rent, but it adds real capacity and a third site. A second shift can't do that."

**5. Title: "Dunmore takes about 9 months to produce, so waiting puts next fall at risk"**
*On screen:* a timeline: Approve Thursday (Nov 2026) → build-out (~9 months) → producing (~Aug 2027) → new cafes need supply (fall 2027).
*Say (~2 min):* "Dunmore needs about nine months before it can make food. If we approve Thursday and start right away, it's ready around next August, and the cafes need it that fall. That leaves a cushion of only a month or two. Every month we wait comes straight out of that cushion."

**6. Title: "Decision today: approve the Dunmore lease and build-out budget"**
*On screen:* "Approve: (1) Dunmore lease, $9,400/month. (2) Build-out budget, $610,000. Owner: [name]. Start: [date]."
*Say (~1.5 min):* "To recap: we're at 94% and already turning away orders. We need 30% more by next fall, and a second shift only gets us to 26%. Dunmore is the option that closes the gap, but only if we start now. I'm asking for the lease and the $610,000 build-out budget today. [Name] will own the build, and we'll start on [date]." Leave this slide up for questions.

## Gaps to fill before Thursday

1. **How much output does Dunmore add?** This matters most. The case rests on Dunmore clearing 30%, and none of your numbers say it does. Slide 4's title is only true if it does. If Dunmore adds less, or only about as much as a second shift, the argument changes.
2. **Is "+26%" for Eastside alone or for the whole system?** I assumed the whole system. If it's Eastside alone, Option B falls further short, and slide 4 should say so.
3. **Rent timing.** If rent starts at signing, you pay about $85,000 (9 months × $9,400) before Dunmore produces anything. Say so before a board member asks.

## Hard questions

I'd have a backup slide ready for each, parked after slide 6:

- **"Mill Street is at 81%. Why not use that?"** This is the most likely question from the non-food members. You need an answer, such as location, product mix or delivery routes.
- **"What does Dunmore cost to run, beyond rent?"** You gave me rent and build-out only. Bring staffing and operating costs, or say they're still being scoped.
- **"What if the 9 months slips?"** Have a fallback, such as a second shift as a stopgap.

## Notes

- **Don't compare the numbers directly.** The $210,000 is revenue, not profit, and $180,000 is a cost. If you say "lost sales would be about $420,000 a year at that pace," call it a rough pace and not a forecast. I left that out of the slides.
