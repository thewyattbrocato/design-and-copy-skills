**Use B.**

- **It's concrete and local.** A specific child on the street in question makes the problem real in the first 15 seconds. Council members hear generic claims all night, but a nine-year-old and a delivery van on Alder Street is hard to forget.
- **It does the job of a hook without being manipulative.** "She's fine" keeps it from being a tragedy pitch, and "the street she was crossing is the one I'm asking you to change" ties the story directly to your ask.
- **A wastes your best 20 seconds.** Council members already have your name and affiliation on the sign-in sheet or agenda. Say them second, after you have their attention. "Walk you through our proposal" also signals a long, dry presentation.
- **C is a claim with no face on it.** "Cities across the country" is easy to nod at and forget. Opponents can also dismiss it with "Alder Street isn't those cities." Keep the evidence for the middle of your talk, where it supports the story.

**Suggested flow for 3 minutes**
1. Open with B (about 15 seconds).
2. Say "I'm Dana Ortega with Cedar Hill Neighbors for Safe Streets, and I'm asking you to approve a protected bike lane on Alder between 4th and 12th." This gets the ask out in the first 30 seconds.
3. Give one or two pieces of evidence, such as C's point with a number, ideally from Alder or a comparable local street.
4. Close by repeating the ask and the vote you want.
