# Slide, spoken, or handout

Ask three questions about each piece of content, in this order:

1. **Do they need to see it to get it?** Put it on a slide. This covers comparisons, patterns, and anything with a shape, like "this spot is much worse than that one."
2. **Does it need your voice?** Say it out loud. This covers the why, the story, the nuance, and the ask. If a slide would just hold the sentence you're about to say, drop the slide text and say it.
3. **Will someone want to check it or look it up later?** Put it in the handout. This covers completeness and proof, which people want available but don't want to read in the room.

A slide should hold one idea that people can take in within a few seconds. Whatever doesn't fit goes in one of the other two places.

## Applied to your audit

**On slides (4 or so):**
- **The headline:** 38 near-misses in 6 weeks, and 32 of them (84%) were at two locations. A simple bar chart of dock 6 (23), freezer door (9), and other (6) shows this at a glance.
- **The timing:** a chart of near-misses by hour, with 2–4 pm visibly spiking. Put the real count on it. "Most" is vague, and managers will want the number.
- **The recommendations:** three fixes, each paired with the problem it solves. Dock 6 gets a one-way rule and a stop line. The freezer door gets mirrors. The 2–4 pm spike gets a staggered handover.

**Said out loud:**
- **The cause of the timing:** second-shift handover overlaps with peak inbound trucks. This is your main insight, and it needs your explanation more than a bullet.
- **One concrete near-miss from the footage:** a specific story will stick better than the statistics.
- **A one-sentence credibility line:** "We went through six weeks of logs and camera footage." That's enough in the room.
- **A one-sentence definition of a near-miss:** someone will ask what counted, so have a short answer ready.
- **The ask:** what decision you need from them, and by when.
- **The 6 scattered incidents:** mention them briefly ("the rest were spread out with no pattern") instead of giving them a slide.

**In the handout:**
- The full incident log.
- The list of camera angles you reviewed.
- The full counting method.

The handout is what lets a skeptical manager verify your work afterward, so it doesn't need to be presented. Add a line on the last slide, such as "Full log, camera coverage, and method in the handout," so people know it exists.

## Common mistakes
- **Putting the method on a slide.** It feels rigorous, but it uses up attention before you've reached the findings. Keep it short in the talk and complete in the handout.
- **Putting the full log in the handout with no summary.** Add a one-page summary at the top so the handout is useful without the slides.
- **Reading recommendations off the slide.** Show them as three short lines, then use your speaking time on why each one fits its problem.
