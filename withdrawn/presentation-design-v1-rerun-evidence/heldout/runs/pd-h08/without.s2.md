Keep 10 slides, and make the deck an argument for funding phase 2 rather than a project review. Since the committee already knows the project exists, the order is results, then what phase 2 is and costs, then the ask.

**Main deck (about 1 minute each)**
1. **Title (1):** Show it while you start talking. It doesn't need its own time.
2. **Monthly active patients, trend (10):** This is the headline proof that patients use it.
3. **Front-desk call volume before and after (12):** This is your most concrete operational benefit.
4. **Adoption: top 3 vs bottom 3 clinics (9):** It shows the gap and sets up why phase 2 matters. It's more useful than the 14-row table.
5. **Cost to date vs budget (19):** It builds trust that you spend money well.
6. **Three problems we hit (16):** A short, honest slide on what went wrong. Mention the login fix verbally.
7. **Phase 2 scope (20)**
8. **Phase 2 cost and timeline (21)**
9. **Risks if we don't fund phase 2 (22)**
10. **The ask: approve phase 2 funding (25):** End here instead of on "Thank you." Leave the remaining time for questions.

**Backup slides (after the ask, hidden or in an appendix)**
These are the ones the committee is likely to ask about, so have them ready:
- **8:** Adoption table for all 14 clinics.
- **13:** Staff time saved, with the method. The estimate is soft, so don't lead with it, but have it ready if someone asks about ROI.
- **14:** Patient satisfaction.
- **17:** The login fix.
- **18:** The security and privacy review. Executives often ask about this for patient data.
- **23:** Alternatives considered. Someone will ask "why not X?"
- **11, 15, 24:** Message volume, patient quotes, and the roadmap beyond phase 2.

**Cut entirely:** Agenda (2), team (3), 2023 background (4), vendor selection (5), architecture (6), timeline since 2023 (7), and Thank you (26). The committee already knows this material, and none of it helps them decide on funding.

**Two tips:**
- Say your recommendation in your first 30 seconds: "I'm asking you to approve phase 2 funding, and here's why." Then the data supports a conclusion they already know is coming.
- If you need to cut more, drop slide 6 (problems) or slide 4 (top 3 vs bottom 3). Slide 4 is the one I'd keep, because it makes the case for phase 2 best.
