**Keep 9 slides (plus an optional title), about 1 minute each.** The committee is deciding on funding, so build the talk around the decision. Lead with the ask and the proof that phase 1 worked. Then cover phase 2 and the risk of not funding it.

**Recommended order**

1. **#25 The ask: approve phase 2 funding.** Open with it, so the committee knows what they're evaluating.
2. **#10 Monthly active patients, trend.** This is the clearest single picture of adoption.
3. **#12 Front-desk call volume before and after.** It shows concrete operational impact.
4. **#14 Patient satisfaction scores.** It shows the patients value the portal.
5. **#19 Cost to date vs budget.** Executives will want to know whether phase 1 delivered on budget.
6. **#16 Three problems we hit.** It builds credibility and shows you know where the weak spots are. Fold the login fix into one line on this slide, so #17 can be dropped.
7. **#20 Phase 2 scope.** What the money buys.
8. **#21 Phase 2 cost and timeline.** What it costs and when it lands.
9. **#22 Risks if we don't fund phase 2.** It closes the case for approving.
10. **#25 again, or #26.** End by restating the ask. Reuse #25 instead of a thank-you slide.

Count: that's 9 distinct slides, and #25 appears twice (open and close).

**Optional swaps, if you have time or the data is strong**
- **#9 (top 3 vs bottom 3 clinics)** is worth including only if the gap makes the case for phase 2. If phase 2 targets low-adoption clinics, swap it in for #14.
- **#13 (staff time saved)** is a strong ROI slide, but the estimate and method make it easy to get pulled into debate. Keep it only if the numbers are solid, and put it right after #12.

**Cut, and why**
- **#1–3 (title, agenda, team):** The committee knows the project, and 10 minutes doesn't need an agenda.
- **#4–7 (background, vendor selection, architecture, timeline):** This is history and mechanics, and they aren't deciding on any of it.
- **#8 (all 14 clinics):** Too dense. #9 or the trend covers it.
- **#11 (message volume), #15 (quotes):** Nice-to-have. Put one good quote on the satisfaction slide if you want it.
- **#17, #18 (login fix, security review):** Move these to the appendix. Be ready for a security question, though. If the committee is likely to ask, keep a one-line "passed security and privacy review" on #16 or #19.
- **#23, #24 (alternatives, roadmap beyond phase 2):** Appendix. Alternatives will come up in Q&A. The roadmap can distract from the decision.
- **#26 (thank you):** End on the ask instead.

**Tips for the 10 minutes**
- Aim to speak for about 7 minutes and leave about 3 for questions. Executives usually interrupt.
- Keep the cut slides in an appendix at the end of the deck, not deleted, so you can jump to them during Q&A.
- Send the full 26-slide deck as a pre-read, if the format allows.
