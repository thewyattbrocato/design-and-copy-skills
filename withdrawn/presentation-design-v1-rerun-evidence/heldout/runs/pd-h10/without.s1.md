I'd avoid "overwhelmingly" in the headline. 9 of 14 is 64%, which is a solid majority but not overwhelming. All 14 came from your own newsletter list, so they're likely warmer to your product than the typical store owner. Someone in the room is likely to point that out, and if they do, it undermines the rest of your case. A more accurate headline can still make the case for the build.

## Deck (5 slides, about 5 minutes)

**Slide 1: Headline (45 sec)**
**"2 in 3 independent hardware store owners we interviewed would use online restock ordering."**
Subtext: 9 of 14 said yes (64%), and only 2 said no. 12 of 14 (86%) are open to it.
*Say:* "We talked to 14 store owners in September. Two-thirds said yes outright, and only two ruled it out."

**Slide 2: The demand picture (60 sec)**
A simple bar or stacked bar showing Yes 9 / Maybe 3 / No 2. Label it with counts as well as percentages.
*Say:* "The three 'maybes' all said it depends on price. That's a pricing question, not a demand problem. The two 'no's prefer calling their rep, so they're a relationship preference and not a rejection of the idea."

**Slide 3: Why now, the pain with the current options (60 sec)**
**"36% (5 of 14) volunteered that their distributor's ordering portal is slow or confusing."**
*Say:* "Nobody prompted them on this. The existing alternatives are frustrating enough that owners brought it up on their own."
Note that the yes answers were conditional on register integration. That condition is the strongest argument for the specific build you're proposing, so say so here.

**Slide 4: What we're confident in and what we're not (45 sec)**
- Confident: there's real interest, and register integration is the deciding condition.
- Not yet known: whether interest turns into usage. The sample is small (14) and came from our newsletter, so it skews toward engaged customers.
- Not yet known: price sensitivity.

*Say:* "I'd rather flag this now than have it surface after we've built."

**Slide 5: The ask (60 sec)**
**"Approve a 10-week build of restock ordering with register integration."**
Add two de-risking pieces:
- Run 15–20 more interviews, or a short survey of non-newsletter stores, during the first 2–3 weeks.
- Test pricing with the 3 "maybes" before launch.
- Optional: set a checkpoint at week 3 or 4, where you confirm the broader sample matches the interviews before full commitment.

*Say:* "The signal is strong enough to start. This plan lets us validate the rest while we build."

## Why this works better with leadership
- "2 in 3" is honest and still persuasive. It's a figure nobody can easily attack.
- Slide 4 shows you've already considered the objection, which builds credibility for the ask.
- The checkpoint gives approvers an easy yes, since the risk is bounded.

If your VP insists on stronger language, "a clear majority" or "strong early demand" is defensible. "Overwhelmingly" isn't, and a percentage next to it (64%) will contradict the word on the slide.
