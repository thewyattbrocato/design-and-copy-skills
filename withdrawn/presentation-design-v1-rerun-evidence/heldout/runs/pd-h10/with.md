**5 minutes, 3 content slides plus a backup. Takeaway: approve the 10-week build, because 9 of 14 owners said they'd use it if it connects to their register, and the integration is what they're asking for.**

I'd push back on "overwhelmingly." 9 of 14 is 64%. That's a solid majority, but with 14 interviews it isn't overwhelming. All 14 also came from your own newsletter list, so they're your most engaged customers. Someone in that room will notice, and a headline that overreaches gives them a reason to discount the real finding. The accurate framing is still strong:

- **64%** (9/14) would use it.
- **86%** (12/14) are not opposed. 9 said yes and 3 said maybe, depending on price.
- Only 2 of 14 said no.

## Slides

**1. "Nearly two-thirds of hardware store owners we interviewed would use online restock ordering"**
- Visual: a large **9 of 14 (64%)**, with a simple bar split of 9 yes / 3 maybe / 2 no.
- Small footer: "14 interviews, Sept., independent hardware owners."
- Your VP gets her percentage on the headline slide. If she wants stronger wording, "a clear majority" is the most this data supports.

**2. "The register integration is the condition, not a nice-to-have"**
- Visual: the quote or condition, "…if it connected to their register system."
- Support: 5 of 14 called their current distributor portal slow or confusing.
- This slide makes the case for the integration scope, which is what makes the build 10 weeks.

**3. "Approve a 10-week build of restock ordering with register integration"**
- Visual: the ask, with a visible placeholder line: **Owner: [name] · Decision by: [date] · Cost: [x]**.
- Add one line: "Price is the open question for the 3 'maybes'. We'll test it during the build." Only say that if it's true.
- Leave this slide up during questions.

## Backup slides (after the ask)
Prepare for the three hardest questions:
1. **"Isn't this biased toward your fans?"** Say it first, before they do: "These were newsletter subscribers, so treat 64% as a ceiling, not a market estimate." Then offer a cheap check, such as 5 to 10 calls to non-subscribers during week 1. That check is my suggestion, not something in your data.
2. **"What about the 'maybes' and the 'nos'?"** The maybes hinge on price. The 2 nos prefer calling their rep, so keep phone ordering as an option.
3. **"Why 10 weeks?"** You'll need your engineering breakdown for this one. I don't have it, so I haven't made anything up.

Calling it a ceiling will make the ask more credible, not less. If you want a stronger case, the best addition is a few verbatim quotes from the 9 yeses. I can fit them into slide 2 if you send them.
