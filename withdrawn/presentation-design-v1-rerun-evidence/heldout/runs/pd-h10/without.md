I'd drop "overwhelmingly" and build the case on what the data supports. 9 of 14 is 64%, which is a solid majority but not overwhelming. All 14 came from your own customer newsletter, so they're engaged existing customers, and 14 interviews can't estimate a population rate. If anyone in the room asks how they were recruited, "overwhelmingly" is the first thing to fall apart, and the credibility of the whole ask goes with it. The honest version is still strong.

## Deck (5 slides, about 1 minute each)

**Slide 1: Headline**
> **Nearly two-thirds of store owners we interviewed (9 of 14) would use online restock ordering, if it connects to their register**

Sub-line: 14 interviews with independent hardware store owners, September.

**Slide 2: What we heard**
- **9 of 14 (64%)**: yes, if it integrates with their register system
- **3 of 14 (21%)**: maybe, depends on price
- **2 of 14 (14%)**: no, they prefer calling their rep
- Only 2 of 14 rejected it outright. 12 of 14 (86%) are a yes or a "price-dependent" maybe.

Chart idea: one stacked bar of 9 / 3 / 2. Show the counts, not just the percentages.

**Slide 3: Why now**
- 5 of 14 (36%) said their current distributor's ordering portal is slow or confusing, so there's an opening.
- The register integration is the condition for the yes votes. It's the core of the product, not an add-on.
- Speaker note: the "no" owners said they prefer calling their rep. This is a relationship preference, not a complaint about the idea.

**Slide 4: What we're confident about, and what we're not**
- Confident: demand is real among our existing customers, and integration is the deciding factor.
- Not yet known: whether this holds beyond newsletter subscribers, and what price the 3 "maybes" would accept.
- Presenting this up front makes the ask look disciplined, not shaky.

**Slide 5: The ask**
> Approve a 10-week build of restock ordering with register integration.

Add one or two of these, since they make approval easier:
- A checkpoint at about week 4: pricing test with the 3 "maybes" and a few non-newsletter stores.
- A pilot with the 9 "yes" owners as the launch cohort.
- The success metric you'll report back on, such as pilot adoption or orders placed.

## Talking points for your VP
- "64%" is the number to put on the slide. It's accurate and still persuasive.
- If she wants a stronger framing, "86% didn't say no" is defensible when labeled as "yes or price-dependent." Don't present it as 86% demand.
- Leadership approves risky builds more readily when the risks are named and bounded. A 10-week build with a week-4 validation point is an easier yes than a build justified by an inflated number.
