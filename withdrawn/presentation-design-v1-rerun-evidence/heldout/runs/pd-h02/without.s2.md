Your content is solid. The problem is density and structure, not substance. Forty people on a big screen will read the slide instead of listening to you, and several slides are lists where they should be one point.

**Across the deck**
- Give each slide one takeaway, and make the title say it. "Velocity" becomes "We delivered 15% more, and hit our commitments more often."
- Aim for 3 or fewer items per slide, with big numbers or a chart instead of sentences. Put the detail in your speaking and the appendix.
- Your audience is the whole product group, so lead with outcomes. Raw story points matter less to them than commitment accuracy and what shipped.

**Slide by slide**
1. **Title:** Fine as is.
2. **Overview:** Cut it. It restates the deck, and the slides that follow already show the order. Spend the first 20 seconds on the headline instead: "Faster, more predictable, one notable incident, and here's what's next."
3. **Velocity:** Replace the text with a simple bar chart of sprints 14–19 and annotate Sprint 17 ("2 out, holiday week"). Show two big stats beside it: **84% commitment accuracy (from 71%)** and **carry-over 9 → 4**. Drop the 41.8 vs 36.5 average to a spoken aside.
4. **Shipped features:** Seven bullets is a wall. Group them by who benefits, for example Dispatchers (dark mode, driver-assignment modal, bulk edit), Customers and subcontractors (saved filters, CSV export, role-based access), and Platform (notification rewrite). If you have screenshots of two or three, use those instead of text.
5. **Quality:** Show three before/after numbers: defects 31 → 17, P2 fix time 6.2 → 3.5 days, dispatch coverage 58% → 71%. Move the flaky-suite swap to the appendix or say it aloud. Keep the CSV gap, but move it to slide 7, where it becomes an action.
6. **Incident:** Present it as a short timeline: what happened, impact (3h10m, 1,140 alerts), cause, fix. Cut the config details to one plain-English line, like "a queue setting was too low". Put the follow-up on its own line, because that's what the room cares about.
7. **Q4 plan:** Six unranked items reads as a wish list. Pick the top 3 and say why they matter. Link them to what you just showed: the CSV test and the queue load test come from the quality gaps and the incident, and subcontractor phase 2 continues what you shipped. Move the hire and the monthly-cadence investigation to a "Also exploring" line.
8. **Thank you:** Replace it with a slide that ends on something actionable, such as "Decisions and feedback I need from you" (the cadence change is a good one). A closing slide that stays up during Q&A should give people something to respond to.

**One narrative tip:** The notification rewrite appears as a win on slide 4 and as the cause of the incident on slide 6. Name that connection yourself. It makes the deck more credible, and it sets up the load-testing follow-up.
