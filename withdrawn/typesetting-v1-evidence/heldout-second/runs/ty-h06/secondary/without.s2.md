Set it as a centred, typographic menu with generous spacing, not as a grid. Here's how to do it in Word or Pages.

**Type**
- **Headings** (Starters, Mains, Puddings, and the restaurant name): a classic serif such as Garamond, Baskerville, or Caslon, in capitals with letter-spacing (+1 to +2 pt). Size 14–18 pt for section names.
- **Dish names:** the same serif or a clean sans such as Gill Sans or Avenir, 11–12 pt, regular or semi-bold.
- **Descriptions:** 9.5–10.5 pt, a shade lighter (dark grey, not pale grey, so it prints well).
- Use at most two typefaces. Don't use italics for descriptions.

**Layout**
- **Margins:** 18–20 mm on all sides. A4 is 210 × 297 mm.
- **Centre the heading block**, then left-align the menu items. Centred dish lists look like a flyer.
- **Each dish is two lines:** the name and price on the first line, the description underneath.
- **Price alignment:** put the price on the name line, right-aligned, using a right tab stop with dot leaders. In Word, open Paragraph, then Tabs, set a right-aligned tab at the right margin, and choose dots as the leader. Type the name, press Tab, then the price. Don't use table columns; that's what makes it look like a spreadsheet.
- **Spacing:** about 10–12 pt between dishes, and 24–30 pt before each section heading. Use the paragraph spacing settings, not empty lines.
- **Section headings:** add a thin rule or some extra space below them. Avoid boxes and shaded backgrounds.
- **Footer text** (allergy line and service charge): small, 8.5–9 pt, centred, with a little space above it. Put it near the bottom margin, not in the body.

**Content fixes to make before printing**
- Add currency symbols. "4.5 each" and "24" are unclear without them. If you're in the UK, use £4.50 (with two decimals, so prices align) and £24.00.
- Make the oyster price clear: "£4.50 each."
- Keep the service charge wording as you wrote it, but check the figure is 12.5% and not 12.5 of another unit.

**Printing**
- Print on 120–160 gsm paper for a light menu, or 250 gsm card if you want it to feel sturdier. Check your printer's maximum card weight first.
- If you're printing at home, keep to the printable area, about 5 mm from the edges. Ask a print shop for bleed if you're printing a full-colour background.
