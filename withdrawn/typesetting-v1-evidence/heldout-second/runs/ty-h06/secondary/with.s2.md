Set it as a single column of text, not a table. Use paragraph spacing and right-aligned tab stops for the prices, and give each element one clear size. The content stays as you wrote it.

**Type sizes (four levels, each with one job)**

| Role | Size | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Restaurant name | 28–34 pt | Centered, one line, extra space below |
| Section heads (Starters, Mains, Puddings) | 13–14 pt | All caps, letter spacing +1 pt, space before ~18 pt, after ~6 pt |
| Dish names | 11–12 pt | Regular or semibold, same line as the price |
| Descriptions and closing lines | 9.5–10 pt | Lighter grey, not italic |

Use one typeface family, or a serif for names and a sans for descriptions, and no more than two. Avoid faux bold or italic; use the real faces.

**Item layout**

1. Split each item into dish name, description, and price. For example, "Oyster of the day, shallot vinegar 4.5 each" becomes **Oyster of the day** on the first line, "shallot vinegar" as the description below, and "4.50 each" on the right. Check the split for the ambiguous ones, like "Whole grilled bream, salsa verde, new potatoes", where you may want "Whole grilled bream" as the name.
2. Put the price on the name line with a **right-aligned tab stop at the right margin with dot leaders**. In Word: Paragraph → Tabs → Position at the text width, Alignment Right, Leader 2 (dots). In Pages or Google Docs, use the equivalent tab setting. This is what gives the classic menu look.
3. Use a bit of space between items (about 4–6 pt) and keep descriptions on their own line. Don't use bullets or table borders.
4. Use left alignment throughout. Only the restaurant name is centered.

**Closing lines**

Put the allergy line and the service charge note at the bottom, in the smallest size, with extra space above them. Keep them separate from the menu sections.

**Two things to decide before printing**

- **Currency.** The prices have no currency symbol. Add one (for example £) for consistency, and decide whether to show two decimals (4.50, 12.50, 7.50) so they line up. I'd recommend two decimals.
- **Fit.** Set margins to about 15–20 mm, check that everything fits on one A4 page, and print a test copy at actual size before the final run.
