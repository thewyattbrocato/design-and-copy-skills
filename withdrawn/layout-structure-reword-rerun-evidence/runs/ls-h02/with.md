Several of these problems compound each other. Here they are, most damaging first.

**1. The first screen doesn't say anything.**
It's a video and a logo. A newcomer can't tell what the event is, when or where it happens, or what to do next. There's no date, no location and no "Register" button. The description is a long paragraph below the sponsor grid. Most people leave when they can't answer "what is this and is it for me?" in a few seconds.
- Put a one-line description, the date and place, and one primary call to action over the video.
- Make sure the text stays readable on top of the video (scrim or overlay).
- Check that autoplay video isn't slowing the load, especially on mobile.

**2. Sponsors come before the content.**
Twelve logos in a 4×3 grid sit above the event description and the speakers. That's the visitor's second screen, and it's about your funding, not their reasons to attend.
- Move sponsors lower, near the footer.
- Or show a single slim row of top-tier logos.

**3. The body paragraph is 1300px wide.**
At normal text sizes that's well over 100 characters per line, which is hard to read. People skip it. A comfortable line is about 45–75 characters, or roughly 600–700px.
- Cap the paragraph at that measure.
- Break it into a short lead, then a few scannable facts (dates, venue, tracks, ticket info).

**4. The nav is left-aligned while the content is centered.**
The nav and the content don't share an edge, so the nav looks like a mistake. It also has 11 items, which is too many for a flat top bar. Visitors can't scan that many, and it will wrap or overflow on narrow screens.
- Group the items down to about 5–7 (for example Schedule, Speakers, Venue, Sponsors, Tickets, More).
- Align the nav with the content column, or offset it by a deliberate whole column.
- Make the ticket or register link the one visually distinct item.

**5. The whole page is centered stacks.**
Every section has the same shape, with centered blocks and grids and no change of pace. Nothing marks which section matters most. Use one flush left edge, give sections different structures, and let one section (probably speakers or the schedule) be the loud one.

**6. Smaller issues**
- **Speakers:** a 3-column grid is fine for peers. Use one image ratio and the same content order in every cell, and put it above the sponsors.
- **Missing content:** there's no schedule, venue or ticket information anywhere in your structure, and those are what people come for.
- **Mobile:** with the nav, sponsor grid and wide paragraph, the stacked order probably pushes everything useful below the fold. Check the single-column order.

**Suggested order**
1. Hero with a one-line description, date and place, and a Register button
2. Short event summary at a readable width, with key facts
3. Speakers
4. Schedule and venue
5. Tickets
6. Sponsors
7. Footer

The nav stays sticky, with 5–7 items sharing the content's left edge.
