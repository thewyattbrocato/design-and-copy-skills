**Pick the single centered column with full-width photos.**

- **The photos are the content.** With 8–12 photos and only 3–4 short paragraphs, a two-column layout would shrink the photos or crop them to fit. A single column lets each photo run at the full width of the column, or a bit wider than the text.
- **The text is too short for two columns.** Three or four short paragraphs would end up as a few stubby lines beside the images. Magazine columns work when there's enough running text to fill them.
- **Phones.** Most readers will probably open posts from a link on a phone, where the two-column layout collapses to one column anyway. If you design the single column first, the desktop and phone versions match.
- **Reading order.** One column keeps the order as story, photo, story, photo, which is how a hike unfolds. Two columns make the eye choose between the text and the pictures.

**How to build it**
- Cap the text column at about 65 characters per line (roughly 36–40rem). Let the photos break out to a wider width, around 60–70rem, or full bleed for the best shots.
- Put a short paragraph between every two or three photos, not all the text at the top. Where it fits, a paired landscape and portrait shot side by side can add variety. Keep the ratios consistent within each pair and don't crop the photos.
- Add a one-line caption under photos that need it, such as the trail name, the spot or the date. Keep captions out of the text measure.
- Make the first screen the best photo, the title, the date and the trail or location. A reader should see what the hike was right away.
- Test it with the longest title you have, a post with 12 photos and one with only 6, and a mix of portrait and landscape photos.
