**Single centered column with full-width photos.**

- **Short text, many photos.** With 3-4 short paragraphs, a two-column layout would leave the text as a thin strip. The photos are the content, and they should get the space.
- **Mobile.** Club members will most likely view posts on phones. A two-column layout collapses to one column there anyway, so you'd be designing the single column regardless.
- **Reading order.** Hiking posts follow a trail, with the story going from start to summit and back. A single column keeps the photos and text in that sequence. Magazine columns break it up, and the eye jumps around.
- **Upkeep.** Volunteers posting after a hike won't want to fiddle with column balance or photo placement. One column means no layout decisions per post.

**Details that make it work:**
- Keep the text measure to roughly 60-75 characters (about 650-700px) so the paragraphs stay readable.
- Let the photos run wider than the text, up to full viewport width on large screens. This gives you a cinematic feel without needing columns.
- Mix in occasional side-by-side pairs for portrait shots or before/after views, but keep that as an exception.
- Put short captions under the photos. They carry much of the storytelling when the paragraphs are this brief.
- Lazy-load the images and serve responsive sizes, since 8-12 full-width photos can make pages heavy.

A two-column layout would only make sense if posts had long text or sidebar content like trail stats and maps. For this format, it adds complexity and doesn't help.
