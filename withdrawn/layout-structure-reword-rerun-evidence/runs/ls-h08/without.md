Yes, there's one structural problem and a few weaker ones.

**Structural: the column widths don't fit the grid.**
- 50% is exactly 6 columns, so the chart is fine.
- 40% of 12 columns is 4.8 columns, which isn't a whole number. The text column either breaks the grid or the "aligned to 12 columns" claim is only approximately true.
- Percentages of the slide also ignore the margins and gutters. Percentages of the content area behave differently from percentages of the slide.
- Fix: use whole columns. 5 columns for text (about 41%) plus 6 for the chart leaves a 1-column gap. If you want the chart to dominate, use 4 and 7 (or 5 and 7 with no spare column).

**Content and hierarchy**
- Five bullets plus a chart means two things compete for attention. If the chart carries the message, cut to 3 bullets or fewer, or make them short annotations of what the chart shows.
- If the title is a topic label ("Q3 Revenue") rather than a takeaway ("Revenue grew 18% on enterprise"), the bullets have to do the explaining. A takeaway title lets you cut some of them.
- A small footer is fine for sources, but check that it's legible when projected and sits on the same left margin as the title.

**Check**
- Align the chart's top edge to the first bullet's cap height or to the title baseline, not just to the column edge. Otherwise the layout looks off even when it's on the grid.

The structure itself, with title top-left, text left, chart right and source at the bottom, is a conventional layout that works. The fix is mostly snapping to whole columns and trimming the content.
