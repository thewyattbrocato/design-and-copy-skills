The chart is mostly fine. Horizontal bars on a zero baseline, sorted order, direct labels, one color and a clear title are all the right choices. I'd only change a few small things.

**Worth fixing**
1. **Title: say the finding.** If the chart is meant to persuade or report, use something like "72% of patients are satisfied with the clinic" instead of "Patient satisfaction survey." Keep a neutral title only if the chart is exploratory.
2. **Add the sample size and survey details.** Put "n = ___ respondents, [dates]" in a subtitle or footnote. Without it, 72% could mean 18 of 25 people or 720 of 1,000, and readers can't judge how much weight it deserves.
3. **Add a comparison if you have one.** A prior survey, a target or a benchmark would give 72% some meaning. If none exists, skip it.

**Optional polish**
- **Color:** one blue is fine. If the point is "most are satisfied," use a strong accent on Satisfied and a muted gray-blue on the other two. Don't use red/green, since it fails for color-blind readers.
- **Labels and axes:** with percentage labels on the bar ends, the value axis and gridlines are redundant. Remove them, or fade them to very light, for a cleaner look. Make sure the labels aren't clipped at the longest bar.
- **Rounding:** 72 + 19 + 9 = 100, so there's nothing to fix. If the underlying data was rounded, add "may not sum to 100% due to rounding."
- **Accessibility:** add alt text or a caption with the takeaway, such as "72% satisfied, 19% neutral, 9% unsatisfied."

**Leave as is**
- Don't switch to a pie. Three ordered categories read well as bars.
- If you want a part-to-whole view, a single 100% stacked bar is the alternative, but it isn't needed here.
