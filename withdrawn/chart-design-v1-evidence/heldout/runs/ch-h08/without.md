Nothing is wrong with the chart. The choices you list are the right ones: ordinal order, a zero baseline, direct labels and a single color. The remaining fixes are small and mostly about context.

1. **Add the sample size and context.** Put "n = ___", the survey dates and the exact question wording in a subtitle or footnote. With a small clinic sample, 72% could be a fairly loose estimate. A reader needs the n to judge that.
2. **Make the title state the finding.** "72% of patients are satisfied with the clinic" tells people what to take away. "Survey results" or "Patient satisfaction" only names the topic.
3. **Fix the axis at 0–100%.** Don't let it auto-scale to the largest bar. Because the bars are directly labeled, you can drop the gridlines and axis ticks entirely.
4. **Check the labels.** Make sure the percentages sum to 100 after rounding (72 + 19 + 9 does). The labels should also be legible against the background, in a readable size and with enough contrast.
5. **Optional: highlight one bar.** You could keep "Satisfied" in the strong blue and mute the other two to gray. A single hue is fine, but this puts the emphasis where the title points. Avoid red and green, which are poor for color-blind readers and add little with only three bars.

Two things to leave alone:
- **Chart type:** a single stacked bar would also work for parts of a whole, but a horizontal bar chart is easier to read, so there's no reason to switch.
- **Sort order:** Satisfied, Neutral, Unsatisfied is both the natural scale order and descending by value, so keep it.

If the teammate has the response counts or can compute a margin of error, a short note such as "±4 pts" would help more than any visual change.
