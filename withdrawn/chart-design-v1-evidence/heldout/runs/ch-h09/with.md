No, two numbers don't need a chart. A sentence does the job and is faster to read:

> Median delivery time fell from 3.2 days last year to 2.4 days this year, down 0.8 days (25%).

If you want something more visual, make the numbers the visual. For example, put "2.4 days" large, with "down from 3.2 last year (−25%)" in smaller text beneath it. That works well in a note or slide.

If you do add a chart, use two horizontal bars with direct value labels, a zero baseline, and no gridlines, legend, or title beyond "Median delivery time (days)". A bar chart has to start at zero. Truncating the axis would make a 25% drop look far bigger than it is.

A line chart with two points isn't worth it, because it implies a trend you can't see from two data points.

A chart becomes worthwhile if you have more data. Monthly or quarterly medians would show whether the improvement was steady or a one-time drop, and a line chart would fit that. Adding the spread (such as the 90th percentile) would show whether slow deliveries improved as much as typical ones.

For a quick internal note, I'd use the sentence.
