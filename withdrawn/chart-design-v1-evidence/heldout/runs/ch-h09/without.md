No, you don't need a chart. Two numbers fit in a sentence, and a chart would add more decoration than information.

Write it as a sentence with the change included:

> Median delivery time fell from 3.2 days to 2.4 days year over year, down 0.8 days (25%).

If you want something more visual, make the two numbers the headline. A small stat tile or a bolded line at the top of the note works, for example "**2.4 days** median delivery (was 3.2)". That's still text, and it reads faster than a two-bar chart.

A chart becomes worth it if you have more data points, such as:
- **Monthly or quarterly medians.** A line chart shows whether the improvement was steady, a one-time drop, or is still continuing.
- **The distribution, not just the median.** The median can hide a long tail, so a histogram or percentiles (p50/p90) would show whether slow deliveries improved too.
- **Breakdowns** by region, carrier, or product line, if the average hides differences between groups.

If you can pull the monthly numbers, a line chart would be the most useful addition. For a quick internal note, the sentence is enough.
