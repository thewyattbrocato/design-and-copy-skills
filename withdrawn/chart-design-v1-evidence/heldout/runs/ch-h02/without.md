**Replace the pie with a sorted horizontal bar chart, with the percentages labeled directly on the bars.**

Eleven slices is too many for a pie. Readers can't compare the small slices by angle or area, and a rainbow legend makes them look up each color. The 1–5% slices are especially hard, because 2% and 3% look almost identical in a pie.

**What to do**
- **Sort the bars largest to smallest.** Meals (38%) and Shelter (21%) then stand out immediately.
- **Put the program name and percentage right on each bar.** This removes the legend and the color lookup.
- **Use one color for all the bars.** Rainbow colors add nothing here, since the labels already identify each bar. Use a second color, such as gray, for the non-program rows. Or highlight only the one or two bars you want the board to notice.
- **Put "Other" last,** even if it isn't the smallest bar, as is conventional.
- **Add a dollar amount** next to each percentage if you have it. Boards usually want both.

**Optional: a grouped version**
Boards often care most about the program vs. overhead split. You could show two views:
1. **Headline:** Programs 92%, Admin 3%, Fundraising and Events 4%, Other 1%. This is a single stacked bar or just a sentence.
2. **Detail:** the sorted bar chart of the seven program areas.

Check the grouping against how your auditors or Form 990 classify Events. If it's fundraising-related, it belongs in that bucket.
