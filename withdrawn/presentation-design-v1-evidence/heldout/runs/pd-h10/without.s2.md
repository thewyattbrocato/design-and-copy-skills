I'd steer your VP away from "overwhelmingly." 9 of 14 is 64%, which is a clear majority but not overwhelming. The sample is also friendly: all 14 came from your own newsletter, so they're engaged customers. Someone in the room will likely point that out. If they catch the stretch, it can sink the approval. A strong, accurate headline is safer and still persuasive.

## Slide-by-slide (about 5 minutes)

**Slide 1: Headline (30 sec)**
> **9 of 14 hardware store owners (64%) say they'd use online restock ordering, if it connects to their register**

Subline: *Customer interviews, September, n=14 independent store owners*

**Slide 2: The demand signal (60 sec)**
A simple three-segment bar:
- **64%** (9) Yes, would use it
- **21%** (3) Maybe, depends on price
- **14%** (2) No, prefer calling their rep

Takeaway line: **86% are open to it. Only 2 of 14 said no.**

Speaker note: Say "open to it" for the 86%. Don't call it "want."

**Slide 3: The pain behind it (45 sec)**
> **5 of 14 (36%) unprompted said their distributor's ordering portal is slow or confusing**

Add one or two direct quotes if you have them. Quotes will carry more weight than the numbers here.

**Slide 4: Why register integration is the key (45 sec)**
The 9 yeses were conditional on the register connection. That condition makes the integration the core of the feature, not an extra. It's also what separates this from the portals owners already complain about.

**Slide 5: The ask and how we de-risk it (90 sec)**
> **Approve a 10-week build of restock ordering with register integration**

Then address the weak spots yourself:
- **Sample:** "These were newsletter subscribers, so they skew engaged. We'd expect lower interest in the broader base."
- **Price:** "3 of 14 said it depends on price. We'll run pricing validation in weeks 1–2, in parallel with the build."
- **Stated vs. actual behavior:** "Interviews show intent, not usage. We'll recruit 5–8 of the 'yes' owners as beta partners and track real orders."

Raising these yourself makes the ask more credible. It also gives leadership a basis to approve without feeling they're betting on thin data.

## Things to avoid on the slides
- "Overwhelmingly," "most owners," or "the market wants." You spoke to 14 people, and all of them were already your customers.
- Rounding 64% up to "two-thirds." It's close, but someone will do the math.
- Presenting the 64% without the register-integration condition. It's the strongest part of your story, and leaving it out would overstate the finding.

If your VP insists on stronger language, "a strong majority" or "clear majority" is defensible. "Overwhelmingly" isn't.
