I did not use "overwhelmingly" or a headline percentage that overstates the data. The data supports a strong but conditional majority, and the deck shows that.

**Why the headline changed:** 9 of 14 is 64%, which is a clear majority. But that "yes" was conditional on register integration, the sample is 14 people recruited from your own newsletter list, and the answers are stated intent. "Overwhelmingly" would not survive a question from anyone in the room who reads the interview notes. If leadership later finds that the number was inflated, it will cost more credibility than the build is worth.

## Slide 1: Title
**Restock ordering with register integration**
What 14 independent hardware owners told us in September

*Notes (30 sec):* We interviewed 14 independent hardware store owners about reordering inventory. We're asking for approval of a 10-week build, and this readout covers where the evidence is strong and where it's thin.

## Slide 2: Headline finding
**Nearly two-thirds (9 of 14) of owners would use online restock ordering if it connected to their register.**
- 3 of 14 (21%): maybe, depending on price
- 2 of 14 (14%): no, they prefer calling their rep

*Notes (45 sec):* The key word is "if." Most owners said yes, but only with register integration. Without it, interest drops. Integration is what we build around.

## Slide 3: Why owners are open to it
**5 of 14 (36%) said their distributor's ordering portal is slow or confusing, unprompted.**
- Over a third raised this on their own, which points to frustration with current tools
- Not a majority, so it supports the case rather than carrying it

*Notes (45 sec):* Over a third brought up their distributor portal without being asked. That's real friction, but we present it as supporting evidence.

## Slide 4: What this sample does and doesn't tell us
- **Small sample:** 14 interviews. One more "no" moves the headline share about 7 points.
- **Our own newsletter list:** These are engaged customers. Lapsed customers and non-readers are not represented.
- **Stated intent:** "Would use" predicts behavior; interviews usually overstate adoption.
- **Conditional:** The 64% assumes register integration.

*Notes (1 min):* This is a strong directional signal from a small, friendly group. It justifies a next step, not a market size estimate.

## Slide 5: The ask
**Approve a 10-week build of restock ordering with register integration.**
- Scope: register integration as the core requirement
- Success measure: owners placing orders through the feature after integration, not sign-ups

What would change our mind:
- Register integration proves unreliable or too costly in the first weeks
- Pilot owners aren't placing orders within the first month

*Notes (1 min):* Register integration is the gate. If it doesn't work, the case weakens, so we check it first.

## Slide 6: Open questions and next steps
- Would owners outside the newsletter list use this? Run 5 to 8 more interviews or a non-newsletter pilot.
- What price moves the 3 "maybe" owners to "yes"?
- Which register systems do owners use? This sets integration scope.
- **Checkpoint at week 6:** review pilot order data against the Slide 5 criteria.

*Notes (45 sec):* The interviews didn't answer these. The first two can run in parallel with the build, and the checkpoint tells us whether the bet is paying off.

**Choices I made that you can change:**
- I framed the ask as a 10-week build with a week-6 checkpoint and kill criteria. That's my recommendation to make approval easier to grant, not something you requested.
- If your VP insists on a stronger headline, the most defensible version is "Most owners would use this if it connected to their register." I'd avoid any number above 64%.
- Before Wednesday, I'd suggest the 5 to 8 non-newsletter interviews if the timeline allows, since that's the biggest gap leadership is likely to ask about.
