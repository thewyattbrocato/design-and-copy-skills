**5 minutes, 3 content slides, 1 appendix slide. Takeaway: a 10-week build of register-connected restock ordering is worth approving, because most owners want it only if it connects to their register.**

I didn't use "overwhelmingly." Nine of 14 is 64%, and 5 of 14 others said maybe or no. Leadership will check the number, and 64% from 14 people recruited from your own newsletter list will not survive that check if the headline says "overwhelmingly." The stronger case is the conditional one: the demand is real, and integration is what unlocks it. Here's a version that lands on that.

**Slide 1: The ask and the finding (about 2 min)**
- **Title:** "9 of 14 owners would use register-connected restock ordering. We're asking for a 10-week build."
- **Visual:** One large number, "64%," with "9 of 14 interviewed owners would use it if it connects to their register" beneath it.
- **Ask, on screen:** "Approve a 10-week build of restock ordering with register integration. Owner: [name]. Decision needed by: [date]."
- Words: about 35.

**Slide 2: Integration is the feature (about 2 min)**
- **Title:** "Owners want this only if it connects to their register, so integration is core scope, not a later add-on."
- **Visual:** One stacked bar of 14 interviews: 9 would use it with register integration, 3 maybe depending on price, 2 prefer calling their rep.
- **Caption:** "Interviews, September. n = 14, all recruited from our customer newsletter list."
- Words: about 30.

**Slide 3: The pain we'd replace (about 1 min)**
- **Title:** "More than a third of owners say their distributor's ordering portal is slow or confusing."
- **Visual:** "5 of 14" as a large number.
- **Caption:** Confirm before presenting whether these five raised it unprompted. If they did, say so; if the question prompted it, say that instead.
- Words: about 20.

**Appendix (not presented, for questions)**
- **Method:** 14 interviews with independent hardware store owners, September. All recruited from our customer newsletter, so they're likely more engaged with us than the wider market. Price sensitivity was only raised as "maybe"; we did not test price.
- **Backup for the likely question "Why not 'most owners'?":** 9 of 14 is a majority but not overwhelming. The two "no" answers and three "maybe" answers are the risk the build needs to reduce.

**Two things to settle before Wednesday:**
1. **The price question.** Three owners said "maybe, depends on price," and we have no price data. If the build plan doesn't include a pricing test, expect that to be the first question. I'd suggest adding a week-1 to week-2 check with a few owners on willingness to pay, and putting that in the ask. That's my recommendation, not something the data established; confirm it's in scope.
2. **Wider sample.** A newsletter-only sample is the easiest objection to raise. If there's time before Wednesday, a few calls with non-subscribers would strengthen slide 2 considerably. If not, the appendix caveat covers it.
