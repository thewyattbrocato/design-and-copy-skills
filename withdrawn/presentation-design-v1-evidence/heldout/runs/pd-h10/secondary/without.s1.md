# Restock Ordering with Register Integration: Customer Interview Readout

5 minutes, 7 slides, with speaker notes.

---

**Slide 1: Title**

**Restock Ordering with Register Integration**
Customer interview findings and a request to approve a 10-week build

*Notes:* We talked to 14 independent hardware store owners in September. This is what they told us, what we don't know yet, and what we're asking you to approve.

---

**Slide 2: The ask**

**Approve a 10-week build of restock ordering that connects to the store's register system.**

- Scope: restock ordering, with register integration as the core requirement
- Duration: 10 weeks
- Decision needed Wednesday

*Notes:* The ask is specific. We're asking for one feature, built around the condition owners named most often: it has to connect to their register.

---

**Slide 3: What we heard**

**9 of 14 owners (64%) would use online restock ordering if it connected to their register.**

| Response | Owners | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Yes, if it connects to register | 9 | 64% |
| Maybe, depends on price | 3 | 21% |
| No, they prefer calling their rep | 2 | 14% |

*Notes:* Nine of 14 said yes, with one condition: the register integration. Three said maybe, and price decides it for them. Two prefer calling their rep. With 14 people, treat the percentage as a directional signal, not a market size.

---

**Slide 4: The pain behind it**

**5 of 14 owners (36%) said their current distributor's ordering portal is slow or confusing.**

- For many owners, the current option is phone calls to their rep
- Portal friction is the frustration we'd be solving

*Notes:* Five owners raised the distributor portal as a problem. That also helps explain the two "no" answers: the existing digital option isn't good enough for them.

---

**Slide 5: What this does not tell us**

- **All 14 came from our customer newsletter list.** These are engaged customers who already chose to hear from us. Non-customers may be less interested.
- **Stated intent, not observed behavior.** "Would use" is not "would pay for" or "would change ordering habits."
- **Price is untested.** Three owners said the answer depends on price.
- **Small sample.** 14 interviews find patterns; they don't size a market.

*Notes:* The recruiting source matters most. Everyone we spoke with already knows us, so 64% is probably an upper bound for independent stores in general. That doesn't kill the case, but it changes how we should build and measure.

---

**Slide 6: Risks and dependencies**

- **Register integration is the critical path.** The nine "yes" answers depend on it.
- **Pricing is undecided.** The three "maybe" answers depend on it.
- **Adoption is unproven.** We have intent data, not usage data.

*Notes:* The biggest risk is integration taking longer than 10 weeks. Confirm feasibility early.

---

**Slide 7: Recommendation**

**Approve the 10-week build, with a checkpoint before full rollout.**

1. Weeks 1–2: confirm register integration feasibility with the register systems our owners use
2. Weeks 3–10: build restock ordering
3. Pilot with a small group of stores, including some not on our newsletter list
4. Test pricing with the "maybe" group before launch
5. Decide on broader rollout based on pilot usage, not interview intent

*Notes:* We're asking for the build, structured so we learn fast. The two checks that matter are early integration feasibility and pilot usage data.

---

## Headline options

"Overwhelmingly" would overstate the result. Nine of 14 is a clear majority, not an overwhelming one, and the 36% who find distributor portals frustrating is a real signal too. These headlines are accurate and still strong:

- "Nearly two-thirds of independent hardware owners would use restock ordering if it connects to their register"
- "9 of 14 owners want restock ordering, on one condition: it connects to their register"
- "Most owners want restock ordering, and register integration decides it"

If the VP wants a percentage on the slide, 64% works, but put n=14 and "recruited from our newsletter list" on the same slide. If a leader sees the percentage without that context, they may later conclude the market is bigger than the evidence shows, and the pilot in Slide 7 is what protects the approval if they do.
