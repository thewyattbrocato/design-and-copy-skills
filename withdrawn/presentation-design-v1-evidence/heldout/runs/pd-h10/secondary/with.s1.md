I couldn't use "overwhelmingly." 9 of 14 is 64%, a clear majority but not overwhelming, and the result holds only if the feature connects to the register. The sample is also all from your newsletter list, so it skews toward engaged customers. A VP who says "overwhelmingly" in front of product leadership invites the first question to be about the sample, so I built the deck around the accurate version. It's still a strong case.

**Slide 1 (opening, ~1 min):** Nine of 14 owners would use restock ordering if it connected to their register.
- Visual: the 14 as a row of dots, 9 filled, 3 half, 2 empty.
- Spoken: who we talked to, what we're asking for, and the three points that follow.

**Slide 2 (~1.5 min):** Most owners want ordering that works with their register, not another portal.
- Visual: stacked bar: 9 yes if integrated (64%), 3 maybe depending on price (21%), 2 prefer calling their rep (14%).
- Label: "n = 14 independent hardware store owners, September interviews."

**Slide 3 (~1.5 min):** Five of 14 owners said their distributor's ordering portal is slow or confusing.
- Visual: one big number, "5 of 14 (36%)," with a short quote from an interview, if you have one with consent.
- This is the pain the feature solves, and it's the reason to build now.

**Slide 4 (~1 min):** The risk is price, and a minority will never switch.
- Visual: the 3 "maybe" and 2 "no" as a simple split.
- Spoken: the 3 "maybe" owners need a price answer before the build pays off. Pricing is a open question for this build.

**Slide 5 (close, ~0.5 min):** Approve a 10-week build of restock ordering with register integration.
- Owner: [name]. Decision date: [date]. Next checkpoint: [date].
- Stays up during questions.
