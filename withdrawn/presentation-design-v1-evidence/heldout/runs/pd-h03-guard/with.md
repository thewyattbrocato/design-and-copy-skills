The deck mostly works. Read in order, the titles carry the argument and the ask, the numbers add up (31 + 9 = 40, and 3 + 4 + 2 = 9), and each slide has one idea. Six slides is about right for 10 minutes. These are the things I'd fix before you send it, most important first.

**Fix before sending**
1. **Check the March 4 date.** March 4 isn't a Saturday in 2026 or 2027. If distribution is on Saturdays, either the date or the day is wrong, and a lead will notice.
2. **Say whether March 4 is the training Saturday.** Slide 4's notes say you train on the first Saturday, and slide 5 says the first shift is March 4. People will wonder if those are the same day.
3. **Make the ask clear for this audience.** Slide 5 says "Pick a role today," but the room is volunteer leads. Do you want them to sign up themselves, recruit people from their teams, or both? Say which in the title or the first line you speak. For example: "Sign up, or name one person you'll bring, today."
4. **Replace slide 6.** A "Thank you" slide wastes your last screen. Put the phone number and logo on slide 5 and end there, so the sign-up QR stays up during questions. Thank the 31 out loud. If you want a closing slide, make it a claim, such as "31 people already make Saturdays work. Help us add 9."

**Worth tightening**
- **Slide 2's title and chart don't match.** The title says families leave because of the wait, but the chart only shows wait times. The 7% who leave before being served appears only in your notes. Put "7% left unserved" on the slide, or the title will claim something the slide doesn't show.
- **Slide 1 says "by March" and slide 5 says March 4.** Use the same deadline in both, such as "by March 4."
- **Slide 3's "costs no money."** Your notes only say the church approved the door and tables. If that covers everything, keep the claim. Otherwise say "no new cost to the pantry."
- **Slide 4 and slide 5 both list the three roles.** That's fine for a short deck. If you want to cut, put the counts (3, 4, 2) on slide 5 and drop the repeat on slide 4.

**For the church hall**
- Make the QR code large, at least about a quarter of the slide, because people will scan it from their seats.
- Use high contrast, since church hall projectors are often dim.
- Keep the text on slide 2's bars large enough to read from the back.
- You're sending this tonight, so someone reading it alone will see the slides without your notes. Slides 2 and 3 especially should make sense without you talking.
