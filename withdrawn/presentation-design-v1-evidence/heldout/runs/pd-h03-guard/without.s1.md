The deck is in good shape. The numbers add up (31 + 9 = 40, and 3 + 4 + 2 = 9), and the titles tell the story on their own. These are the things I'd fix or check before you send it.

**Fix before sending**
- **March 4 may not be a Saturday.** If you mean March 2027, March 4 is a Thursday, and the first Saturday is March 6. Slide 5 says the first shift is March 4, and slide 4's notes say you'll train on the first Saturday. Confirm the date, and make sure the training day and the first shift don't contradict each other.
- **Put the QR code on the last slide too, or end on slide 5.** If you close on "Thank you," the sign-up code is off the screen during Q&A, which is when people are most likely to scan it. Also print the short sign-up URL under the QR code. Projectors in bright halls wash out QR codes, so make it big with a white background.

**Worth tightening**
- **Slide 2 highlight:** The title and notes say families leave at 10 and 11, but only the 11am bar is highlighted. Highlighting both bars matches your message. Label the values as minutes ("52 min"), not bare numbers.
- **Slide 1 wait times:** "About 25 minutes" is a projection, while the 50 is measured. Say "we expect" so nobody holds you to it later.
- **Slide 4 lifting reassurance:** "No one has to lift heavy boxes unless they choose to" is in your notes, but it's the line most likely to settle a hesitant volunteer. Put it on the slide as a short line. Your notes won't be visible to anyone reading the deck on their own tonight.
- **Slide 3 "costs no money":** This is a strong claim. Check that it covers everything, such as signage and supplies, or soften it to "no new spending needed."

**One question**
- **Who is the ask aimed at?** Slide 5 says "Pick a role today," which reads as if the leads themselves will sign up. If you also want them to recruit from their teams, add a line saying so. Something like "Sign up, or bring one person with you" would do it.

Ten minutes for six slides is comfortable, at roughly 1.5 minutes each, with a little time left over for questions.
