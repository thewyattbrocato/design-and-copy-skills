**Fix before sending**

1. **March 4 isn't a Saturday.** Slide 5 says "your first shift is March 4," but the distribution is on Saturdays. In 2027, March 4 is a Thursday and March 6 is the Saturday. Confirm the year and the date on the pantry calendar.
2. **"9 shifts" vs. "9 more volunteers."** Slide 1 counts volunteers, and slide 4's title says "the 9 shifts we need to fill." If someone signs up for more than one shift, these mean different things. Use "9 volunteer spots" or "9 volunteers" on both slides. Check the role counts too: 3 + 4 + 2 = 9, so they agree.
3. **Slide 2's title claims cause.** "The wait is the reason families leave" is stronger than the data, which shows that 7% left and that the waits were long. Someone in the room may ask how you know. Try "Waits of 50 minutes at 10 and 11am send families home unserved." Keep the 7% for the speaker to say.

**Worth a tweak**

4. **Slide 1 notes: "about 25 minutes."** Say "we estimate about 25," since it's a projection, not a measurement.
5. **Slide 3 title: "costs no money."** Only use this if the church's approval covers everything. If volunteers' time or supplies have any cost, say "costs us nothing extra" or name what's covered.
6. **Slide 4 notes: "no one has to lift heavy boxes unless they choose to."** Check whether carry-out involves lifting. If it does, a volunteer may take that as a promise you can't keep. Say "bagging and carry-out are lighter than they look" or describe the actual load.
7. **Slide 6 is a thank-you slide.** The closing should carry the ask. Thanking the 31 is right, but you could make the title "31 of us already show up. We need 9 more." with the logo and phone number below. That leaves the ask on screen during questions.
