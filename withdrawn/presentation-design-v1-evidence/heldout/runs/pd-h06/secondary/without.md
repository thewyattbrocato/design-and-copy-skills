**Use B.** It's the only one that gives the council a reason to care about Alder Street specifically.

- **A** is the standard opener. It's polite but tells them nothing, and everyone who speaks before you will open the same way. Your name and group can come after the story.
- **C** is generic. "Cities across the country" could describe any street in any town, and council members have heard that statistic many times. Alder doesn't appear until the end.
- **B** puts a concrete, local event in the first ten seconds. "She's fine" keeps it from sounding like a scare tactic, and "the street she was crossing is the one I'm asking you to change" connects the story directly to your request.

To make B work in three minutes:

1. **State the ask within the next 20 seconds.** For example: "I'm asking you to approve a protected bike lane on Alder between 4th and 12th." Don't leave the request for the end.
2. **Introduce yourself briefly** after the ask: your name, and that you're with Cedar Hill Neighbors for Safe Streets.
3. **Use one or two supporting points** from C, such as a traffic-safety statistic or a local business benefit, but say them plainly and keep them short.
4. **Close by repeating the ask** in one sentence.

Before Tuesday, confirm the incident is documented somewhere, such as a police report, school notice, or local news story. Council members sometimes ask staff about anecdotes, and a verifiable detail holds up better. Also ask the family whether they're comfortable with the story being told publicly, since it's about a child. You can leave out the child's name either way.
