**Use B.**

It's the only opening that makes the council care about the street before asking them for anything. Each of the others gives them a reason to tune out:

- **A** is the standard preamble. Council members hear it from every speaker, and it spends your first 15 seconds on information they already expect.
- **C** is a claim they've heard many times, stated in general terms. It doesn't say anything about Alder Street, and it could be said about any street in any city.
- **B** puts a specific child and a specific place in the first sentence. It also makes the stakes concrete, and its last line points straight at the request.

Two adjustments:

1. **Make the ask explicit within about 20 seconds.** B's last line implies the request, but say it plainly: "Tonight I'm asking you to approve a protected bike lane on Alder Street between 4th and 12th." Then give your name and group in one sentence, such as "I'm Dana Ortega with Cedar Hill Neighbors for Safe Streets."
2. **Confirm the incident is accurate and reportable.** If a council member or staffer checks the details, the story needs to hold up. If there's a police report, school incident record, or news item, know where it is. Keep the child anonymous, as you have it, and get the family's OK before using even the general details.

Then use the remaining time on the specifics: the crash data for that corridor, what the protected lane would look like between 4th and 12th, and what you're asking the council to do next.
