**Pick B.** It's the only one of the three that gives the council a reason to care about this street before you ask for anything.

- **A** is what every speaker opens with. Council members have heard "good evening, my name is..." dozens of times, and it spends your first 10 seconds on information that's on the agenda.
- **C** sounds like a policy memo. The claims are general, unsourced, and easy to dismiss with "that's not our city." It also gives the council nothing to picture.
- **B** is concrete and local. It names the street, describes a real event, and sets up the ask in the last sentence. The contrast between "She's fine" and "the street she was crossing" makes the case without you having to argue it.

Two things to handle before Tuesday:

1. **Get the family's permission.** The child is a minor, and the story is public once you say it at a meeting. Ask the parents if you can use it, and whether they'd be comfortable with you using her name or would rather you say "a nine-year-old."
2. **Verify the details.** Confirm the date, the location, and that the van was a delivery vehicle. A council member or staffer may check, and an inaccurate detail will overshadow the rest of your three minutes.

After the opening, move to the ask in the next sentence or two, something like: "Tonight I'm asking you to approve a protected bike lane on Alder Street between 4th and 12th." Then give your one or two strongest supporting facts and end with a specific request.
