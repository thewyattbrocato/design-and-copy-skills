Use **B**.

**Why B works:** It puts a specific child on a specific street, and the council can picture it. In three minutes, a story they remember will do more than a list of statistics. "She's fine" lowers the tension and keeps it from sounding like you're trying to shock them. The last line ties the story to your request, so the ask is in your first 20 seconds.

**Why not A:** It spends your opening on your name, your group, and a promise to "walk through" a proposal. Council members hear that kind of opening all night, and it gives them no reason to listen closely. Most councils make you state your name when you sign in or at the podium anyway. If you're unsure, put your name and group after the hook: "I'm Dana Ortega, with Cedar Hill Neighbors for Safe Streets."

**Why not C:** It's a general claim about "cities across the country," with no source and no connection to Alder Street yet. It's also the easiest to wave off, because council members hear claims like it from every project's supporters. Your evidence is still useful, but it belongs in the middle, after they care.

**Tips for using B:**
- Check that the details are right and that the family is comfortable with you telling it. If you can't confirm that, say "a child" and leave out the age and the van.
- Follow with your name and group, then the ask: protected bike lane, Alder between 4th and 12th, approve it Tuesday.
- Put one or two of the best facts from C in the middle, ideally with local numbers if you have them.
- Close by repeating the ask.
