The problem is density, not slide count. Each slide reads like a document, and 40 people in a room can't read and listen at the same time. A good rule for this room: one headline that states the takeaway, at most four short items, and one visual. Move the rest to speaker notes or the appendix.

**Slide-by-slide**

1. **Title:** Keep it. Add the date.
2. **Overview:** Cut it to one line, or drop it. "Velocity, shipped work, quality, one incident, Q4" can be said out loud. Remove "Questions welcome" and "Appendix has more detail" since you can say those too.
3. **Velocity:** Make the headline "Velocity up ~15% over Q2" (41.8 vs. 36.5). Show a bar chart of the six sprints with a dashed Q2 average line. Annotate Sprint 17 as "2 engineers out, holiday week" right on the bar. Pull out two callouts: commitment accuracy 84% (from 71%) and carry-over 9 → 4 per sprint. Put the sprint-by-sprint numbers in notes.
4. **Shipped features:** Seven bullets is too many to absorb. Feature three or four with a screenshot each, such as the driver-assignment modal with conflict warnings, saved filters, and the 50k-row CSV export. Name the rest in one footer line, "Also shipped: bulk delivery-window edit, dispatcher dark mode, subcontractor roles, notification queue rewrite." Screenshots work well on a big screen.
5. **Quality:** Use three big numbers: escaped defects 31 → 17, median P2 fix 6.2 → 3.5 days, dispatch coverage 58% → 71%. Move the flaky-suite replacement to notes. Keep the CSV gap, since it's candid and sets up the Q4 plan, but say it in one sentence.
6. **Aug 29 incident:** This is the slide people will remember, so make it clear. Headline: "Aug 29: 3-hour notification delay." Big number: 1,140 delayed driver alerts. Then a four-step timeline: cause → same-day fix → queue-depth alert (next week) → load-test before future changes. Keep the root cause to one sentence, and describe the config as a process gap, not a person's mistake.
7. **Q4 plan:** Cap it at four items, each with an owner or quarter-timing. Move "investigate monthly releases" to a "still exploring" line. Check the load-testing follow-up from slide 6. It's not in this list, so either add it or say it's now standing practice.
8. **Thank you / Questions:** Make it "Questions?" with your contact info.

**Content checks before Monday**

- **Hiring a senior engineer:** Decide whether this belongs in a product-group deck. If it's an internal headcount topic, cut it.
- **Load testing:** Slide 6 promises it, and slide 7 doesn't include it. Reconcile the two.
- **Sprint 17:** Explain the dip once, on the chart. Don't repeat it.

**Presenting to 40 people on a big screen**

- Keep body text at 24pt or larger, and no more than about six words per line.
- Put the detail in speaker notes, and send the full deck as a linked appendix or handout afterward.
- Practice to a 15-minute target. If the slides are clean, the talk should take about that long.
