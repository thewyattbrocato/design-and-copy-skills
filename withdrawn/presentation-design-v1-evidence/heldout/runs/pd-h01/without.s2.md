# Hollis & Rye board deck: Dunmore commissary decision
**Format:** 7 slides, about 13 minutes of talking, leaving about 2 for questions. Say "central kitchen" the first time you say "commissary" for the two board members outside food.

---

## Slide 1: The ask
**Title:** Approve the Dunmore commissary lease and build-out budget
**On the slide:**
- Lease: $9,400/month
- Build-out: $610,000
- Ready to produce about 9 months after approval

**Say (~1 min):**
"I'm asking for one decision today: approve the Dunmore lease and its $610,000 build-out budget. In the next few minutes I'll show you why we're running out of kitchen, what the alternative is, and why I think Dunmore is the better answer."

---

## Slide 2: How we operate
**Title:** Two kitchens feed all 11 cafes
**On the slide:** A simple diagram with two boxes, Eastside and Mill Street, and arrows to 11 cafes.

**Say (~1.5 min):**
"Hollis & Rye has 11 cafes. We don't cook in each one. Two central kitchens, which we call commissaries, make the food and deliver it. They're our factories. If they're full, we can't grow, no matter how good the cafes are."

---

## Slide 3: We're nearly full
**Title:** Eastside is at 94% on weekdays
**On the slide:** A two-bar chart: Eastside 94%, Mill Street 81%.

**Say (~1.5 min):**
"Utilization is how much of a kitchen's weekday capacity we use. Eastside is at 94%. Above roughly 90%, there's no room for a bad day, an equipment failure, or a big order. Mill Street has some room at 81%, but not enough to cover what's coming."

---

## Slide 4: The cost of being full is already showing
**Title:** We turned away 17 wholesale orders, about $210,000
**On the slide:** Big number: **$210K** in revenue declined over the last two quarters. Smaller text: 17 orders.

**Say (~1.5 min):**
"This isn't hypothetical. In the last two quarters we said no to 17 wholesale orders, roughly $210,000 in revenue. That's demand we already had and couldn't serve, before we've opened a single new cafe."

---

## Slide 5: Growth makes it worse
**Title:** 3 new cafes in 14 months means about 30% more output by next fall
**On the slide:** Timeline with today, +9 months (Dunmore could be producing), +14 months (3rd cafe open), and "next fall" marked.

**Say (~1.5 min):**
"We plan three new cafes over the next 14 months. Together they need about 30% more output than we make today, by next fall. So we have to add capacity, and the question is how."

## Slide 6: Two options
**Title:** A second shift is cheaper and faster. Dunmore is the one that clears the 30%.

| | **A: Lease Dunmore** | **B: Second shift at Eastside** |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | $610,000 build-out | None |
| Ongoing cost | $9,400/mo rent (about $113K/yr) | About $180,000/yr labor |
| Time to benefit | About 9 months | Faster (confirm how fast) |
| Added output | **[Dunmore's capacity: fill in]** | Tops out around +26% |
| Covers the 30% need? | **[Fill in]** | **No, about 4 points short** |

**Say (~3 min):**
"Option B is a second shift at Eastside. It costs about $180,000 a year in labor and tops out around 26% more output. It's cheaper up front and quicker. But it falls short of the 30% we need, and it puts even more load on our one kitchen that's already at 94%. If that kitchen has a problem, a lot of the business stops.

Option A is Dunmore, a third kitchen. It costs more up front: $610,000 to build and $9,400 a month in rent. It takes about nine months before it produces. But it gives us room beyond 30% and a third kitchen, so no single site can stop us."

## Gaps to fill before Thursday
Your notes leave these open, and a board will likely ask about each.

1. **Is the +26% for Eastside alone or for the whole system?** If it's Eastside alone, it's well under 26% for the company, and Option B looks much weaker. Check this first, because the slide 6 comparison depends on it.
2. **How much will Dunmore produce?** The deck says it clears the 30%, but I have no capacity figure. You need one number.
3. **Lease term and rent escalators.** Rent of $9,400 a month is about $113K a year, but a 5-year or 10-year commitment is a very different decision.
4. **Dunmore's running costs.** Rent and build-out are only part of it. Staffing and utilities are missing, and so is the comparison with Option B's $180K a year.
5. **Margin on the $210K.** That figure is revenue, not profit, so I can't give you a payback period. If you can get the margin, add it, because the board will want to know when this pays back.
6. **Timing risk.** If the nine months starts at approval, Dunmore is producing around late summer 2027, which is close to "next fall." Be ready for the question "what if the build runs late?"
7. **Mill Street at 81%.** Someone will ask why it can't absorb more. Have a one-line answer, such as product mix, delivery routes, or the headroom it has.

## Likely questions
- **"Why not do B now and A later?"** A second shift could bridge the 9 months. That's a reasonable answer, but you'd be paying about $180K a year on top of Dunmore. Decide your position before Thursday.
- **"What's the downside if we don't do this?"** About $420K a year in turned-away revenue at the current rate ($210K × 2), plus the new cafes starting short of product.
- **"What if the new cafes open late?"** Have a view on whether Dunmore is still needed, or only needed later.
