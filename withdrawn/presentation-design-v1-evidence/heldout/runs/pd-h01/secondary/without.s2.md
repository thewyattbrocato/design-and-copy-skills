## Appendix: Assumptions and math
- Lost revenue run rate: $210,000 ÷ 2 quarters × 4 = **~$420,000/yr**
- Option B break-even: $180,000 ÷ $420,000 = **43% contribution margin** on the declined orders. If margin is above that, B pays back on those orders alone. *(Revenue, not profit. Confirm margin.)*
- Option B over 14 months: $180,000 × 14/12 = **~$210,000**
- Dunmore rent: $9,400 × 12 = **$112,800/yr**. Over 14 months: **~$131,600**
- Dunmore 14-month total: $610,000 + $131,600 = **~$741,600**, before equipment, staffing, and contingency (unless already in the $610k)

## Fill before Thursday
Your numbers don't yet show Dunmore beats B. These are the gaps a finance-minded board member will probe:

1. **Dunmore's output capacity.** The case depends on it. If it's not in the 30% math, the deck doesn't yet answer "is this enough?"
2. **Is the +26% for Eastside alone or company-wide?** If Eastside produces about half our volume, B adds roughly 13% company-wide, not 26%. This is the biggest risk to the B comparison.
3. **B's time to output.** If it can start in two to three months, B is a strong bridge and the deck should say so.
4. **Margin on the 17 declined orders.** This determines whether B pays back quickly.
5. **Lease terms:** rent start date, term, escalations, free rent during build-out, and whether the $610k includes equipment, permits, and contingency.
6. **Weekend capacity.** Utilization is weekday only. If weekends have slack, that changes the Eastside picture.
