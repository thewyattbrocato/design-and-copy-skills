# Board deck: Commissary capacity and the Dunmore lease
**15 minutes: 7 slides, about 2 minutes each, with the ask on slide 2.**

I put the ask up front because two of your five members aren't in food. They'll follow the rest better if they know where it's going.

---

## Slide 1: Title
**Growing to 14 cafes: securing the kitchen capacity to supply them**

**Say (30 sec):** "Today I'm asking you to approve a lease and a build-out budget for a third production kitchen. I'll show you why we need it, what the alternative is, and why I think this is the right choice."

---

## Slide 2: The ask
**Approve:**
1. The Dunmore site lease: $9,400/month
2. The build-out budget: $610,000

**Say (1 min):** "A commissary is our central kitchen. It prepares food that all 11 cafes sell. Think of it as our factory. We have two, and they're nearly full. I'm asking for approval today because Dunmore takes about 9 months to start producing, and we need the extra capacity by next fall."

## Slide 3: We're nearly out of capacity
**Weekday utilization**

**Say (2 min):** "Eastside is at 94% on weekdays. For the non-food folks, that's like a restaurant that's full every night. There's no room to absorb a busy week, an equipment failure, or a new customer. Mill Street has more room at 81%, but that's not enough to cover what's coming. One thing I'll be ready to address: [explain why Mill Street's 19% can't simply absorb the overflow, e.g. equipment, product mix, delivery distance]."

## Slide 4: Growth and lost revenue
**Left side: what's coming**
- 3 new cafes in the next 14 months (11 → 14)
- About **30% more output** needed by next fall

**Right side: what it's already costing us**
- **17 wholesale orders declined** in the last two quarters
- About **$210,000 in revenue** turned away

**Say (2 min):** "Capacity is already costing us money. We said no to 17 wholesale orders in six months, about $210,000 in revenue. That's before the new cafes open. By next fall we need roughly 30% more output than we make today."

*Note: $210K is revenue, not profit. Say so if asked. Add your margin on it if you have one.*

---

## Slide 5: Two options
| | **A: Dunmore site** | **B: Second shift at Eastside** |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | $610,000 build-out | None quoted |
| Ongoing cost | $9,400/mo rent (about $113K/yr) | About $180,000/yr added labor |
| Time to capacity | About 9 months | Faster (confirm timing) |
| Output gain | [Dunmore's capacity: see gap below] | Tops out around **+26%** |
| Meets the 30% need? | Yes, if sized for it | **No, falls about 4 points short** |

**Say (3 min):** "Option B is cheaper up front and faster. But it has a ceiling. A second shift at Eastside tops out around 26% more output, and we need about 30%. So it falls short even in the best case. It also puts more weight on our busiest kitchen, which is already at 94%. Option A costs more, but it's the only one that clears the bar. It also gives us a third site, so one kitchen going down doesn't stop supply to the cafes."

---

## Slide 6: Why A, and how we manage the risk
- B alone doesn't reach the 30% target
- A adds a third site, which means resilience as well as capacity
- The 9-month build means a decision this week, not next quarter
- **Bridge:** if needed, run a limited second shift at Eastside while Dunmore is built
- **Risks:** build-out overruns, delays past 9 months, and the lease term (see below)

**Say (2 min):** "If we wait, the 9 months pushes Dunmore past the point we need it. The risk of A is a late or over-budget build. The risk of B is that we hit the ceiling and are back here in a year. I recommend A, with a short-term second shift as a bridge if the timeline slips."

---

## Slide 7: Decision and next steps
**Requested today:** approve the Dunmore lease and the $610,000 build-out budget.
**Next:** sign lease → begin build-out → production in about 9 months → in place for the new cafes next fall.

**Say (1 min):** "To recap: we're nearly full, we're already turning away revenue, and the cheaper option can't reach our target. I'm asking for approval of the lease and the build-out budget. Happy to take questions."

## Gaps to fill before Thursday
These are the questions the board is most likely to ask. I can't answer them from what you gave me.

1. **Dunmore's output.** How much capacity does it add? The whole case rests on it covering the 30% gap, and the deck doesn't say so yet.
2. **Lease terms.** How long is the lease, and what are the escalators and exit terms? A $610K build-out inside a short lease is a red flag.
3. **Total cost of A.** Rent and build-out are covered, but Dunmore's own labor and operating costs aren't. Without them, A and B aren't a fair comparison. Rough math from your numbers is about $610K plus $113K/yr rent, against $180K/yr for B.
4. **What "+26%" applies to.** Is it Eastside's output or total company output? If it's Eastside only, B covers even less than it appears to.
5. **Timing.** Today is late October, so "next fall" is about 11 months out. A 9-month build leaves only about 2 months of slack. Say this plainly, and have a plan for slippage.
6. **Payback.** Even a rough view of how $610K is recovered, using wholesale revenue and the new cafes' supply needs, will help the non-food members.
