## Slide 2: How the business works
**Title:** Two kitchens feed all 11 cafes
- Hollis & Rye has 11 cafes
- Two commissary kitchens, Eastside and Mill Street, produce for all of them
- A commissary is a central kitchen. It makes the food once, and the cafes finish and sell it.
- The kitchens are our production capacity. Cafes can't grow faster than they can.

**Say (≈1:00):** "Our cafes don't each cook from scratch. Two central kitchens make most of what we sell and deliver it to all 11 locations. Think of them as our factories. That matters because every new cafe and every wholesale customer draws on the same two kitchens."

## Slide 3: The kitchens are full
**Title:** Eastside is at 94% on weekdays, and we're already turning away revenue
- Weekday utilization: **Eastside 94%**, **Mill Street 81%**
- Last two quarters: **17 wholesale orders declined, about $210,000 in revenue**
- *(Chart: two bars, 94% and 81%.)*

**Say (≈1:30):** "Eastside runs at 94% on weekdays. That leaves almost no room for a bad day, an equipment failure, or a big order. Mill Street has more room at 81%, but not enough to absorb what's coming. The cost is already real. In the last two quarters we said no to 17 wholesale orders, about $210,000 in revenue. That's revenue we could have booked today with no new cafes."

## Slide 4: Growth makes it worse
**Title:** Three new cafes need about 30% more output by next fall

- Without new capacity we keep declining wholesale orders, and we put the new cafes at risk too

**Say (≈1:00):** "We plan to open three cafes over the next 14 months. Together they need about 30% more output by next fall. We're nearly full now, so this isn't a matter of squeezing a little more out of what we have."

## Gaps to close before Thursday

1. **"Why not use Mill Street's spare 19%?"** An outside board member is likely to ask this. Have an answer, such as that 19% is about X% of total output and doesn't cover the 30%.
2. **Is "+26%" for Eastside alone or for the whole system?** If it's Eastside only, Option B delivers much less than 26% overall, which strengthens your case. If it's system-wide, B is closer than the slide implies. Confirm which, and say so out loud.
3. **How much does Dunmore add?** You have its cost and timing but not its capacity. The board needs one line like "Dunmore adds X% of current output."
4. **What does it cost to run Dunmore?** The $9,400 rent and $610,000 build-out leave out its labor and utilities. Option B's $180,000 is mostly labor, so the board may think A is much cheaper than it is. Either add an estimate or be ready to say you don't have one yet.
5. **When does rent start?** If it starts at signing, about $85,000 is paid before the kitchen produces anything.
6. **Is $210,000 revenue or profit?** Say "revenue" every time. Someone will ask about margin, so have a rough number ready.
7. **Lease length.** The board will want to know the term and any exit terms.
