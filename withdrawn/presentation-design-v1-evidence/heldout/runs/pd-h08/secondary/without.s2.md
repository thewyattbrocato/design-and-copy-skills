Keep **10 slides** for a 10-minute slot, about one minute each. The committee already knows the project, so the deck is mostly about cost, value, and the phase 2 decision.

**Main deck, in order:**

1. **Title** (25 sec): state the decision in the subtitle, e.g., "Requesting approval of phase 2 funding."
2. **Monthly active patients, trend** (10): shows the portal is being used.
3. **Front-desk call volume, before and after** (12): the most concrete operational result.
4. **Staff time saved, estimate and method** (13): the value case. Say the estimate's method out loud, since executives will ask how it was derived.
5. **Patient satisfaction scores** (14): patient-side evidence. Cut this if you're running long.
6. **Cost to date vs. budget** (19): establishes that phase 1 stayed within bounds, which makes the funding request more credible.
7. **Phase 2 scope** (20)
8. **Phase 2 cost and timeline** (21)
9. **Risks if we don't fund phase 2** (22): the consequence of saying no.
10. **The ask: approve phase 2 funding** (25)

**Cut, but keep in a backup appendix for questions:**
- Agenda, team, why we started, vendor selection, architecture, and timeline (2–7): the committee knows the project. If someone asks about the vendor or architecture, use the backup.
- Adoption by clinic table and top-3 vs. bottom-3 comparison (8, 9): useful if someone asks which clinics need help, and it could support a phase 2 rollout plan.
- Patient quotes (15): good color, but it's a cut if you need the time.
- Three problems and login fix (16, 17): prepare the login fix answer in case someone asks whether the problems are resolved.
- Security and privacy review (18): this matters for patient data. If the committee has not raised it, keep it in the backup and have the one-line result ready.
- Alternatives considered (23): the likely question is "why this option and not another?" Keep it ready.
- Roadmap beyond phase 2 (24) and thank-you/questions (26): use the closing slide as Q&A, but you don't need to show it in the main deck.

**One suggestion:** consider moving the ask (25) to slide 2, right after the title. Executives often decide faster when they know what is being requested before the supporting data. If you do this, the ask slide can also serve as the closing slide.
