**Ten minutes, five slides after the title, and the takeaway is "the portal is working, and phase 2 is worth funding."** The committee needs evidence of value, the cost of the next step, and the cost of waiting, then a decision. Titles are my suggested claims. Fill the bracketed numbers from your data.

## Keep, in this order

1. **Title (1).** Outside the count. Put the ask in the subtitle: "Patient portal: requesting approval for phase 2." Speak the takeaway in the first minute.
2. **Adoption (10).** Retitle: "Monthly active patients grew from [X] to [Y] across 14 clinics." About 2 min.
3. **Staff time (13).** Retitle: "The portal saves front desks about [N] hours a week." Keep the estimate and method as a one-line footnote. About 2 min.
4. **Phase 2 cost and timeline (21).** Retitle: "Phase 2 costs $[X] and finishes by [date]." Add the scope in one line, since the scope slide (20) moves to backup. About 2 min.
5. **Risks if we don't fund (22).** Retitle: "Without phase 2, [N] clinics stay on manual intake and [specific cost]." About 2 min.
6. **The ask (25).** The closing slide. Make it "Approve $[X] for phase 2, owner [name], start [date]." It stays up during questions. About 1.5 min.

That is five content slides at roughly two minutes each, with the ask as the last one.

## If time shrinks further

Drop slide 10 and fold its growth number into slide 13's claim. Then fold slide 22's risk into the ask slide's subtitle. That gives a three-slide talk: value, cost, ask.

## Assumptions

- You are presenting live, and the committee will decide in the room, so the risk and backup slides matter.
- Slide 10's claim assumes the trend is up. If adoption is flat or uneven, the top-3 vs bottom-3 slide (9) should move into the main deck, because phase 2 becomes a story about lagging clinics.
