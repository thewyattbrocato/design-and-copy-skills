# A simple test for each piece of content

Ask these three questions in order:

1. **Does the room need to see this to follow the point or make a decision?** Put it on a slide.
2. **Is it the reasoning, a story, or an emphasis that makes the point land?** Say it out loud.
3. **Is it evidence someone might want to check or look up later?** Put it in the handout.

Slides carry the conclusions, your voice carries the reasoning, and the handout carries the proof.

## Applied to your audit

**On slides (about 4 to 5):**
- **The headline number:** 38 near-misses in 6 weeks.
- **Where they happened:** one bar chart showing dock 6 at 23 (about 60%), the freezer door at 9 (about 24%), and 6 scattered (about 16%). Managers should see at a glance that two spots account for most of the problem.
- **When they happened:** one chart of near-misses by hour, with the 2 to 4 pm window highlighted. Add a short label such as "2nd shift handover + peak inbound trucks".
- **Recommendations:** one slide pairing each problem with its fix.
  - Dock 6: one-way rule and stop line.
  - Freezer door: mirrors.
  - 2 to 4 pm: staggered handover.
- **A source line in small text:** "6 weeks of logs + camera footage."

**Said out loud:**
- **How you did the review.** One sentence is enough: "I went through six weeks of logs and the camera footage."
- **Why 2 to 4 pm is risky.** Explain it in your own words: two shifts overlap while trucks are arriving, so there are more people and more forklifts in the same space.
- **One concrete dock 6 near-miss from the footage.** A 20-second description will stick better than any statistic.
- **Why each fix fits its problem.** For example, why a stop line works at a crossing and mirrors work at a blind door.
- **The 6 scattered incidents.** Say there was no pattern worth acting on. They don't need a slide.
- **Your definition of a near-miss, in one line.** Managers will wonder what you counted, so tell them briefly.

**In the handout:**
- The full incident log.
- The list of every camera angle you reviewed.
- The full counting method.
- Optionally, a one-page summary of the findings and recommendations. Add owners and dates if you can, so it works as an action sheet.

## Why those calls

- **The incident log, camera list, and method are credibility material.** They matter if someone doubts the numbers, but reading them in the room would bury your message. Keep them out of the slides and be ready to point to them: "Full log and method are in your handout."
- **The 2 to 4 pm finding is the one most likely to be cut.** It's the most useful insight because it explains why the incidents happen and supports a recommendation. Keep it on a slide.
- **Hand out the packet at the end, or tell people to ignore it until then.** Otherwise they'll read ahead while you talk.
- **If you expect hard questions, add one or two backup slides** after your closing slide. A chart of incidents by week would be a good one. You won't present them, but you can jump to them if someone asks.
