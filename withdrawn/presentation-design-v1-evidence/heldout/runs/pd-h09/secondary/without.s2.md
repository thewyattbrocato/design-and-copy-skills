The rule I'd use: **slides show what the room needs to see to follow you, you say the reasoning, and the handout holds what someone might look up later.** Each piece of content gets sorted by what it does in the presentation.

| Content | Where it goes | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 38 near-misses in 6 weeks, 23 at dock 6 | Slide | This is the headline. One chart of the three hotspots (23 / 9 / 6) makes the point faster than a sentence. |
| The 2–4 pm pattern | Slide, with one chart | Show the hourly counts. Put the actual number on the slide (e.g., "X of 38 between 2 and 4 pm"), not just "most." |
| Why 2–4 pm: handover overlaps peak inbound trucks | Say it | This is your interpretation. Say it as "we believe" unless you verified it, and explain the mechanism out loud. A bullet would drop the reasoning. |
| The three recommendations (one-way rule and stop line, mirrors, staggered handover) | Slide, one per hotspot | Each one should sit next to the problem it fixes. Keep the slide to the action and the owner. |
| Why each fix matches its location | Say it | This is the argument. Walk through each one briefly. |
| The decision you need from managers (approve, owner, date, cost) | Slide, last | This is the slide people will remember. Make it the closing slide. |
| Counting method for a near-miss | One line on a slide or footnote; full method in the handout | Managers will ask "how do you define a near-miss?" The definition affects every number, so it needs to be visible, but the detail doesn't belong on the slide. |
| Six weeks of data, camera coverage limits | Say it | Name the limitation yourself before someone raises it. Don't clutter the slide with it. |
| Full 38-row incident log | Handout (appendix) | Reference material. Nobody needs to read it in the room. |
| List of camera angles reviewed | Handout | Supports credibility. Someone may want to check coverage later. |
| Per-location and per-hour tables | Handout | Chart on the slide, exact numbers in the handout. |

**A few checks before you finalize:**

- **Do you have an hourly breakdown?** "Most between 2 and 4 pm" needs a count to be defensible. If you don't have one, get it before the meeting.
- **Do the numbers reconcile?** 23 + 9 + 6 = 38, so they do. Keep them consistent everywhere.
- **Silent test:** if you muted yourself, would the slides still make sense? If not, move the missing content onto the slide.
- **Handout test:** would someone reading it alone next month understand the findings, the method, and the ask? If not, add the missing context.
