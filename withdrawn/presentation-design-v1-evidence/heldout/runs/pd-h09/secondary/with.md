**Assumptions:** a live talk to the managers, about 15 minutes, with a decision wanted at the end. If your slot is different, the slide count changes, but the sorting rule doesn't.

**Takeaway (say it in the first minute and repeat it at the close):** Most of the 38 near-misses happen at dock 6 and in the 2–4 pm handover window, and three changes target those places.

## The test for each piece of material

Ask two questions. Does the room need it to make the decision? Can it be understood at a glance on a screen? Then sort:

- **On the slide:** the conclusion, shown once, with one visual.
- **Said out loud:** the reasoning, the caveats, the counting method in one sentence, and the ask.
- **In the handout or appendix:** anything a manager might want to check later, or that would clutter the screen.

## Slides (about 4 content slides plus the title)

1. **"Dock 6 crossing accounts for 23 of 38 near-misses."** One bar chart: dock 6 (23), freezer door (9), scattered (6). Say the 61% figure out loud.
2. **"Most near-misses fall between 2 and 4 pm, when shift handover meets peak inbound trucks."** One time-of-day chart. Your notes say "most," so put the exact count on the chart before the meeting.
3. **"Three changes target the three clusters."** Three short rows: dock 6 one-way rule and stop line; freezer door mirrors; staggered handover. Each row names the cluster it addresses.
4. **The ask.** The decision you want, with an owner and a date. Leave this slide up during questions.

No agenda, no "thank you" slide, and no "questions?" slide.

## Said out loud

- The 61% and why it matters.
- Why the 2–4 pm overlap is the likely cause, and what you did and did not verify on camera.
- The counting method in one sentence, such as "a near-miss is any event where a forklift and a person or vehicle came within X of each other." Use your actual definition.
- The six-week window and any camera blind spots. Managers will ask about these, so raise them yourself.
- The ask, read off the last slide.

## Handout or appendix

- The full incident log (38 rows).
- The list of camera angles reviewed. Include the coverage gaps here.
- The counting method in full, including what did and did not qualify.
- The hourly breakdown behind slide 2.
- The reasoning for each recommendation.

Tell the room the handout exists, on the closing slide or in your spoken close. Hand it out at the end, not at the start.

## Backup slides

Park one backup slide for each of the two or three questions you expect, after the closing slide. Likely candidates:

- "Why not just restrict forklifts from dock 6?"
- "Is 2–4 pm riskier, or just busier?"
- "How do we know six weeks is representative?"

## Two things to check before you build

- Your exact count for the 2–4 pm window. "Most" is not enough for slide 2.
- Whether the camera coverage gaps change any of the three clusters. If they do, that goes on slide 1 or in your spoken caveat, not just in the appendix.
