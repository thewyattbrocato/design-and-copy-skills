## The takeaway

Put this on the first slide or say it in the first minute:

> Most of our 38 near-misses happen at the dock 6 crossing during the 2–4 pm handover, so a one-way rule and stop line there should come first.

Every slide either supports that sentence or goes to the appendix.

## The test for each fact

Ask two questions: does it change the decision, and can the audience take it in at a glance? If it changes the decision and fits on a glance, it goes on a slide. If it changes the decision but needs explaining, you say it. If it only proves your work, it goes in the handout or appendix.

## Structure for 15 minutes

Skip the agenda and the "thank you" slide. Aim for roughly five content slides:

1. The takeaway and the number: 23 of 38 at dock 6.
2. Where the rest sit: freezer door and scattered events.
3. When: the 2–4 pm window and why.
4. The dock 6 fix: one-way rule and stop line.
5. The freezer and handover fixes: mirrors and staggered handover.
6. The ask: approve the three changes, with an owner and date for each.

Put the appendix after the last slide, outside the count.

## Things to check before you build

- **Exact numbers.** You wrote "most" for the 2–4 pm window. Get the count. Don't put "most" on a slide, and check that 23 of 38 is correct (it's about 61%, so "nearly two-thirds" is accurate but "two-thirds" is not).
- **Hard questions.** Plant managers will likely ask about the cost of the stop line, whether the one-way rule can be enforced, and what staggering the handover does to staffing. Prepare one backup slide for each.
- **Speaker notes.** Write them only if you ask for them. If so, use a short outline per slide (the point, the number to say, the hand-off line), not a script.
