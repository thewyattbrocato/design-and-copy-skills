**Assumptions:** a live room, about 12 minutes with questions, and a decision wanted at the end (approve the three fixes). If any of that is wrong, the split below still applies, but the slide count changes.

**The test for each item:** does the room need it to decide, and can they take it in faster on a screen than from your voice? If not, it goes to the handout or stays out.

## On the slides (about five content slides)

Each title is the claim, not a label.

1. **"Dock 6 accounts for 23 of our 38 near-misses."** One bar chart with three bars: dock 6, freezer door, scattered. The number is the visual.
2. **"Near-misses cluster between 2 and 4 pm, when shift handover meets peak inbound trucks."** One time-of-day chart. Put the 2–4 pm band in a highlight color.
3. **"A one-way rule and stop line at dock 6 target our largest cluster."** A layout sketch with the proposed route and stop line marked. Keep the text to the three fixes at this site.
4. **"Mirrors at the freezer door and a staggered handover address the rest."** Two short lines, one per fix, each with the count it targets (9 at the freezer door; the handover window).
5. **"Approve three changes today."** The ask: each action with an owner and a date. This slide stays up during questions.

Title slide with the takeaway as its line is fine. Skip the agenda, the "thank you," and the "questions?" slide.

## Said out loud

- **How you counted.** One sentence: "We reviewed six weeks of logs and camera footage and counted an event as a near-miss when [your definition]." Give the full method in the handout.
- **Why the time window matters.** Explain the link between the handover and the truck peak in your own words. Say it as a hypothesis unless you have checked it.
- **The six scattered events.** Mention them once, aloud, so no one asks where they went.
- **Limits.** Say which camera angles you could not see, and that the counts are from footage you reviewed, not every shift.
- **The hard questions.** Prepare answers and keep them out of the deck unless asked:
  - *Is 2–4 pm just the busiest time, with more forklift movement overall?* This is the one to check before the meeting. If you have forklift movement or traffic counts by hour, compare near-misses per movement. Without that, the timing finding is weaker than it looks.
  - *Why these three fixes and not others?* Name the reason each one matches a cluster.
  - *What did the camera review miss?* Answer from the camera list.

## In the handout or appendix

- The full incident log (all 38 rows, with time, location and what happened).
- The list of every camera angle reviewed, with coverage gaps noted.
- The counting method in full, including edge cases and how you resolved them.
- The time-of-day breakdown in table form, and the location breakdown for the six scattered events.
- The rationale for each recommendation and what you considered and rejected.

Each fact should have one home. The incident log and camera list should be cited once, in the handout, and nowhere on the slides.
