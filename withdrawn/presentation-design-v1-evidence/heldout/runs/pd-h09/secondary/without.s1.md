The test I'd use for each piece is: **does the room need it to follow the argument while you're talking?** If yes, it goes on a slide. If it's the reasoning behind the slide, it's something you say. If people will look it up later or check your work, it goes in the handout.

**On the slides:** Keep only what the audience has to see to agree with the point. One claim per slide, with the evidence shown directly.
- Dock 6 has 23 of 38 near-misses. A simple plant-layout diagram with the count at each location works better than a list.
- Near-misses cluster between 2 and 4 pm. A single bar chart by hour makes this visible at a glance.
- One slide per recommendation, each tied to the evidence it answers: one-way rule and stop line for dock 6, mirrors for the freezer door, staggered handover for the time cluster.
- A short footnote with the counting method, so the 38 is defined where they first see it.

**What you say out loud:** The reasoning and the judgment calls, which don't fit on a slide without clutter.
- Why you think the 2–4 pm cluster happens. The handover overlapping with the inbound truck peak is a hypothesis unless you've confirmed it with staffing or dock schedules. Say it as a hypothesis, and say what would confirm it.
- What the 6 scattered events were and why they don't fit a pattern. Leaving them out without comment invites questions.
- The trade-offs of each recommendation: what the one-way rule costs in travel time, what the staggered handover costs in coverage. Plant managers will ask about these, and they're the decisions you need from them.
- What you're asking them to approve.

**What goes in the handout:** Reference material people will want after the meeting or use to check you.
- The full incident log (all 38 rows, with date, time, location, and a short description).
- The camera angle list, so anyone can see what was and wasn't observed.
- The counting method, written precisely enough that someone else could reproduce your count. This is the most important handout item for credibility.
- A breakdown table for the 9 freezer door and 6 scattered events, if you want to show them without cluttering slides.

**One thing to check before you present:** If dock 6 has more or better camera coverage than the freezer door or the rest of the plant, part of the concentration may come from where you looked rather than where the near-misses happened. Confirm coverage is comparable across locations, or say the limitation out loud and point to the camera list in the handout. Managers who catch this later will trust the rest of the deck less.
