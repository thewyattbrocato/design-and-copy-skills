Here are subject line options, grouped by approach.

**Clear and direct**
- New weekday pickup hours: 3-7 pm starting Oct 6
- Pickup changes at Greenfold Market: what's new
- Greenfold pickup update: longer weekday hours, no more Saturdays

**Lead with the benefit**
- More time to pick up: weekday window now 3-7 pm
- Four more hours to grab your order, Mon-Fri

**Lead with the Saturday change**
- Heads up: Saturday pickup ends Oct 4
- Last call for Saturday pickup (plus new weekday hours)

**Reassuring**
- Pickup hours are changing. Prices and ordering aren't.
- A new pickup schedule, same 9 pm order deadline

My pick is **"Pickup changes at Greenfold Market: new weekday hours, no more Saturdays."** It names the sender, covers both changes, and doesn't bury the Saturday news, which is the part members are most likely to be bothered by. If you want a softer tone, use the "Prices and ordering aren't" option.

The weekday window actually goes from two hours to four, so "Four more hours" in the benefit option is wrong. It should say "two more hours." Swap that in if you use it.

Today is Oct 7, which is after both the Oct 4 Saturday cutoff and the Oct 6 start date. If the email is going out now, drop the "ends Oct 4" and "starting Oct 6" wording and use present or past tense, such as "Pickup hours have changed." If the dates are right and the email went out earlier, ignore this.
