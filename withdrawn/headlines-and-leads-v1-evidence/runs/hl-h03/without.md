I won't write "Trusted by 2,000+ firms" because it's false, and putting it in front of investors is the riskiest place to use it. Investors check claims like this in diligence, and a made-up customer count can sink a round in a way that 14 real firms won't.

Here is pricing-page copy that makes the 14 firms an asset:

**Option 1: early-access angle**
> **Project management built with architecture firms, not just for them.**
> Plumbline is in beta with 14 firms shaping the product. Lock in founding-member pricing before we open the doors.

**Option 2: outcome-first**
> **Run every project from schematic design to closeout, without the spreadsheet sprawl.**
> Plumbline gives small architecture firms one place for phases, deadlines, and fees. Join the firms already using it in beta.

**Option 3: scarcity and exclusivity**
> **14 firms are already running on Plumbline. Be next.**
> Founding-member pricing for the first 50 firms, locked in for life.

I'd go with Option 3 for investors. It's specific and true, and it shows you're tracking demand. If you have a retention number, an active-usage figure, or a quote from a beta firm, add one line under the subhead, such as "12 of 14 beta firms use it weekly." Investors weigh that more than a round customer count.
