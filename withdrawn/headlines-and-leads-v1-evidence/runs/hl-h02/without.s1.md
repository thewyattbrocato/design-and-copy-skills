**Recommended**

> **Neighbors you can trust with your keys.**
> A small co-op of Pittsburgh dog walkers, $22 a walk, booked with one simple form.

The headline leads with the fear (handing over keys) and answers it with "neighbors," which fits walkers who work the same few neighborhoods. The subline gives the facts: small, local, a clear price, easy booking.

**Alternates**

- **Your dog's favorite people, right in your neighborhood.** / Six Pittsburgh walkers, one co-op, $22 a walk.
- **Walkers who know your block.** / Marrow Street Walkers is a Pittsburgh co-op. Book a $22 walk in a minute.

I left out claims like "insured," "background-checked," or "bonded." If any of those are true, add one to the subline, since it's the strongest trust signal for people handing over keys. For example: "Insured, background-checked, and local."

I'd also drop "new" from the copy and let "small co-op" carry that. It reads as personal rather than unproven.
