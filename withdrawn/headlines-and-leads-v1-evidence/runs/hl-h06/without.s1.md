Here are some options, grouped by angle.

**Money-leak angle**
- "Your bank statement has secrets. We'll show you."
- "Stop paying for things you forgot you had."
- "Every dollar you're losing to auto-renew, found."

**Control angle**
- "See every subscription. Cancel the ones you don't want."
- "Your money, minus the subscriptions you forgot about."
- "Connect your bank. Find every recurring charge. Kill the ones you don't want."

**Punchy and playful**
- "That $14.99 a month? Yeah, we found it."
- "Subscriptions have a way of sticking around. Not anymore."
- "Forgotten subscriptions, found."

**My picks**
1. **"Stop paying for things you forgot you had."** It keeps your cofounder's core idea but is more direct, and the "stop" gives it an action.
2. **"That $14.99 a month? We found it."** It's specific and a little cheeky, so it's the most memorable of the set. A concrete number makes people picture their own forgotten charge.

**Subhead idea:** a punchier headline works best with a plain line underneath that explains the product. For example: *"Connect your bank, see every recurring charge in one list, and flag the ones to cancel."*

Your cofounder's version isn't bad. It's clear and it names the pain. What it lacks is energy and a promise of what happens next. If you want to keep it close, try "Find the subscriptions you forgot about. Cancel the ones you don't need."

I can also write versions in a particular tone, such as more premium, more irreverent, or more trust-focused. That last one may matter because the app asks for bank access.
