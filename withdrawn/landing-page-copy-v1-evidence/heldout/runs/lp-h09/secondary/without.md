Honest take: You've got strong *material* here—22 years, transparent about repairs, no commission shenanigans—but it reads like a business directory listing, not from a person. It's all facts, no voice.

**What's actually missing:**

1. **Why Wendell, specifically.** "22 years" is buried. You're not just experienced; you're the person who's tuned the same church hall piano for two decades. That's expertise, not commodity.

2. **What good tuning actually *means*** to the customer. You mention 90 minutes and $130, but why should they care? A well-tuned piano plays in tune, responds evenly, feels different under the fingers. That matters to musicians and matters to people who just want their kid's lessons not to sound off.

3. **Personality.** Piano tuning is craft work. Right now it reads clinical. You could let a tiny bit of warmth through—you know your customers, their pianos, their situations.

4. **The reassurance angle is flipped.** "I'll tell you before, not after" is good, but it reads defensive (like you're promising not to be a jerk). Flip it: lead with confidence in your diagnosis, frame it as respect for your customer's time and money.

5. **Scope could be clearer.** "Mostly upright pianos in homes and church halls, plus a few school music rooms"—reorder this. Lead with "I tune pianos in homes, churches, and schools" then *maybe* mention uprights if it matters (grands are different work, but for your market it probably doesn't).

The bones are solid. It just needs voice and to lead with what actually matters to someone booking you: that you know what you're doing and won't waste their time.
