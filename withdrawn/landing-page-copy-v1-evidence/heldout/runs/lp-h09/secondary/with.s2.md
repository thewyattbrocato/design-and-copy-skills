Your page has all the facts that matter — 22 years, specific price, radical honesty about repairs — but it's buried under a utilitarian tone that makes those virtues invisible. Here's what's actually missing:

1. **No sense of craft.** Piano tuning isn't a commodity service, but you write it like scheduling an oil change. Nothing says why someone should *feel* that their piano is in the right hands.
2. **No argument for why tuning matters.** A reader might think "I'll get around to it eventually." A good piano that's been neglected until it's unplayable is a tragedy — but your reader doesn't know that's at stake.
3. **The honesty gets buried.** "I don't sell pianos or take commission on repairs" is your real differentiator — it's the thing a piano owner needs to hear — but it reads like a disclaimer, not a reason to trust you.
4. **Weak opening.** Starting with your name and what you do invites skimming. Start with who you're for and what changes.
