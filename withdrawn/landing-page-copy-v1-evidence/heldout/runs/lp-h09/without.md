Honestly, it's not flat so much as bare. The trust-building parts are already strong: 22 years, a flat price, "I'll tell you before I start, not after," and no commission. Those are specific and believable, and I wouldn't "punch up" them into marketing-speak, because the plainness is why people will trust it.

**What it's missing:**
- **An opening hook.** It starts with a job description. The first line should say what the customer gets, not what you do.
- **You as a person.** There's nothing about why you do this, what you like about it, or what a visit is like. This is the biggest gap, and only you can fill it.
- **Texture.** "Upright pianos in homes and church halls" is a good detail. A specific detail like a memorable piano or a common problem would make it stick.
- **A stronger ending.** "Texting a time that suits you" is fine, but the call to action is the last thing people read, so it can do more.

**A tightened version using only what you've told me:**

> **Tuning pianos around Dunmore for 22 years.**
>
> I'm Wendell Prasad. Most of my work is upright pianos in family homes and church halls, with a few school music rooms too. I'll treat yours like it matters, whether it's a hand-me-down upright or the hall piano everyone sings along to.
>
> **What to expect**
> A standard tuning takes about 90 minutes and costs $130. If your piano has been neglected for a long time, it may need two visits. I'll tell you that before I start, not after.
>
> **No upsell, ever**
> I don't sell pianos and I don't take commission on repairs. If something needs fixing beyond tuning, I'll explain what it is, and you decide.
>
> **Book a visit**
> Call 555-0142, or text me a time that suits you.

**To make it better, add one or two of these in your own words** (I didn't invent any):
- Why you got into tuning
- A typical problem you find in neglected pianos, and how you fix it
- What people say afterward, or a short testimonial
- Which suburbs or areas count as "in and around Dunmore"

Even two sentences of personal detail would do more than any rewording of what's there.
