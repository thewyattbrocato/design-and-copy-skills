Your concept is good: a script wordmark in gold reads as warm and a bit upscale, and a leaf for the dot of the "i" is a nice, ownable detail. But the execution you describe will struggle in all three places you plan to use it. I'm working from your description, so treat this as likely problems, not a verdict on the file.

**Main concerns**

1. **Hairline strokes**
   - They are the biggest risk. At 32px they will disappear or turn to mush.
   - On paper cups, flexo printing tends to fill in or break up very thin lines, and the cup's curve and seam make that worse.
   - On a sign, hairlines vanish at distance, and they're hard to cut in vinyl, routed signage, or paint.
   - Fix: thicken the thinnest strokes a lot. Keep some contrast between thick and thin, but make the thin strokes sturdy. A good test is to print it about 1 inch wide and check it from arm's length.

2. **Gold on white contrast**
   - #c9a227 on white is about 2.4:1, well below the 3:1 usually wanted for graphics and far below 4.5:1 for text.
   - Thin strokes in a mid-light color make this worse, because they look even paler than the color suggests.
   - Fix: darken the gold for the primary version (something like #9a7b12 to #a8841a), or put the lighter gold on a dark background such as deep green, charcoal, or navy, where it looks richer.

3. **Tiny leaf**
   - It will be invisible at favicon size and will probably blob on cups.
   - Fix: make it noticeably bigger than feels natural on the full logo, and give it a simple, bold shape with no veins or fine detail.

4. **Metallic gold as flat color**
   - A flat gold hex often looks mustard or beige in print. A true metallic would need foil or a special ink, which costs more.
   - Fix: order printed proofs on the real cup stock before committing.

**Favicon**
Don't shrink the full wordmark to 32px. Make a separate mark: a bold "P" in the script, or just the leaf, on a solid background tile (dark green with gold, or gold with white). Test it at 16px too, since browser tabs often render that size.

**Build a small logo set**
- Full wordmark (sign, menu, website header)
- A simplified or heavier version for cups and small print
- A one-color version, plus a reversed version for dark backgrounds (kraft paper and dark green both suit tea)
- A standalone icon (the "P" or leaf) for the favicon, social avatar, and stamps or stickers

**Smaller points**
- Check legibility of the script itself. Fancy capital "P" and "b" forms can make "Pemberly" hard to read quickly. Ask a few people who haven't seen it to read it aloud.
- Leave generous clear space around it, since delicate logos get crowded easily.

If you can export a PNG or SVG later, I can give more specific notes. In the meantime, a quick test is to shrink it to 32px and to about an inch wide on paper. If the leaf and thin strokes don't survive, thicken them.
