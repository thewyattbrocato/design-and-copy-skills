I can't see the file, so this is feedback on the concept as you've described it. The idea is nice: a script wordmark with a leaf as the "i" dot is a charming, fitting touch for a tea shop. The execution details are where I'd worry, because the logo has to work at three very different sizes.

**Biggest risk: thin gold hairlines on white**
- **Contrast is low.** #c9a227 on white is about 2.4:1. That's fine for a large decorative element, but thin strokes in that color will look washed out, especially in daylight on a sign.
- **Hairlines disappear.** At 32px they will vanish or blur into broken fragments. On paper cups, small printed hairlines tend to fill in or drop out, depending on the printer. On a sign, they vanish at any real viewing distance.
- **The leaf will be lost.** A tiny leaf above the "i" is a nice detail at large sizes. At small sizes it becomes a smudge, or it makes the "i" look like it has a stain.

**Script legibility**
"Pemberly" has a lot of connected, similar-looking letters (m, b, e, r, l, y). With very low stroke contrast, they can blur into a wavy line. Check that the name reads clearly at about 1 inch wide. If it doesn't, the script is too delicate.

**What I'd do**
1. **Thicken the strokes.** Keep some thick-thin contrast for elegance, but set the thinnest stroke to a weight that survives your smallest use. Test it by shrinking the logo to about 1 inch wide. If any strokes break up, thicken them.
2. **Make a darker version for small uses and print.** Keep the gold for large uses like the sign. For cups and small digital uses, try a deeper gold or bronze (around #8a6d12), or pair the gold with a dark green or charcoal. Metallic foil or gold vinyl on a dark background can look great on a sign, but flat gold ink on white often reads as mustard.
3. **Don't use the full wordmark as the favicon.** At 32px, use a separate simple mark, such as a bold "P" or just the leaf, in a solid shape. That can be gold on a dark or green square for contrast.
4. **Make a few versions.** You'll want a one-color version, a reversed version (light on dark), and a stacked or compact version for small spaces. Set a minimum size below which the full wordmark isn't used.
5. **Consider a dark sign background.** Gold on deep green, navy, or black will look richer and be much easier to read than gold on white.
