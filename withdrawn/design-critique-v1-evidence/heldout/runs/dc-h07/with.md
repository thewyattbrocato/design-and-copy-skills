**Go with B, with one change: put a slim summary strip above the table.** Fairly confident, based on the descriptions alone.

**Why B wins for this task**
- **It puts the job first.** Dispatchers mostly chase late loads. B opens on a list sorted by lateness, so the worst load is the first row. In A, the table sits under a map and four tiles, and it's the smallest of the three areas. On 27 inches, the table probably gets about a third of the height. That means few rows visible and more scrolling, and it's the area they use most.
- **A's tiles take the most space and prompt the least action.** On-time %, active trucks and avg dwell are context numbers that change slowly. A dispatcher will read them once and then stop looking. Only "late loads" leads to an action, and it's just a count. It doesn't say which loads are late.
- **A's map is always on, but it's rarely the right tool.** Most of the time, dispatchers need to know which load, how late, and who to call. A map of every active truck gives them dots without that information. B shows the map when a row is selected, so it's tied to the load they're working on.
- **B uses the 27-inch screen for rows.** A full-width table can show many more loads and columns (ETA, delay, driver, customer, next stop) without scrolling. Filter chips like "Late >30 min", "At risk", or "My region" turn the chase into one click.
- **A's sidebar costs width.** Navigation is rarely used on a screen people sit on all day, and it takes permanent space.

**What to watch in B (ranked)**
1. **Major: no overview.** Without tiles, a dispatcher can't see at a glance whether the day is going badly. Fix: add a single slim row above the chips with late count, on-time %, and active trucks, with the late count clickable to filter the table. Skip the big tiles.
2. **Major: the map loses context on each click.** If the panel replaces the view or covers rows, the dispatcher loses their place. Fix: make it a docked panel that keeps the table visible. Selecting another row should update the map, and the arrow keys should move between rows.
3. **Moderate: no way to see a cluster of problems.** For example, a storm or a closed highway causing five late loads would be hard to spot in a list. Fix: add a toggle for a full map view, or let the late loads group by region.
4. **Moderate: navigation is hidden.** Without a sidebar, keep a compact top bar for the other sections.

**What to keep from A:** the idea of surfacing the key metrics on the main screen, and the map as a first-class view. B should carry both, just smaller.

**When A would be better:** if the dispatchers' main job is routing and rebalancing trucks geographically rather than handling late loads. You said it's lateness, so B fits.
