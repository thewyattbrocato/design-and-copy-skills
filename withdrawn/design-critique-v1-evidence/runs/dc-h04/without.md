**Ship Option B**, with a few changes.

**Why B wins for this audience**

1. **Repeat donors don't need persuading.** They already believe in the mission. A hero photo and an emotional headline are built to win over strangers. Returning supporters want to finish quickly and feel their gift is useful.
2. **Phones.** A full-width photo pushes the form below the fold and costs load time on mobile. Option B puts the form at the top, with no image to load.
3. **Concrete beats abstract.** "$50 stocks one shelf for a month" tells people what their money does. "Every child deserves a reading room" is a sentiment almost nobody disagrees with. Priya's "14 books this term" gives the same proof in one line.
4. **B asks for a better gift.** The monthly toggle is the biggest revenue lever for a returning donor, and A doesn't have one.
5. **A's form is incomplete.** It shows amounts and a button but no card fields, so the real form is either hidden or on another page. An extra step costs completions.

**What I'd change before launch**

- **Fix the mobile layout.** Two columns will stack on a phone. Put the form first and the stories after it, or collapse them to one story above the form. Supporters should reach the amount buttons without scrolling.
- **Preselect an amount.** Use $50, which matches the headline. Add an "Other" option with a numeric keypad.
- **Default the toggle carefully.** Consider defaulting to monthly, with the one-time option clearly visible. Test this, and don't hide the one-time choice. Repeat donors resent feeling tricked.
- **Make checkout fast.** Add Apple Pay and Google Pay at the top of the form, since most of your traffic is on phones. Use autofill-friendly fields and large tap targets. Keep the page to a single screen of input.
- **Recognize returning donors.** Add a line like "Thank you for giving again" if you can detect them. A prefilled form is even better.
- **Show the math for each amount.** When someone picks $25, $100, or monthly, update a line under the buttons, for example "$100 stocks two shelves." This keeps the headline's concrete framing.
- **Add a small trust cue.** Include a short "secure payment" note, tax-receipt language, and the nonprofit's EIN near the button.
- **Consider one small photo.** Add a single small image of a child, or a real photo next to a story. "No photo" is fine, but a small one adds warmth without hurting load time. Test this rather than assuming.
- **Verify the stories.** Make sure the names, ages, and numbers are real and have consent. Children's stories need care. First names only is good, and consider changing details if needed.
- **Add a thank-you page.** Show the impact line again, such as "You stocked a shelf," and invite a share or a monthly upgrade.

**How to settle the board**

I'm judging from the descriptions and general fundraising patterns, not your data. If the board stays split, run an A/B test for 2–4 weeks with completion rate and average gift as the metrics. Your existing donor base is probably enough to see a difference. I'd expect B to win on completion rate, but the test is the way to confirm it.
