**What a variable font is**

A normal font family is a separate file for each style: Regular, Bold, Light, Italic and so on. A variable font packs all of those into one file and lets you slide smoothly between them. Weight can go from thin to heavy, and some fonts also adjust width or slant. Think of a dimmer switch instead of a few fixed light settings.

**Is it better?**

Usually, yes, for websites:
- **Faster loading:** One file is often smaller than four or five separate ones.
- **More flexibility:** Your designer can use any weight, like a semi-bold that's slightly lighter than standard Bold.
- **Works everywhere:** All current browsers support them.

The downsides are minor. If you only use one or two styles, a variable font can be slightly bigger than the two regular files you'd otherwise load. A few very old browsers also don't support them, but that's rarely a concern now.

**Can you put it on your website?**

I can't tell from the file name. It depends on the license, and a font file being in your hands doesn't mean you can publish it online. Some things to check:
- **Ask your designer:** Did they buy a license, or is it a free font? Was the license for web use specifically? Many font licenses cover print and desktop use, but web use is separate and often priced by traffic.
- **Look for a license document:** It may have come with the file, or you can find it on the site where the font was bought or downloaded. Look for words like "web," "webfont," or "embedding."
- **Check whose name is on the license:** Licenses are often tied to the buyer. If your designer bought it, it may need to be transferred to you or re-bought for your business.
- **If it's from Google Fonts or another open-license source:** You're almost certainly fine, but confirm.

Ask your designer: "Does the license for Marlowe cover web use for my bakery's domain, and is it in my name?" Keep the answer and the receipt on file.

**A technical note**

Browsers prefer the `.woff2` format, which is a compressed version of `.ttf`. Your developer, or you with a free converter, can make one. Many font licenses also have rules about converting, so check that too.
