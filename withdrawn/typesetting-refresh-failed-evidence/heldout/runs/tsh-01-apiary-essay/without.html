The Year the Clover Came Late

By Odalys Wren

It was the second week of June before the white clover opened on the lower meadow, almost a month behind where it should have been. Marguerite, who has kept hives on that slope for thirty-one years, stood at the fence and said, “I’ve never seen the bees so patient — or so hungry.”

We had fed the colonies sugar syrup through May, which nobody likes doing. It keeps them alive, but it isn’t honey, and it changes what a hive does with its time. The queens kept laying anyway. By the solstice there were more bees in the boxes than we had frames for, and the “swarm calendar” we print every spring was useless.

What saved the season was the linden along Mill Road, which flowered for nine days in July and gave us the lightest, most floral honey we’ve bottled since 2016. You can read how we split the strongest colonies in our field notes at https://thistledownapiary.example/field-notes/splits, and the harvest dates are on the shop page.

The lesson, if there is one: late isn’t the same as lost. We bottled 412 jars this year. Marguerite says she’ll take the patient bees over the punctual ones any day.
