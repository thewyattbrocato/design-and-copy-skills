Here's a reason you can use to push back: **the current message is actually well-designed**. It hits the three things users need in an error state—what went wrong, reassurance that nothing's lost, and what to do next—in 13 words. That clarity and brevity matter when someone's stressed.

The "coldness" complaint is usually about tone, not content. The current message isn't rude or dismissive; it's just straightforward. That's not a flaw in a utility toast.

**But** if the designer wants warmth without losing clarity, here are some middle-ground options:

- "Oops! We couldn't save, but your changes are safe. Try again?"
- "We had trouble saving, but your work is still here. Give it another shot?"
- "Something went wrong with saving, but don't worry—your changes are safe. Ready to try again?"

These add humanity (the casual language, the acknowledgment of the hiccup) while keeping the reassurance + action intact.

I'd suggest showing these alternatives and asking: *What specifically felt cold?* Is it that "Couldn't save" sounds too blunt? Or the overall tone? That'll guide whether you actually need to change it or just tweak the phrasing. Sometimes "warm" and "clear" are the same thing, and you're already there.
