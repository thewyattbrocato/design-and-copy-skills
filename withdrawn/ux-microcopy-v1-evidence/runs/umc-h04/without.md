Here's the copy. I made two changes to your brief, explained below it.

**Headline**
Before you go: 30% off for the next 3 months

**Body**
You've been scheduling with Quillpost, and we'd hate for your queue of drafts and send times to go quiet. Stay on your plan and we'll take 30% off your next 3 months. Your schedules, subscriber lists, and templates stay exactly as they are.

This offer ends in **10:00**

**Buttons**
- Primary: **Keep my plan at 30% off**
- Decline: **No thanks, continue canceling**

**Where I differed from your brief**

1. **The decline button.** "No thanks, I'd rather overpay" is confirmshaming. It tends to annoy people who are already leaving, and it adds risk under the FTC's click-to-cancel push and similar state rules. If you want a bit of personality, "No thanks, I'll pay full price" or "Not now, continue canceling" keeps the edge without insulting anyone. You can A/B test your original wording against these if your retention person wants to.

2. **The countdown.** It should be a real deadline. If the timer resets on reload or the offer reappears after it hits 0:00, that's a fake-urgency pattern and a deceptive-practices problem. Tie the expiry to a server-side timestamp per user and don't show the offer again after it lapses. Otherwise, drop the timer and say "Available today only."

Also, since the 30% isn't decided yet, make sure the test is wired to apply a discount people actually receive when they click. Promising a discount in the UI that billing doesn't honor is the worst outcome here.

I can write variants for the A/B test, such as a pause-subscription option or a different discount framing.
