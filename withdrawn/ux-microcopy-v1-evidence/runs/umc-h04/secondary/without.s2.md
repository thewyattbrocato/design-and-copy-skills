Here's copy for your subscription cancellation retention screen:

**Headline:**
"Wait—we can make this work"

**Body:**
"You're about to lose access to Quillpost, but here's the thing: we want to keep you. For the next 10:00, we're offering 30% off your subscription. That's a meaningful difference month-to-month.

If you're on the fence, this might tip it. If you're done, we get it—but at least give yourself a reason to stay."

**Accept button:**
"Yes, give me 30% off"

**Decline button:**
"No thanks, I'd rather overpay"

---

The headline avoids defensive "are you sure?" territory and reframes this as a problem-solving moment. The body names the elephant (they want to leave) but makes the offer concrete with the monthly savings angle. The decline button does the work you want—it makes walking away feel actively foolish.

One note: the 10:00 timer assumes you'll animate a real countdown. If it's static text, that urgency deflates fast—people will close and reopen the app and see the same timer. A real countdown (even if it resets on each session) hits harder.
