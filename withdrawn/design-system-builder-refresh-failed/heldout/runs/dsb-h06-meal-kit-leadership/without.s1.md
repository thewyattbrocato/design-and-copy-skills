# Talking points: design system investment

## Opening (the one-sentence version)
"I'm asking for about a quarter of two people's time to make every change to our website and app faster, cheaper, and more consistent. I'll also measure whether it worked, so you can check the return."

## 1. Frame it as a problem they already feel
Skip components and talk about symptoms they'll recognize:
- **Speed:** A promo, a new plan tier, or a checkout change has to be built separately for web and app, across three squads. Each squad solves the same problems on its own.
- **Consistency:** Customers see slightly different buttons, forms, and error messages across the site, the app, and different squads' features. At the moment of ordering, that reads as less trustworthy.
- **Rework:** When we fix something like an accessibility issue, a checkout bug, or a rebrand tweak, we fix it in several places, and we sometimes miss one.
- **Cost of change:** Without a shared foundation, each new squad, feature, or campaign costs a little more than the last.

## 2. Use an analogy they'll get
"It's the central kitchen versus every squad cooking from scratch. You get the same quality every time, new dishes launch faster, and when a recipe changes you change it once."

## 3. Tie it to what each of them cares about
- **COO:** Fewer duplicated efforts across squads, more predictable delivery, and less "why did this take three weeks?"
- **Head of sales:** Faster turnaround on promos, landing pages, and seasonal or partner campaigns. A consistent experience supports conversion and brand trust.
- **Finance:** This is a small, bounded cost (about 0.5 FTE for a quarter) that targets recurring engineering and design spend. It has defined checkpoints, and we'll report results.

## 4. Be upfront about the missing numbers
This works in your favor, because finance will trust you more if you say it yourself:

"I don't have a hard figure for how much time we lose today. That's partly because nobody has tracked it. So the first part of this quarter is measuring it, and I'll come back with real numbers rather than guesses."

If someone pushes for an estimate, don't invent one. You could say: "Here's the shape of it. If each of three squads loses even a few hours a sprint to rebuilding or reconciling UI, that adds up quickly. But I'd rather show you measured data than my back-of-envelope."

## 5. The ask and the plan
- **Ask:** About 25% of two people's time for one quarter.
- **Month 1, baseline:** Audit where we duplicate work. Survey the three squads on time lost. Review recent tickets for UI inconsistency bugs. Pick 2–3 metrics.
- **Months 2–3, deliver:** Build the pieces used most in the highest-value flows (e.g. checkout, menu selection, account/subscription management) and adopt them in at least one squad on web and app.
- **End of quarter:** Report results and recommend whether to continue, scale, or stop.

## 6. How we'll know it worked
Pick two or three of these, whichever you can realistically measure:
- Time from design handoff to shipped for comparable UI changes (before vs. after)
- Number of UI-inconsistency or "fix it in N places" bugs
- Share of key flows built from shared pieces
- Squad-reported time saved (a simple survey before and after)

## 7. Likely questions and answers
- **"Why now?"** Three squads and two platforms are the point where duplication starts compounding. It's cheaper to fix now than after more features pile up.
- **"What do we give up?"** Be specific. Name what those two people would otherwise work on, and say you've considered that tradeoff.
- **"Can't this wait?"** It can, but the cost doesn't pause. Every quarter without it adds more inconsistent UI to clean up later.
- **"What if it doesn't pay off?"** "Then we'll know within a quarter, we'll have lost a small amount of time, and we stop."
- **"Is this a one-time thing?"** Be honest. A design system needs some ongoing upkeep, but it's smaller than the initial build. Say you'll bring a recommendation at the end of the quarter.

## Closing
"I'm not asking you to take this on faith. I'm asking for one quarter, a clear baseline, and a check-in at the end so you can decide with real data."
