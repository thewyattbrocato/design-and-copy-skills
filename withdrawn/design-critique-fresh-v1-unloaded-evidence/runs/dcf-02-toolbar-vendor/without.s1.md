**No, I wouldn't buy it.** Overlay toolbars like this don't deliver what the pitch claims.

**Why**
- **They don't make a site compliant.** Automated tools can detect only a fraction of accessibility problems, and overlays can't fix the hard ones. Examples are missing or poor alt text, broken keyboard navigation, unlabeled form fields, and confusing checkout flows.
- **Regulators have already acted on this claim.** In January 2025 the FTC fined accessiBe, one of the biggest overlay vendors, $1 million for deceptive claims that its tool made sites compliant. "Fixes automatically" is the kind of claim that drew that action.
- **They don't prevent lawsuits.** Plaintiffs' firms regularly sue sites that have overlays installed, and some use the overlay's presence as evidence of a problem.
- **They can make things worse.** Many screen-reader and keyboard users find overlays intrusive, and some turn them off or block them.
- **"Legal protection included" needs scrutiny.** Ask for the exact terms in writing. These are usually narrow, such as capped reimbursement with many conditions, not a promise that you won't be sued or won't lose.

I'm not a lawyer, so for real legal exposure a short consult is worth it. But the technical point stands on its own.

**What I'd do with the same $600 a year and a freelancer**
1. **Scan** a few key pages (home, a category, a product page, cart, checkout) with free tools: WAVE, axe DevTools, and Lighthouse. These catch the obvious issues.
2. **Test by hand.** Try to buy a bike using only the keyboard (Tab, Enter, Esc), then try again with a screen reader (VoiceOver on a Mac is built in). You'll find the real problems quickly.
3. **Fix the common ones.** Your freelancer can handle these:
   - Alt text on product images. With sixty pages this is very doable.
   - Sufficient color contrast.
   - Labels on form fields.
   - Descriptive link and button text.
   - A visible keyboard focus outline.
   - Sensible heading structure.
4. **Check your theme.** If your platform offers an accessibility-ready theme, switching may fix a lot at once. The platform usually owns checkout accessibility, so confirm what it covers.
5. **Publish a short accessibility statement** with an email or phone number for people who hit a barrier, and respond promptly when they do.
6. **Consider a one-time professional audit** of your key pages if you want more confidence. It costs more than $49 but is a real deliverable.
