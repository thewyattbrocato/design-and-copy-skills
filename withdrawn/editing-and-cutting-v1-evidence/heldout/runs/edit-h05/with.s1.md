Hi everyone,

I want to talk about the packaging your Fernwood boxes arrive in. We've lined them with foam coolers since we started shipping in 2021. For a long time I told myself that was just the cost of getting fresh food to your door safely. It never sat right with me, and I suspect it never sat right with many of you either.

So in January I sat down with Priya, who runs our packing floor. We listed everything that goes into a box and what we could change without compromising food safety. Our goal was to replace the foam with something you can put in your curbside recycling or compost, by the end of summer.

It was not easy. The first material we tried was a molded plant-fibre liner from a supplier in the next province. It looked great, but in test shipments it kept food cold for only about 18 hours. Our far-suburb routes can take up to 30 hours in hot weather. We dropped that supplier, which cost us about six weeks and a fair amount of money in samples.

The second was a recycled-paper liner, made of layered corrugated paper wrapped around a thin paper-based barrier. In our trials it kept food at a safe temperature for up to 36 hours, which covers even our longest routes. It arrives flat, so it takes less warehouse space, and it goes in your curbside paper recycling with no special steps. We started shipping with it on March 4th, and every Fernwood box leaving our warehouse now uses it.

Two things have not gone perfectly. First, the new liner costs about 40 cents more per box than foam. We're absorbing that, and we don't plan to raise prices because of it this year.

Second, and I'm a little embarrassed about this one, in the first two weeks a number of boxes arrived with soggy bottoms. A handful of you wrote in, and you were right to. The ice packs were sweating more than they did inside the foam, and the paper soaked it up. We've switched to an ice pack with a sealed outer film, and the soggy-box emails have stopped. I'm sorry for the mess it made of some of your dinners.

One piece is still unsolved: the ice packs themselves. They're plastic film filled with non-toxic gel, and they can't go in curbside recycling. They're reusable, and many of you keep them for lunch bags and picnics, but that isn't a real answer for everyone. We're working on a returnable program. You'd leave the packs in the empty box on your porch, and our driver would collect them on the next delivery. We'll trial it with about 200 households in the fall, and if it works, we'll roll it out to everyone. I'll share how the trial is going in our September newsletter.

Thank you to Priya and the packing team, who were patient while we changed how every box is assembled, and to all of you for putting up with a few bumps.

You can always reply to this email and it comes straight to me. I read every one.

Warmly,
Dana
Founder, Fernwood Meal Kits
