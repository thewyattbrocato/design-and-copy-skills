Hi everyone,

I want to talk about the boxes your Fernwood meal kits arrive in. Many of you have noticed, and several of you have told me directly, that we've been lining our boxes with foam coolers since we started shipping in 2021. For a long time I told myself that was just the cost of getting fresh food to your door safely. But it never sat right with me, and I suspect it never sat right with many of you either.

So back in January, I sat down with Priya, who runs our packing floor. We listed everything that goes into each box and what we could realistically change without compromising the food. We landed on a goal: replace the foam with something you can put in your curbside recycling or compost, and do it by the end of summer.

It was not easy. The first material we tried was a molded plant-fibre liner from a supplier in the next province. It looked and felt great, and in early tests it kept food cold for about 18 hours. Our far-suburb routes can take up to 30 hours in hot weather, though, and by the end of that window the food wasn't cold enough to be safe. We dropped that supplier, which cost us about six weeks and a fair amount of money in samples.

The second material was a recycled-paper liner: layered corrugated paper wrapped around a thin paper-based barrier. It performed much better, keeping food at a safe temperature for up to 36 hours, which covers our longest routes with room to spare. It also ships flat, so it takes up less warehouse space, and you can break it down and put it in your paper recycling with no special steps. We started shipping with it on March 4th, and as of today, every Fernwood box uses it.

I want to be open about two things that haven't gone perfectly. First, the new liner costs us about 40 cents more per box than the foam did. We've decided to absorb that for now rather than pass it on, and we don't plan to raise prices because of it this year.

Second, and this is the one I'm a little embarrassed about: in the first two weeks, a number of boxes arrived with soggy bottoms. A handful of you wrote to tell us, and you were right to. The ice packs we were using sweated more than they had in the foam coolers, and the paper soaked up that moisture. We've since switched to an ice pack with a sealed outer film, and the soggy-box emails have stopped. I'm sorry for the mess it made of some of your dinners.

One piece is still unsolved: the ice packs themselves. They're plastic film filled with a non-toxic gel, so they can't go in your curbside recycling. Many of you keep them for lunch bags and picnics, which is great, but I don't think that's a real solution for everyone. So we're working on a returnable ice pack program. You'd leave the packs in the empty box on your porch, and our driver would collect them on the next delivery. We plan to trial this with about 200 households this fall, and if it works, we'll roll it out to everyone.

Thank you to Priya and the whole packing team, who have been incredibly patient while we changed how every box is assembled. And thank you to all of you for putting up with a few bumps along the way. Your feedback is what makes Fernwood better every week.

If you want to know more, reply to this email and it comes straight to me. I read every one. I'll share how the ice pack trial is going in an upcoming newsletter.

Thank you, as always, for choosing Fernwood and for letting us feed you and your families.

Warmly,
Dana
Founder, Fernwood Meal Kits
