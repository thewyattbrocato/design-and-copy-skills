Hi everyone,

Since we started shipping in 2021, your Fernwood boxes have been lined with foam coolers. I told myself that was the cost of getting fresh food to your door safely. It never sat right with me, and I suspect it didn't with many of you either.

In January, I sat down with Priya, who runs our packing floor, and we set a goal: replace the foam with something you can put in your curbside recycling or compost, by the end of summer.

It wasn't easy. The first material we tried was a molded plant-fibre liner from a supplier in the next province. It looked great, but in our test shipments it kept food cold for only about 18 hours. Our far-suburb routes can take up to 30 hours in hot weather, so we had to drop that supplier. That cost us about six weeks and a fair amount of money in samples.

The second was a recycled-paper liner: layered corrugated paper wrapped around a thin paper-based barrier. In our trials it kept food at a safe temperature for up to 36 hours, which covers even our longest routes with room to spare. It arrives flat, so it takes less warehouse space, and it goes straight into your curbside paper recycling. We started shipping with it on March 4th, and every Fernwood box that leaves our warehouse now uses it.

A few things haven't gone perfectly. The new liner costs about 40 cents more per box than foam. We're absorbing that rather than passing it on, and we don't plan to raise prices because of it this year.

The other one I'm a little embarrassed about: in the first two weeks, a number of boxes arrived with soggy bottoms. A handful of you wrote in, and you were right to. The ice packs were sweating more than they had inside the foam, and the paper soaked it up. We've since switched to an ice pack with a sealed outer film, and the soggy-box emails have stopped. I'm sorry for the mess it made of some of your dinners.

One piece is still unsolved: the ice packs themselves. They're plastic film filled with non-toxic gel, and they can't go in curbside recycling. Many of you keep them for lunch bags and picnics, which is great, but that isn't a real solution for everyone. We're working on a returnable program. You'd leave the packs in the empty box on your porch, and our driver would collect them on the next delivery. We'll trial it with about 200 households in the fall, and if it works well, we'll roll it out to everyone.

Thank you to Priya and the packing team, who were patient while we changed how every single box is assembled, and to all of you for putting up with a few bumps. Your feedback makes Fernwood better every week.

You can always reply to this email and it comes straight to me. I read every one. I'll share how the ice pack trial is going in our September newsletter.

Warmly,
Dana
Founder, Fernwood Meal Kits
