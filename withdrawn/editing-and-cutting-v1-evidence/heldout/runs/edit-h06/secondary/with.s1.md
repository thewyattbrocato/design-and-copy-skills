I punched up the hero below, but I didn't write the two stats. "97% of users save 5+ hours a week" and "Trusted by 12,000 freelancers" are claims about users you don't have yet. Putting them on a public launch page would mislead visitors, and it could create legal exposure for you (FTC rules on fake testimonials and stats apply to startups too). Placeholders wouldn't fix that, because they tend to ship unchanged on launch day.

Changes from the original: the headline leads with outcomes instead of a category label, the subhead cuts "easy to use, all-in-one" and "reducing stress" (generic filler) and uses your three real beta facts, and the line under the button tells visitors the beta is free without making a claim you can't back up yet.

**Social proof that's true today**, if you want something under the button:
- "Now in beta. Join early and shape what we build next." This works with zero users.
- If you have any real signups, use the actual number: "Join 84 freelancers in the beta." Even a small real number builds more trust than a big fake one.
