I didn't add the Friday deadline or the "4 spots left" line. Enrollment stays open until the first class, so a midnight Friday cutoff would be false, and a prospect who replies or shows up Friday would find out. Here's a tighter version that creates urgency with facts you can stand behind:

**Subject:** Fall wheel-throwing starts October 14. 9 spots left

Hello friends of Pinewick,

Registration is open for our six-week Fall Wheel-Throwing class for beginners. No experience needed. We meet Tuesday evenings, 6:30 to 8:30pm, starting October 14, one week from today.

The $240 fee covers all clay, glazes, and two firings. Class is capped at 12 so everyone gets plenty of attention from the instructor. Nine spots are still open.

Register on our website, or reply to this email and we'll sign you up.

Warm regards,
The Pinewick team

Changes:
- The subject line and the "9 spots left" line carry the urgency. A real count is more persuasive than a vague deadline, and it holds up if someone checks.
- "One week from today" ties the start date to the calendar without inventing a cutoff.
- Cut the filler ("we are excited to let you know," "wonderful opportunity," "warm and supportive environment") and merged the details into fewer sentences.

If you want a real deadline, you could set one, such as "Registration closes Wednesday, October 12, so we can confirm the class size." Then the Friday line would be true, as long as you hold to it.
