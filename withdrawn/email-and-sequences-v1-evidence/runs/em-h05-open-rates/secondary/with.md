**What's going wrong**

Your two best subjects (34% and 38%) name a specific thing a reader can act on: a place with a condition, or a gear question they probably have. Your weakest ones (15-19%) name the newsletter instead of what's in it. "#112," "#113: June edition," "#115," and "Newsletter: summer reading list" tell a subscriber which issue this is, not whether it's worth opening. Numbering also gives no reason to open this week and not next.

Two other things to check:
- **Sender name.** You use "Fernwick Trail Notes" in one subject and "Trail Notes" in another. If the From name varies too, recognition suffers. Keep it identical every week.
- **Small sample.** Six sends, with topic and season mixed in, can't prove the pattern. The direction is still clear enough to act on.

**Subject lines and preview text for this issue**

Lead with the hikes. They're time-sensitive and concrete, which is what the two winners had.

1. **Three trails reopened this week** (31 characters)
   Preview: [Trail 1], [Trail 2] and [Trail 3]: what's open now and what to check before you go.

2. **Do day hikes need a bear canister?** (34 characters)
   Preview: A reader asked what [area] actually requires. Short answer inside.

3. **Trekking pole tips for steep, wet trails** (40 characters)
   Preview: Short version: [one tip]. The one most people skip is in the last section.

Put the issue number in the body header if you still want it, not in the subject line. Fill in the bracketed trail names, the area, and the tip before sending, and confirm the bear canister answer against the actual local rule.
