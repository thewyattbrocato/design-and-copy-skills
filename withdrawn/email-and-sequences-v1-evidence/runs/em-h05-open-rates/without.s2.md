**What's going wrong:** your subject lines are the likely cause, not your list or content. The pattern is clear in your data:

| Subject line type | Open rate |
|---|---|
| Issue number or generic label (#112, #113, #115, "Newsletter: summer reading list") | 15-19% |
| Specific and reader-relevant (Heather Ridge snow, rain shell question) | 34-38% |

The four weak ones tell a reader nothing about what's inside. An issue number is useful to you but gives a reader no reason to open. "Newsletter:" is the same problem. The two strong ones name a place, a number, or a problem the reader has. The 38% on the rain shell question is a good rate, so the audience is engaged when given a reason.

Two caveats. Six sends is a small sample, and I can't see send time, preview text, or content differences. "Summer reading list" may also have been a weaker topic for this audience, not just a weak subject line. I'd still bet heavily on the subject lines, since the split is so consistent.

Also check your preview text. If it's blank or defaults to "View in browser" or the first line of the header, you're wasting your second-biggest open driver.

**Subject lines for the next issue:**

1. "3 hikes just opened up (plus a bear canister question)"
2. "Three trails are open again this week"
3. "Are you carrying your bear canister wrong?"
4. "3 newly open hikes and one trekking pole mistake"
5. "Your trekking poles are probably set wrong"

**Preview text, paired in order:**

1. "Trail conditions for each, a quick pole tip, and a reader's canister question answered."
2. "Where to go this weekend, what to expect underfoot, and one gear tip."
3. "A reader asked, and the answer isn't what most hikers assume."
4. "Conditions on the newly open trails, plus a 2-minute fix for sore knees on descents."
5. "Also in this issue: three hikes that just opened and a bear canister Q&A."

Fill in the actual trail names or the real pole tip where you can. Specifics like "Heather Ridge" did better than generic wording. If the pole tip is a concrete mistake, such as length on descents, name it in the subject line.

**My recommendation:** lead with #1 or #2 as the main send, since the open hikes are your most timely and useful content. Use #3 or #5 as an A/B test against it. Since the rain shell question did best, a question aimed at a gear worry may be your strongest format.

**Going forward:**
- Drop the issue number from the subject line and put it in the email header.
- Write every subject line around one specific thing in the issue.
- Run a simple A/B test each week so you build more than six data points.
