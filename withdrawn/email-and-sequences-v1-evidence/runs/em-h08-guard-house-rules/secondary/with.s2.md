Subject: Dispatch 31 — notes from the bench

Hello friends,

This week I finished the walnut stool, and I learned something I would rather have learned some other way.

I rushed the clamps on the glue-up. Nothing looked wrong until I pulled them off and found a joint had opened up on one leg. It's the kind of discovery that makes your stomach drop. I didn't want to scrap a piece of walnut over one bad joint, so I cut a spline into the opening, glued it in, and trimmed it flush. The leg is solid again, and I'm glad I didn't throw the whole thing out.

The lesson is boring and true: slow down on glue-up day. Dry-fit every joint first. Lay out your clamps and cauls before you open the glue, not after. Check for square and flatness while the glue is still workable. Give yourself more time than you think you need, because the glue doesn't care about your schedule.

The stool is going to my sister's house. It's the first piece I've made that is headed straight to someone I love, and that made the repair feel more personal than it might have otherwise. I hope it holds up for many years of use.

Next issue, I start a workbench vise. A good vise is one of those tools that makes everything else in the shop easier, and I'm looking forward to working through the hardware, the layout, and the parts that will test my patience.
