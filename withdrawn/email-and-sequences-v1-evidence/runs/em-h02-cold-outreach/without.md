Here's a sequence that reads like one person writing to another. Brackets are placeholders.

## Email 1: Cold intro

**Subject:** Bookkeeping for [Clinic Name]

Hi Dr. [Last name],

I'm a freelance bookkeeper in Tucson. For the past six years I've done the books for small animal clinics around Arizona and New Mexico. I found [Clinic Name] on the state licensing board list and wanted to introduce myself.

I handle the monthly bookkeeping, payroll reconciliation, and sales tax filing, so the numbers are closed out and you're not chasing them. I know clinic books have their own quirks, like inventory and retail sales mixed in with services, so I'm not learning that on your dime.

If you already have someone you're happy with, no need to reply. If you're ever looking for help, or just want a second opinion on how your books are set up, I'm glad to talk for 15 minutes with no obligation.

Thanks for your time,
[Name]
[Phone] | [Website, if any]
[Mailing address]

*If you'd rather not hear from me, just reply "no thanks" and I won't write again.*

## Email 2: Follow-up (about 5–7 days later)

**Subject:** Re: Bookkeeping for [Clinic Name]

Hi Dr. [Last name],

I'm following up on my note last week in case it got buried. Clinic inboxes are busy.

One thing that might be useful even if you're not looking for help: sales tax is where I see small clinics get tripped up most, especially with the split between taxable retail items and non-taxable services. If you ever want a quick look at whether yours is set up correctly, I'm happy to do that.

Either way, no pressure.

[Name]

## Email 3: Final follow-up (about 10–14 days after #2)

**Subject:** Re: Bookkeeping for [Clinic Name]

Hi Dr. [Last name],

This is my last note, so I won't keep filling your inbox. If bookkeeping, payroll, or sales tax ever becomes something you'd like off your plate, you can reach me at [email] or [phone].

Wishing you and the team at [Clinic Name] a good [season/rest of the year].

[Name]

## Notes

- **Verify the sales tax claim in email 2.** Make sure the taxable-retail versus non-taxable-services split matches how it works in the states you file in. Arizona's TPT and New Mexico's GRT differ from standard sales tax, and a clinic owner will notice if the wording is off. Swap in a different specific if you'd rather.
- **Legal requirements.** Even from your personal address, commercial email under CAN-SPAM needs a physical mailing address and a working opt-out. I included both. Honor any opt-out right away and don't send follow-ups to anyone who declines.
- **Personalize the first line.** For 40 clinics, spend a minute on each. Mention the town, a service they list, or something from their website. That does more than anything else to keep it from feeling like a blast.
- **Send them individually.** Skip BCC and mail merge if you can. Spread sends over a few days, because 40 identical emails at once can land in spam.
- **Subject lines.** Plain ones like these tend to get opened more than clever ones.
