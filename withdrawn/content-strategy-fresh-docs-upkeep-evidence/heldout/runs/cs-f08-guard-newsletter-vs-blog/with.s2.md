I'd pick the monthly newsletter, with one caveat.

**Why the newsletter:**
- **A schedule is easier to keep than a mood.** A blog you post to "whenever we feel like it" tends to go quiet for four months, and that looks worse than never having started. A monthly deadline gives a part-time person a reason to sit down and write.
- **It reaches people who already care.** Board game audiences, especially Kickstarter backers, want to follow along while a game is being made. An email list is an audience you own. A blog needs search traffic or social posts to get read.
- **It's a smaller job.** A newsletter can be 300 to 500 words: what we're prototyping, one photo or a playtest finding, and what's next. Longer posts take more effort per piece, and the effort is what makes people stop.
- **It builds your launch list.** When you eventually run a campaign, the newsletter is what turns into day-one backers.

**The caveat:** Write each issue so it can also live on your site as an archive page. That gives you blog-style content, which helps with search and gives new visitors something to read, without a second publishing job.

**When I'd pick the blog instead:** If one of you enjoys writing long design-process posts, like postmortems or "why we cut this mechanic" essays, and would write them anyway, the blog suits that. Those posts also last longer in search. But I wouldn't plan the whole program around that, because it relies on enthusiasm and not on a routine.

You could start with the newsletter, and if a particular issue turns into something longer, publish it as a blog post as well.
