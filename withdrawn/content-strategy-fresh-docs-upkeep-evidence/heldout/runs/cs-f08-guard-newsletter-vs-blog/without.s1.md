I'd pick the blog.

**Why it fits a part-time runner:** A monthly newsletter is a deadline you have to hit every month, whether or not there's anything worth saying. For a small studio where one person is doing this on the side, that's the thing that kills it. Months with a stalled prototype or a crunch on a Kickstarter become missed issues, and a newsletter that skips a month loses the trust that made it worthwhile. A blog has no cadence to break. A quiet stretch is just a quiet stretch.

**Why it fits board games specifically:** Design work produces good long-form material: why a mechanism got cut, what playtesting revealed, how a component decision played out. Those posts are interesting to other designers and to players. They also keep getting found through search long after publishing, whereas a newsletter issue is mostly read once, in the week it lands.

**The catch:** A blog doesn't bring people back by itself. Readers have to remember to check it, and that's where a newsletter is stronger. It reaches people directly and builds a list you own, which matters a lot when you launch a game.

So my suggestion is to start with the blog and add a simple email signup from day one. Then when a post goes up, or when you have a launch or playtest call, you can email subscribers. That gets you most of the newsletter's benefit without a monthly deadline. If the blog posts turn out to be coming steadily, you can always bundle them into a digest later.
