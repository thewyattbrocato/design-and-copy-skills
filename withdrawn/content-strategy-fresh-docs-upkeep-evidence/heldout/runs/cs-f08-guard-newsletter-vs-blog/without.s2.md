I'd pick the monthly newsletter.

**Why it fits a part-time runner:**
- **A cadence beats good intentions.** "Whenever we feel like it" tends to become "every four months, then never." A monthly deadline forces output, and part-time projects usually die from silence, not from bad posts.
- **It's lower effort per issue.** A newsletter can be short: what we playtested, what broke, a sketch, a decision we made. A blog post implies something polished and longer, and that's a bigger lift to start.
- **You own the audience.** Board game fans who sign up for email are your future Kickstarter backers and preorder buyers. Blog readers mostly arrive via search or social, then disappear unless you've captured them somehow.
- **It suits "what we're designing."** Ongoing work-in-progress updates are exactly what newsletters do well. They build a sense of following along with the studio.

**What you give up:** blogs are better for long-term discoverability (search traffic, shareable deep-dives). A newsletter alone won't bring in many new people.

**My suggestion:** start with the newsletter, and keep a simple archive of past issues on your site, so they're still findable. If one issue turns into a really good long piece, publish it as a blog post too. That gets you most of the blog's benefit without committing to it.

The one caveat: if the person running it is a stronger writer than organizer, or only gets inspired in bursts, the blog could suit them better. But for a small studio building an audience, I'd go newsletter.
