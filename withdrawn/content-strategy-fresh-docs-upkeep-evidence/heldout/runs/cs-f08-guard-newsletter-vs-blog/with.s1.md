I'd pick the blog.

**Why the blog fits a part-time runner:**
- **No deadline treadmill.** A monthly newsletter is a promise, and part-time owners miss promises. A missed month looks like the studio went quiet, while a blog with a gap between posts just looks like a blog.
- **Design work is naturally long-form.** Playtest findings, why you cut a mechanic, how a prototype evolved: these are the things people who like board games enjoy reading, and they need room and images.
- **Posts keep working.** A good post on how you balanced a worker-placement economy gets found through search and shared for years. A newsletter issue is mostly read once, in the week it lands.
- **It's easier to staff.** Someone can write one post when they have something to say, and the designers can contribute without a monthly assignment.

**The catch:** a blog has no built-in audience. People have to find it, and you don't have a way to reach them when you launch something. That matters a lot for a game studio, because your Kickstarter or preorder moment depends on having people to tell.

So my real recommendation is the blog, plus a simple email signup on it. Send a short note only when you publish something good or have news. That gets you the lasting content and the owned audience without committing to a monthly cadence. If the signup list grows and you find you have enough to say regularly, you can turn it into a proper newsletter later.

If the person running it would rather write short and often than long and occasionally, flip this and go newsletter. But with a part-time owner and a small team, I'd bet on the blog.
