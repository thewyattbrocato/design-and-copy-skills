I'd pick the blog.

**Why it fits a part-time runner:** A monthly newsletter is a deadline, and deadlines are what kill part-time projects. Miss a month or two and it feels like failure, so people quietly quit. A blog with no schedule can go quiet for six weeks without looking broken, and it picks back up without any awkwardness.

**Why it fits a board game studio:** Your best material is probably lumpy. A playtest that exposed a design flaw, why you cut a mechanic, a look at how you made the art or components, or a postmortem on a prototype are all worth a long post. They don't arrive on a monthly cycle, and some months you'll have nothing worth saying. A newsletter would make you fill space anyway.

**Longevity:** Blog posts get found through search and shared for years. A newsletter issue mostly gets read once, then sits in an inbox.

**The tradeoff:** A newsletter is better at pulling people back. Email reaches fans directly, whereas a blog depends on people remembering to visit or finding you through search. For a studio that will eventually launch games (Kickstarter or otherwise), an email list is valuable.

So my suggestion is to run the blog and add a simple email signup that sends people a note when a new post goes up. You get the low-pressure cadence and still build the list. If the blog turns out to be consistent and you want a regular touchpoint later, you can add a monthly digest then.

The one caveat is that a blog only beats a newsletter if the person running it actually does write posts. If they'd never post without a deadline, the newsletter's forced rhythm might serve you better.
