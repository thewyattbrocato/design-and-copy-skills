Keep it tiny: tie updates to events you already have, not a separate project.

1. **Add a "Last reviewed" date to each page.** It takes 12 edits once and makes staleness visible to you and to readers.

2. **Update on release, not on a calendar.** Add one line to your ship checklist: "Does this change touch any help page?" Most drift comes from shipped changes, so this catches it at the source.

3. **Use support as your detector.** When you answer a question that a help page should have covered (or got wrong), fix the page right then, before closing the ticket. Your real questions tell you what matters.

4. **Do a 30-minute quarterly pass.** Put it on both calendars. Open each page, click through the steps, and check the screenshots. Prune anything that's no longer worth maintaining.

5. **Prefer less content.** Fewer screenshots, fewer exact UI labels, and links to the product instead of long walkthroughs. Pages with less detail go stale more slowly.

6. **Split ownership.** You take pages 1–6 and your cofounder takes 7–12, so nobody assumes the other one has it.

With 12 pages, steps 2 and 3 do most of the work. The quarterly pass is your safety net.
