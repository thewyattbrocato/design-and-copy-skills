Keep it tiny: make it a habit, not a project.

1. **Tie docs to releases.** Add one line to your ship checklist: "Did this change anything users see? If so, update the matching help page." This catches most drift.

2. **Add a "last reviewed" date** at the top of each page. It makes staleness visible, and readers can see you care.

3. **Quarterly 30-minute pass.** Put it on both calendars. Split the 12 pages, six each, and skim for wrong screenshots, renamed buttons, and dead links. Update the date.

4. **Let support tell you.** When a customer asks something a page should have answered, or says "the docs say X but...", fix the page that day. Support tickets are your best staleness detector.

5. **Keep pages lean.** Fewer screenshots and less detail mean less to go stale. Describe things by name ("Click **Add event**") rather than pixel-perfect images where you can.

6. **Assign owners.** Each page gets one of you as owner, so nothing is "someone's job."

If you only do two things, do #1 and #4. They cost almost nothing and catch the changes that actually matter.
