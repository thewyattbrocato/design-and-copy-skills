**Stuck decision:** who owns each page, and what tells you it's stale.

**1. Split the pages by owner (10 minutes).** Put all 12 in a sheet with columns: page, owner, last checked, what it depends on (e.g. a screenshot, a setting name, pricing). Give each page one owner. You take half and your cofounder takes half, split by who knows that part of the product best.

**2. Tie review to releases, not the calendar.** Add one line to your release routine: "Which help pages mention what we changed?" The owner of those pages fixes them before shipping. This catches most staleness, because pages go stale when the product changes.

**3. Use support as your signal.** When you answer a question that a help page should have covered, or that it got wrong, fix the page right then. Spend two minutes on it before you close the ticket.

**4. Do a quarterly sweep.** Each of you opens your pages and clicks through every step, then updates "last checked." That's about an hour each.

**5. Cut or merge.** If two pages overlap, or one covers a feature almost nobody asks about, merge or delete it. Fewer pages means less to keep current.

*Note: "last edited" doesn't tell you a page is right, so only update "last checked" when you've actually walked through the steps.*
