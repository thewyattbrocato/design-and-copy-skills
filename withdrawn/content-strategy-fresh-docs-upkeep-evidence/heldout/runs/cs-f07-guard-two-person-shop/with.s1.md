**Direction:** Tie help-page updates to product changes, and add a short monthly check. Don't try to audit all 12 pages.

**1. Give each page an owner.** Put a one-line table in a shared doc with columns for page, owner and last checked. Split the pages between the two of you by who knows that area best.

**2. Update on triggers, not on a schedule.** Add one line to your release or ship checklist: "Which help pages mention what we changed?" Pricing, UI, integrations and settings changes are the usual causes.

**3. Do a 30-minute check on the first of each month.** Each of you walks through your pages while using the widget, then updates the "last checked" date. Fix what's wrong, and delete any page you'd be embarrassed to defend.

**4. Let support tell you.** Whenever a customer question shows a page was wrong or unclear, fix it that day. Add a "Was this wrong? Email us" link at the bottom of each page.

**5. Start this week.** Spend one hour making the table and doing the first pass. That pass also tells you how stale things really are, and I can't judge that without seeing the pages.

**Cut:** Merge or retire pages nobody can keep current. Fewer than 12 accurate pages is better than 12 doubtful ones.
