Stuck decision: who owns each page, and what tells you it's stale.

**1. Split the 12 pages between you two.** One name per page, in a small list: title, owner, last checked. Whoever answers support for a topic owns that page.

**2. Tie updates to releases, not the calendar.** Add one line to your release checklist: "Which help pages does this change touch?" Search the help pages for the feature name or the screen label you changed. Fix those pages before you ship.

**3. Let support tell you what's wrong.** When a customer asks something a page should have answered, fix or add that page the same day. Keep a tally of "page was wrong or missing" so you can see which pages need attention.

**4. Do a 30-minute check each quarter.** Each of you walks through your pages with the product open and follows the steps. Update "last checked" as you go.

**5. Cut something.** Any page nobody can vouch for, or that covers a feature you barely support, gets merged into another page or removed. With 12 pages, fewer and correct beats complete and shaky.

To start this week, make the list and do one pass today. That pass also tells you how out of date things really are, which is the thing you're worried about.
