Keep it small and tie it to things you already do:

1. **Add a "last checked" date to each page.** It makes staleness visible, and you'll see at a glance which pages are overdue.

2. **Update docs when you ship.** Put one line in your release checklist or PR template: "Does this change affect a help page?" Most drift happens because a feature changed and nobody remembered the docs.

3. **Let support tell you what's wrong.** When you answer a question that a page should have covered, or that a page got wrong, fix the page right then. That's the cheapest, most reliable signal you have.

4. **Do a quarterly pass.** Put 45 minutes on the calendar every three months. With 12 pages, that's about 4 minutes each. Skim, click through the steps, fix what's off, and update the date.

5. **Cut pages you don't need.** Fewer pages means less to maintain. Merge or delete anything rarely used.

Also, screenshots go stale fastest. Use them sparingly, or describe the steps in text instead.

If you only do one thing, do #2. With a team of two, tying docs to shipping is what keeps them current.
