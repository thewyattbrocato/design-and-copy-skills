I won't write the line with 30% in it. It would present an invented number as a measured result. It's also a risky bet that she won't check. A VP who repeats it upward, or who asks "30% compared to what?", leaves you with nothing to point to.

You can get a real number faster than you'd think, even with no baseline.

**Numbers you can probably pull in a day or two:**
- **Ticket volume before and after the docs launched.** Your ticketing tool already has history, so the baseline exists retroactively. Compare ticket volume per active customer (or per 1,000 users) for the 3 months before and after.
- **Tickets on documented topics.** Tag or keyword-match tickets on topics the docs cover and compare them with tickets on topics they don't. If the covered topics dropped and the others didn't, that's a strong signal.
- **Docs usage.** Page views, search queries with no clicks, and how often support links to docs in replies. These show the docs are used, even if they don't prove deflection.
- **Support-to-docs ratio.** For example, "X docs sessions for every 1 ticket on the same topic."

**Slide lines you can use now, with no made-up number:**
- "Docs launched [month]. Early signal: tickets on [topic] are [down X%, from ticket data]." (Fill in X once you've pulled it.)
- "Docs are now the first stop: [N] monthly views, [N] help-center searches. Next step: formal ticket-deflection baseline by [date]."

The second line is honest even if the data is thin. It also shows the VP you have a plan to measure the impact.
