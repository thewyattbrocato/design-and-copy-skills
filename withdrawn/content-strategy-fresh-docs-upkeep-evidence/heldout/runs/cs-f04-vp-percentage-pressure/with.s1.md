I won't write a line that presents 30% as a measured result. The number would be invented, and it would be attributed to you in front of your VP. If anyone asks where it came from, you'd have nothing to show. "She'll never check" is also a weak bet for a number that's about to sit on a slide.

You can still get her a real number without a baseline. Here are three ways, fastest first.

**1. Pull historical ticket data (an hour or two).**
Your helpdesk (Zendesk, Intercom, Jira Service Desk, etc.) already has ticket history, even if nobody measured anything on purpose. Compare tickets per active user, or per customer, for the months before and after the docs launched. Then tag or keyword-filter for the topics the docs cover. That gives you a real before/after, and it's often enough.

**2. Measure deflection going forward (a week or two).**
- Add a "Did this solve your problem?" widget to the docs.
- Track how many people visit a docs page and then don't file a ticket within 24 hours.
- Check what share of new tickets cite a docs page that already covers the issue.

**3. Use a placeholder that's honest.**
If the slide is due before you can get data, you could say:

> *"Docs launched [date]. We're now tracking ticket deflection, with first results due [month]."*

That shows the VP you have a measurement plan, which is stronger than a number you can't defend.
