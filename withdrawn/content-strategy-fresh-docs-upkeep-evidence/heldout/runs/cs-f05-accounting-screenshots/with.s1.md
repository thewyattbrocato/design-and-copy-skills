**Pick: change them.** Make the steps the main content and stop relying on annotated screenshots.

**Why not keep them:** A screenshot with an arrow is wrong as soon as the vendor moves a button, and nothing flags it. You only find out when a customer gets stuck, which is the pattern you described.

**Why not drop them entirely:** Some steps are hard to find in words, such as a setting buried in a menu. Removing every image would make those steps worse.

**What to do instead**
1. **Write each integration as text steps.** Name the menu or field as the vendor labels it, and say what to copy from there into Marlowpine. Labels usually survive a redesign better than layouts do. Where the vendor offers a stable link or a search term for the settings page, use that.
2. **Add a "you'll know it worked when…" step on the Marlowpine side.** This is the part you control and it doesn't go stale. A customer who hits a changed screen can still see that something is off and describe it.
3. **Keep a screenshot only where words can't locate the thing.** Leave off the arrow and crop it tight. Put the date and the vendor's product version in the caption, so a reader can tell it's old.
4. **Make staleness visible to you.** Each page gets a named owner (whoever handles integration support today) and a "last checked" date. Someone walks through all three connections end to end on a regular schedule, and also when a vendor announces a redesign. Add a "this page didn't match my screen" link to each page, so the next report reaches you before it becomes an angry email.

**Cut:** Delete the arrow overlays, and every screenshot that only repeats what the text now says.

**Open questions:** How often to do the walkthrough depends on how often each vendor ships changes, and I don't have that.
