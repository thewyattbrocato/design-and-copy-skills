**Pick: change them.** Replace most screenshots with text steps, and keep a few only where text can't do the job.

**Why not keep them:** A screenshot with an arrow goes stale the moment the vendor moves a button, and nothing tells you it has. The page looks authoritative and is wrong, which is why you hear about it from customers.

**Why not drop them entirely:** Some steps are hard to find in text alone, like a buried settings page or an unlabeled icon. Without any visuals, those steps get harder for users.

**What to do instead**
1. **Write steps around labels, not positions.** For example: "In Settings → Integrations → API access, click *Create key*." Labels and menu paths usually survive a redesign even when the layout doesn't. A user can often still follow slightly outdated text, but not a screenshot of a screen that no longer exists.
2. **Keep screenshots only for ambiguous steps.** Aim for one or two per integration, such as the spot where people get lost. Crop tightly to the relevant control. A tight crop is less likely to be invalidated by changes elsewhere on the screen.
3. **Show the Marlowpine side instead.** Screenshots of your own screens are yours to control and update with your releases. Put the "paste your key here" step there, not in the vendor's UI.
4. **Put a "Last verified: <date>" line on each page.** Users can tell how much to trust it, and you can see what's overdue.
5. **Catch redesigns before customers do.** Once a quarter, or when a vendor announces a change, have someone run each connection end to end with a test account. Also add a "This didn't match my screen" link on each page, so reports reach you directly instead of arriving as angry support tickets.

With three vendors and few screenshots, the quarterly check should take well under an hour per tool.
