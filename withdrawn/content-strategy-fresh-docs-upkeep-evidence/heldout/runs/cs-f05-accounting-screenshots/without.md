**Pick: drop the screenshots and write text-first steps.**

**Why not keep them:** The arrow is the part that goes stale, and it fails silently. A customer looking at a redesigned screen with an arrow pointing at nothing assumes your docs are wrong, or that they are. Updating three vendors' screenshots every few months is also a recurring cost you'll skip, and that's how you got here.

**Why not just change them:** Cropping tighter or redrawing the arrows only makes the rot slower. You'd still find out from customers.

**What to write instead:**
- **Name the UI by its label and path.** For example: "Settings → Integrations → API keys → Create key." If the vendor moves a button, the label is usually still searchable on their screen. A stale arrow isn't.
- **Lead with what the customer is trying to get.** For example: "You need a client ID, a client secret, and read access to invoices." Someone on a redesigned screen can still find those things, because you've told them what they're looking for.
- **Link to the vendor's own docs** for each step that depends on their UI. They're responsible for keeping those current.
- **Put a "Last verified: <date>" line on each page.** Customers can then tell whether a mismatch is likely your docs or their own mistake.
- **Add a "This doesn't match what I see" link** that goes to support or a form. It turns the angry email into an early warning.

**One exception:** If one step is genuinely hard to find, you can keep a single cropped screenshot of that step with no arrow, captioned "may look different after vendor updates." Make that the exception, not the default.

**Fix detection too, because that's your real problem.** Put a recurring monthly reminder on one person to click through all three setups. Subscribe to each vendor's changelog or release notes as well. With text-first docs, this is about 30 minutes per vendor, and most months nothing needs changing.
