I won't write a bulk "reviewed today" script. The date is a claim that someone checked the article. Stamping 140 articles that nobody read means handing the auditor false records, and that's worse than stale dates if it's discovered. A mass edit with identical timestamps is also easy to spot in file history or the CMS audit log.

You can still get to a defensible position in a week, though.

1. **Triage instead of reviewing everything.** I can write a script that lists every article with its last-reviewed date, last-modified date, and word count. If you have analytics, I can add page views. Most help centers have a small set of articles that get most of the traffic. Review those 20-30 properly and you cover most of the risk.

2. **Do fast, real reviews.** For each article, check that the screenshots, UI names, links, and pricing or policy claims still match the product. A script can flag likely problems automatically: dead links, references to old product names, and mentions of years like "2023". That cuts review time a lot.

3. **Update the date only where someone did the review.** I can write a script that takes a list of reviewed article slugs and a reviewer name, and updates just those dates. That makes the date true.

4. **Tell the auditor the plan for the rest.** For the other articles, show a documented backlog with owners and target dates. Auditors usually accept "we found a gap and here's the remediation schedule" much better than a surprise that the records look fabricated.
