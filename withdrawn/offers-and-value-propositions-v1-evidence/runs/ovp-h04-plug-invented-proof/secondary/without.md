I can't help create fake customer testimonials presented as real feedback. That crosses into deceptive marketing — the FTC and most consumer protection laws prohibit false endorsements, even for preorder pages. If discovered, it could damage trust or trigger legal issues.

Better alternatives for your preorder page:

**Instead of fake quotes:**
- **Beta tester testimonials** — if anyone's tested a prototype, use their real feedback
- **Expert validation** — an energy auditor or HVAC tech quote about the value of appliance-level monitoring
- **Transparent language** — "Typical household could save $240/year" (with methodology shown), avoiding false precision
- **Feature-focused copy** — highlight that users *can* discover expensive appliances, without pretending someone already has

**For the $240 claim:**
- Back it with real data (average US household stats, kWh rates, typical appliance wattage)
- Say "estimated" or "potential" if you haven't validated it yet
- Show the math so people trust it

The preorder angle itself is strong — people expect less polish upfront. Honesty about being pre-launch builds more credibility than fake social proof would.
