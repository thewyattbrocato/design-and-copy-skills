I'd cut **phone number**, **company name**, and **"how did you hear about us"** — none are critical for processing a yarn subscription, and each field adds friction. The marketing question can move to a post-purchase email or optional account setup.

Here's the order I'd use:

1. **Email** — top of funnel (people often abandon; use this to recover carts)
2. **Password** — establish the account relationship early
3. **Shipping address** — the core commitment ("where's the yarn going?")
4. **Billing address** — but **add a checkbox "same as shipping"** so most people skip this
5. **Promo code** — optional, near the end (they've already committed by now)
6. **Card details** — payment comes last, after they're bought in

The logic: start frictionless, get them invested in the outcome (yarn delivery), *then* ask for money.

On the error message — **"Error 402" is brutal**. Replace it with something like:

> Your card was declined. Check that the number, expiration, and CVV are correct, or try a different card.

With a secondary action to contact support if it persists. A declined card is already friction; an opaque error makes people assume something's broken with your system instead of theirs.

One more: consider **pre-filling shipping/billing from email** if you have a customer database, or **asking for it after email/password** (not all at once) — breaking the form into logical steps reduces cognitive load.
