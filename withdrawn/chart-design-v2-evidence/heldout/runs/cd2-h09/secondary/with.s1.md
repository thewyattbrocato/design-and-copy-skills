The chart is in good shape. The geometry checks out, and the main thing I'd fix before print is the claim's support, not the drawing.

**What's working**
- **Scale is honest and consistent.** Every bar is 40 px per day from a zero baseline (9.8 → 392, 6.0 → 240, 3.1 → 124), so the lengths match the values.
- **Labels sit correctly.** Each value sits about 6 px past its bar end.
- **Order is clear.** The bars are sorted by value, so the eye reads the ranking top to bottom.
- **Color is restrained.** One orange accent for Old Town, gray for context. The two colors also separate in grayscale, so they survive a black-and-white print.
- **Text is direct.** Workshop names are labeled at the bars, with no legend.

**What I'd change**
1. **Add the sample size.** The headline compares averages, and an average over a few dozen jobs can swing on one stuck repair. Put the number of repairs per workshop in the subtitle or a footnote, e.g. "Average days from drop-off to pickup, 2025 (n = 142 Old Town; …)". If the counts are small, consider showing the median too.
2. **Say "average" in the headline.** The headline reads "take 9.8 days," but the subtitle says it's an average. Using "average" in the headline keeps the two consistent: "Old Town repairs average 9.8 days, well above 3 to 6 days at our other workshops."
3. **Add a source line.** For an annual report, a short "Source: co-op repair log, 2025" at the bottom is standard and helps the chart stand alone.
4. **Optional: a reference line.** A thin dashed line at about 4.7 days, labeled "other workshops, avg," would make the comparison visible without the reader doing arithmetic. That figure is the unweighted mean of the four other workshops; if you use it, say so or weight it by job count.
5. **Optional: draw the zero baseline.** A thin vertical rule at x=100 would make the zero start visible. It's already honest, so this is cosmetic.

Nothing else needs changing. Once you've confirmed the job counts, the first two edits are the ones that matter for print.
