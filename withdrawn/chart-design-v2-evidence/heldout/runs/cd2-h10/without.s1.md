A pie chart is fine here. You have three parts of a whole, one of them clearly dominant, and the audience will take it in at a glance. Pies break down with many slices or near-equal values, and neither applies.

- **Label the slices directly.** Put the category and percentage on or next to each slice, with no legend. For example, "Hardware 62% (769)".
- **Order the slices.** Start at 12 o'clock with the largest slice and go clockwise: hardware, software, other.
- **Use color to focus attention.** Give hardware a strong color, software a muted one, and "everything else" gray.
- **Put the takeaway in the title.** Something like "Hardware drove 62% of 1,240 repair tickets" works better than "Tickets by category".
- **Keep it flat.** Skip 3D, shadows and exploded slices.

If you want a cleaner look, a donut with "1,240 tickets" in the center works well. A single horizontal stacked bar is also good if the slide is wide. A bar chart would be overkill for three numbers.
