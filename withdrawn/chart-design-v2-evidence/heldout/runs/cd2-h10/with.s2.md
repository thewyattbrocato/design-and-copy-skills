A pie is fine here. The three categories are exclusive and add up to 100%. The point is a quick "mostly hardware" read, and a slide gets seen once, not monitored. The skill's pie rule fits: two to five parts that sum to a whole, with the values printed on the slices.

If you want a safer, cleaner option, use a **single horizontal 100% stacked bar**. It carries the same message, and the slice lengths are easier to compare. Sorted horizontal bars with the percentages on them are a third option.

**If you use a pie:**
- Put the labels on the slices, like "Hardware 62%", and leave out the legend.
- Order the slices largest to smallest, starting at 12 o'clock and running clockwise.
- Color hardware with one strong accent and make software and other muted grays. Hardware is the story.
- Make it flat, with no 3D, shadows, or gradients.
- Use a finding as the title, for example "Hardware drove 62% of our 1,240 repair tickets this year". Also add the ticket count to the slide.
- Add the raw counts as a secondary label if the room cares about them. They come to roughly 769 hardware, 310 software and 161 other.
- Say what "everything else" holds in a footnote or speaker note. A 13% catch-all with no explanation invites questions.

I'd skip a donut, which adds nothing, and any "exploded" slices.
