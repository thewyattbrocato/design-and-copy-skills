A pie is fine here, and it fits the skill's rule for pies. You have three parts that sum to one whole, and the point is a quick "most of it is hardware" read. A single horizontal 100% stacked bar is a good alternative. Both work, so pick one using the notes below.

**Pie**
- It suits a slide where people glance at it for a few seconds. The 62% slice is clearly the biggest, and that's the message.
- Print the values on or next to the slices, for example "Hardware 62%". Don't use a legend.
- Order the slices largest to smallest, starting at 12 o'clock and going clockwise. Make "Everything else" the last slice.
- Use a flat 2D pie with no 3D, shadows or exploded slices.
- Color hardware with one strong accent. Make software and "everything else" muted grays or a pale tint of the same hue.

**Single 100% bar**
- Choose this if you want a cleaner look or the slide is wide. The segments are easier to compare by length than by angle, and the labels fit inside or directly above each segment.
- It's less familiar as a visual, but nobody will misread it.

**Skip**
- A donut doesn't help here. Use a pie instead, or put "1,240 tickets" in the center if you want the total on the slide.
- Three separate bars are accurate but dull. They also give up the "parts of one whole" feel.

**Title:** put the finding in the title, such as "Hardware made up 62% of our 1,240 repair tickets this year." The chart then supports the sentence, and you don't have to explain it aloud.
