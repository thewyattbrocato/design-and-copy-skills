A pie works here, so you can use one. A single 100% stacked bar would work slightly better.

**Why a pie is fine:** The three parts sum to one whole (62 + 25 + 13 = 100). Three slices is under the usual limit of about five. The point of the slide is a coarse "most of it is hardware" read, and a pie shows that well. It's also a one-off all-hands slide, not a dashboard people will keep checking.

**Why a single horizontal bar is slightly better:** Lengths on a shared baseline are easier to compare than angles. That matters most for 25% against 13%, which are harder to tell apart as slices. A bar also leaves room for labels inside or directly above each segment, so you don't need a legend.

**If you go with the pie, these will make it read well:**
- **Order and start:** Start the largest slice at 12 o'clock and go clockwise in descending order: Hardware, Software, Other.
- **Labels:** Print the category and percentage on or next to each slice, like "Hardware 62%". Skip the legend.
- **Color:** Make Hardware the accent color and the other two muted grays or tints. Don't give each slice its own saturated hue.
- **Flat style:** No 3D, no exploded slices, no shadows.
- **Total:** Put "1,240 repair tickets, 2026" in the title or as a small note.
- **Title:** State the finding, for example "Hardware accounts for 62% of our 1,240 repair tickets", not "Tickets by category".

**If you want the bar:** Draw one horizontal bar spanning the slide width, split 62 / 25 / 13 with the same labels and colors.

**One check:** Make sure the three categories are mutually exclusive, so each ticket is counted once. Your percentages sum to 100, so they almost certainly are. If a ticket could be both hardware and software, this wouldn't be a part-of-whole chart.
