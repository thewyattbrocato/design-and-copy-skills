The chart has two problems: six equally saturated colors give every clinic the same emphasis, and the legend makes the reader match colors to names. Fix both and the Dunmore story jumps out.

1. **Make Dunmore the only colored line.** Set the other five to one light gray (for example #BDBDBD) and give Dunmore a strong accent such as a dark blue or red. The eye goes to the one colored line first.

2. **Replace the legend with direct labels.** Delete the legend. Put "Dunmore" at the right end of its line, and either label the gray lines as a group ("Other 5 clinics") or skip their names entirely since they aren't the point.

3. **Annotate Dunmore's start and end values.** Add data labels at Q1 2023 ("42 min") and Q4 2025 ("71 min") on the Dunmore line. The two numbers you care about then appear on the chart itself.

4. **Optionally shade the range the other clinics stayed in.** A light gray band from 35 to 50 behind the chart shows "these stayed here" without five lines competing for attention. This works well if the other five are close together, which your numbers suggest.

5. **Strip the gridlines.** Remove the vertical gridlines and most or all of the horizontal ones. If you keep any, make them very light and keep only a few. The y-axis labels are enough for reading values.

6. **Rewrite the title as the takeaway.** Replace "Wait Time by Clinic" with something like: *"Dunmore's ER wait rose from 42 to 71 minutes since 2023; other clinics stayed between 35 and 50."* The title now says what the chart shows, and the chart backs it up.

7. **Check the y-axis range.** Something like 30 to 80 minutes puts the lines in the chart's middle and makes the Dunmore climb clear. A zero baseline would flatten the differences, which hides the story. Just make sure the axis is labeled, and the reader can see it isn't starting at zero.

In most charting tools, steps 1 through 3 and 5 are quick. Set the series colors, turn off the legend, add data labels to specific points, and remove the gridlines in the axis settings.
