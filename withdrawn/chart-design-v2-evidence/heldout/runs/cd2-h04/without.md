It feels off because the chart's visual story is much bigger than the data's.

**What's wrong**

1. **The truncated axis is the main problem.** Bars encode value by length, so the baseline has to be zero. With the axis at 4,800–4,960, the bar heights are roughly 20, 61, 105 and 150 units. Q4 looks about 7.5× taller than Q1, but the real difference is 130 tickets, or about 2.7%.
2. **The title claims more than the data shows.** "Huge momentum" describes about 0.9% growth per quarter (+41, +44, +45). That's steady and healthy, but it isn't huge. A skeptical viewer will see the gap between the headline and the numbers and start doubting the rest of the deck.
3. **There are no data labels.** Without numbers on the bars, people have to read the truncated axis to judge the size of the change. Labels would show right away that the values are 4,820 and 4,950.
4. **The 3D effect and bright green add nothing.** 3D distorts bar heights and makes them harder to compare. The bright green says "celebration" before anyone has read the data.

**What I'd change**

- **Start the y-axis at 0.** The four bars will look nearly identical, which is accurate. If the team wants to show the small changes, use a line chart or a chart of quarter-over-quarter change. A line chart doesn't need a zero baseline, but its axis should still be labeled clearly.
- **Put values on the bars** (4,820 / 4,861 / 4,905 / 4,950), and add the percent change if you like.
- **Use a flat, muted single color,** with no 3D.
- **Write a title that states the finding without hype.** For example:
  - "Tickets resolved grew steadily each quarter, up 2.7% since Q1"
  - "Resolution volume up 130 tickets (+2.7%) from Q1 to Q4"
- **Add context if you have it.** Incoming ticket volume, backlog size, or headcount would show whether this is real progress. If volume rose faster than resolutions, the chart is hiding that. If it was flat, the story is steady throughput.

The underlying story is fine: four straight quarters of growth is a good result. It will be more credible if the chart presents it as steady and consistent instead of dramatic.
