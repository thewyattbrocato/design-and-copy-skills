The chart makes a 2.7% increase look like a rocket launch, and the design choices are what create that impression.

**Why it feels off**

- **The truncated axis.** Bar length encodes value, so bars must start at zero. Starting at 4,800 makes Q1's bar 20 units tall and Q4's 150, so Q4 looks 7.5x bigger. The real difference is 4,820 to 4,950, or +130 tickets (+2.7%).
- **No labels.** Without numbers on the bars, readers judge by the distorted shapes and can't see how small the change is.
- **The title editorializes.** "Huge momentum!" claims more than the data shows. Quarter-over-quarter growth is about 41, 44, and 45 tickets, roughly 1% per quarter. That's steady, not explosive.
- **Decoration.** The 3D effect distorts bar heights and adds visual noise. Bright green reads as celebratory, which reinforces the overstatement.

**What I'd change**

1. **Start the axis at 0.** The four bars will look nearly identical. That is the accurate picture, and it's a fine message: stable, consistent volume.
2. **Label each bar** with its value (4,820, 4,861, 4,905, 4,950) so the numbers carry the story.
3. **Use a flat, neutral color** (gray or your brand's neutral). If you want to highlight something, highlight Q4 in your accent color, not everything in bright green. Remove the 3D effect.
4. **Retitle to state the finding.** For example: "Tickets resolved grew steadily: +130 (+2.7%) from Q1 to Q4." Or, "Steady growth of about 1% per quarter." Either is defensible in a deck and survives scrutiny.
5. **If you want to show the trend rather than the totals, use a line chart.** Lines don't require a zero baseline, so a narrower y-axis is acceptable there. But label the axis range clearly and keep the title honest about the size of the change.

If the team wants to make a momentum case, the honest version is "consistent, compounding growth." Overstating it is the thing that's likely to backfire when someone checks the axis.
