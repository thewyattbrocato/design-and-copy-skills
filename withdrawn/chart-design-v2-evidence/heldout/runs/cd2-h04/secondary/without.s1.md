It feels off because the chart makes a 2.7% change look like a rocket launch, and the title asserts a conclusion the numbers don't clearly support.

**What's distorting it**

- **The truncated axis is the main problem.** Bars are measured from the 4,800 baseline, so Q1 is drawn at 20 units tall and Q4 at 150. The last bar looks 7.5x the first, but the real change is 4,820 → 4,950, or +2.7%. A zero-based axis would show four nearly identical bars, which is the accurate picture.
- **The growth is small.** Quarter-over-quarter changes are +0.85%, +0.9%, and +0.9%. That's steady, modest growth, not momentum in the sense the title implies.
- **The title editorializes.** "Huge momentum!" is a claim the chart has to prove, and the chart is what's inflating it.
- **The 3D effect and bright green add emphasis without information.** 3D bars also distort heights through perspective, and bright green reads as "good news" before anyone looks at the numbers.
- **No labels hide the scale.** Without values on the bars, viewers can't see that the axis is cropped, so they're left with the visual impression alone.

**What I'd change**

1. **Start the bars at zero.** If you need to show small differences, use a line chart instead, with the y-axis labeled and the range stated. Don't crop a bar chart.
2. **Label every bar with its value.** Honest labels make the small size of the change obvious, which is the point.
3. **Use a flat, neutral color.** Use one muted color for all bars, or gray for the earlier quarters and one accent for Q4. Drop the 3D.
4. **Make the title state the finding.** For example: "Tickets resolved rose 2.7% over four quarters (4,820 → 4,950)." That's specific, defensible, and still positive.
5. **Add context the chart is missing.** Is "resolved" growing faster than "opened"? If intake is rising too, resolutions going up may not mean the backlog is shrinking. A same-quarter comparison from last year would also show whether this is seasonal. If you have a target or capacity number, include it.

If you want to keep a stronger message, the honest version is a zero-based bar chart with labels plus a callout like "+130 tickets/quarter over the period, +2.7% total." That's a real win stated plainly, and it holds up if someone checks the axis.
