The chart feels like overselling because the visual change is far bigger than the real change.

**What's off**

1. **The truncated axis is the main problem.** Resolved tickets went from 4,820 to 4,950, which is +130, or about 2.7%. The axis starts at 4,800, so Q1's bar is 20 units tall and Q4's is 150. Q4 looks about 7.5× taller than Q1 when it's 1.03× larger. A bar's length is supposed to be its value, so cutting the baseline breaks that.
2. **The title makes a claim the data doesn't support.** "Huge momentum!" is an opinion, and 2.7% over three quarters is steady, modest growth. The chart then supports the claim with exaggerated bars, and people notice that.
3. **The 3D effect and bright green add hype.** 3D makes bar heights harder to read and adds nothing. Loud color adds emphasis the numbers don't have.
4. **There are no value labels.** Without numbers, viewers judge the bars by eye, which is exactly what the axis cut distorts. With labels, they'd see 4,820 vs. 4,950 and the drama would disappear.
5. **There's no comparison.** Resolved tickets mean little without a target, last year's figure, or incoming volume. Resolving more could mean the team got better, or just that more tickets came in.

**What I'd change**

- **Start the bar axis at 0.** The four bars will look nearly equal, which is accurate. If you want to show the small upward trend, use a line chart with a labeled, zoomed axis instead of bars.
- **Use a plain, factual title.** For example, "Tickets resolved per quarter rose 2.7% from Q1 to Q4 (4,820 → 4,950)". If you want the positive spin, "Resolutions up every quarter this year" is true and still upbeat.
- **Print the values on the bars:** 4,820 / 4,861 / 4,905 / 4,950.
- **Use flat, solid bars in one muted color.** Highlight only Q4 if you want a focal point. Drop the 3D and the gradients.
- **Add one comparison.** A target line, last year's quarters, or tickets received per quarter would show whether the team is keeping up or pulling ahead.
- **Add a short takeaway line** under the chart, such as "+41, +44, +45 per quarter, so growth is consistent and slightly accelerating."

The honest story is steady, consistent improvement, and that can hold up in front of skeptical readers. If you want it to read as momentum, the quarter-over-quarter increases (+41, +44, +45) are a better thing to show than inflated bars.
