Your coworker is partly right, but the chart isn't misleading enough to need redoing.

The zero-baseline rule applies mainly to bar charts, where bar length is the encoded value, so a truncated axis makes a 22 look like a fraction of a 24. A line encodes value by vertical position, not length, and readers don't expect a line chart to start at zero. Plotting 21.4 to 24.9 on a 20 to 26 axis is a standard choice that keeps the daily variation readable.

The real caveat is that a 3.5°C range on a 6°C axis makes the day-to-day swings look dramatic. Whether that's misleading depends on your message:

- **If the point is the daily fluctuation or a specific spike or dip**, keep the axis as it is and label it clearly.
- **If the point is that the lake stayed fairly stable all month**, a wider axis (for example 0 to 30) would show that honestly, though the variation would be hard to see.

Either way, make sure the axis is labeled with units (°C), the tick values are readable, and the title or caption says what the range is. If you want, I can suggest a caption, or sketch what a 15 to 30 version would look like so you and your coworker can compare them.
