No, she isn't right about this chart. A line chart doesn't need to start at zero. Zero baselines matter for bar charts, where bar length is the value, so a truncated axis exaggerates differences. A line encodes value by position, and a 20-26 range shows the July variation accurately.

A zero baseline would also work against you here. Celsius has an arbitrary zero, and starting at 0 °C would squeeze the 21.4-24.9 range into the top sixth of the plot, hiding the day-to-day movement you want to show.

To keep the chart honest and easy to read, I'd make these small changes:

- **Label the axis clearly**, for example "Water temperature (°C)," and use whole-degree gridlines.
- **Put the range in the caption or title**, such as "Daily water temperature, Lake Verity, July (range 21.4-24.9 °C)," so readers see the actual spread.
- **Consider 21-25 as the axis range.** It fits the data more tightly, and the 26 top adds no information.

If the point of the chart is that the lake stayed fairly stable all month, a line chart with a narrow axis can still make that case as long as the caption states the 3.5 °C spread. If you want to emphasize stability more strongly, a bar chart would need a zero baseline, but I wouldn't recommend that here.
