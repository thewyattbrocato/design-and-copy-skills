No, she's not right. A non-zero baseline is fine here.

**Why zero isn't required**
- **Line charts encode position and slope, not bar length.** The zero-baseline rule matters for bar charts, where the bar's height or area is the value, so a truncated axis distorts the comparison. A line chart shows how values change over time, and a zoomed axis shows that honestly.
- **Zero °C isn't "no temperature."** It's just the freezing point of water, so it has no special meaning for a lake in July. Starting at 0 would squash the data into the top fifth of the plot and hide the day-to-day pattern.
- **Your range fits the data.** Values of 21.4–24.9 on a 20–26 axis leaves a little padding and doesn't exaggerate anything.

**Where she has a point**
A zoomed axis makes a 3.5 °C swing look dramatic to someone who glances at the chart without reading the scale. To keep it from misleading anyone:
- Label the y-axis clearly, with units (°C) and visible tick marks or gridlines.
- Don't crop tighter than you have, for example 21 to 25. Your 20–26 range is reasonable.
- If the audience is non-technical, add a short note or title that states the actual range, such as "Daily water temperature, July: 21.4–24.9 °C."

You can tell your coworker that the zero-baseline rule applies to bar charts, and that for a line chart of temperature, forcing zero would hide the information the chart is there to show.
