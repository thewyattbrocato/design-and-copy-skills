# Fall campaign concept: "Bring the flat."

## The idea

Most people's first visit to Spoke Yard is a flat tire, according to our volunteer sign-in sheet. The campaign makes that moment the invitation: **if you've got a bike with a flat that's been leaning in the garage, bring it to us.** It's a one-sentence ask that anyone can repeat, and it points at a problem people already have.

A second thread runs alongside it. We have 64 members and want 100 by December 31. We show the count publicly and update it as it changes, so "36 to go" is a real number that neighbors can watch.

## Why it fits us

- **It starts from our own data.** The flat tire comes from our sign-in sheet, not a guess. We aren't inventing a problem for people.
- **It speaks to beginners without condescension.** Beginners get a 30-minute walkthrough on their first visit, so someone who has never touched a tool is covered. The ask is small: bring the bike.
- **It matches how we run.** We're volunteers in the back of an old laundromat, not a polished shop. The campaign sounds like neighbors helping neighbors because that's what we are.
- **The offer is simple.** $9 a month covers the stand and tools during open hours, plus the shelf of donated used parts, free with membership.
- **The goal is public and honest.** A real target (64 to 100 by Dec 31) gives people a reason to join now without any invented scarcity.

## How it shows up

### Poster

**Headline:** Bring the flat.
**Subline:** Wednesdays and Saturdays, 10 to 4. Back of the old laundromat on Pell Street.
**Body:** Use the stand, the tools and the free shelf of donated parts for $9 a month. First visit includes a 30-minute walkthrough. Our instructors are all volunteers, so the money goes to keeping the stand open.
**Footer:** "64 members. Help us get to 100 by December 31." [add: sign-up link or QR code]

The design is a single image of a flat tire against a plain background, with the headline in big hand-lettered type. It should look like it was made by people who fix bikes, not by an agency. We print a version with a tear-off strip for the laundromat's own bulletin board. [Ask the owner: do we have permission to post there?]

### Email

**Subject line options:**
- "Got a bike with a flat?"
- "Bring the flat. We'll show you the rest."

**Body (short):**
> That bike in your garage with the flat tire? It's the most common reason people first walk into Spoke Yard.
>
> Here's what happens when you do. A volunteer gives you a 30-minute walkthrough. You use our stand and tools. If you need a part, check the shelf of donated used parts, which is free with membership.
>
> Membership is $9 a month. We're open Wednesday and Saturday, 10 to 4, in the back of the old laundromat on Pell Street.
>
> We have 64 members and want 100 by December 31. [add: sign-up link]

The same email goes to our current list with one change: "Know someone with a bike that's been sitting? Forward this." Current members are our best route to new ones. [add: a real member line, only with their written permission]

### Instagram post

**Image:** A flat tire on the stand, shot in our shop, with "Bring the flat." as text on the photo.
**Caption:**
> Most first visits to Spoke Yard start the same way: a flat tire. Wednesdays and Saturdays, 10 to 4, back of the old laundromat on Pell Street. $9 a month gets you the stand, the tools and a shelf of free donated parts. All our instructors are volunteers. We're at 64 members and aiming for 100 by Dec 31. [add: link in bio]

For a follow-up, a simple graphic updates the member count whenever it changes (for example "71 of 100"). The number must be real on the day it's posted. If a member wants to appear in a photo, get their consent first and credit them as they prefer.

### Shop window

This is where the count is most visible. A hand-painted sign or a large paper thermometer in the window reads **"Bring the flat."** Below it is a tally: **64 members → 100 by Dec 31.** A volunteer updates the number by hand each open day. Our hours are listed in plain type, and an old inner tube hangs in the glass as a prop. It should be readable from the sidewalk in a few seconds.

A passer-by doesn't need to read a paragraph. They see the flat tire, the open hours and a number they might help change.

## What we need from the owner

- A sign-up link or QR code, and confirmation of who updates the count and how often.
- Permission to post at the laundromat, and approval for any photos used.
- Member quotes: **none yet**, so none appear above. If a member agrees to be quoted, we add the line with their written permission. Until then the concept works without them.

## Plain fallback

If the board wants something quieter, drop the "Bring the flat." line and run the same four pieces with the facts alone: hours, $9, the free parts shelf, the 30-minute walkthrough and the 64-to-100 goal. It still works, but it gives people less reason to remember us.
