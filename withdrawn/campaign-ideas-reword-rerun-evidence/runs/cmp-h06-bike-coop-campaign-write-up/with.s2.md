# Fall campaign concept: "Bring the Flat"

## The idea

**Your first visit is probably a flat tire, so bring it.**

Our volunteer sign-in sheet says a flat is the most common first visit. The campaign turns that into the invitation. The bike with a flat that's been leaning in a hallway is the easiest, most ordinary way to start. We'll walk you through it, and the $9 membership keeps you coming back.

The goal is 64 members now to 100 by December 31, so 36 more people.

## Why it fits us

- **It's true and it's ours.** The line comes from our own sign-in sheet. It doesn't depend on a slogan or a discount, and nobody can fact-check it into trouble.
- **It lowers the barrier.** The hard part of joining a repair co-op is probably feeling like you need to already know what you're doing. [Assumed. Check by asking a few current members at Saturday's open hours why they nearly didn't come.] "Bring the flat" says the starting point is small and common, and every beginner gets a 30-minute walkthrough on the first visit.
- **It shows what the $9 buys.** Membership covers the stand and tools during open hours, plus the shelf of donated used parts, free with membership. A flat tire shows all of that in one job: stand, tools, a part off the shelf, and a volunteer showing you how.
- **It fits a volunteer-run shop.** We have no staff, and the campaign never pretends otherwise. The people showing you how are volunteers who do this because they like it.
- **It's a real goal.** The 100-by-December-31 target is genuine, so the campaign can count honestly toward it.

## How it shows up

Each piece works on its own if the others never run.

### Poster

Large type: **BRING THE FLAT.**
Under it: *It's the most common first visit at Spoke Yard. Wednesdays and Saturdays, 10–4, in the back of the old laundromat on Pell Street.*
Bottom strip: *$9 a month. Stand, tools and a shelf of free used parts with membership. Beginners get a 30-minute walkthrough. Volunteer-run.*
Add a simple drawing of a flat wheel. It should be easy to read from across the street, and the laundromat location needs to be clear because that's the hardest thing to find.

### Email

**Subject:** Is there a flat tire in your hallway?

Body, short: the sign-in sheet shows a flat is the most common first visit. Say what happens next: you come in on a Wednesday or Saturday between 10 and 4, a volunteer gives you a 30-minute walkthrough, and you use the stand and tools. If you need a part, check the shelf of donated used parts, which is free with membership. Close with the numbers: *We have 64 members and want 100 by December 31. Membership is $9 a month.* One button: **Join Spoke Yard**.

Send it to our existing list, and ask current members to forward it to someone with a bike they've stopped riding.

### Instagram post

A photo of a flat tire on the stand in the shop, with the caption on the image: **"The most common first visit? A flat."**

Caption: *We checked the sign-in sheet. Most people's first time at Spoke Yard starts with a flat tire. Come in Wednesday or Saturday, 10–4, and a volunteer will walk you through it. $9 a month for the stand, the tools and the used-parts shelf. 64 of us so far, aiming for 100 by Dec 31.*

For a follow-up, a carousel of the steps with the 30-minute walkthrough, as long as members in the photos have agreed to appear.

### Shop window

Two parts:

1. A big hand-lettered sign: **"BRING THE FLAT. Open Wed & Sat, 10–4."**
2. A **member counter**: a poster-board scale from 0 to 100 with a marker at 64, moved up each time someone joins. Label it *"Members: 64 of 100 by Dec 31"* and update the number to match the real count. It shows people walking by that this place is active and that there's room for them.

If the shop is in the back of the laundromat, ask the laundromat whether we can put a sign or the counter in their front window or on Pell Street. That's where passersby will see it.

## What I need from you

- **Ask the owner:** Can someone try a first visit before joining, or is membership needed to use the stand? The copy has to say which.
- **Ask the owner:** Does the walkthrough come with membership, or is it separate? I've described it only as "on your first visit."
- **Ask the owner:** Do we have permission to put a sign in the laundromat's front window or on Pell Street?
- **Add:** a real member line or photo, only with written permission. I haven't written any quotes, and the concept doesn't need one.
- **Update the counter** each week. If it falls behind, take it down rather than show a number that's wrong.

## Plain fallback

If "Bring the Flat" feels too jokey for the board, use the straight version: **"Fix your own bike. $9 a month. Wednesdays and Saturdays, 10–4."** It carries the same facts and the same four pieces, with the 64-to-100 counter in the window. It loses the sign-in-sheet hook, which is what makes the idea specific to us, so I'd go with "Bring the Flat."
