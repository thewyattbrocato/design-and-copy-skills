# Fall campaign concept: "Bring the flat."

**The idea:** The most common first visit to Spoke Yard is a flat tire, so we make the flat the invitation. A flat is a small, fixable problem, and you can fix it here for $9 a month, with a volunteer next to you.

## Why it fits us

**It's true, and it's ours.** The line comes from our own sign-in sheet. No other shop can say it the same way.

**It answers the real hesitation.** I'm assuming the hesitation is "I don't know enough to walk into a repair shop," and that's my guess, not something we've checked. A flat is the least intimidating bike problem there is. Naming it as the usual starting point tells a beginner that people like them are who we expect. Two other facts back this up: beginners get a 30-minute walkthrough on their first visit, and the instructors are volunteers, not staff.

**It makes $9 concrete.** "Membership" is abstract. "Bring the flat" gives people a specific job to do on a specific day. The membership covers the stand and tools during open hours (Wednesday and Saturday, 10 to 4), plus the shelf of donated used parts, free with membership.

**It gives the board a real goal.** We're at 64 members and want 100 by December 31. That's 36 more, and we can show the count honestly because it's real.

## How it shows up

Each form works alone, and all four share one line: **"Most first visits are a flat tire. Bring yours."**

### 1. Poster

- **Headline:** "Got a flat? That's how most people start."
- **Body:**
  - Open Wednesday and Saturday, 10 to 4, in the back of the old laundromat on Pell Street.
  - $9 a month for the stand, the tools and a 30-minute walkthrough on your first visit.
  - The shelf of donated used parts is free with membership.
- **Footer:** "Volunteer-run. No staff, just neighbors who know bikes."
- **Look:** One drawing of a flat tire, with plenty of white space. Put it where bikes already are, such as racks, café windows and the laundromat itself.

### 2. Email

- **Subject:** "Bring the flat"
- **Preview text:** "It's the most common first visit we get."
- **Body, short and plain:**
  - Open with the sign-in sheet fact: the most common first visit is a flat tire.
  - Say what happens next: you get a 30-minute walkthrough, then the stand and tools for the repair, with a volunteer nearby.
  - Add the $9 a month, the open hours and the free used-parts shelf.
  - Close with the goal: "We're at 64 members and want to reach 100 by December 31."
- **Single ask:** "Come on a Wednesday or Saturday and bring the flat."
- **Send it to** current members and our existing list. Ask members to forward it to one friend with a flat.

### 3. Instagram post

- **Format:** A short carousel.
  - **Slide 1:** A photo of a flat tire, captioned "This is the most common first visit."
  - **Slide 2:** "30-minute walkthrough on your first visit."
  - **Slide 3:** "Stand and tools, Wed and Sat, 10 to 4."
  - **Slide 4:** "$9 a month. Used parts shelf free with membership."
  - **Slide 5:** "64 members. Help us get to 100 by Dec 31."
- **Caption:** "Flat tire? Perfect. That's where most people start. Pell Street, behind the old laundromat."
- **Photos:** Use our own shop, and get permission from anyone who appears in them.

### 4. Shop window

- **Main display:** A large hand-lettered tally that reads "64 of 100 by December 31."
- **Update it by hand** on every open day, so it always shows the real count. This works as a progress bar and as a reason to stop and look.
- **Smaller sign below it:** "Bring the flat. Wed + Sat, 10–4. $9 a month."
- **Optional:** Put a single flat tire in the window, with a hand-written sign on it: "Start here."

## What I need from the owner (you or the board)

- **Ask the owner:** Who updates the window tally, and how often? It only works if the number is always current.
- **Ask the owner:** Can we get photos of the shop with consent from anyone in them? I haven't assumed any.
- **[add: member line, once permitted]:** You have no usable member quotes yet. Nothing here depends on them. If a member gives written permission later, one real line could go on the poster, credited.
- **[assumed]:** That fear of not knowing how is what holds beginners back. Ask three current members at Saturday's open hours whether that matched their experience.
- **Check before printing:** The sign-in sheet says a flat is the most common first visit. It doesn't say most members started that way, so the copy sticks to the first claim.

## Plain fallback

If the board wants something simpler, drop the flat-tire hook and run a straight membership message. Use the same four pieces, with the headline "Fix your own bike. $9 a month." and the same facts and 64-to-100 goal. It needs nothing beyond the facts you gave me and the window tally. It's clear, but it's less memorable and doesn't give a beginner a first step.

**My pick is "Bring the flat."** It's built on our own sign-in sheet, so no one can say it isn't true.
