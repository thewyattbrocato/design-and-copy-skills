**The pick: "Bring your 'I'm not creative.'"**

Most skill ads promise to unlock your creativity. Your future students are already telling you they don't have any. This campaign answers that sentence directly: you don't need to be creative to start, you need Tuesday nights.

**Three forms (each works alone)**

1. **Post or flyer:** "Most emails we get start with 'I'm not creative.' Last spring, 31 of 46 students signed up again. Six Tuesdays. 8 seats. Wheel-throwing starts Oct 21."
2. **The door:** A sign at the street entrance next to the hardware store: "Not creative? Upstairs. Parking behind the building." It's local and a little funny, and it needs no budget or platform. [Ask the owner: is the sign allowed on that door?]
3. **Reply to the inquiry emails:** Answer the "I'm not creative" email with: "Good, that's who this class is for. Six weeks, $240, clay and firing for up to 4 pieces included, and your pieces come back in about a week now that we have a second kiln." This turns an existing habit into the campaign's strongest conversion piece.

**Other ways to go**

- **The first-pot angle:** Lead with the second kiln. "Pieces back in a week, not three" means you're holding finished work mid-class. It's concrete and a real change, but it speaks to people who already want pottery.
- **The "31 of 46" angle:** Lead with the return rate as proof, with no persuasion language. It's strong, but it works best as a supporting line, as in form 1.
- **Eight seats:** Make the small class the story. It's true scarcity, since there's one class and 8 seats. It's a good closing line, but it won't be the idea.
- **Tuesday-night ritual:** Sell the 6:30 to 9 evening above Dunmore Street as the reason to go. It's atmospheric but generic without photos you haven't mentioned.

I'd pick the first because it's the only one built on something your buyers actually say. The others are facts that support it.

**Fallback:** A plain details post: six-week beginner wheel class, Tuesdays 6:30 to 9, starts Oct 21, 8 seats, $240 including clay and firing of up to 4 pieces, pieces back in about a week, parking behind the building.

**Two notes**
- **Date check:** Oct 21 falls on a Wednesday in 2026, so confirm the start date. With registration opening next week and the class starting Oct 21, the window is also short, so decide how you'll say when registration opens.
- **Quotes:** I only used your phrase "I'm not creative" as a pattern. If you want to quote a real student's email, get their permission first.
