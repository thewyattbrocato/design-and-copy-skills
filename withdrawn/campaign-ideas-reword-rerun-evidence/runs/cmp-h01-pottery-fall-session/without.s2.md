# Three directions for Kilnside's fall wheel class

**One thing to check first:** the class starts Oct 21, which is a week from today. If registration opens next week, it may open the day the class starts, or very close to it. Please confirm the date. It changes the urgency in every version below.

## 1. "Not creative? Good." (the objection)
Most of your students start with this doubt, so make it the campaign. Other ads promise you'll discover your creative side. This one doesn't argue with the doubt. It says it doesn't matter.

- **Headline:** "You don't have to be creative. You have to be here Tuesday."
- **Supporting line:** "Six weeks, eight seats, clay included."
- **Proof:** 31 of last spring's 46 students signed up again.
- **Format:** a series of "I'm not creative" lines, ideally real quotes from your emails with permission, each answered with the plain facts of the class.

## 2. "Back in a week" (the payoff)
This one leads with the new kiln. Pottery usually means waiting weeks to see your work. Now it's about one.

- **Headline:** "Make it Tuesday. Hold it next week."
- **Supporting line:** "We added a second kiln, so pieces come back in about a week, not three."
- **Details:** $240 covers clay and firing for up to 4 pieces.
- **Weakness:** it's a good reason to choose you over another studio, but it doesn't speak to the hesitation people actually have.

## 3. "Mud upstairs" (the place)
This one trades on being a small local studio above the hardware store.

- **Headline:** "Tools downstairs. Mud upstairs."
- **Supporting line:** "Kilnside Studio, above the hardware store on Dunmore. Parking out back."
- **Scarcity:** "One class this fall. Eight seats."
- **Weakness:** it's charming and memorable, but it doesn't address the doubt that stops people from signing up.

## What I'd pick
**Direction 1 is the spine.** It's the only one built on something true that your students say, and the only one a generic "learn a new skill" ad can't copy. The 31-of-46 figure backs it up: most people who start do come back.

I'd use the other two as supporting material:
- The kiln line goes in the body copy as a reason to sign up now.
- The hardware-store line works as a sign-off or location tag.
- "Eight seats, one class" carries the urgency, since it's true and you don't need to invent a deadline.

**Sample ad:**
> "I'm not creative."
> You're in good company. Most people who email us say it before they sign up. 31 of last spring's 46 students came back for another session.
> Six Tuesday evenings, 6:30–9, starting Oct 21. Eight seats. $240, with clay and firing for up to 4 pieces included.
> Kilnside Studio, above the hardware store on Dunmore. Parking behind the building.

## What I need from you
- Permission to quote people's emails, or at least anonymized versions. The quotes would make Direction 1 much stronger.
- A real photo from the studio or a class. I wouldn't use stock images.
- Whether "31 of 46" can be written as "two out of three." It rounds to 67%, so that's accurate.

I'd also avoid saying students "love it" or "feel creative" unless they've told you that. I've kept everything to the facts you gave me.
