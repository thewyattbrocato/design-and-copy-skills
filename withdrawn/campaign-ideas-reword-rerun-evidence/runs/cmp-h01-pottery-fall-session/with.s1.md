# Pick: "Not creative? Good. Tuesday, 6:30."

**The idea:** Most people who email you say "I'm not creative," so make that sentence the way in. The campaign talks to those people directly and never tries to talk them out of it.

The class is six weeks of Tuesday evenings that start with a wheel and no talent required. Your own facts back this up. Most people say they're not creative before signing up, and 31 of 46 signed up again.

## Three forms
Each one works even if the other two never run.

1. **Poster and post:** Plain type that reads "Not creative? Good. Six Tuesdays, one wheel, 8 seats." Below it: "Starts Oct 21. Above the hardware store on Dunmore, parking behind." If you want real voices, use actual lines from your emails. Get each person's okay first and credit them, or leave names off: [add: real student line, with consent].
2. **A moment in class:** On week one, each student writes their own version of "I'm not creative" on a slip. You keep the slips in a jar and hand them back at the last class, next to their pieces. You don't have to say what it means. It also makes a good photo, if students agree to be photographed.
3. **Your reply to those emails:** Write a short standard reply for anyone who says it. For example: "Almost everyone says that before they sign up. The class is built for it. $240 covers clay and firing of up to 4 pieces. Pieces come back in about a week, thanks to our second kiln." It reaches the people most likely to sign up, and it costs nothing.

**Ask the owner:**
- What exact date and time does registration open? [add]
- Are you willing to photograph students and use their words, with consent?

## Other directions you could take
- **"Back in a week":** The second kiln means pieces come back in about a week, not three. It's a strong practical hook, but it speaks to people who already want to throw. It works better as supporting copy.
- **"31 of 46 came back":** Proof from last spring. It's good for the second wave of messaging, but it would be a weak opening line.
- **"8 seats, one class":** The scarcity is real. I'd use it as the closing line rather than the idea.
- **"Find us above the hardware store":** This is a charming local hook. It's a detail to include, not a campaign.
- **"Eat dinner off something you made":** This would be a warm product image. I'd skip it because it implies outcomes you haven't told me about.

## Why this one
It's the only direction built on something your buyers actually say, and it's what makes you different from every "learn a new skill" ad. Those ads promise that anyone can do it. This one starts from the doubt, and the jar gives you an offline version of it.

## Plain fallback
Run a straightforward announcement:
- Six-week beginner wheel class, Tuesdays 6:30–9, starting Oct 21.
- 8 seats.
- $240 including clay and firing of up to 4 pieces.
- Pieces come back in about a week.
- 31 of 46 spring students signed up again.

It needs nothing from you beyond the registration date.
