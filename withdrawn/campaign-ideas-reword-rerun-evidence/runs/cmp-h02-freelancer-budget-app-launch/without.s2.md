# Campaign: "Budget what you've banked"

**The idea:** Every budgeting app assumes a paycheck. Yours doesn't. Your income isn't the problem, the monthly budget is. The campaign shows freelancers how badly a normal budget fits their year, then offers Tally Nest as the fix.

## The hook: "My Lumpy Year"
Build a small shareable feature, either in the app or as a free web page:

- A freelancer enters (or imports) 12 months of income.
- It generates a card showing their income shape, with a line like *"Best month: $9,400. Worst: $1,100. A $4,200/month budget would have failed you 7 times."*
- The card ends with "Budget what you've banked → Tally Nest."

Freelancers like comparing notes on feast-or-famine income, so the card should get shared. It also shows the problem before you pitch the solution. A web version doesn't need an app install, so it can spread beyond iPhone users.

## Positioning
- **Tagline options:** "Budget what you've banked." / "Your income is lumpy. Your budget shouldn't break."
- **Offer:** The free first month works in your favor. Frame it as "Try it through one real lumpy month," not a generic free trial.
- **Tone:** Two-person indie team, made by people who get it. Lean into that instead of looking like a fintech company.

## Rough timeline (Oct 14 → Nov 3)
- **Week 1 (now):** Build the income-shape card and a one-page landing site with an email waitlist. Pull numbers and quotes from your beta users (see below).
- **Week 2:** Seed the card with beta testers and 10–20 freelancer creators (designers, writers, photographers, developers, video editors). Offer a free year and ask for honest posts, with no script. Start posting build-in-public and "lumpy income" content on X, Bluesky, and LinkedIn.
- **Week 3 (launch):** Do a coordinated push on Nov 3: Product Hunt, Indie Hackers, relevant subreddits (follow each one's self-promo rules), freelancer newsletters, and an email to your waitlist. Be in the comments all day.
- **After launch:** Share the aggregate "lumpy year" stats (anonymized, opt-in) as a follow-up post. That gives you a second news hook.

## Do this now: turn the beta into proof
You don't have numbers yet, so pull a few cheaply:

1. Ask beta users 2–3 questions. For example: "What did you use before?" and "What changed for you?"
2. Get 3–5 quotable testimonials, with permission.
3. Pull one or two honest usage stats, such as how many people came back in week 4.

Only use real numbers and quotes. A small beta with real quotes will do better than inflated stats.

## Questions
- Do you have a budget, or is this all organic?
- Who are your beta users? A particular type of freelancer would sharpen the targeting.
- Is the app able to import or enter past income easily? That decides whether the shareable card is practical in 3 weeks.
