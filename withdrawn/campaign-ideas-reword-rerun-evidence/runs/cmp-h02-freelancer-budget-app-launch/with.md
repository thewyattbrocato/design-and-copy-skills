**The idea: "Bank First."** Most budgets start with what you *should* earn. Tally Nest starts with what you've *already banked*. The campaign makes that a stance: freelancers stop budgeting against income that hasn't arrived.

Buyer truth [assumed, so check it in your beta feedback]: freelancers dread the "monthly budget" because the month it assumes never shows up. A good month makes the budget feel pointless, and a thin month makes it feel like a scolding.

**Three forms**

1. **The "Month 0" post series (social, short video or carousel).** Show a normal budgeting app's "monthly income: $5,000" field next to a freelancer's real last four months, with amounts they supply. Caption: "Your income isn't monthly. Your budget shouldn't be." Each post uses a different freelancer type, such as designer, photographer or writer. Use only people who've agreed, and credit them and disclose that they're beta users. [add: 3 consenting beta users and their real figures]
2. **The launch-day screen (in the product and the App Store page).** Make the first-run screen a single question: "How much have you banked so far?" The first screenshot in the App Store listing is that screen, with the line "Budget what you've actually banked." It works even if no social runs, because the product demonstrates the idea within a minute.
3. **The "Bank First" note (DM, email or community posts).** A short, useful piece that stands without the brand: a one-page "what to do with the money you've banked so far" checklist for freelancers, posted in freelancer communities and sent to your beta list. It ends with one line about Tally Nest and the free first month, then $6 a month. Follow each community's self-promotion rules. [add: where you're already a genuine member]

**Why this one:** it's a single sentence a stranger can repeat ("the budgeting app that starts from what you've banked"). It's news in a headline, since it contradicts the category default. It stays true when checked, because it describes how the app actually works.

**Plain fallback:** a straightforward Nov 3 launch built on the free first month. Lead with "Free for a month, then $6. Budget from what you've actually banked." Put it on the App Store page, a launch post and a beta-list email, with no series or community work.

**Ask the owner:**
- Can 2–3 beta users be named or quoted, and will they share real numbers? If not, use your own anonymized figures only if they're real, or skip the post series and run forms 2 and 3.
- Do you have any beta numbers, even a few, such as retention or a user count? Pull them together before Nov 3 so you can use one honest stat if it's good. Don't state any until you have it.
