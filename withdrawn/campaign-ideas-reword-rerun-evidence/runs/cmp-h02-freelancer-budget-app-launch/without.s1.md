# Campaign: "Budget what you've banked"

**The idea:** Every other budgeting app assumes a paycheck. Freelancers don't have one, so the monthly budget fails them. Tally Nest's pitch is that it only works with money that has already landed. The campaign makes that contrast the whole story.

**Hook:** *"Your income isn't monthly. Why is your budget?"*

## The centerpiece: "Lumpy Month" share card
Inside the app (and as a free web tool, if you have time), a freelancer enters their last 6 months of income. It generates a shareable card showing their income as a jagged bar chart next to the flat line a normal budgeting app assumes. The caption could be "My income vs. what my budget app thinks I make."

Why it fits you:
- It's visual, relatable, and something freelancers will post because it validates a frustration they already have.
- It doubles as a top-of-funnel tool, since people can use it without installing anything.
- It's small enough for two people to build in a couple of weeks.

## Timeline (Oct 14 → Nov 3)
- **Now to Oct 20:** Build the share card. Write a short founder story: why you built this, and who the lumpy-income person is.
- **Oct 20 to Nov 2:** Seed it with your beta users first, and ask them for their cards and a quote. Then post in places freelancers already gather: r/freelance, designer/dev/writer Slacks and Discords, indie hacker communities, and freelancer newsletters. Pitch 5 to 10 small newsletters and podcasts directly with the "budget what you've banked" angle.
- **Nov 3:** Launch on the App Store. Post the founder story, and run the card as the main call to action. Consider Product Hunt the same week.
- **Week after:** Re-share the best user cards, with permission.

## Offer
Lean on "free for the first month" but add a nudge for the first 100 or so sign-ups, like a founder-note onboarding or a lifetime discount on the $6/month price. That's a cheap way to get early reviews, which matter a lot for a new App Store listing.

- **Target freelancer:** I'd pick one (designers, developers, writers, or creators) and aim the first push at them rather than all freelancers.
