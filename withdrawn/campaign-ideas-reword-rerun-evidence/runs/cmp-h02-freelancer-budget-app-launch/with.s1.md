**The idea: "Bank first, budget second."** Tally Nest never asks what you *expect* to earn. It only budgets what's already in the bank. The campaign says that plainly and lets freelancers test it on their own messy income.

Buyer truth [assumed, check against your beta users' own words]: freelancers don't fail at budgeting. Monthly budgets fail them, because the month's income is a guess and the budget is built on it.

**Three forms (each works without the others):**

1. **A post or short video: "The $4,000 month that wasn't."** Show a normal monthly budget app next to Tally Nest, using the same lumpy income: a big month, then two thin ones. The monthly budget says you're "over" in the thin months. Tally Nest only shows what's banked and what that covers. Caption: *"Your income isn't monthly. Why is your budget?"* You can make this with sample numbers you label as an example. Don't present it as a real user's story.

2. **An in-app moment: the first screen after signup.** Instead of "set your monthly budget," it asks "What's in the bank right now?" Then it shows how many weeks that covers. This is the product demonstrating the claim within the first minute, and it works as a screen recording for the App Store preview.

3. **A message to freelancer communities: "Paste your last 3 months, see your nest."** A free, no-signup page or short guide where someone enters three lumpy months of income and sees what "bank first" would have looked like. It's useful without the app, and it ends with the download. [Ask the owner: can you build this small calculator before Nov 3? If not, make it a static worked example.]

**Launch mechanics:**
- Lead with the free first month. It lowers the risk of trying a budgeting app, and $6 a month is easy to state up front.
- Offer the first month to your beta users in exchange for a review or quote, **only with their consent**, and credit them by name or as "beta user" as they prefer. [add: real beta quotes]
- Don't use numbers like "X freelancers saved $Y" until you've pulled them from the beta.

**Ask the owner:**
- Pull even a handful of real beta facts: how many people, how many came back weekly, and one or two quotes. One true sentence beats any claim I could write.
- Which communities do you have real access to (subreddits, Slack groups, newsletters, your own followers)? Post as yourselves, openly as the makers.

**Plain fallback:** a launch-day post from the two of you, saying why you built it, what "bank first" means, and that the first month is free. Add a 30-second screen recording of the first-screen moment. It needs nothing beyond what you have.

**Why this pick:** "Bank first, budget second" is one sentence a stranger could repeat. It names a real tension (irregular income against a monthly format) instead of describing features. It also stays true if anyone checks, because it's literally how the app works.
