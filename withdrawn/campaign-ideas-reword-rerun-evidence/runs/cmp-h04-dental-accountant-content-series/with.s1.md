**The idea:** Read your books the way you read a radiograph. Each part takes one line of a dental practice's financials and shows the owner what's normal, what's a shadow, and when to look closer.

Dentists already trust this skill in the operatory. The series lends it to a document most of them avoid. Anyone could repeat it as "the accountant who reads your P&L like an X-ray." It uses no client stories, only labeled hypotheticals with round numbers.

**The six parts**
1. **Production vs. collections.** The number on the schedule isn't the money in the bank.
2. **The blurry film.** Miscategorized loan principal, owner draws and personal spend make a P&L unreadable.
3. **Overhead.** What the percentage is made of and where it hides.
4. **The team line.** Payroll, associate comp and what's reasonable.
5. **What you actually make.** Salary, distributions and the "where did it go" feeling.
6. **Before you sign.** Reading the numbers before a big purchase, a buy-in or a practice acquisition.

**Three forms, each of which works alone**
- **LinkedIn post:** one image or sentence from the part, with the "shadow" and the question to ask. It runs 150 to 200 words.
- **Monthly email:** the full part, about 400 words, ending on one thing to check this week.
- **One-page "how to read it" sheet:** a plain PDF per part, no logo wall. Collect all six and it becomes a small guide to your own books.

**Part one draft (email, with the LinkedIn cut below it)**

**Subject: The number on your schedule isn't the money in your bank**

Dentists look at an X-ray and see what's actually there, not what the patient says is going on. Your financials deserve the same read, and the first film I pull is production against collections.

Production is the value of the work your team did, at your fee schedule. Collections is the cash that landed. They will never match, and that's normal. What matters is the shape of the gap.

Here's a made-up month with round numbers:

- Gross production: $100,000
- Insurance write-offs and adjustments: $25,000
- Net production: $75,000
- Collected: $70,000

The $25,000 gap is contracts doing what contracts do. The $5,000 gap is the shadow, because that's work that was done and isn't paid yet. One month of it means nothing. Six months of it growing means something is leaking. It could be claims that never got followed up, patient balances nobody is chasing, or payments posted to the wrong place.

Most practices never see this because the numbers live in two places. Your practice software reports production. Your books record what hit the bank. Unless someone ties the two together, each looks fine on its own.

**One thing to check this week:** pull last month's collections from your practice software and compare it to the deposits in your bank account.
