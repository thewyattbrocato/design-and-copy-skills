The concept works. "Bring a pocketful. Leave with a garden." is warm and easy to say, and it points at trading rather than saving money. The "No seeds to trade? Come anyway." line keeps it welcoming. One thing needs fixing before it prints.

**Check the date.** March 14 is a Saturday in 2026, but that date has already passed (today is Oct 14, 2026). The next March 14 is in 2027, and it falls on a Sunday. Saturday that week is March 13. Either the date or the weekday is wrong, and a mismatch on a flyer sends people to an empty park. Confirm which one is right. Adding the year, or at least checking it against the event listing, would also help.

**Smaller suggestions, all optional:**
- **"Seed packets from last year welcome"** could be read as store-bought packets or as seeds saved last year. If you mean either, "Last year's packets welcome, too" would be clearer. It also overlaps a bit with "Bring seeds you saved."
- **Rain plan or contact.** Outdoor events in March are weather-dependent. A line like "Rain or shine" or a phone, email, or web address for updates would help.
- **Visual.** The idea came from people handing envelopes to strangers, so a hand-to-hand envelope illustration or photo would carry the concept better than generic seed or flower art.
- **Hierarchy.** The date, time, and place are the most practical details, and they sit in the subline. Make sure they're large enough to read from a few feet away, not just the headline.
- **"Leave with a garden"** is a little poetic, but "Free" and "Bring seeds you saved, leave with something new" ground it, so I'd keep it.

Once you've confirmed the date, the copy is ready to print.
