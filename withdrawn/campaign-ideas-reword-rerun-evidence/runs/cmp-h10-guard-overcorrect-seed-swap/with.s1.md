The concept is sound, but check the date before you print.

**Date problem:** Today is October 15, 2026, and Saturday, March 14 was in 2026, so that date has already passed. March 14, 2027 falls on a Sunday. Either the swap is next March and "Saturday" is wrong, or the date is wrong. Confirm the actual day, date, and year with whoever booked the pavilion. Adding the year, or at least getting the weekday right, would also help people who keep the flyer for months.

**What works**
- **The idea:** It comes from something you saw, people handing envelopes to strangers, and the headline captures that. "Bring a pocketful" is concrete and easy to picture, and "leave with a garden" is a good payoff.
- **Tone:** "Come anyway" welcomes people with nothing to trade, which is probably your biggest barrier.
- **Small print:** The volunteers at the long table take the pressure off beginners.

**Small things to consider**
- **"Free" vs. "not about saving money":** The two don't conflict, because people need to know it costs nothing. But "Free seed swap" opens the subline with the money angle. You could lead with the place and time and put "free" later, for example "A seed swap at Alder Park… Free, and no seeds required."
- **Subline length:** It carries the date, time, place, the ask, and the reassurance. Put the logistics (day, date, time, place) on their own line in larger type so they're readable from a distance, and keep the "come anyway" sentence as a separate line.
- **"Seed packets from last year":** This could be read as seeds from the 2025 swap, or as year-old seeds in general. "Last season's seeds welcome" would be clearer.
- **Missing details:** Consider adding a rain plan, since it's outdoors in March, and a contact email or page for questions.

Once you've confirmed the date, I think it's ready for the printer.
