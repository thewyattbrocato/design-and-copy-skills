Fake first-person customer posts, a "lost 12 pounds" claim from people who haven't received a box, and "4.9 stars from 2,000+ reviews" with no shipped product are fabricated testimonials and invented ratings. The FTC treats that as deceptive advertising. Undisclosed posts from team and friends also break its endorsement rules, and its fake-review rule carries per-violation penalties. Weight-loss claims draw extra scrutiny. There's also a practical risk: launch is when people look hardest, and one employee's friend outing the campaign would hurt you more than a quiet first week.

You can still make the launch feel organic and social with real posts. Here's a campaign that does that.

**Campaign idea: "First Box" (pre-launch founding table)**

Recruit 50 to 100 real people (friends, team, local food creators, neighbors) to be founding tasters. Send each a free first box in exchange for an honest post. They tag you and disclose with #gifted or "I work at Fennel & Fig." The rule is that they can say anything true, including criticism. Honest, specific posts from people who actually cooked the food are more persuasive than invented ones, and they're legal.

**8 posts you can use right now**

Team and founders can post these from personal accounts with the disclosure included, and none of them make claims you can't back up.

1. "I work at Fennel & Fig, and in two weeks we ship our first box. I've tested the braised fennel and white bean recipe four times at home. Here's the version that finally worked. 🌿 #teamfennelfig"
2. "Why 'Fennel & Fig'? Because we couldn't stop arguing about this pairing in our test kitchen. Photo of the argument-ender below. (I'm on the team.)"
3. "Founding tasters wanted: we're sending 50 free first boxes in exchange for honest posts, good or bad. DM me or the brand. Launching [date]."
4. "Behind the scenes: 6 a.m. at the prep kitchen, portioning the first test boxes. Nothing's shipped yet, but this is what it looks like. (Fennel & Fig team.)"
5. "We tested 31 recipes and cut 23. Here are the 8 that made the launch menu, and the one we still can't agree on."
6. "#gifted: I got an early box from Fennel & Fig. Cooked the [dish] on a Tuesday in 35 minutes. What worked, what I'd change. Honest take in the caption."
7. "Question for you: what's the worst part of weeknight dinner? We're building the menu around your answers. Best replies get a free first box."
8. "Launch week: here's what's in box one, what it costs, and how to skip or cancel. No surprises."

**For social proof**, collect real reviews from founding tasters first, then use whatever rating and count you actually have, with real numbers. For health claims, stick to what's true, like "high-protein options" or "calorie info on every recipe," and skip outcome promises.
