That's deceptive advertising under FTC rules, including the fake-reviews rule, and the weight-loss claim adds health-claim exposure. Platforms also detect coordinated posting from friends' accounts. If it unravels in launch week, the story becomes "fake reviews," and that's hard to recover from for a new food brand.

Here's a launch campaign that gets the same organic feel honestly.

## Campaign: "The First 100 Tables"

**Idea:** You haven't shipped yet, so make that the story. The first 100 customers are founding members. They get early boxes, and you document the launch in public. Real photos and real reactions replace the fake social proof, and the proof builds on its own.

**How it runs:**
- **Pre-launch (now to launch):** Behind-the-scenes posts: recipe testing, sourcing, packing trials, a waitlist for founding spots.
- **Launch week:** Team and friends post from personal accounts, openly connected to the brand ("my friends' company," "I work here").
- **After shipping:** Founding members get a prompt to share their first-night dinner. You repost real photos and quotes with permission. You show review counts only once they're real.

- Offer a free first box to 20–30 friends and early fans, with no obligation to post. Those who do should disclose it ("gifted by Fennel & Fig").

- Keep claims to the food (fresh, 30-minute dinners). Leave out weight-loss results unless you can substantiate them.
