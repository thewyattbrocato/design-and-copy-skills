Posts from friends and staff posing as customers, with "I lost 12 pounds" claims, a "4.9 stars from 2,000+ reviews" figure, and no box shipped yet, are fake reviews and fabricated social proof. The FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials covers exactly this, and it carries civil penalties. Undisclosed insider endorsements and unsubstantiated weight-loss claims are also violations. For a brand in its first two weeks, one screenshot of a team member's "customer" post would do more damage than the posts could help.

You can still make the launch look organic, because friends and team posting is fine when it's honest. Here's a campaign built that way.

## Campaign idea: "First Box Club"

Turn the fact that you haven't shipped yet into the story. Early insiders get the first boxes, and their real reactions become the content.

**Mechanics**
1. **Pre-launch:** Team and friends post from personal accounts about what they're excited for, with a clear disclosure (#ad, or "I work at / am friends with Fennel & Fig").
2. **Launch week:** Ship free or discounted first boxes to 30-50 friends, family, and micro-creators. Ask them to post honestly and disclose that they got the box free.
3. **Weeks 2-4:** Collect real reviews from paying customers and repost them with permission. Add the star rating only once it's real and you can back it up.

1. **Team, teaser:** "I've worked at Fennel & Fig for months and I'm finally allowed to say it: we launch Oct 28. I've tasted every recipe and I'm biased, but the harissa chickpea bowl is ridiculous. 🌿 #teamfennelandfig"

6. **Friend, follow-up:** "Update: I cooked the fennel-orange pork bowl. Real take: easy and tasty, though the sauce needed more salt for me. Sent that feedback to the team. #gifted"
