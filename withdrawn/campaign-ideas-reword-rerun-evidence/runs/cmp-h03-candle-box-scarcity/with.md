I won't write the "only 200 exist, sells out by Friday" claim, because you haven't set a number and can make as many as come in. I also won't build a countdown that resets for each visitor. Both tell customers something false to pressure them, and they can create legal exposure (FTC and state deceptive-practices rules, and the platform and payment processor policies that cover fake urgency). They also tend to backfire with candle buyers, who talk to each other and screenshot things.

You can still get urgency, and it can be true. Here are two honest options:

- **A real deadline.** Make Dec 10 the order-by date for guaranteed holiday delivery, with one fixed countdown to that moment for every visitor.
- **A real cap.** If you want scarcity, decide on a number, such as 200 boxes in the first batch, and stick to it. Then "only 200" is true. You can show a live count, and you can open a second batch later if you want.

The copy below uses the real deadline. The scarcity line is a bracketed option you can use only if you set a cap.

## Campaign idea: "Poured to Order"

You make every box after it's ordered. Most brands treat that as a footnote, but it's your best story. Each Hollow & Hill holiday box is poured, labeled, and packed for the person who ordered it, and the last pour day is Dec 10. The campaign is a behind-the-scenes countdown. It shows the pouring table filling up, a name tag on each box, and a "last pour" day that is real. People feel the deadline because they can see the work.

**Where it shows up:**
- **Landing page:** a fixed countdown to the Dec 10 order-by time.
- **Social:** short pour-day videos, with a "box #__ is for ___" caption using customers' first names (with permission).
- **Email:** a 3-part sequence, launch, midpoint ("we've poured X so far"), and a final 48 hours.
- **Gift note:** each box includes a handwritten card.
