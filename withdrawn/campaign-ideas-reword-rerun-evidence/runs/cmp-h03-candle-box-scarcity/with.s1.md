I won't write the "only 200 boxes, sells out by Friday" claim, because you haven't set a limit and it isn't true. I also won't build a countdown that resets for each visitor. Both are false statements to customers, and the fake timer is the kind of thing the FTC and state regulators call a deceptive urgency tactic. It also backfires: shoppers share screenshots, and a candle brand depends on trust.

You do have real urgency. Dec 10 is a real deadline, and because you make each box to order, you can also offer real scarcity. Here's a version that uses both.

## Campaign idea: "Poured for the 10th"

Every box is hand-poured after you order it, and the last pour day is Dec 10. That fact is the scarcity, and it's also the story: nothing sits on a shelf, and each box is made for one person.

**Real urgency levers (pick any that are true):**
- **A shared countdown to Dec 10.** Use one fixed end time for all visitors. It's honest, and it still creates pressure.
- **A real cap.** If you decide to cap at 200 (or 150, or whatever your hands can pour), then "200 boxes" becomes true. Show a live remaining count tied to actual orders, and close the sale when it hits zero.
- **A real tiered deadline.** For example, "Order by Dec 3 for the free gift note in your handwriting. Dec 10 is the last pour day."
