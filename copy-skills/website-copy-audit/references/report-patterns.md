# Report patterns

Load for scores, a pre-launch verdict, a competitor comparison, a message brief, or a sign-up path or email review. Every example is invented and generic.

## One finding, in order

> **Major · Pricing, plan table.** "Pro: everything you need." Names no limit, so a buyer cannot tell Pro from Team, and the page calls the same plan "Business" in the footer. Fix: state what Pro includes and caps, and use one name on every page: "Pro: up to 10 projects, shared workspace."

> **Blocker · Home, banner.** "Only 3 spots left this month." Nothing on the page or in the brief says why there are three. A reader who notices the number never changes will distrust the rest. Fix: remove it, or state the real cause and end date if the owner has one.

Quote the text exactly, say what the reader does or concludes because of it, then fix it. A fix is a rewritten line or an instruction a writer could act on today, never "improve clarity".

## Scores (only on request)

Score each area 0 to 4. Say what the score means in one clause and quote the evidence.

| Score | Reads like |
|---|---|
| 0 | Absent or misleading |
| 1 | A stranger would be confused or misled on a key point |
| 2 | Works for people who already know the product; gaps for newcomers |
| 3 | Clear and honest, with minor leaks |
| 4 | Clear, specific, supported; nothing a reader would stumble on |

Any blocker caps the overall score at 1, and say so. Scores rank fixes within one site; they do not compare sites or measure conversion.

## Pre-launch verdict

Answer yes, no, or yes-after, in the first line. List the blockers that make it a no, then the majors worth fixing before launch, then what can wait. A page with unfilled placeholders is not ready; say which ones.

## Competitor comparison

- Use only copy the user supplied or you fetched; record the date and the page.
- Compare on the same few dimensions: who it is for, the main promise, proof, price visibility, terms, tone.
- Say what each rival does better, then where the owner's copy is clearer or more specific. Do not infer a rival's results, numbers or intent.
- Close with the one gap worth closing and the one distinction worth keeping.

## Message brief (systemic problems)

When every page says something different, give a brief instead of rewriting everything:

- Who it is for, in the customer's words.
- The one thing it does for them, in a familiar category word.
- Why believe it: the proof the owner has, and placeholders for what they lack.
- The one action each page asks for.
- Terms to use and terms to retire.

Keep it to a few lines; the owner or landing-page-copy turns it into pages.

## Sign-up path and first email

Follow the reader from the promise that brought them: the ad or link, the page, the form, the confirmation, the first email. Check that each step repeats the same promise and name, that the price and renewal are stated before the money step, and that the confirmation says what happens next and when. Flag any step that asks for more than the previous step promised.
