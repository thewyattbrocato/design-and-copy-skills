# Stories, hooks and reconstructed detail

Load when copy tells what happened to a person or business (customer story, case study, founder story, anecdote), or opens with a line built to make someone stop, click or watch. Every example is invented and generic. A label belongs inside the story, not in a note beside it.

## What kind of story is it

A story is a claim about events, so its first words say which kind it is.

| Kind | Needs | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| Real event | The user's facts | Plain telling, details as given |
| Illustration | Nothing real, so no figures | "Picture a..."; no name, number or quote |
| Composite | The user says it blends cases | "A pattern we see..."; never one named person or result |
| Someone else's | A source the user can name | Credited; never implies we were involved |

Default to a real event as given. Change to an illustration when none exists and a scene is still wanted. A composite with rounded-up numbers does not go in a case-study or testimonial slot: say so in one line and offer the illustration or the best single real case.

A benefit paragraph with a character's name pasted on is not a story. With thin facts, tell the few given, with a placeholder for each missing proof (`[approved quote]`); with almost none, give an outline with visible slots and the request message from claim-fixes.

## Retelling a true story

- **What may move.** Default to changing nothing the conclusion rests on: who did what, what got in the way, the method, the result and its cost. Names, place, dates and the order of minor events may move for privacy or flow, and the story says so ("details changed"). A figure rounds against the claim: a rise of roughly a tenth is "about a tenth", never "over a fifth".
- **Leave the setback in.** A failed first attempt is part of what happened; smoothing it into a straight climb changes the account.
- **Speech and thoughts.** Default to no invented speech. Words printed as a person's are verbatim and approved by them (the quote rule). In a spoken retelling or a labelled illustration, reconstructed speech may carry the gist, introduced as such ("what she said, roughly"). A real person never gets thoughts, motives or the line they "wished they had said" in published copy.

## Consent and anonymising

Default to publishing a customer story only once the customer has approved the final text: name, role, company, figures and quote. If approval is missing, mark `[customer approval]` and treat the piece as a draft. For an anonymous version, use a role and a size band, and drop anything that identifies by itself (a one-of-a-kind product, a street, an unusual date) even when the notes contain it.

## Emotion

Default to letting the facts carry the feeling: the closing line gives the reader something to weigh (what changed, what it cost, who it suits), not only a mood. A "someone waited and regretted it" story needs a real event and a true window; fair-persuasion owns how much pressure is acceptable.

## Hooks

A hook is honest when the content keeps its promise as the reader will understand it.

- **Read it literally.** Take the hook as a flat claim. If the content does not support it as written, narrow the hook ("cut my commute" over a move that saved twenty minutes becomes "twenty minutes off my commute").
- **Opposite openers.** A line that states the reverse of the content ("Sleep is overrated" over a piece arguing for sleep) stands only if the opening lines resolve it and the piece holds that view in part. Otherwise write the hook the content earns.
- **Curiosity versus bait.** Default to naming the real thing and withholding only the answer, which arrives at once. Bait is a gap whose answer is smaller than the promise, or never comes, or shows its cost only after the click. headlines-and-leads has the craft; this is the truth test.
- **Standing.** "I was where you are" and "from broke to six figures" hold only if the user said so about this path.
- **Proof and staging.** A count, rank or reach figure in a hook is a claim with a basis; where none was given, write `[figure and basis]` and a hook that works without it. No scene scripted to look unplanned, no actor presented as a customer; a labelled dramatization is fine.

A specific true opener beats a vague teaser; the user's voice wins except where a line would state something false.

## When the request pushes the other way

| Request | Deliver instead |
|---|---|
| "Realistic customer story with a quote and result; we have none yet" | The request message and `[approved quote, name, role]` slots; a labelled illustration with no figures if wanted |
| "It is really three clients; present it as one" | The composite labelled as a pattern, or the strongest single real case |
| "Put it in quotes; they said roughly that" | The paraphrase unquoted, or the quote once they approve the words |
| "Clickbait is fine; accuracy does not matter" | The hooks asked for, each concrete and kept by the content, and a line on why the bait versions are left out |

Give one line of reason, then the work; never refuse the whole task.

## Quick checks

- The story's kind is clear from its first words; no person, quote, number or scene appears that the user did not give.
- Every quoted line is the customer's own words, with approval given or flagged; changed details are announced.
- Each hook, read literally, is supported by what follows, and its gap closes in the opening lines.
