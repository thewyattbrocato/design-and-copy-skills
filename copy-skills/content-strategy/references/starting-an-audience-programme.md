# Starting an audience programme

Load when a team is beginning to publish for an audience and little exists yet: "start a newsletter", "we have no audience", "pick one channel", "write the mission", "plan the first quarter". The capacity gate in SKILL.md still rules: a programme the stated hours cannot carry for a year is worse than a smaller one. Use the user's figures or a placeholder such as `[subscribers at review date]`. A narrow ask (just the mission, just the channel) gets only that part.

## Reader and mission

Default: one reader you can picture. What they need to know (not what you want to sell them), and why they would care that you are the one telling them. Change when the team truly serves two readers: pick the one that wins when needs clash and say who is left out. "Everyone who..." in the mission, or three audiences in the plan, means the choice is not made.

Write the mission in one or two plain sentences with three parts: who it is for, what they get each time, and what it lets them do or become. Leave out how you earn. Default to a plain name that says what it is; change when a consumer brand needs a memorable one (positioning-and-messaging).

## Niche and angle

Default: narrower than feels safe, widened only after people keep coming back. Narrow by reader, situation or method, then find an angle few others hold: a smaller reader, two proven areas combined, a channel nobody in the trade uses yet, a manner of your own.

Check: if this vanished next month, would a reader notice and complain? If its headlines could sit unchanged on a rival's site, the angle is not found.

To test an angle, send two versions to small slices of an existing list for a few weeks and compare clicks, replies and unsubscribes; with no list, talk to a handful of readers who are not being sold to. Small samples hint, they do not prove; say so.

## One format, one main channel

Default: one format on one channel until it holds its rhythm. Choose the channel with three questions: what can the team do well week after week, are the readers already there, and who can change the terms of the relationship? A site or list you own gives control and needs traffic. A platform you rent gives traffic and can change its rules without notice, so on a rented base put a way to join an owned list on every piece from the first week, with a reason to join that is not a trick.

Change when the first channel is steady: add a second then, with its own goal, one wanted action and its own version, never a pasted copy.

## Cadence and calendar

Default: the rhythm the stated hours can hold for a year, with some parts of the format fixed (opening, order, sign-off) so the work gets lighter. With hours unknown, write `[hours per week]` and size to the smallest plausible rhythm.

Each calendar row carries a due date, topic, author, owner, status, the outcome the reader gets and the one action wanted. A piece with no stated reason to exist is cut. Hold the next two weeks firm and the month after loose.

## Ideas on purpose

Default: audit what exists first, then draw ideas from sources the user can reach: questions readers actually ask (support, sales, search data if they can pull it), recorded conversations with the person who knows the subject, customers asked what keeps them stuck. Change when nothing exists and no readers are reachable: the first job is a few conversations, not a calendar.

## What to measure

Default: people who chose to hear again, and replies. Likes, visits and follower counts describe activity. If the business already sells, compare subscribers with non-subscribers on whatever the user can pull. A list counts people who already care; when the aim is reaching people who have never heard of you, say the list is only part of the picture.

## Continue or cut

Set this before launch: a review date the team can afford, the measure above, and what result means continue, change the angle or stop. Quitting after one weak month is a failure; so is running on with no reader answering after a fair test. Name who makes the call.

## Money later

Default: no revenue plan until readers come back. When asked, name options and add them one at a time. Sell sponsors the stated plan, the current numbers and a price, never an audience that does not exist yet, and label sponsored and affiliate content to readers. For a forecast of subscribers or revenue, decline the number and give what to measure (starting list, joins per piece, return rate) with the arithmetic left as placeholders.

## Before handing back

- The mission names reader, what they get and the outcome, and contains no "everyone".
- One format and one main channel are named, with what will not be done yet.
- Every calendar row has an owner and a reader outcome; the rhythm fits the stated hours.
- A review date and a continue-or-cut rule appear.
- No subscriber, reach or revenue figure appears that the user did not supply.
