# Freshness and briefs

Load for stale-content triggers, a review log, a page or type brief, or making the case for the work without numbers. Never invent a figure; use the user's or leave a placeholder.

## What makes it stale

Set triggers by event, not only by calendar: a price, product, policy or screen changes; a rule changes; support keeps answering the same question. Anything stating a price, deadline, version or legal term gets the shortest cycle. Give each kind of content its trigger and who watches for it.

## Review log

One line per item: last review, by whom, next review, and what would bring it forward. An item with no owner cannot be logged, so assign owners first. Judge freshness with two sources at least, such as a read-through plus what support hears, or usage plus a check by someone outside the team. Record a first pass so later passes mean something.

## Brief for a page or type

A brief lets writer and designer work at once, before any copy exists. Write it as a document, not copy or a heading outline:

- Job: what the reader should do or grasp, and why the business cares.
- Reader: which one wins, and what they already know.
- Order: what is seen first, second, and only on request.
- Facts that must appear, each with its source and who confirms it (placeholder where none).
- Source material: current, stale, to be written, or needs an expert.
- Terms and links: the label to use, where it points, the standard next step.
- Owner and reviewers: who decides, who is only consulted, how long they get.
- Staleness: what change makes it wrong, and who watches.

For many alike items (a changelog entry, a help article) write one template per kind, with the states design must hold: empty, very long, error, and label rules. List what must be replaced before launch; placeholder text never passes as final.

## Making the case

Start from the listener's pain. Pick one frame: readers cannot finish a task, the team does duplicate work, or numbers. Numbers come only from the user. With none, name the three to pull first and how (items with no owner, the help desk's top repeat questions, the time the last launch spent in review) and write the argument with placeholders such as `[repeat tickets per month]`. Never compute savings from a guessed rate.

Offer a small first step: one neglected but useful area, a defined scope, a before-and-after measure, a short time box and a named reviewer. Tie the request to the planning calendar and word the reason in the listener's terms.
