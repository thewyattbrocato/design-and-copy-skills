# Owners and review

Load for owner tables, review and approval delays, an emergency path, policy versus guideline, or a starter style guide. Never invent a title, headcount or figure; use what the user said or leave a placeholder.

## Owner table

One row per kind of content (for example clinic leaflets, release notes, pricing page): the owner, who supplies facts, who writes, who checks, who signs off, who publishes. One person may fill several cells. An empty cell is a finding, and the first fix is to fill it. The owner may be part of an existing job, written into it. Where nobody can be named, name one representative per team who carries the standards back.

The owner decides whether a request fits the purpose. They do not rewrite everything, and they are not the only gate for every change.

## Find the delay before fixing it

Ask or assume (and label which): where requests start, what counts as urgent, who touches a piece and in what order, how long each wait is, where work bounces back, how long the whole trip takes. If the user gives a delay in days, use it; do not estimate savings. Then:

- Remove a layer unless it guards a named risk (legal for claims about money, support for accuracy of steps). Give each reviewer a scope, such as pages with a given tag, and a time limit after which silence means yes.
- Run reviews side by side, with one merged set of comments and one person who settles conflicts.
- Let one approver decide wording, so the same sentence is not argued four times.
- Write an emergency path: who may call it, who is told, what is skipped, how short the route is, and a look-back afterward.
- Say what happens when a step runs late, and how the new rule reaches the people it affects: involve them early, name what each gains, track use.

## Policy or guideline

A policy is mandatory (legal, accessibility, security) and has an enforcer. A guideline is good practice and can bend for a stated reason. Mark every rule as one or the other.

## Starter style guide

One or two pages, built from questions that keep coming back: the name of each feature or plan, number and capital-letter habits, how to write a date or price, words to avoid, the tone for errors and billing. Name an owner, keep it searchable, point to an existing manual for the rest, and add a rule only when the same question returns. Where many people contribute, build checks into their templates. Voice is a few habits, each with its limit ("plain, not curt"); tone shifts with the reader's state, warm after success and calm and exact when something failed or money is involved. With positioning-and-messaging installed, take voice from it.
