# Evidence and cuts

Load for the evidence list, reading usage data, a report, ranking candidates against capacity, or retiring content. Scale to the pages, hours and people the user has.

## The list

A list answers one decision, such as "what do we fix first" or "what can go before the move to a new system". Columns serve that decision and no more: a stable ID, title, location, kind, who it is for, owner (or "none"), last real change. Add usage figures only if the user can pull them. Add a notes column; surprises land there. IDs must survive into the recommendations.

Not every item needs a reading. List everything cheaply, then read a few closely. If the list is too long to read, choose the few on purpose and say how: some from each owner, some from the busy and quiet corners, some from deep in the tree, none picked because they are loved or notorious. State the total (or `[pages in scope]`), how the few were chosen, how many, and what they cannot show. Never turn a hand-picked few into a rate.

To keep an estate honest over time, look at one section a month.

## Usage is a clue

Low traffic may mean nobody can find a page. A page few visit may be required by law. "Last edited" can be old and still right. Pair any cut with an owner check, a purpose check and a look at what links to it.

## Who is it for

When one piece serves several readers, name the one that wins when their needs clash and say why; mark it for the user to confirm. If a second reader needs something the page cannot give, that is a gap to fill elsewhere, not a longer page.

## Candidates against capacity

For each candidate state who writes it, who checks it, how often, for how long, and what happens when that person leaves. Use the hours the user gave, or leave `[hours per week]`. Score 1 to 5 on: required (law, contract, a core task), serves the winning reader, meets a real need, effort to do well (high effort scores low), payoff. Rank, draw the line where the hours end, and give each cut a reason in a few words. Fewer, better-kept items is the likely result.

## Report

Write for the reader who will act: leaders get a few findings, each with the decision it implies; the team gets a summary; the person doing the work gets the sheet. End with a call per item or group (keep, fix, merge, retire, assign owner) and the first week's work. A finding without a next step is not a finding. Give a percentage only if it comes from supplied data.

## Retiring

Decide by purpose, not age. Keep what law or contract requires or what still serves the winning reader. Merge duplicates into the best copy. Update what is still needed but out of date. Retire the rest: redirect or show a plain "no longer available" with a pointer, drop it from menus and search, check inbound links, keep what must be retained, log the decision and date, and tell the affected owners.
