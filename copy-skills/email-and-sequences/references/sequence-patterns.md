# Sequence patterns

Starting points to adapt to the reader's rhythm and the user's facts. Spacing and counts are defaults, not rules; the user's plan wins.

## Welcome and onboarding

- Message 1, right away: deliver exactly what the sign-up promised (the file, the code, the first lesson) and say what comes next and how often. One link.
- Next messages, one per step the reader needs to succeed: the first quick win, then the common stuck point, then a real example.
- Ask for something bigger (a call, an upgrade, a referral) only after useful messages, and make it the whole point of that message.
- Spacing: days apart early, widening. Stop on the goal reached.

## Trial

- Tie each message to what the person has or has not done, using only events the user says exist. If there is no usage data, write by day of trial and say so.
- Near the end: the plain date and what happens then (what is kept, what is charged, how to cancel). No countdown theatre.
- Stop on conversion or cancellation.

## Launch or promotion

- Announce what it is and who it is for; a reminder before any real deadline with the date and time; a final notice only if the deadline is real.
- A reason for any discount, drawn from the facts given. No reason given means no invented one.

## Newsletter

- One promise, one voice, a steady slot. The subject names the main item. Links earn their place; one primary action.

## Cart, sign-up or checkout reminder

- Reminder (what is waiting, one button), then an answer to the likely worry (shipping, returns, setup) from facts given, then help. An incentive only for a first order or a lapsed buyer, if the user allows.
- Stop at purchase. Do not stack with other triggers the same day.

## Win-back and quiet subscribers

- Say what is new or what they would miss, from facts given; make staying or leaving easy. Last message: a plain "still want these?" with one click either way. Then stop mailing.

## Renewal and expiry notices

- Date, amount and what changes, first. How to renew, change or cancel, second. No fear copy. Billing mail takes a descriptive subject, never a curiosity one.

## Cold outreach

- First note about four sentences. Follow-up one adds something new (a relevant fact, a shorter ask, an easier option). Follow-up two closes the loop and names the exit. Then stop; stop at once on a no. Spacing of a few working days between touches.
