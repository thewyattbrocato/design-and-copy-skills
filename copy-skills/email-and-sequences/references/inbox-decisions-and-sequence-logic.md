# Inbox decisions and sequence logic

Load for opens-but-few-clicks newsletters, subject-line sets, trial or onboarding series, and reputation questions. The user's plan and facts win over every default here.

## First screen and links

- The top of the email must work alone: what it is, why it matters to this reader, one link. Detail goes below; repeat the same call at the end.
- Early links take most clicks, so rank before cutting. For a many-link email, return a keep list (one primary link, at most two secondary, in the reader's order of need) and a skip list (each cut link with where it can live instead: a later issue, the destination page, a footer).
- The email earns the click; the destination holds the detail.

## Subject sets

- Vary the type, not the wording: plain statement, a specific number or date the user gave, a question the body answers, a reader-situation line.
- Reminder subjects name what is waiting or what changed. A countdown is not a variation.
- Skip spam-bait habits (all caps, stacked punctuation), but never swap a specific word for a vaguer one to dodge a filter.

## Series logic

- Value before ask: early messages must be useful if the reader never buys. Say which message carries the first ask.
- Each message opens by acknowledging the step before it, using only events the user says are tracked.
- Vary the angle: answer, example, shortcut, offer of help. The same pitch in new words is a repeat.
- A trial email states what happens on the last day: charge or none, amount, where to cancel. If the user has not said, use a placeholder.

## Reputation

- Ask once, early, that the reader add the sender to contacts.
- Silent addresses hurt delivery for everyone: after one plain "still want these?", suppress them. Watch complaints and bounces by mailbox provider, not only in total.

## Testing

- One change per test, sent at the same time to similar halves, judged on the later action (reply, purchase, activation), not the open. On a small list call a result a hint and run fewer, bolder tests.

## Before and after

Newsletter with six equal links and 150-word tips:

- Before: six topics, six "Read more" links, nothing to do first.
- After: best tip first in three sentences with one button naming the action; two one-line pointers below; the rest saved for next issue; the same button last.
