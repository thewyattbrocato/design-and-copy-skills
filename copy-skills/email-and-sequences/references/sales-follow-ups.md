# Sales follow-ups

Load for a written message in a deal that already has a conversation behind it: a recap, a reply to "send me more information", a message to a buyer who went quiet, the last note before closing a deal as lost, or a kind decline. Not for a first cold message. Calls and voicemail are in sales-conversations, if installed; whether a line is pressure is in fair-persuasion. Evidence on what moves a stalled buyer is thin, so treat these defaults as starting points.

## Recap after a conversation

Default: send it the same day in the existing thread, about 100 words, in three parts: what the buyer said they want, in their words; what was agreed; one next step naming who, what and when. End with one line inviting corrections. Change when nothing was agreed (say what you heard and ask the one question that would settle the next step; invent no decision) or a formal document was requested (a short cover note above it).

- Use only figures and commitments the buyer stated. Anything promised travels with the recap; no new pitch. "Let me know" is not a next step.

## "Send me more information"

Default: one short reply that asks what the material should help them decide, offers the single item that answers it, and proposes a time to go over it. Attach nothing else. Change when they named exactly what they need (send that, with a date to discuss it) or the request is a polite exit (one line, leave the door open once, then stop).

## A buyer who wants to change and went quiet

Default: this is the first of the two follow-ups, so it must bring something the last message did not: a recommendation with its reason and the condition that would change it, a smaller first step, or a real way to lower the risk, all from facts the user gave. Name the likely worry in a clause, as a guess. A restated offer, a repeated cost of waiting, a new deadline or "did my last note get buried" brings nothing, and a buyer afraid of choosing wrong hears each as one more risk. Change when the buyer never agreed there is a problem (ask one question about what has changed; recommend nothing) or named a date they are waiting on (say you will write after it, then do).

## The last check-in

Default: the second and last follow-up, and it comes only after the first one added something. Say what was last agreed and what has not happened, then give both exits in one line: carry on with [step], or close this and revisit in [month]. Two or three sentences, no guilt. With no reply, close the deal on your side and stop. Change when the delay is the seller's: send what the seller owes first and skip the check-in.

## Declining a poor fit

Default: decline early and kindly. One sentence of reason (size, use case, timing), what the product does not do, and a better route only if the user named one. No pitch inside a decline. Change when the contact could introduce a better-fit account: add one line asking.

## One stop rule across channels

The two follow-ups cover the person, not each channel. A call or voicemail planned alongside them counts against the same two, and a no on any channel ends all. A recap, or a reply to something the buyer asked for, is an answer, not a nudge, and uses neither.

## Pair

Invented: a sign shop quoted menu boards for a restaurant group, and the owner went quiet after saying the quote looked right.

- Before: "Hi Dana, just following up on the quote. Our print slots for next month are filling fast, so I'd hate for you to miss out!"
- After: "Hi Dana, you said the quote looked right and that the open question was getting every location on one design. I'd suggest starting with [two locations] on the [design] so you can see it in place before committing the rest; if it isn't right, [what happens, from the user's facts]. If that suits you, I can send the order for those two on [day]."
