# Campaign ideas

An idea is what to say or do so the right person gets it fast and repeats it. The default failure is a nicer wording of the brief: a launch announcement, a pun, a hashtag, five rephrasings of one thought. This skill finds a buyer truth and a real tension, builds directions that differ in approach, cuts them with simple gates, and hands the words to the neighbors.

## Do not produce

- "Introducing X!" as the concept, or the brief restated in a better font.
- Several directions that are one idea reworded, or only puns, hashtag challenges and "behind the scenes" by reflex.
- A strategy memo with no idea in it, or a concept that needs a budget, permission or fact the user has not given.
- Invented customers, quotes, results, numbers, rival weaknesses, scarcity or deadlines, a staged mistake or leak, fake "organic" posts or fake users, or an unconsented use of real people's content or data.
- A villain or fear the buyer's situation does not contain, especially in health, money and safety.

## When not to use

- The final headline set, taglines and first lines: headlines-and-leads if installed. This skill delivers the idea and a few sample lines.
- Who it is for, the category and the one-sentence position: positioning-and-messaging if installed. Price, trials and guarantees: offers-and-value-propositions if installed.
- Whether a claim is true and provable: honest-claims if installed. Whether a flow steers unfairly: fair-persuasion if installed. Run every concept through both ideas anyway.
- A full page, email series or content calendar: landing-page-copy, email-and-sequences or content-strategy if installed.
- Ads or posts the user already wrote, and only wants fixed: edit them, do not replace them with a concept.

If a neighbor is not installed, use ordinary judgment. A concept, brand voice or constraint the user already chose wins: work inside it and say you kept it.

## Method

A small request can skip to steps 3, 5 and 7. Keep scratch work to a few lines; the user wants the result, not the process.

1. **Reframe the brief.** Name what must change in the buyer's mind or behavior, as a question the brief does not answer yet ("how does someone feel the value in the first minute?"). Keep the original deliverable too. If the deliverable is fixed, use the reframe only to find the angle.
2. **Write the bare claim.** One plain sentence of the point, from the user's facts only. If it needs a fact the user did not give, it gets a placeholder.
3. **Find the buyer truth and the tension.** The truth is something the buyer already feels, not a feature: what they already do or complain about, in customer words, tickets or reviews the user supplied. With none, state it as [assumed] and say where to check it. The tension is real and local: an old habit against a new one, effort against result, what the category makes everyone do.
4. **Diverge on approach.** Aim for at least six directions in scratch. Vary the door, not the wording: demonstrate it, break a category habit, let the user's situation carry it, make something useful that stands without the brand, say less. Distinctness is the check, not the count. Show the pick and the plain fallback; list the other directions in one line each only when asked or when the user is choosing.
5. **Cut, then cluster.** Drop what only the first-thought crowd would produce. Look for a pattern in the survivors; a pattern is often the idea. Build siblings from it, then pick.
6. **Gate the pick.** One sentence says it, and that sentence suggests more than one execution. Remove each element in turn; what still works is the idea. A stranger could repeat it. It would be news in one headline. It stays true if anyone checks. It needs nothing the owner has not given. Details in [directions-and-gates.md](references/directions-and-gates.md).
7. **Carry it and hand off.** Show the idea in three forms that differ in kind (for example a post, a physical or product moment, a message). Each form must still work if the other two never run. Give the pick with one reason, then a plain fallback.

## Judgment calls

- **Idea or words.** Default to finding the idea. If the user has a concept, write for it and say so. If they ask only for captions or lines, give them, with one line saying a different idea might serve better and what it is.
- **Bold or safe.** Default to one bold direction and one plain fallback, labeled. Boldness needs a product reason; shock alone is not one. Change to safe when the category is regulated, sensitive or the owner is cautious.
- **System or single piece.** Default to one controlling idea with forms. A one-off post stays a one-off.
- **Humor.** Use it when the idea is funny with the facts straight. Not for money, health, errors or loss.
- **Who is the hero.** Default to the buyer. The product is how they get out of the tension.
- **Provocation.** Fine about a true thing and inside the law, platform rules and the owner's consent. Never at a vulnerability, or by mocking a rival; compare only on facts the user gave.
- **Sponsored or borrowed voices.** If a creator, customer or staff voice is used, the concept says it is disclosed, credited and consented. Authentic does not mean undisclosed.
- **Platform tricks.** Skip hashtags, filters and apps unless the buyer's goal needs them. Do not state platform limits or "studies show".

## Missing facts

Many requests give a product and little else. Do not invent customers, numbers, quotes, rivals, scarcity or traction. Build the concept from the situation, mark gaps inline ([add: real customer line], [assumed: who is buying]), and for any concept that needs a missing fact, a permission or a real deadline, add one line: "Ask the owner: ...". Ask a question first only when the session is interactive and the answer changes the idea; otherwise deliver with the brackets. Never reply with only questions. If the user pushes for a fake countdown, a "leak", planted posts or made-up proof, decline that part in one sentence and give the honest version that works (a real deadline, a stated play, an invited submission) in the same answer.

## If the idea already works

If a supplied concept is specific, true, and shows up in more than one form, say so and change at most a word or one execution. Do not replace it for taste.

## Output

Deliver the concept first, with nothing before it: the idea in one sentence, three forms (each one to three lines), what it needs from the owner, and the plain fallback. When a count is asked for (three captions, five directions), give exactly that number and no more. Show the other directions only if asked or when the user is choosing, one line each. At most two one-line notes, and only if they change what the owner does. Honor any length cap and count rather than assert. The reason: the owner will paste this into a brief or a thread, and every unrequested section is something to delete.

## Quick checks

- The directions differ in approach, not wording; one breaks a category habit, and the fallback is plain.
- The pick is stated in one sentence a stranger could repeat, and it is true if checked.
- Every fact, quote and proof is the user's or a visible placeholder; no staged or undisclosed voices.
- Each form names its owner ask; the fallback is real and needs less.
