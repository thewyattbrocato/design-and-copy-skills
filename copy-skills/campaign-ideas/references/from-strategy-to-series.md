# From strategy to series

Load when the idea has to run as several pieces or follow earlier work, when the product matches its rivals', when a concept could belong to any brand, or when the directions bunch up. These are checks to run and fold into the concept, not sections to print.

## Three layers

The strategy is what should change in the buyer's mind or behavior; the concept is the thought that makes it change; the execution is one way of showing it. A "concept" that names a medium ("a video of customers") is an execution; one that restates the benefit ("planning made easy") is the strategy.

Default: take a supplied strategy as given and work on the concept. Change when the strategy is a stack of benefits: pick the one this buyer cares about most first.

## More doors

After the six doors in the main reference, if directions still bunch up, try these. Pick by what the user's facts can carry; how many to try is taste.

- **Flip a negative.** A real grumble the product answers becomes the point.
- **Say what they think.** The sentence in the buyer's head at the moment of need.
- **Teach one true thing.** A fact from the user's material that changes how the product is seen.
- **Set a test.** Invite buyers to check a claim themselves, conditions stated.
- **Do something.** The brand acts and the act is the message (a fix, a free service). Needs the owner's approval and budget; mark it.

## Name-swap gate

Add to the gate list. Hide the brand and put an unrelated company's name on the idea, then a rival's. If it works as well under both, the product is tacked on: tie the idea to something only this product, or the way its buyers use it, has, from the user's facts. The repeat gate asks whether people can say the idea back; this asks whether they would credit the right brand. Pass both; report only a failure.

## Series

Pieces in a series share a device; the three forms in the main method differ in kind.

- **Siblings, not copies.** Name what stays fixed (the device, the shape of the line, the look) and what each piece adds: a new situation, a turn, a fact. Pieces that could swap places are one idea said twice.
- **Series or one piece.** Default: a series only when you can say what the third piece adds; otherwise ship one piece and say the idea has no more in it. Change when the brief fixes a run, such as a weekly slot, and plan the run around the device.
- **Would anyone miss it.** For ongoing content, ask who would write in if it stopped after a month. If nobody, add something people use or enjoy without the sell. A launch burst can skip this.

Worked pair (invented): a library opens late. Before: three posts reading "Open till 9" over different shelf photos. After: timestamped pieces ("8:40 pm"), each showing who is there and why. Fixed: timestamp and layout; each piece adds a person. Needs: [add: real hours] and consent for any real patron shown.

## Earlier work

With past work supplied, name the two or three things that carry over (the line, the character, the look, the device) and what may change. Default: renew the idea inside the kept cues; "stay fresh" is not a reason to start over. Change when the cues are generic, copied by rivals or never reached anyone, or the user wants a break and says why. The look itself is brand-identity's if installed.

## Same product as everyone's

Default: take the angle from use, not a claimed edge: who uses it and when, what it spares them, who stands behind it, how it is bought, something useful around it. Any "better" needs a user fact or a visible bracket. Whether to claim a difference is positioning-and-messaging's call.

## Lines and limits

Default: a plain working line, labeled as working; the final tagline is headlines-and-leads' job. Drop it when the idea and the brand's cues already carry it.

If the facts point at price, stock, a product flaw or a confusing sign-up, say in one line that a campaign will not fix it, then still deliver the idea.
