# Directions and gates

Load this for a dull category, a stuck brief or a pick between supplied concepts. The scenarios are invented.

## Doors to vary

Different doors give different ideas; synonyms do not. Try several, and keep a plain version as the fallback:

- **Demonstrate.** Show the result being used instead of saying it.
- **Break a habit.** Skip what everyone in the category does (hide the price table, show the price board; answer instead of announce).
- **Let the situation carry it.** Put the message where and when the buyer meets the problem.
- **Make something useful.** A tool, guide or service people would use without the brand, with the brand on it.
- **Say less.** State the true small thing.
- **Invite.** Ask people to contribute, with consent, credit and a stated reward.

## Gates

Run the pick, not every direction. Say only what fails.

1. **One sentence.** Can the idea be said in a line that suggests several executions? If it only suggests one, it is a single execution; ask what else it could be.
2. **Remove each element.** Take out the line, the visual, the twist, one at a time. If the idea survives, that element was decoration. If nothing survives, it is too fragile.
3. **Repeat.** Would a stranger say it back after two seconds, without the brand name?
4. **News.** Could it be reported as one headline about something that happened?
5. **True if checked.** Every claim is the user's fact. A joke built on a false premise fails.
6. **Needs.** List any fact, permission, budget or real deadline the idea needs. If it is missing, the form is shown with a placeholder and an owner ask.
7. **Fair.** No manufactured fear or scarcity, no one's content used without consent, voices disclosed.

## Bad to better (generic)

- Brief: launch a project-planning feature. Bad: "Introducing Timeline: plan like never before!" Better: direction from a buyer truth, "You already have the plan; it is just scattered across four places." Forms: a post showing the four places, a one-page template people can copy, an in-product first-run line. Needs: the owner confirms the feature does what the post shows.
- Brief: loyalty perk for a cafe. Bad: three captions praising "rewards you'll love". Better: the perk becomes a small ritual the regular already has ("your usual, remembered"). Forms: the cup note, a counter sign, a short message. Fallback: a plain post stating the perk and its dates. Needs: the real terms [add: perk rules].
- Brief: "something viral". Bad: a staged leak with planted reactions. Better: a stated play, an invitation to share a real use with credit and a real reward, labeled as the brand's own. Needs: the owner approves the reward and credits.

## When stuck

Change the problem: swap the medium or budget, start from the tension instead of the product, or write the plain version first and ask what it is missing. Do not talk a good idea until it is dull; pick, then sharpen.
