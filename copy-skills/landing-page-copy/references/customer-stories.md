# Customer stories

Load when the copy tells one customer's change: a case-study page, a homepage card, a pull quote, or a spoken script. Section order is in page-types.md. What may move in a retelling, kinds of story, consent and anonymising are in honest-claims (stories-and-hooks.md). Every example is invented.

## Hero and turn

- **Hero.** Default to the customer as a named person, or as a role in a size band when anonymous, chosen to look like the reader you want. Change to the role that carried the decision when the buyer is a committee. The product is a tool in their hands: "Ines moved the crew schedule into a shared calendar", not "the platform streamlined scheduling".
- **Turn.** Find the one moment things went differently, an action someone took rather than a feeling or a feature switched on. If the notes hold only a before and an after, the story has no middle yet; see thin notes below.
- **Setback.** Default to keeping the failed first week, the slow colleague or the cost, in a sentence, with what fixed it. The reader's doubt is "will this work for someone like me", and the struggle is the answer. Some teams prefer to show only the win; that is taste, and it reads as an ad. Change when the customer will not approve the line: leave it out rather than soften it.

## Shape

1. Open on the customer's situation, not the product or a scene set for effect.
2. Context in one or two sentences: only what places them (kind of business, size, what they used).
3. The middle: what they decided and did, in order, and what got in the way.
4. The result with the words that scope it ("by her own count", "over the first quarter") and what it took in time, money or someone's habits. A figure with no basis gets `[how counted]`.
5. End when the reader knows what changed: the customer's approved words, or one ask for readers in the same position ("If your schedule still lives on a whiteboard, start with one crew"). Not a recap of benefits.

## Versions by size

Default to one set of facts in every version; shorter means fewer details, never a new claim or flourish.

- **Page.** As long as the middle needs. The headline names the person or role and the change.
- **Card.** About 100 words. It still holds a person or role, what changed, and one figure or quote with its basis. A card that is only a quote and a logo is not a story.
- **Pull quote.** One sentence, verbatim, approved, with name and role. Pick the line that says what changed over praise.
- **Spoken or video.** About a minute, roughly 150 words. One person, one turn, one result, short sentences that sound natural read aloud. Say a figure once, with its basis.

## When the notes are thin

Default to delivering the story's shape with what exists: the facts given, told plainly, and a visible slot for each missing piece (`[approved quote]`, `[figure and how counted]`, `[what went wrong first]`). Change to outline plus questions when there is no event or no result to tell. The questions then replace commentary, at most three, and ask for events: what happened the week they started; where it first stalled or failed; when they first saw it working, and what they said. A one-line claim such as "partnered with us and saw big growth" gets the same slots (who, what changed, how measured), not polish.

## Quick checks

- The hero is a person or role, and no sentence has the product doing the changing.
- The change hangs on a stated action or event, not only an adjective like "transformed".
- A setback or cost appears unless the notes say there was none.
- Every figure has its basis beside it and every quote is the customer's supplied wording.
- A shorter version contains no fact the page lacks; the card still has a person and a change.
- The ending is a next step or the customer's words, not a summary of features.
- Nothing invented: every name, quote and number came from the user.
