# Editing and cutting

Editing means removing what gets in the reader's way while keeping what they need. The default failure is editing that adds: longer text after "tighten", a new buzzword for the old one, a change list with no text. The other failure is touching what already works. Subtract on purpose, and leave good lines alone.

## Do not produce

- A swapped buzzword ("harness" for "leverage") or a fancier word for a plain one.
- Added intensifiers, exclamation marks, emoji or enthusiasm the original did not have.
- A deleted true qualifier, condition, price term, disclosure or the ask.
- A run of same-length fragments passed off as punchy.
- A rewrite of lines that already work, or an unrequested change of voice.
- A change log or critique instead of the edited text.
- Text over a stated length, or longer than the original after "shorten".
- A new fact, number, quote or claim the original did not carry.

## When not to use

- Whether a claim is true or supportable: honest-claims, if installed. Here, keep every fact and each true qualifier as given.
- Rebuilding a page's argument or order: landing-page-copy, if installed. This skill moves the point up and cuts what serves no one.
- New headlines or openings from scratch: headlines-and-leads, if installed. Interface strings: ux-microcopy, if installed.
- Code documentation, translation, and book-length manuscripts.

The user's style guide, voice sample and marked lines win over every default below.

## Before you edit

Name the piece's one point and its reader. If you cannot, the trouble is structure or thinking, not words: say so in one line, and still deliver a best edit. Decide what must survive: the point, every fact and number, true qualifiers, required disclosures, the ask, the only proof, anything the user marked, and the author's voice where it works.

## Editing order

For one sentence, start at step 3.

1. **Structure first.** Put the point where the reader decides or acts: first, unless the piece is a story the author built to build. Cut whole paragraphs or items that answer no reader question, and the weakest of several similar ones, before touching words. Block cuts change quality; word cuts only change length.
2. **One idea per paragraph.** A paragraph that holds two splits; a single sentence of throat-clearing before the point goes.
3. **Sentences.** Subject and verb early. Move dates, conditions and asides out of the middle, put a condition before the instruction it governs, and turn buried verbs back into verbs. Keep passive voice when the receiver is the point or the actor is unknown; "use active voice" is not law. Split a sentence that carries two ideas.
4. **Words.** Delete a word at a time until meaning would be lost, then restore the last one. Cut announcements ("It's important to note", "In order to"), stacked intensifiers, repeated pairs, and hedges the user can stand behind. Keep a hedge that carries real scope or uncertainty; when unsure, keep it.
5. **Abstractions.** Replace middle-ground nouns ("solutions", "capabilities", "experience") with what the reader does or gets. If the concrete thing is not in the text, use a bracketed placeholder or a plain true sentence. Never invent the specific.
6. **Template voice.** Remove stock openers and closers, "not just X, it's Y", "whether you're X or Y", reflexive triplets, a dash in every sentence, and closing questions. Replace each with the content it stood in for, not a synonym. The long list is in [template-voice.md](references/template-voice.md).
7. **Rhythm.** Read it aloud in your head. Vary sentence length, and put the important idea in the shortest sentence or at a sentence end. Repeating a plain term is fine; do not rotate synonyms to avoid it, and keep one term per concept. If cutting made it curt, restore one human line.

## Cutting to a limit

Cut in this order: sections, then weakest sentences, then words. Never cut what "Before you edit" says must survive; if it will not fit, compress everything else and say what is tight in one note. Count the final text yourself (use `wc` if a shell is available), and treat "about N" as within ten percent. Over the cap means cut again, and be under it, not just near it. A character limit counts spaces. If the user gave no limit and asked to tighten, aim for roughly a fifth shorter, unless meaning would go; check the result is shorter than the original. Test the shortest cut for lost context: a line the reader cannot decode alone is too short.

## Judgment calls

- **Light touch or full edit:** full edit by default. When the user asks for a light touch, fix only errors and the worst few problems.
- **Voice:** keep the author's quirks that work, including jokes, deliberate fragments and dashes in a brand voice. Change voice only when asked, and then say the anchor.
- **Jargon:** plain words by default. Keep a term every reader shares (expert audience); define a term the reader may not know at first use.
- **Dashes, lists and groups of three:** limits, not bans. Roughly one dash per paragraph; a list only for parallel items.
- **"Sounds like AI":** the cause is vagueness plus a few tics. The fix is specifics (or placeholders) and uneven rhythm, not fancier vocabulary.
- **Reading level:** simplify for the stated audience; formulas are alarms, the ear decides.
- **Translation headroom:** for interface strings, leave room for other languages rather than cutting to the last character.

## If the text already works

Say so and change at most a word. Name the lines you kept in a sentence, not a list. When asked to choose between two edits, pick one and give the reason in a sentence; do not write a third unless asked.

## When facts are missing

Never invent a number, quote, review, rating, customer, deadline, stock limit or result to make a vague line specific. Use a visible bracket (`[time saved]`, `[customer name]`) or a softer true sentence, and still deliver the edit. Ask one question only if the session is interactive and the answer is cheap; otherwise deliver.

## Output

Deliver the edited text first, with nothing before it. For a critique ("what would you cut"), give the cuts and why, briefly, and the edited text only if asked. When a count or set is asked for (one version, three options), give exactly that. Add at most two one-line notes, only for what the user must act on or check: a placeholder, a cut that was close, a line kept on purpose. No change table, no explanation of every edit unless asked. Answer in the user's language.

## Quick checks

- Every fact, number and true qualifier from the original is still there, and nothing new was added.
- The point comes early, and the voice is still the author's.
- Within any limit, counted; shorter than the original if asked to shorten.
- No swapped buzzword, no new tics, no uniform fragments.
- Lines that worked were left alone.
