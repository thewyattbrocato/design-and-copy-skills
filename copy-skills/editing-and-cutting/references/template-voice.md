# Template voice

Stock phrasing is what text sounds like when nothing specific was available to say. The fix is never a different stock phrase. Ask what the phrase stood in for (a fact, a step, a reason, a number), put that in, and if it is not known, use a bracketed placeholder or delete the sentence. Examples are English; the same patterns appear in other languages.

## Kinds, and what each stands in for

- **Stock openers.** "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving landscape of", "Are you tired of", "We're excited to share", "Let's dive in". They stand in for the first real sentence. Start with it: who this is for, what happened, what changes.
- **Announcing connectives.** "It's important to note that", "When it comes to", "Moreover", "Additionally", "Furthermore" as openers. Delete; if the sentences do not connect without them, the order is wrong, so fix the order.
- **Inflated verbs and empty adjectives.** "Leverage", "unlock", "elevate", "streamline", "empower", "seamless", "robust", "cutting-edge", "game-changing", "world-class". They stand in for what happens. Name the action and its result in the plainest verb that is true. If you cannot, cut the claim.
- **Contrast template.** "Not just X, it's Y", "More than a tool, it's a partner", "It's not about X, it's about Y". State Y. Keep a contrast only when a real reader holds the X belief.
- **Audience template.** "Whether you're a beginner or a pro", "For teams of all sizes". Name the actual reader, or drop the line.
- **Reflexive triplets.** Three adjectives or three parallel benefits where two ideas existed. Keep the ones that are separate facts; cut the padding item. Do not pad up to three or trim down to two for symmetry.
- **Middle-ground nouns.** "Solutions", "experiences", "capabilities", "offerings", "ecosystem", "journey". Say the thing the reader does or gets.
- **Dash habit.** A dash in nearly every sentence. Keep at most about one a paragraph, for a true aside or a final turn; use a period or comma for the rest. If the author's voice sample uses dashes on purpose, keep them.
- **Colon titles and rhetorical questions.** "The X: Why Y matters"; "What if there were a better way?" Say the point; ask a question only if it is the reader's real one.
- **Hedge stems.** "Kind of", "somewhat", "may potentially", "we believe that", "it could be argued". Delete when the claim is one the author stands behind; keep a qualifier that is real scope ("in our tests", "for most plans").
- **Closers.** "In conclusion", "In summary", "Ready to take the next step?", "Feel free to reach out". End on the last useful sentence or the one concrete ask.
- **Excitement punctuation.** Exclamation marks, emoji bullets, bolded phrases in every paragraph. Drop unless the voice sample uses them.

## Short pairs

- Before: "In today's competitive landscape, our innovative platform empowers teams to unlock their full potential by gathering every open task in one list."
  After: "Your team sees every open task in one list. [Add the one result you can prove.]"
  The claim had no content, so the edit states what the product does and flags where proof would go.
- Before: "It's not just a planner, it's a complete productivity ecosystem that moves unfinished tasks to the next day."
  After: "It plans your week and moves unfinished tasks to the next day."
  The contrast and the noun were replaced with what the original says it does.
- Before: "We believe this may potentially help reduce costs in some cases."
  After: "This may help reduce costs in some cases."
  The stacked hedge goes; the scope qualifier "in some cases" stays unless the author's evidence supports a flat claim.
- Before: "Our solution is a robust, seamless, best-in-class way to manage invoices, and setup takes about five minutes."
  After: "Set up invoicing in about five minutes."
  The adjectives had no content and went; the one fact stayed, and nothing was added.
- Before: "Furthermore, it's important to note that deliveries may be delayed."
  After: "Deliveries may be delayed."
  Announcement deleted, true qualifier kept.
- Before: "Ready to transform your workflow? Contact us today! We'd love to hear from you!"
  After: "To get started, reply with your team size."
  Two closers and two exclamation marks became the one ask.

## Over-correction

Removing every pattern makes a new pattern: all fragments, no warmth, one mood. After the pass, read the text once as the reader: sentences should differ in length, one of them may be plain and friendly, and the author's own jokes, rhythms and word choices should survive. If a phrase here appears in the user's text and works in context, leave it.
