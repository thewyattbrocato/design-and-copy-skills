# Pressure in sales conversations

Load when a call script, pitch, follow-up, voicemail, trial or pilot plan is written or reviewed, when a reply to a stalled buyer is wanted, or when someone asks for a line that closes harder, adds urgency or keeps the buyer from saying no. Every example is invented. Whether a figure is true belongs to honest-claims; the stop rule for written follow-ups is in email-and-sequences.

A seller may recommend, ask for the decision and say what waiting costs. The failure is a move that works only because "not yet" or "no" is awkward to say, or because the buyer cannot see what is being done. Keep the goal and change the means.

## A buyer who is stuck

Default: when a buyer has said they want to change but has not moved, do not argue the cost of waiting again; they know it. Agree the problem in one line, then make the decision smaller: one recommendation with its reason and what would change it, a short list of what is still open, a first step that can be undone, or a plain statement of what happens if it does not work. Change when the buyer has not yet admitted the problem or its size: ask them to work it out in their own figures instead of handing them a scarier number, and when the stall is missing information, supply the facts first.

A fear appeal needs a real, sized danger and a step the buyer can take. A buyer who is already worried and quiet gets the remedy, not more dread.

## Time and obligation

Default: a deadline is a date that exists apart from this buyer, with its cause, the same for everyone and never reset. "Price goes up Friday", "two slots left" and "free month if you sign today" fail when the cause is the seller's wish to close. "Prices may rise" is fine only if a rise is planned. Change when the user supplies the real date and reason; use it as given.

Default: ask for time with a reason and an exit: "To hold your start date I need a yes or no by [date]." Guilt for the seller's effort ("the least you can do is reply") is not a reason.

## Authority, price and what is withheld

- **Props.** Default: say who decides and what it depends on. "Let me check with my VP" when the answer is already fixed, or a high opening quote built to be bargained down, is theatre. Open with a price you would hold; any change has a reason and is open to anyone in the same position.
- **Price questions.** Default: answer a direct question, or give a range and what moves it, then ask for context. Change when pricing is truly custom: say what it depends on. Holding the number back until after a demo is a gate.

## Questions and options

- **Leading runs.** Default: ask what you do not know. A run of questions each built to collect a yes ("you'd agree wasted time is a cost?") builds commitment, not understanding. Change when checking a fact the buyer holds, worded so "no" is easy.
- **Hypotheticals.** "If we built that, would you sign?" extracts a promise about something that does not exist. Record the request; say what exists today and what is not promised.
- **A gap planted for a rival.** Asking whether they would "also" need what a competitor does badly passes only when the capability is real and you would raise it for a buyer who gains you nothing.
- **Option sets.** Default: a few options, each fitting a described buyer, one labelled recommendation with its reason and the condition that would flip it. A set where two options exist only to make a third look right fails; rebuild it from real buyers.
- **Skipping the objection.** "Sign now, we will sort it out later" hides the open point. Name it and settle it, or leave it open in writing.

## The ask, a no, and persistence

Default: ask plainly and put "not yet" in the same breath: "Shall I send the agreement, or would you rather settle [open point] first?" 

Default: a stated lack of interest is a no. One clarifying line is allowed ("Is it timing, or not a fit?"), then stop, in every channel; moving from email to phone to get around a no is the same pressure. A closing note that names the exit is an honest last message. Set no touch count here; series length follows the buyer's rhythm and the email stop rule, and any number you choose is a judgment call to label as one.

Default: a voicemail or first note says who is calling and why in its first sentence. No "returning your call", no reply prefix, no borrowed familiarity.

## Trials and pilots

Default: the trial or pilot ends on the agreed date, stated up front. If the contract is late, offer an extension at a stated price or a dated read-only period; pulling access to speed the signature is leverage, not need.

Default: nothing is bought until the buyer says yes in writing, and the plan names a success test, a decision date and who signs. Change when the buyer asked for automatic continuation: then the first charge date and amount are in the agreement and a reminder goes out before it.

## Targeting a moment

Never aim urgency at a trigger event such as a job loss, a death, an illness or a debt; reach people by role and need, with a plain note of what the service does. That such moments lift conversion is exactly why they are refused.

## Keep these

A clear recommendation, a real deadline with its cause, a direct ask, one follow-up that adds something new and a candid "shall I close this out?" note all stay.

## Before and after

- "Only two slots left, and the price rises Friday." → "The next onboarding group starts [date]; this price holds until [date]." Use only dates the user gave.
- "Totally understand! But think what another quarter of this costs you." → "You said this needs fixing and the open point is rollout. I would start with [smaller step]; if it misses [test], you stop and owe nothing."
