# Patterns and fixes

Load for an audit or a redesign. Every example is invented and generic. Each row gives the device, what is wrong with it, the fair form that serves the same goal, and when a variant passes.

## Start (joining, choosing a plan, beginning a trial)

| Device | Why it harms | Fair form | Acceptable when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free trial that renews silently | The person pays without having decided to | Charge date and amount before the card field, a reminder before the charge, one-step cancel | A card is taken up front but all three are present |
| Three tiers where the middle exists to make the top look cheap | The choice is rigged, not informed | Tiers someone would sensibly buy, each named for the situation it fits | A real "most chosen" label with data behind it (honest-claims) |
| A percentage saving with no money figure | Percentages hide the base | The saving in money beside the percent, and the yearly total beside the monthly equivalent | The base price is also shown |
| "Monthly or annual?" with no way to defer | Forces a commitment to continue | The same screen with the plan that costs less per month marked, and the reason stated | The person can switch plans later, and the rule is stated |
| Countdown on a considered purchase | Rushes a decision that needs thought | Nothing, or a real deadline with its cause (honest-claims) | The deadline is real and was given by the user |

## Add (extras, upsells, cross-sells)

| Device | Why it harms | Fair form | Acceptable when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-ticked insurance or extras | Charges for inattention | Unticked, priced, one-line reason | The item is required by the product, and is labelled as required |
| Post-purchase "add this now" that bills on one tap | A charge with no decision | An offer once the need is met, with the price shown and a clear confirm | The person has a same-weight "No thanks" |

## Stay (retention, cancellation, downgrade)

| Device | Why it harms | Fair form | Acceptable when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-screen cancel, required survey, phone-only exit | Exit costs more than entry | One screen: consequences, the cancel action, one optional alternative | A confirmation step guards a destructive action, with the reason given |
| "We're sad to see you go" with a guilt image | Shame as leverage | A plain heading and the facts: what ends, when, what is kept | A warm line after the decision is confirmed |
| Discount that equals the normal price | Fake concession | A real change, available to anyone in that situation, offered once | The offer fits the stated reason for leaving (price, low use) |
| Offer repeated at each step | Turns exit into a negotiation | One alternative, once, and a neutral decline | The person asks to see options |

## Share (consent, permissions, referrals)

| Device | Why it harms | Fair form | Acceptable when |
|---|---|---|---|
| One tick joining terms, marketing and sharing | Forces unwanted agreements | Required terms stated plainly; each optional item its own unticked box | Items are truly inseparable, and the text says so |
| One box means yes, the next means no | Accidental consent | One polarity: every optional box is an opt-in | Never mix on one screen |
| Notification permission asked at first launch with no reason | Asked before the value is clear | A pre-prompt saying what it enables and how often, "Turn on" and "Not now" | The person has just chosen a feature that needs it |
| Referral asks that read the address book or auto-send | Speaks in the sender's name without consent | The sender writes or edits the message and sees the reward for both sides | The sender sends each message |
| Review request that rewards only top ratings | Biases what the public sees | A request open to any honest review, any incentive disclosed | No incentive is offered |

## Return (reminders, streaks, notifications)

| Device | Why it harms | Fair form | Acceptable when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily loss threat tied to a streak | Compels by fear of a made-up loss | A progress view and a reminder the user set | The streak is the user's own goal and can be paused |
| Reminders at any hour, no cap | Pressure by volume | A cap, quiet hours, and a setting that works | Time-critical alerts the user asked for |
| Alarm about the account used as a sales hook | Fear with no danger behind it | The real limit and date, plus the next step | A real risk exists, stated at true size |

## Emotional levers by dose

- **Fear:** a documented risk, true size, a remedy beside it. No imagined scene, no hint of cover-up.
- **Guilt and shame:** none. A decline never carries them.
- **Status and exclusivity:** only when real (a tier with real extras, a beta that really is invite-only). Otherwise state what the thing does.
- **Identity:** "for people who plan ahead" only if the reader would claim it freely and may leave it.
- **Comparison:** to a reachable peer. A sense of being far behind drives people away, not toward.
- **Vulnerable moments** (billing failure, cancelling, after an error, grief, debt, illness, children): the bar rises; remove pressure rather than tune it.

## Before and after

- "No thanks, I'll pay full price" → "No thanks".
- "Your cancellation is final. Are you sure you want to lose everything?" → "Cancel plan. Your files stay for [days], then they are deleted. [Cancel plan] [Keep plan]" (use a number only if the user gave one).
- "Offer ends in 09:59" with no cause → remove it, or "[real end date and reason]".
- "Add travel cover" ticked → the same line unticked, with a sentence on what it covers and its price.

## Measures

Track conversion alongside signs of regret, such as refunds, disputed charges, complaints and early cancellations. Judge a retention change by customers who stay and are content, not only by the cancel button's click rate.
