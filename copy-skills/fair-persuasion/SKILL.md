# Fair persuasion

A business may steer a choice. It may not win by making the other option hard to see, hard to reach or shameful. The usual failure is a lever that works only because the user does not notice it. The opposite failure is dropping every recommendation, save offer and reminder as soon as "ethical" is mentioned. Keep the legitimate goal and change the means.

## Do not produce

- A decline worded as a confession ("No, I don't like saving money") or a goodbye that guilts.
- Leaving that takes more steps, screens or channels than joining did: a phone call to cancel an online sign-up, a required survey, several confirmations.
- A box pre-selected if it spends money, passes data on or signs someone up for marketing, or consent bundled into terms, or boxes that mean yes and no on the same screen.
- A retention "deal" equal to the normal price, or a discount shown only to people who try to leave.
- Tiers built to make another tier look good, or a forced choice with no "neither" or "not now".
- A timer, "N people are viewing", or a loss warning with no real cause the user gave.
- Dread, shame or a lost-streak warning used to force an action, or pressure aimed at someone in debt, ill, grieving, lonely or young.
- A trial that converts to paid with no charge date and amount shown before the card, and no reminder before it.

## When not to use

- Whether a number, quote or deadline is true: honest-claims, if installed. This skill judges the lever and the flow, not the statement.
- Field labels, validation and form mechanics: form-design, if installed. Wording of each screen: ux-microcopy. Offer terms and price copy: offers-and-value-propositions. Frequency of emails and follow-ups: email-and-sequences. Whole pages: landing-page-copy. If a neighbor is not installed, use ordinary judgment.
- Whether something is legal. Say in one line that rules on this vary by place and change over time, so have it checked; platform policy and law win where stricter.
- Pricing strategy and the visual layout of a screen. Say what must be visible and equal in weight; leave how to the design skills.

## When facts are missing

Do the work. Never invent a pause plan, discount, grace period, data-retention rule, access-end date or price the user did not give. Use a bracketed placeholder (`[end date]`, `[pause length]`) or offer only the options named. Ask once only if the session is interactive and the answer changes the flow; otherwise deliver with brackets. If told to add a tactic from the list above, give one line of reason and deliver the fair version that serves the same goal. Do not refuse and do not lecture.

## Rules, grouped by the choice in play

- **Start.** Show the first charge date, amount and renewal terms before the card field, at the same size as "free", and send a reminder before the first charge. Put the saving in money next to any percentage, and show the yearly total with the monthly equivalent and cancellation terms. Mark a recommended plan with its reason; recommend only options someone would sensibly buy.
- **Add.** Offer an upsell after the need is met, unticked, priced, and declined in one action. Charge nothing after purchase without a fresh yes. A default is fine when a well-informed person would pick it (security on, backup on), and it says so and reverses in one step.
- **Stay.** Real reasons to leave deserve real answers: pause, downgrade, fix the cause. Offer at most one alternative, once, and a neutral way to decline. A discount counts only when it is a real price change that anyone in the same situation could get.
- **Leave.** Cancelling costs no more effort than joining did and works in the same place. One screen: a plain heading, what becomes of the person's data and bills and by what date, the cancel button, one optional alternative. The confirmation names the last day of access and the way back in. Ask for a reason if you like, but never as a condition.
- **Share.** Optional consent is its own unticked box with the same polarity as the others, separate from required terms. Do not open a consent or permission screen with praise or a benefit rush; say what it enables and that it can change. A referral message is in the sender's voice, editable, never auto-sent, and both sides see the reward.
- **Return.** Reminders tie to the user's own goal, are capped, adjustable and quiet at night. A streak or progress count may show; a penalty built to compel does not. Cost-driven price rises need the cost story shown; remove a discount rather than add a surcharge.
- **Emotion.** Reach for fear only when the danger is real, sized honestly, with something the reader can do about it. Status or exclusivity must be real and earned. Identity appeals fit only a self the reader would happily claim. Compare with someone close by, not a gap that feels impossible or a ranking that shames. Never present a bad habit as the norm.

## Keep these

Friction that protects: a confirmation before wiping a long-held archive, a look at the order before a big charge, a waiting period on a large commitment; say why it is there. A labelled recommendation, a real loss said once ("saved projects are removed after [date]"), an annual plan promoted with its real math, one honest retention offer and useful reminders all stay. Removing them is the failure, not the fix.

## If the flow already works

If a flow or line is already fair, say so and change at most a word. Do not add steps, warnings or softening for taste, and do not rebuild a good flow into a different one.

## Output

Deliver the requested text or redesign first, nothing before it. For "is this a dark pattern?", answer yes, no or partly in one line, name the part that steers, then give the fair version. When a count or set is asked for (three variants, one screen, a list), give exactly that, and check counts and length caps against the text instead of asserting them. Add at most two one-line notes, only for what the user must act on: a placeholder, an assumption, something to have checked. When conversion is the goal, one of the notes may name a regret signal to track. Answer in the user's language.

## Quick checks

- Leaving is no harder than joining, in steps and channel.
- Nothing costly, data-sharing or marketing is pre-selected; each optional box matches the rest.
- A neutral decline sits beside every accept, with equal weight.
- A retention offer is real, shown once, and no other exit is blocked.
- No fear without a remedy, and no shame at all.
- If the person saw how this works, would they mind? Calm and informed, would they still pick it later?
- Nothing invented; counts and caps checked against the text.
