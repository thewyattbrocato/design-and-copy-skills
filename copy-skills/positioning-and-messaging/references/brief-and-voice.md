# Message brief and voice guide

## The one-page brief

Write each field as a sentence a customer could say. Omit a field you have nothing true for rather than padding it; mark gaps in brackets.

1. **One-liner**: what it is, for whom, in a category word a stranger knows.
2. **Controlling idea**: the one sentence people repeat.
3. **For / not for**: the situation that makes someone care a lot, and who should look elsewhere.
4. **Their current fix**: the real alternatives, with what each does well.
5. **Differences**: each with its proof, or [unproven: how to show it], and what it lets the customer do.
6. **Objections**: the two or three real ones, each with a factual answer from what the user supplied.
7. **Proof on hand**: the figures, quotes and checkable facts the user gave, with basis and date. Everything else is a placeholder.
8. **Words**: the customer's phrases to use, the internal ones to avoid, one name per thing.
9. **Variants**: by role or buying stage. Emphasis and proof change; the claim does not.
10. **Voice**: see below.

Pages and emails draw from this brief and cite it, so a change to the claim is made once, here.

## Voice as habits

A voice is a few repeatable choices, not a mood. For each trait give: the habit, its boundary (the same trait taken too far), and a pair.

> **Plain about money.** We state the amount and the date before the reason. Not blunt to the point of cold.
> We say: "Your plan renews on 3 March for $12." We don't say: "A quick heads-up about your subscription journey!"

Then add:

- How tone shifts by surface: pricing, errors, onboarding, cancellation, apology. When a person is hurried or upset, the voice recedes to the plainest version.
- One anchor (a real sentence the product already uses that sounds right) and one deliberate departure (what we never do, even though competitors do).
- Mechanical choices where they matter: a word to prefer or ban, how numbers and dates are written.

The user's existing voice wins. With none, infer one from their own writing and label it inferred.

## Worked pair (invented product)

Before: "Parcelwise is a next-generation shipping solution built for modern small businesses."

After: "Parcelwise prints the right courier label for each order and tracks it for you, so a shop sending twenty parcels a day stops copying addresses into three courier sites." Their current fix: copy and paste between courier sites. Controlling idea: one place to ship, whichever courier is cheapest today. [unproven: "cheapest" needs a rate comparison the user can show.]
